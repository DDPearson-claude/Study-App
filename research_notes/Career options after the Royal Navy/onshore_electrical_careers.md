# UK onshore electrical and engineering careers for an ex-submariner marine engineer who is also a qualified electrician (leaving the RN around 2032)

Research date: 14 November 2026. All pay figures are 2025–2026 unless flagged. "Primary" means the employer's, regulator's or government's own page. Recruiter salary guides and trade blogs are marked as secondary.

## 1. Data centres: UK build-out, staffing demand, roles, pay, shift patterns, employers, ex-forces fit and certifications

### Takeaway
The UK government designated data centres as Critical National Infrastructure in September 2024. It has since named five AI Growth Zones (AIGZs), credited with £28.2bn of investment and 15,000+ jobs, and announced hyperscaler pledges of tens of billions of pounds (Microsoft £22bn, Google £5bn, Blackstone £10bn at Blyth). Electrically qualified shift engineers and critical environment technicians earned roughly £45k–£60k base in London and the South East in late 2026, typically on 12-hour continental rotas (14 shifts per 28 days). AWS runs an advert aimed at candidates with military experience, and Microsoft lists military trade certification as a preferred qualification. The jobs forecasts are contested, though: one think tank estimates permanent operational jobs at about a quarter of the industry figure.

### Cited Findings
**Policy and designation**
- On 12 September 2024 the Technology Secretary designated UK data centres as Critical National Infrastructure, the first new CNI designation since Space and Defence in 2015. It brings a dedicated CNI data infrastructure team, prioritised access to the NCSC and coordinated emergency-services access — [GOV.UK press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/data-centres-to-be-given-massive-boost-and-protections-from-cyber-criminals-and-it-blackouts)

- UK Compute Roadmap (July 2025): the UK "will need at least 6GW of AI-capable data centre capacity by 2030 – a threefold increase", with AIGZ sites of at least 500MW each by 2030 and at least one above 1GW — [UK Compute Roadmap PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6878b5dda52cca025ef5bd87/uk_compute_roadmap_web_accessible.pdf)

### Inferences
- An RN ETME (submariner) brings 24/7 watchkeeping, permit-to-work discipline, HV/LV switching, diesel generators, chilled water and HVAC, UPS/battery systems and alarm response. That maps closely onto shift engineer and CET duties. With an NVQ Level 3 and 18th Edition, the main gaps are likely to be civilian HV/LV Authorised Person tickets (often funded by the employer) and BMS/EPMS familiarity.
- AIGZ campuses (Newport from 2027, Cambois from 2028, Wales, Lanarkshire and the North East) will need permanent operations teams around 2030–2035. That suits a 2032 exit, though how many jobs there will be is disputed (techUK 40,200 vs Verdant about 10,400 by 2035).
- Realistic 2026 base pay is about £45k–£60k in London/M4 for experienced shift engineers, plus overtime and bonus. A 14-shifts-in-28 rota gives about 168 hours a month and long blocks off, but includes nights and weekends.

### Gaps
- No dated UK adverts with pay were found for Google, AWS (pay "competitive"), Digital Realty, Kao Data or Ark technician roles.
- The Uptime Institute 2026 survey primary page and the techUK full report were not opened; the figures above come from the techUK press release and secondary summaries.
- I found no data on what share of data centre hires are ex-forces, beyond AWS's military-focused advert and Microsoft's wording.

## 2. Industrial and maintenance: multi-skilled maintenance engineer and maintenance electrician (manufacturing, pharma, food), pay and shift premiums

### Takeaway
Multi-skilled or electrically biased maintenance engineers in UK food, FMCG and pharma earned a median base of about £52.5k in 2026. Rotating 4 on 4 off days and nights averaged about £53.6k, against about £46.9k for fixed days. Night work carries roughly a 12% premium, and dated adverts run from about £40k (rural, days) to £63.5k (automated food plant, 2D2N4O). Confident PLC fault-finding commands a clear premium.

### Inferences
- Submarine marine engineering (pumps, hydraulics, compressors, diesel, HVAC, LV switchboards) plus an NVQ 3 electrical qualification is close to the "multi-skilled, electrically biased" profile these adverts ask for. PLC/SCADA fault-finding is the most likely skills gap and is worth targeting with ELC funding before leaving.
- Of all the options, 4 on 4 off rotating work most resembles RN shift life: about 182 shifts a year and long breaks, but nights. Fixed-days roles pay about £5–7k less in base.
- Pharma pays a percentage shift premium (for example 27%) on top of base. Adverts often quote a total that already includes the allowance, so compare like for like.

### Gaps
- No ONS ASHE primary breakdown was opened for maintenance or "electricians and electrical fitters" (SOC 5241) by industry. The SimWest survey covers food and FMCG only and is a recruiter survey with an unstated sample size.
- No pharma-specific pay survey was found. Pharma figures come from individual adverts.

## 3. Employed electrical contracting: JIB national hourly rates for 2025 and 2026 by grade, annual earnings with overtime, London vs regional

### Takeaway
JIB national rates (job employed, transport provided) from 5 January 2026 are £18.38/hr for an Electrician, £20.08 for an Approved Electrician and £22.70 for a Technician, up 3.95% on the 2025 rates of £17.68, £19.32 and £21.84. Increases of 4.6% (January 2027) and 4.85% (January 2028) are already agreed. Inside the M25 the rates are about 12% higher (2026: £20.58 / £22.48 / £25.47). On a 37.5-hour week an Approved Electrician earns about £39k nationally or £44k in London before overtime, which is paid at time and a half, with double time after Saturday lunchtime.

- JIB rates bind only JIB-member firms, and many employers pay above them — [Elec-Mate](https://www.elec-mate.com/guides/jib-pay-scales-2026); [Total Skills](https://www.totalskills.co.uk/guides/electrician-salary-uk)

### Inferences
- Worked example (my arithmetic): a 2026 Approved Electrician on £20.08 working 5 hours a week overtime at time and a half earns about £39,156 + (£30.12 × 5 × 52 ≈ £7,830), roughly £47k nationally, or about £52.5k in London. Lodging-away work adds about £265 a week tax-free at £53.09 a night.
- Projected to 2032 (assumption only): if increases after 2028 continue at about 4% a year, the 2032 Approved national rate would be about £25.75 an hour. This is not an agreed figure.
- A new NVQ Level 3 + AM2 holder normally enters at the JIB "Electrician" grade (ECS Gold Card). Moving to Approved usually needs inspection and testing qualifications plus post-qualification experience (this grading rule is my understanding and was not verified on the JIB grading page).
- Contracting pays below data centre and FMCG maintenance base salaries, is more site-variable (travel, lodging) and is cyclical with construction, but it builds the commercial experience needed for self-employment.

### Gaps
- JIB grading criteria for Approved Electrician and Technician were not opened.
- No ECA report on typical take-home pay including overtime was found. Regional differences outside London are not set by the JIB, which has a single national rate, and no reliable regional pay survey for employed electricians was found.

## 4. Self-employed electrician: day rates, setup costs (scheme, insurance, test kit, van), Part P, realistic earnings, who succeeds and risks

### Takeaway
2026 day rates are about £250–£400 for domestic work (London £350–£500) and £300–£500 for commercial or specialist work. Labour-only subcontracting pays less (£220–£350). Joining a competent person scheme costs about £700–£850 in year one, and the total start-up (used van, tools, test kit, insurance, scheme) is about £12k–£22k. Trade guides suggest established sole traders keep about £40k–£55k net in the provinces, more in the South East. Cashflow (20% CIS deductions), pricing discipline and marketing drive success more than technical skill.

### Cited Findings
**Competent person schemes and Part P**
- NICEIC Domestic Installer 2026/27 fees: application £95 + VAT (£114) plus 12 months' certification £600 + VAT (£720), with assessment of one Qualified Supervisor included. Second year onward: £595 + VAT (£714), or £575 + VAT by single direct debit. Additional assessments £430 + VAT; optional pre-assessment £430 + VAT. Minimum £2m public liability insurance required — [NICEIC](https://niceic.com/for-the-trades-1/professional-standards/schemes/domestic-installer-scheme)
- NAPIT: the Electrotechnical Assessment Specification (EAS) membership covers commercial/industrial work or work outside England and Wales, with "initial fees from £605 + VAT" and renewal from £540 + VAT. The Competent Person Scheme covers Part P self-certification in England and Wales — [NAPIT electrical schemes](https://www.napit.org.uk/schemes/electrician.aspx)
- Secondary: NAPIT's electrical joining fee is £655 + VAT (£786 including VAT) per its form v1.4 (09.26), and its renewal price is not published. NICEIC's Approved Contractor + Domestic Installer bundle is £1,542 including VAT on entry. Part P self-certification applies in England; Wales, Scotland and NI run their own arrangements — [TrainAR, Oct 2026](https://trainar.ai/resources/niceic-or-napit)
- NAPIT scheme members must hold at least £2m public liability insurance and are responsible for their work's compliance with the Building Regulations for 6 years — [NAPIT Schemes Explained PDF](https://www.napit.org.uk/downloads/Schemes-Explained.pdf)

**Day rates and charge-out rates (trade and consumer guides, secondary)**
- Checkatrade-based typical rate is about £50/hr (usually £45–£60), with a day rate of about £400 and emergency rates of £80–£100/hr. Typical job prices: EICR about £180; consumer unit about £485; full rewire £3,900–£10,000 — [Ringzel, Oct 2026](https://ringzel.com/en-gb/blog/what-to-charge-as-an-electrician/)
- Domestic day rate £250–£400 (Checkatrade about £400; trade guides say £250–£350 is more typical); commercial £300–£500; specialist work (testing, EV, data centres, hazardous areas) £300–£450 — [Trade Tax Specialists, Aug 2026](https://www.tradetaxspecialists.co.uk/blog/cis-basics/self-employed-electrician-rates)
- Regional day rates for 2026: London £350–£500; South East £320–£450; South West £280–£400; Midlands £260–£380; North East £240–£350; Scotland £250–£380; Wales £240–£340. Specialist work (EV, solar/battery, fire alarms) commonly £350–£500 anywhere — [BuilderExpert, 10 Nov 2026](https://builderexpert.uk/electrician-day-rate-uk)
- Subcontract day rates are £220–£350, depending on region and sector — [Sendwork](https://blog.sendwork.com/electrician-rates-uk). Sole trader rates: South East £280–£380; Midlands/North £220–£300; first fix on new build £220–£280; commercial £300–£450 — [Trade2Base, May 2026](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/how-to-start-electrical-business-uk)

**Setup costs**
- A used panel van costs £8,000–£15,000; signwriting £300–£600; racking £400–£800; initial tools and test equipment £2,000–£4,000 (multifunction tester and similar) — [Trade2Base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/how-to-start-electrical-business-uk)
- An example annual cost build for a sole trader outside London: van £7,000; scheme £650; public liability (£5m) and professional indemnity £650; test instrument calibration and replacement fund £450 — [squote, Oct 2026](https://squote.app/knowledge/costs/electrician-hourly-rate-guide)
- Worked 2026/27 example: £280/day × 200 days gives £56,000 gross, minus £11,200 CIS deducted at 20%. Expenses include van costs of £6,500 (14,000 miles at HMRC 55p/25p), test kit and tooling £2,200, and insurance £700 — [Trade Tax Specialists](https://www.tradetaxspecialists.co.uk/blog/cis-basics/self-employed-electrician-rates)

**Realistic earnings**
- At £50/hr, 6–7 billable hours a day and 45–48 weeks, gross is £67.5k–£100.8k. After £10k–£18k of overheads, tax and NI, a provincial sole trader realistically takes home £40k–£55k; London/SE electricians charging £65–£80/hr can take more — [Sleepless Tradesman, Jan 2026](https://sleeplesstradesman.com/guides/how-to-set-up-self-employed-electrician-uk)
- Typical self-employed net profit is £45k–£70k (training provider, secondary) — [Total Skills](https://www.totalskills.co.uk/guides/electrician-salary-uk)
- The hourly rate should be calculated over 6.5–7 billable hours, not 8, because of unpaid certification and notification time — [squote](https://squote.app/knowledge/costs/electrician-hourly-rate-guide)

### Inferences
- Likely to succeed: people with good local networks, reliable admin and certification habits (BS 7671 paperwork), a niche such as EICRs for landlords, EV/solar/battery or commercial maintenance contracts, and a cash buffer of at least three months. RN habits of documentation, safety and reliability are an asset, but a submariner leaving in 2032 will probably need one to three years of civilian domestic or commercial experience first. Schemes require evidence of recent work, and the Qualified Supervisor needs technical-responsibility experience.
- Risks: irregular work, unpaid quoting time, CIS cashflow, late payment, no sick or holiday pay, 6-year compliance liability, van downtime, and price competition from low-rate subcontractors. Employed JIB rules ban side work, so a gradual move into self-employment while on the JIB is not allowed.
- A low-risk path is one to two years employed (JIB or maintenance) after leaving, then self-employment once local contacts and the NICEIC/NAPIT assessment are in place.

### Gaps
- No authoritative (official) statistics on self-employed electricians' earnings were found. All day-rate figures come from trade blogs and consumer guides.
- Insurance quotes vary widely and none were opened from an insurer.

## 5. Low-carbon electrical work (EV charge points, solar PV, battery storage, heat pumps): demand outlook to 2032, extra quals (MCS), pay

### Takeaway
The Warm Homes Plan commits £15bn this Parliament to upgrade up to 5 million homes by 2030. It targets up to 3 million more homes with rooftop solar and 450,000+ heat pump installs a year by 2030, and estimates up to 180,000 additional jobs. The Future Homes Standard takes effect on 24 March 2027 (low-carbon heat, and solar on most new homes). The ZEV mandate requires 80% of new cars to be zero-emission by 2030, and DfT estimates public charger demand at 250,000–550,000 in 2030. Electricians add Level 3 EV, solar PV or battery awards (about £300–£700 each), and firms need MCS (about £1.1k in year one) to access grants. Pay is about £35k–£50k employed or £300–£500/day self-employed.

### Cited Findings
**Warm Homes Plan and Future Homes Standard**
- By 2030 the Warm Homes Plan aims to triple the number of homes with solar ("panels on the rooftops of up to 3 million more homes"), deliver over 450,000 heat pump installations a year, and upgrade up to 5 million homes. It commits £15bn over this Parliament, supporting up to 180,000 additional jobs by 2030 — [GOV.UK Warm Homes Plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/warm-homes-plan/warm-homes-plan-html)
- WHP funding includes £2.7bn of grants and £2bn of low-interest loans ("universal offer on heat pumps, solar panels, batteries"). The Boiler Upgrade Scheme rises to £2.7bn (up to £7,500 per heat pump), and a £5bn Warm Homes Fund will be overseen by a new Warm Homes Agency — [GOV.UK WHP](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/warm-homes-plan/warm-homes-plan-html)
- Heat pump workforce: about 10,000 trained and active installers, roughly 4,000 FTE. This "would need to increase to approximately 12,000 FTE installers by 2030", a threefold increase — [GOV.UK WHP](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/warm-homes-plan/warm-homes-plan-html). The MCS Foundation instead cites about 8,650 accredited installers against a need for 40,000+ workers by 2030 — [MCS Foundation written evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/169349/html) (the two definitions differ)
- About 200,000 of the 450,000 heat pumps in 2030 are expected to be in new-build homes — [WHP Technical annex](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/warm-homes-plan/warm-homes-plan-technical-annex)
- Future Homes Standard: full technical specification published 24 March 2026, with standards in force from 24 March 2027 followed by a one-year transition. Gas boilers will not comply, and solar panels will be included on most new homes. BUS gives £9,000 from July 2026 (2026/27) for heat pumps replacing oil or LPG — [GOV.UK CBGDP heat and buildings factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/carbon-budget-and-growth-delivery-plan-heat-and-buildings-factsheet/cbgdp-investor-factsheet-heat-and-buildings-web-accessible)
- MCS-certified installations more than tripled from 117,717 (April 2021–March 2022) to 360,640 in 2025. 0% VAT on domestic renewables runs to March 2027 and then reverts to 5%. The median heat pump installation cost £12,908 in June 2026 — [MCS Foundation evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/169349/html)
- 25% of 2026 MCS installations were on new builds; 59% of homes built in Q1 2026 had MCS-certified solar — [MCS Low Carbon Landscape 2026](https://mcscertified.com/low-carbon-landscape-2026)
- Secondary (citing MCS): January–June 2026 saw 148,531 rooftop solar installs, 36,000 battery installs and just over 25,000 heat pumps — [Reonic](https://reonic.com/en-gb/blog/mcs-certification-cost)
- Under DESNZ schemes, microgeneration installers must hold MCS certification (or an equivalent for solar and batteries). Over 276,000 MCS-certified products were installed in 2025 — [GOV.UK WHP](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/warm-homes-plan/warm-homes-plan-html). The redeveloped MCS:2025 scheme was approved under the Clean Heat Market Mechanism from 12 December 2025 — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/clean-heat-market-mechanism-heat-pump-installation-certification-scheme)

**EV charging and the ZEV mandate**
- ZEV mandate: cars rise from 22% in 2024 to 80% by 2030, and vans from 10% to 70% by 2030 (from the September 2026 review consultation) — [DfT ZEV Mandate Review PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ab52a53734e2435f202f032/zev-mandate-review-consultation.pdf)
- DfT says the widely quoted "300,000 chargepoints by 2030" is "not a government target". The original 2022 estimate was a range of 280,000–720,000; the 2024 update gives 250,000–550,000 public chargers (EVSE) in 2030 — [GOV.UK EV charging statistics FAQ, Feb 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electric-vehicle-charging-device-statistics-information/electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-frequently-asked-questions)
- Secondary: the public network passed 121,000 chargers (Zapmap 2026). From 1 October 2026 the EAS expects each person fitting a chargepoint to hold a Level 3 EV qualification personally. EV courses cost about £300–£700 + VAT. Installers earn £300–£500/day, about £34,500 salaried, and £45k+ when experienced — [Reonic](https://reonic.com/en-gb/blog/how-to-become-ev-charger-installer)

Last registration is 31 August 2028 and the qualification fee is £90 — [City & Guilds](https://www.cityandguilds.com/qualifications-and-apprenticeships/building-services-industry/electrical-installation/2921-electric-vehicle-charging). Note: Reonic names the units 2921-31/32/33, which conflicts with City & Guilds' own 2921-34 listing; prefer City & Guilds.
- EV charger installation is notifiable under Part P. A domestic installation is invoiced at about £800–£1,500 and takes 3–5 hours (secondary) — [Total Skills EV guide](https://www.totalskills.co.uk/guides/how-to-become-ev-charger-installer)

**Solar PV and battery storage**
- Solar panel installer pay is £25,000 (starter) to £47,000 (experienced), on 40–45 hours a week — [National Careers Service](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/job-profiles/solar-panel-installer)
- Qualified electricians can add City & Guilds 2922-34 (Level 3 small solar PV) or LCL Awards' Level 3 solar PV and electrical energy storage (EESS) awards. Entry requires 18th Edition and Level 3 electrotechnical or an ECS Gold Card — [Solar Surveys guide, Oct 2026](https://solarsurveys.uk/guides/become-a-solar-installer)
- Secondary: employed solar installers earn £35k–£50k and self-employed £300–£500/day; battery add-ons are worth £3k–£8k per job — [Total Skills solar guide](https://www.totalskills.co.uk/guides/how-to-become-solar-panel-installer)

**MCS certification costs (firm-level)**
- Secondary (citing MCS): for one technology, year one costs about £1,090 plus £30 per installation (certification body about £650, consumer code £385, MCS fee £55), then about £890 a year. A financial protection product is needed for each installation under the redeveloped scheme — [Reonic](https://reonic.com/en-gb/blog/mcs-certification-cost)
- NICEIC MCS for one technology costs £855 ex VAT in year one for 2026/27 (£95 application + £705 certification + £55 licence). NAPIT starts from £830 ex VAT, or £1,100 for two technologies such as PV and battery — [Solar Surveys](https://solarsurveys.uk/guides/mcs-certification-solar-installers). This conflicts with Reonic's reading of a 2025 NICEIC fee sheet (£675 application for one technology); the fee years differ.

### Inferences
- Demand to 2032 looks strongest for solar plus battery (retrofit and new-build under the FHS from 2027–28) and home EV charging (ZEV 80% in 2030). Heat pumps are mainly plumbing/heating work, but the electrical side (supply upgrades, controls) is a niche for electricians.
- Demand is policy-dependent: grant levels, the VAT change in April 2027 and ZEV mandate review outcomes could move volumes. Low-carbon work fits best as an add-on to self-employment or a job with an MCS-certified firm, rather than as a stand-alone employed career.
- A submariner's battery and DC systems background is relevant to battery storage work, but civilian Level 3 awards are still needed.

### Gaps
- No primary data was found on employed EV/solar installer salaries beyond the National Careers Service figure.
- The MCS primary cost page was not opened directly (it was cited via Reonic).
- No forecast was found for the number of electrician jobs specifically needed in EV and solar to 2032.

## 7. Ex-forces job support: CTP (including CTP Assist), Forces Employment Charity, Armed Forces Covenant/ERS employers, Civil Service guaranteed-interview scheme, veteran-friendly recruiters

### Takeaway
Resettlement entitlement depends on exact length of service. The full Core Resettlement Programme (training grant, up to 35 days of resettlement time) requires more than six years, while 4–6 years gives only the lighter Employment Support Programme. ELC lower tier needs six years of qualifying service and must be registered while serving. A leaver at "about six years" should therefore check eligibility early: serving just over six years unlocks considerably more support. CTP Assist is for personnel on recovery duties and medical leavers, not routine leavers. The Civil Service's "Great Place to Work for Veterans" scheme guarantees progression to the next stage for most vacancies if minimum criteria are met, with no time limit after leaving. 162 employers received ERS Gold in 2026.

### Cited Findings
**CTP programmes**
- The CTP is a partnering agreement between the MOD and Reed in Partnership, with the Forces Employment Charity, since 1 October 2024 — [GOV.UK CTP](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/career-transition-partnership); [Service Leavers' Guide June 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4e19cc09605a87f4354531/Service_Leavers_Guide_June_26.pdf)
- Core Resettlement Programme: "available to personnel who have served for more than six years", from two years before discharge to two years after, with up to 35 days of Graduated Resettlement Time depending on length of service. Employment Support Programme: 4–6 years' service, from two years before to two years after discharge. CTP Future Horizons: under 4 years — [GOV.UK CTP](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/career-transition-partnership); [GOV.UK employment support](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/getting-employment-support-when-leaving-the-armed-forces)
- CTP Assist "is available to support personnel on Recovery Duties (PRD) and other service leavers who face significant additional challenges", "regardless of the time served" — [GOV.UK CTP](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/career-transition-partnership). PRD leavers are referred by their unit and paired with Specialist Employment Consultants at Personnel Recovery Units, including Plymouth and Edinburgh — [Forces Employment Charity CTP Assist](https://www.forcesemployment.org.uk/programmes/career-transition-partnership/ctp-assist)

Wording conflict: GOV.UK says "more than six years" while DMB says "6+ years"; confirm with the resettlement office.
- RN resettlement centres include RC Plymouth (HMS Drake), RC Portsmouth (HMS Nelson) and RRC Rosyth (HMS Caledonia) — [Service Leavers' Guide June 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4e19cc09605a87f4354531/Service_Leavers_Guide_June_26.pdf)

**Learning credits**
- ELC: £1,000 (lower tier) or £2,000 (higher tier) per financial year, for up to three years, or an aggregated lower-tier claim of £3,000. - Electrical examples: ELC can fund City & Guilds Level 3 courses (for example 2365 Level 3, 2382 18th Edition, 2391 Inspection and Testing) at ELCAS-approved centres, but not Level 2 — [Total Skills military-to-electrician guide](https://www.totalskills.co.uk/guides/military-to-electrician)

- The Forces Employment Charity works with Reed in Partnership on CTP delivery for all service leavers: CVs, training funding advice, jobs, events — [Forces Employment Charity](https://www.forcesemployment.org.uk/who-we-help/service-leavers/)
- Op ASCEND is a free, government-backed scheme run by the Forces Employment Charity for veterans who left more than 2 years ago. It offers specialist advisers, tailored pathways and direct access to veteran-friendly employers. FEC also advises on setting up a business — [GOV.UK finding a job as a veteran](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/finding-a-job-as-a-veteran)

**Civil Service schemes**
- Great Place to Work for Veterans: if minimum criteria are met, veterans "progress to the next stage for most vacancies – whether that's an interview or an online test" — [Civil Service Careers](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/great-place-to-work-for-veterans)
- Eligibility: at least one year in HM Armed Forces (Regular or Reserve); in transition or left; not already a civil servant. "There is no maximum time limit from when you left" — [GOV.UK, 2020](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/making-the-civil-service-a-great-place-to-work-for-veterans)
- Going Forward into Employment allows direct appointment to roles up to first-line manager, on fixed terms of up to two years — [GOV.UK employers' guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/an-employers-guide-to-hiring-veterans/an-employers-guide-to-hiring-veterans-html)

**Covenant and ERS employers, veteran-friendly recruiters**
- Covenant signatories can make pledges such as guaranteed interview schemes; a searchable signatory list exists — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/finding-a-job-as-a-veteran)
- ERS Gold holders must have engaged with the CTP in recruiting service leavers and registered on the Forces Families Jobs portal (criteria as of September 2025) — [GOV.UK ERS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-employer-recognition-scheme/defence-employer-recognition-scheme)
- 162 employers received ERS Gold on 15 July 2026, across manufacturing, construction, transport and other sectors — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/162-employers-across-the-uk-recognised-for-going-above-and-beyond-for-the-armed-forces-community)
- Examples of Gold holders: Network Rail (about a tenth of its workforce is from an Armed Forces background; it partners with CTP) — [Network Rail](https://www.networkrail.co.uk/careers/armed-forces); Northrop Grumman UK — [Northrop Grumman](https://www.northropgrumman.com/who-we-are/global-presence/northrop-grumman-in-the-uk/uk-careers/veterans-and-reservists-uk); VGC Group (construction; ex-forces recruitment programme) — [VGC](https://vgcgroup.co.uk/news/defence-employer-recognition-scheme-gold-award)
- Engineering recruiter Carbon60 holds ERS Gold (2024) and calls itself one of the UK's "Top 25 Employers for Veterans" — [Carbon60](http://www.carbon60global.com/blog/carbon60-carbon60-project-services-once-again-receive-gold-in-the-defence-employer-recognition-scheme)
- Forces-focused job boards carry defence engineering adverts: the White Ensign job board (for example Babcock submarine roles) — [White Ensign](https://www.whiteensign.co.uk/jobs/marine-engineer-helensburgh-gb-g84-8hl); Forces Families Jobs — [Forces Families Jobs](https://www.forcesfamiliesjobs.co.uk/jobs/electrical-engineering-technician-devonport-plymouth-gb-pl1-4sg/2755590-1). Other routes are listed by the Covenant (Step Into Health, Advance into Justice, RBL, Cobseo Employment Cluster) — [Armed Forces Covenant sources of help](https://www.armedforcescovenant.gov.uk/sources-of-help/)

### Inferences
- The six-year threshold matters most here. Serving just over six years secures CRP (IRTC grant, resettlement time) and ELC lower tier (registered in service), which together could fund an 18th Edition update, 2391 inspection and testing, an EV/solar award or a PLC course before 2032. Leaving at exactly six years or less risks dropping to ESP and losing ELC lower tier. Confirm exact dates with the unit resettlement officer and register for ELC now.
- For civilian MOD/SDA/DE&S posts, the Great Place to Work scheme effectively guarantees an interview if the minimum criteria are met, and there is no expiry.
- No primary evidence was found of a formal "guaranteed interview for veterans" scheme at the named data centre operators. AWS's military-focused advert is the closest equivalent.

### Gaps
- The ERS Gold 2026 list itself was not opened, so it is not confirmed whether Babcock, BAE, CBRE, JLL or Equinix hold Gold.
- No primary JSP 822 page was opened for the ELC rules (secondary summaries citing JSP 822 were used).
- No list of veteran-specialist electrical recruiters (as opposed to general engineering ones) was found.

## 8. Overall comparison of onshore options: pay range, work-life balance, job security, use of submarine plus electrician experience

### Takeaway
Data centres and FMCG/pharma shift maintenance currently pay the highest base salaries for this profile (about £45k–£63k), but involve 12-hour day/night rotas. Defence-support work makes the most direct use of submarine experience, with daytime hours and long programmes, at about £41k–£55k. Employed JIB contracting starts lowest (£36k–£44k base nationally) but is the stepping stone to self-employment, which has the widest range (about £40k–£70k net) and the highest risk. Low-carbon work is best treated as an add-on specialism.

### Inferences
- Best pay in the early years: London/M4 data centre shift engineer, or an automated FMCG/pharma plant on rotating shifts (about £55–60k base in 2026 money).
- Best use of submarine experience and long-term security: Babcock, SDA or Rolls-Royce submarine support at Clyde, Devonport, Barrow, Derby or Bristol, though base pay is somewhat lower and roles are location-locked to naval bases.
- Best work-life balance: defence day roles (Faslane is a Monday to Thursday, 36-hour week) or fixed-day maintenance roles. Continental 4 on 4 off gives many days off but includes nights.
- Highest ceiling and highest risk: self-employment, ideally after one to two years' civilian experience and combined with EICR, EV or solar niches.
- Suggested pre-2032 preparation (inference): secure CRP and ELC eligibility, then use ELC/IRTC for 18th Edition (current amendment), 2391 Inspection and Testing, HV Authorised Person training, PLC fault-finding, and possibly CDCTP or Level 3 EV/solar awards.

### Gaps
- All pay figures are 2025–2026. No reliable forecast of 2032 pay exists beyond JIB rates agreed to 2028.
- No like-for-like total-compensation comparison (pension and benefits) across sectors was found. Civil Service and defence pensions are likely more valuable, but this was not quantified.
- ONS ASHE primary tables were not opened, so official occupational medians are secondary-sourced.
