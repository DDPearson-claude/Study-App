# Qualifications and funding for an RN ET(ME) Submariner who is also a nearly qualified electrician (research as of November 2026)

Scope: UK only. Sources were checked between 9 and 14 November 2026 unless a page date says otherwise. Provider prices are examples taken from individual training-centre pages. They vary by centre and date, and some include VAT while others do not; each one is marked.

---

## 1. What civilian qualifications and registrations RN ET(ME) Submariner training gives, and the EngTech and IEng routes

### Takeaway
The submariner ME branch gives a Level 2 maritime apprenticeship on entry. Level 3 (NVQ, diploma or apprenticeship) and then a Foundation Degree come with the Leading Hand and Petty Officer qualifying courses. Leading Hand with an NVQ Level 3 is the realistic EngTech point inside a six-year career. IMarEST, IET and IMechE all run Navy-specific fast-track routes; the IET and IMechE routes charge no application fee for EngTech, and the MOD reimburses fees. IEng through the Navy routes needs at least Petty Officer, a Foundation Degree, HNC or HND, and post-course experience. That may be out of reach before 2032.

### Cited Findings
**What the role gives**
- On the RN careers page, the ET(ME) Submariner role lists "A Level 2 Apprenticeship in Maritime Mechanical Electrical Mechanic" as the qualification gained. The "opportunities" listed are GCSEs and A-levels, "a Level 3 Advanced Apprenticeship in Engineering Manufacture", and "eventually... a degree like Mechanical Engineering, depending on your career progression". — [RN: ET(ME) Submariner](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/roles/engineering-technician-marine-submariner)
- The GOV.UK apprenticeship vacancy for the same role (posted 9 July 2026) names the standard as "Maritime mechanical and electrical mechanic (level 2)" and the award as "a Level 2 Diploma in Maritime Mechanical and Electrical Mechanic". Training is 10 weeks of basic training, 33 weeks of role training, then submarine equipment training and sea time, ending in the Dolphins exam. — [Find an apprenticeship: ME (Submariner)](https://www.findapprenticeship.service.gov.uk/apprenticeship/VAC2000041643)
- By comparison, Accelerated Apprentice (Submariner) entrants follow "Maritime mechanical fitter (level 3)", which lasts 3 years 6 months and is described as "equivalent to 2 A-Levels". — [Find an apprenticeship: AAS (Submariner) ME](https://www.findapprenticeship.service.gov.uk/apprenticeship/reference/2000041681). The AAS role page lists "An Advanced Apprenticeship (level 3) in Engineering" and "the opportunity to complete a Foundation Degree in Mechanical or Electronic Engineering depending on your career progression". — [RN: AAS Submariner](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/roles/accelerated-apprentice-scheme-submariner)
- Degree Top Up Scheme entrants (direct-entry Leading Hands) get a Level 3 Diploma (Maritime Mechanical Fitter or Maritime Electrical Fitter), a Foundation Degree in Mechanical or Electronic Engineering, a CMI Level 5 Certificate in Leadership and Management, and a "Fully paid BEng(Hons)" after promotion to Petty Officer. — [RN: Submarine Engineering Rating – Degree Top Up](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/roles/submarine-engineering-rating---degree-top-up-scheme)
- Foundation degree mechanism (shown for Weapon Engineering): the 26-week POETQC at HMS Collingwood leads to the "Award of Foundation degree from Portsmouth University for successful completion of Leading Engineering Technician and Petty Officer Engineering Technician Qualifying Courses". — [RN IDT: POETQC](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/international-defence-training/onsite-courses/weapon-engineering/petty-officer-engineering-technician-qualifying)
- 2017 (old; may have changed): the redesigned 18-month LETQC (ME) at HMS Sultan gave a Technical Certificate so trainees could "progress to obtain a level 3 NVQ shortly upon return to sea as LETs". — [Navy News, 13 Mar 2017](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/news/2017/march/13/170313-engineering-revolution-continues-to-gather-pace)
- HMS Sultan's Nuclear Department (part of the Defence Academy) trains in nuclear engineering, radiation protection and nuclear safety, and "collaborates with the civil nuclear industry for training purposes". — [RN: HMS Sultan](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/locations-and-operations/bases-and-stations/hms-sultan)

**EngTech: Navy-specific routes**
- IET, Defence Engineer Registration Scheme (DERS), RN Marine Engineering: EngTech requires an "NVQ L3 qualification in your trade, or suitable qualification at QCF3... or above; or completion of a programme leading to NVQ L3". The application is endorsed by your ETO and sent to the IET, and you must be an IET member before applying. — [IET: Professional registration as a marine engineer (RN)](https://www.theiet.org/career/professional-registration/professional-registration-for-the-armed-forces/professional-registration-in-the-royal-navy/professional-registration-as-a-marine-engineer)
- IET Armed Forces fees (current year): the EngTech application costs £0. Technician Membership (TMIET) is £100, a discounted Armed Forces rate. Student or apprentice membership is £36 for the length of the course. The Engineering Council EngTech entrance fee is £22.26 and the annual fee £24.06. A registered EngTech member pays £221 a year (£228 next year). — [IET: Armed Forces fees](https://www.theiet.org/membership/become-a-member/armed-forces-membership/membership-fees-for-armed-forces-members)
- IMechE Special Application Process (SAP) for RN Marine Engineers: open to those "serving in the Royal Navy, or [who] have left the Service within the last 12 months". EngTech is open to "Personnel with NVQ3 or on completion of their apprenticeship", with "No application fee - No interview". Fees are reimbursed by the MOD each year under "2024DIN01-071". The SAP IEng route is for "Senior Ratings, Warrant Officers and Engineering Officers" and needs "HND, FdEng, BEng Hons or the equivalent". Free apprentice membership is offered if you are "not yet ready for EngTech". — [IMechE: Royal Navy](http://imeche.org/membership-registration/become-a-member/armed-forces-and-mod/royal-navy)
- IMarEST Defined Route (with the RN): a short form, confirmation of RN qualifications and certificates, then a mandatory Professional Review Interview. It is available for EngTech/MarEngTech, IEng/IMarEng and CEng/CMarEng. — [IMarEST: Royal Navy defined route](https://www.imarest.org/membership/defined-route/uk-royal-navy.html)
- IMarEST RN application form (2026), Submarine Service EngTech route for a Leading Engineering Technician (ME): certified copies of the "Level 3 NVQ Certificate", "Advanced Modern Apprenticeship Certificate", "Professional Provisional Examination for PO ET (ME)" and "Nuclear Propulsion Systems Course (NPSC) Certificate", plus a CPD plan and code of conduct. The PO ET(ME) Submarine EngTech route needs an FdEng, HND, OND or HNC plus POETQC. The Submarine IEng route needs ET(ME) with a BEng, POETQC, Category B Nuclear Watchkeeping, NPSC and NPOC; or an FdEng, HND or HNC with POETQC, Category A2 Nuclear Watchkeeping, NPSC, NPOC, NPSupC and a Nuclear Chief of the Watch assignment order. — [IMarEST RN Marine Engineer application form (PDF, 2026)](https://www.imarest.org/static/c189b876-05e8-430d-a260379d5f2642db/3d60f3ff-9891-4456-b4bbc97a174ef8f4/RN-Application-Form-Marine-Engineer-26.pdf)
- IMarEST: MarEngTech, a marine-speciality badge on top of EngTech, costs "£32 after an initial fee" a year. — [IMarEST: EngTech](https://www.imarest.org/membership/professional-registration/engineering/engineering-technician.html)
- The IET also runs a separate "Electrician EngTech" route for electricians, "based on the qualifications and experience held by electricians". — [IET: EngTech for Electricians](https://www.theiet.org/career/professional-registration/engineering-technician-engtech-for-electricians)

**IEng: what it needs**
- IET DERS, RN ME, IEng: substantive rank of at least PO ET(ME), having completed the post-Faraday POETQC (or the pre-Faraday PO route with 2 years in a Technical Engineering Appointment). You also need "a Technical Engineering Appointment in the substantive rank, post completion of training, for at least two years", and "an appropriate Bachelors degree... or a Higher National Certificate, Diploma or Foundation Degree... plus evidence of additional learning". Equivalent knowledge can be considered. — [IET: RN marine engineer registration](https://www.theiet.org/career/professional-registration/professional-registration-for-the-armed-forces/professional-registration-in-the-royal-navy/professional-registration-as-a-marine-engineer)
- IET Armed Forces fees: IEng application £261, Engineering Council IEng entrance £54.29, annual £41.97. — [IET: Armed Forces fees](https://www.theiet.org/membership/become-a-member/armed-forces-membership/membership-fees-for-armed-forces-members)
- Engineering Council 2026–27 guide: registration is through a licensed institution, so join "one closest to the discipline of engineering you work in". IMarEST is licensed for EngTech, IEng and CEng. — [Engineering Council Guide to Professional Registration 2026-27 (PDF)](https://www.engc.org.uk/media/irxjp1vg/guide-to-professional-registration-26-27.pdf)
- Open University BEng (Hons) Q65: "Your study will give you the skills expected of an Incorporated Engineer". — [OU: BEng Q65](https://www.open.ac.uk/courses/engineering/degrees/bachelor-of-engineering-q65)

### Inferences
- A six-year ET(ME) SM career probably ends at Leading Hand, or possibly a junior Petty Officer. EngTech (by NVQ L3, or the IMarEST Submarine LET route with NPSC) is the registration to target while serving. IEng through DERS or SAP needs PO plus at least 2 years in post, so it is unlikely before 2032. IEng is more realistic later through the standard civilian route, using an HNC, FdEng or OU BEng plus experience.
- The IMechE SAP route closes 12 months after leaving. If EngTech is wanted through IMechE, apply before leaving or by about mid-2033 at the latest.
- The IET suits an electrician-leaning career (BS 7671 publisher, Electrician EngTech, 25% book discount). IMarEST suits a marine or offshore career, and the MarEngTech badge links to its Navy-specific nuclear-course recognition. Registration can be moved between institutions later.
- Catch: the IMarEST LET Submarine route lists both a "Level 3 NVQ Certificate" and an "Advanced Modern Apprenticeship Certificate". It is not clear which Level 3 a current ET(ME) SM receives. The role page's "Level 3 Advanced Apprenticeship in Engineering Manufacture" wording differs from the "Maritime mechanical/electrical fitter (level 3)" wording used for the AAS and DTS entries. Ask the Education Officer or ETO.

### Gaps
- No primary source confirmed which university awards the ME-branch (rather than WE) Foundation Degree, or whether a standard-entry ET(ME) SM (not a DTS entrant) gets a funded BEng top-up.
- The exact current Level 3 award for standard-entry ET(ME) SM at Leading Hand could not be confirmed from public pages.
- The Engineering Council UK-SPEC IEng page returned a 404, so the IEng criteria above come from the IET and IMechE armed forces pages.
- Return-of-service or notice-period rules attached to RN courses (which could affect leaving "around 2032") were not researched.

## 2. Electrician pathway after the NVQ Level 3: AM2, JIB grades, ECS Gold Card and BS 7671 (2382, 2391, 2396)

### Takeaway
NVQ Level 3 plus the AM2 (or AM2E/AM2S) gives the JIB "Electrician" grade and, with a BS 7671 qualification and the ECS HS&E test, the ECS Gold Card. "Approved Electrician" also needs 2 years working as an electrician and C&G 2391-52. "Technician" needs 5 years as Approved plus Level 4, for example C&G 2396 or an HNC. BS 7671 changed during 2026: Amendment 4:2026 was published on 15 April 2026 and became the only valid version from 15 October 2026. The matching 2382-26 is now the course to take. No date has been announced for a 19th Edition, and one may appear before 2032.

### Cited Findings
**AM2 family**
- NET: "The AM2 should be taken by those following the Level 3 NVQ (C&G 2357 or EAL 1605)". It "is the final unit within the NVQ and should not be taken until all other units have been completed". Apprentices on the Standard take the AM2S (pre-September 2023) or the AM2S v1 (from September 2023). — [NET: AM2](https://www.netservices.org.uk/am2); [NET: AM2S](https://www.netservices.org.uk/am2s); [NET: AM2S v1](https://www.netservices.org.uk/am2s-v1)
- NET recommended first-attempt fees as of 1 October 2026, quoted by a centre: AM2 £885, AM2S/AM2E £965, AM2S v1 £1,340, AM2D/AM2ED £1,535, all excluding VAT. Centres may vary. — [Logic4training: AM2](https://www.logic4training.co.uk/courses/electrical/electrical-level-3-nvq/am2-assessment). Another centre lists AM2E v1 (2346 registered after 4 September 2023) at £1,235 + VAT, with resits from £190 + VAT per section. — [Electrical Courses Ltd: AM2](https://www.electricalcoursesuk.co.uk/am2)
- The AM2 is a 3-day assessment; one centre charges £1,062 inc VAT (AM2) and £1,158 inc VAT (AM2S/AM2E). — [Able Skills: AM2](https://www.ableskills.co.uk/electrician-training-courses/am2-electrical-course)

**JIB grades (JIB Handbook 2026)**
- Electrician: a registered apprenticeship, or the full Level 3 NVQ (Electrotechnical Services / Installing Electrotechnical Systems) or the Level 3 Apprenticeship Standard, "plus the industry assessment of occupational competence (the AM2)". The JIB Mature Candidate Assessment or the Experienced Worker Qualification with AM2E also count. — [JIB Handbook Sept 2026 (PDF)](https://www.jib.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/JIB-Handbook-September-2026-nc.pdf)
- Approved Electrician: must have met the Electrician grade, plus "two years' experience working as an Installation or Maintenance Electrician subsequent to the satisfactory completion of training and immediately prior to the application". You also need inspection and testing competence, for example "C&G 2391-52... OR Both the C&G 2391-50... AND the C&G 2391-51", or EAL/LCL equivalents, or the NET AAC. — [JIB Handbook 2026, Section 4](https://www.jib.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Handbook-2026-Section-4-Grading-Definitions.pdf)
- Technician: Approved grade plus "five years' experience working as an Approved Electrician", plus "A higher technical qualification... Level 4... (such as an HNC...)" or "A recognised BS7671 course in Design and Verification". Site Technician also needs 3 years in a supervisory capacity. — [JIB Handbook Sept 2026 (PDF)](https://www.jib.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/JIB-Handbook-September-2026-nc.pdf)
- ECS summary: Approved needs Core grade plus at least 2 years; Technician needs Approved "for at least five years... with at least three years in a supervisory capacity" and Level 4 or above. — [ECS: JIB Grading](https://www.ecscard.org.uk/content/JIB-Grading)

**ECS Gold Card and HS&E**
- The ECS card's reverse can show "additional vocational qualifications such as inspection and testing qualifications or... CompEx". The Installation Electrician gold card requires the industry Level 3 apprenticeship standard or an approved JIB Level 3 competency-based qualification. — [JIB Handbook 2026 Section 10 (ECS)](https://www.jib.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Handbook-2026-Section-10-Electrotechnical-Certification-Scheme.pdf)
- ECS HS&E Assessment: a pass is "valid for two years" for applying for a card. It can be taken online with remote invigilation through MyECS, and the CITB/CSCS test is not accepted. — [ECS: HS&E Assessment](https://www.ecscard.org.uk/HSE-Assessment). One partner centre charges £47.50 + VAT per assessment. — [ISCVE: ECS assessments](https://iscve.org.uk/ecs-card-scheme/ecs-card-assessments). ECS fees are on the [ECS Fees page](https://www.ecscard.org.uk/content/Fees), which links a payment-details PDF (not opened).
- Secondary source (Elec-Mate, quoting ECS, October 2026): the gold card needs Level 3, a current HS&E and "a recognised BS7671:2018 qualification", and is "typically valid for three years". The Experienced Worker card (Level 3 technical certificate without the NVQ) lasts "18 months, not renewable". — [Elec-Mate: ECS gold card requirements](https://www.elec-mate.com/guides/ecs-gold-card-requirements-2026)

**BS 7671 status and future**
- IET: "BS 7671:2018+A4:2026 has now been published". The Brown Book (A2:2022 + A3:2024) "remains a valid standard until 15 October 2026". A4 adds a chapter on stationary secondary batteries, functional earthing and bonding for ICT equipment, Power over Ethernet, and a major revision of Section 710 (medical locations). — [IET: Updates to 18th Edition](http://www.theiet.org/bs-7671-18th-edition-wiring-regulations/updates-to-18th-edition)
- Amendment 3:2024 was issued on 31 July 2024. It added definitions and a regulation on bidirectional and unidirectional devices (for example solar and battery). — [IET: BS 7671:2018 A3:2024 PDF](https://electrical.theiet.org/media/1g5mcnus/bs-7671-2018-amendment-3-2024.pdf)
- City & Guilds: "2382-26 is the latest version (Amendment 4)... centres must use this version from 16 April 2026". The qualification fee is £69 (C&G typical price), with 35 guided learning hours. The 2382-26 last registration date is 31 December 2029 and last certification 31 August 2030. — [C&G 2382](https://www.cityandguilds.com/qualifications-and-apprenticeships/building-services-industry/electrical-installation/2382-requirements-for-electrical-installations)
- 19th Edition: a secondary source says that as of 14 October 2026 "we found no announced date for a 19th Edition in the IET and BSI sources we checked". — [electrician.wiki: BS 7671](https://electrician.wiki/standards/bs-7671). An electrical contractor's blog guesses "around 2028 at the earliest" from the roughly 10-year edition cycle (17th in 2008, 18th in 2018). This is explicitly "an educated guess, not a schedule". — [Capital Electricians blog](https://capitalelectrician.co.uk/blog/bs-7671-amendment-3-and-19th-edition)
- 2382-26 course prices (3 days, classroom): £395 + VAT (LCL equivalent) — [Skills Training Group](https://www.skillstg.co.uk/courses/18th-edition-courses); £415 inc VAT — [The BTC](https://thebtc.co.uk/brochure/18th-edition-courses/); £495 + VAT — [PASS](https://www.electricaltrainingcourse.co.uk/18th-edition/18th-edition-2382); £525 + VAT — [Waterside](https://www.waterside-training.co.uk/commercial-training/electrical-engineering/18th-edition-wiring-regulations); £650 (3.5 days) — [Glasgow Kelvin](https://www.glasgowkelvin.ac.uk/business-industry/city-guilds-18th-edition-iet-wiring-regulations-2382-26). A 1-day A4 update plus exam for existing 2382-22 holders costs £195 + VAT — [PASS update](https://www.pass.co.uk/city-and-guilds-2382-18th-edition-amendment-4-update-course).

**C&G 2391 (inspection and testing)**
- Three options: 2391-50 Initial Verification, 2391-51 Periodic Inspection, and 2391-52 Combined. Assessment is an online multiple-choice test plus a practical. The 2391-52 C&G fee is £157, with 50 GLH and 67 hours TQT. The last registration date is 31 August 2028. Assessments are "to be amended to align with amendment 4". — [C&G 2391](https://www.cityandguilds.com/qualifications-and-apprenticeships/building-services-industry/electrical-installation/2391-initial-and-periodic-electrical-inspection-and-testing)
- Course examples: 5 days over 5 weeks (Thursdays), £995 inc exams, "time served" and ideally 2382 required — [West Suffolk College](https://www.wsc.ac.uk/courses/plumbing-and-electrical/207-level-3/15473-city-and-guilds-2391-52-combined-inspection-and-testing-26et574037); 5 days, £1,190 — [Crawley College](https://www.crawley.ac.uk/courses/city-and-guilds-initial-and-periodic-electrical-inspection-and-testing-2391-52/); 7 days over 2 weeks, £1,300, needs a minimum of 2 years' industry experience — [Glasgow Kelvin](https://www.glasgowkelvin.ac.uk/business-industry/city-guilds-level-3-award-in-inspection-and-testing-2391-52)

**C&G 2396 (design, Level 4)**
- Level 4 Award in the Design and Verification of Electrical Installations (2396-01), for experienced electricians working "with minimal or no supervision". Assessed by an assignment and a written exam; 30 GLH and 100 TQT. The C&G fee is £108.80. The last registration date is 31 August 2028. — [C&G 2396](https://www.cityandguilds.com/qualifications-and-apprenticeships/building-services-industry/electrical-installation/2396-design-erection-and-verification)
- Course example: 3 months of evenings plus a 40-hour home project; entry needs 2391-52 (or 2394/5) and 2382; £790. — [Crawley College 2396](https://www.crawley.ac.uk/courses/electrical-installations-design-and-verification-2396-01/)

### Inferences
- Approved grade may have to wait until after leaving. JIB wants 2 years "working as an Installation or Maintenance Electrician... immediately prior to the application". Submarine ME service may not be accepted as that experience; the JIB has not been asked. If it is not accepted, Approved arrives about 2034 and Technician about 2039 at the earliest. Getting 2391-52 and 2396 while serving still helps, because the qualifications will be ready.
- Last-registration dates (2391 and 2396 on 31 August 2028, 2382-26 on 31 December 2029) suggest City & Guilds will replace or re-version these awards around then. That could be tied to a possible 19th Edition. If 2382-26 is taken now, expect to need a 19th Edition update course before or soon after 2032.
- The ECS gold card lasts about 3 years and the HS&E pass 2 years. A card bought in 2027 would need renewing around 2030 and again soon after leaving, so time the first card for when the NVQ and AM2 are done.
- A4's new content (stationary batteries, ICT functional earthing, PoE) is directly relevant to data-centre and battery-storage work. A 2382-26 certificate is a sensible early buy.
- Practical risk: the NVQ L3 (C&G 2357, or 2346 for experienced workers) needs site evidence of electrical installation work. It is unclear whether this can be collected while serving on submarines, so it may need leave periods or a civilian employer. The AM2 cannot be booked until all other NVQ units are complete.

### Gaps
- The cost of an ECS Gold Card application and of JIB grading was not opened (payment-details PDF not fetched).
- Whether RN marine engineering experience counts towards the JIB "two years' experience" for Approved grade: no source found.
- Which NVQ (2357 or 2346) and therefore which AM2 variant applies depends on the person's registration, which is not known.
- Whether the NVQ portfolio and AM2 can be completed while serving (evidence rules, leave): no source found.

## 3. Specialist qualifications in demand: cost, duration, validity

### Takeaway
Most offshore and hazardous-area tickets expire: GWO BST every 2 years, BOSIET 4 years, CompEx 5 years, CCNSG 3 years, offshore medicals about 2 years. Buy them in the last 6–12 months of service, not early. Non-expiring or long-life awards are safe to collect early: EV charging (2921-34), solar PV and battery (2922-34/2923-34), and PLC courses. HV Authorised Person or Senior Authorised Person courses only prepare you; the authorisation itself is a written appointment by the employer. No EUSR or ECITB HV authorisation scheme was found.

### Cited Findings
**CompEx (hazardous areas, gas and vapour Ex01–Ex04)**
- Five days, with an online exam (60 questions, 1.5 hours) plus four practical assessments. Successful candidates must take the CompEx refresher "every five years". — [SETA Southampton: CompEx Ex01–04](https://www.setatraining.co.uk/courses/compex_ex01-04.html)
- Price £870, 5 days (Aberdeen), "Valid for 5 years", renewed through the Recognised Practitioner Programme (RPP, formerly the refresher). — [EPIT: CompEx Ex01–04](https://www.epitgroup.com/product/compex-ex01-ex04-training-2026)
- £995 + VAT (Hull). Entry evidence required: "Level 3, or ECS Gold Card, or National Diploma or equivalent in Electrotechnical and/or Instrumentation". — [HOTA: CompEx](https://www.hota.org/training-courses/compex-electrical-mechanical-and-city-guilds-training-courses/compex-gas-(electrical)-ex01-ex04.aspx); £1,095 + VAT — [C&P Training](https://www.cptrainingservices.co.uk/course/compex-ex01-ex04-gas-vapours-course); £1,100 ex VAT — [East Coast Training Academy](https://www.eastcoasttrainingacademy.co.uk/courses/compex-ex01-ex04-hazardous-area-full)

**HV: Authorised Person and Senior Authorised Person**
- HV Authorised Person, City & Guilds accredited: 3 days, £845, "Valid for 5 years". Covers HSG 85, HTM 06-02 and JSP 375 (MOD). Aimed at personnel "already appointed as Authorised Person (Electrical High Voltage) or... progressing towards being appointed". — [EPIT: HV AP](https://www.epitgroup.com/product/high-voltage-authorised-person-2026)
- AP HV Operation and Safety (APHV1): 5 days, £1,565 + VAT, City & Guilds Assured, for staff "who may be appointed as Authorised Person". There are sector variants for NHS (HTM 06-03) and MOD (JSP 375 Vol 3). — [PPL Training: APHV1](https://ppltraining.co.uk/training-course/mechanical-and-electrical-safe-systems-of-work/high-voltage-commercial-mod/authorised-person-high-voltage-module-1-operation-and-safety)
- Senior Authorised Person, Safe Switching of HV Systems: 5 days, £1,495 + VAT (Stockton), written and practical exams, City & Guilds accredited. — [PASS: SAP HV switching](https://www.electricaltrainingcourse.co.uk/person-sap-switching/hvso4-senior-authorised-person-safe-switching-operation-of-high-voltage-power-systems-sap); Senior/Authorised Person HV (industrial and commercial), 5 days, £1,560 ex tax — [RelyOn](https://shop.relyon.com/Course/CourseDetails/155535/2/SeniorauthorisedPersonHighVoltageIndustrialAndCommercial)
- EUSR (energy and utilities) offers SHEA Power: a 1-day safety-awareness scheme for operational power sites, with "SHEA Power EUSR registration... valid for three years". Delivery can be face-to-face or remote live. — [EUSR: SHEA Power](https://eusr.co.uk/schemes/shea-power)
- ECITB CCNSG Safety Passport (often required on engineering construction sites such as refineries and power stations): a 2-day course, card "valid for three years". From 7 September 2026 renewed cards are "valid for 39 months... from the date of the renewal assessment". If a card expires you must redo the 2-day course. ECITB gives no grants. — [ECITB: CCNSG](https://www.ecitb.org.uk/professional-development/ccnsg/); [ECITB news, Sept 2026](https://www.ecitb.org.uk/news/changes-to-ccnsg-safety-passport-renewals); prices £198 inc VAT — [3t](https://www.3tglobal.com/training-services/courses/ecitb-ccnsg-safety-passport/); £225 inc VAT — [East Coast](https://www.eastcoasttrainingacademy.co.uk/courses/ccnsg)

**EV charging (C&G 2921)**
- 2921-34 is the Level 3 Award for design and installation of domestic and small commercial EV charging (available from 30 August 2024). 2921-32 and 2921-33 cover large-scale design/QA and installation/maintenance (C&G fees £129 and £130). Last registration for 2921-32/33 is 31 August 2028. — [C&G 2921](https://www.cityandguilds.com/en/qualifications-and-apprenticeships/building-services-industry/electrical-installation/2921-electric-vehicle-charging)

**Solar PV and battery storage (MCS-related)**

## 4. Merchant Navy conversion: MCA routes to an Engineer Officer (EOOW) or Electro-Technical Officer CoC

### Takeaway
The MCA has an MoU with the RN that sets out conversion routes, but it is written for the surface fleet. For submariners the MCA says it is "currently in discussions". It will review individual cases by email (page updated 9 October 2026). The surface-fleet flowchart shows ratings gaining a Watch Rating certificate quickly. Reaching EOOW still needs merchant ship time (about 9 months with an MNTB Training Record Book), written exams, HELM, STCW courses and an oral. The RN HV Competent Person course exempts you from the operational-level HV course.

### Cited Findings
- MCA: "We have a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with the Royal Navy, giving a route to Merchant STCW Certification based on the education and training, as well as experience, gained whilst in the Navy." We can raise your case directly with our examiner for review... email engineering@mcga.gov.uk with your Rank, service history and any relevant education and training." ### Inferences
- A submariner leaving in 2032 should email the MCA well before leaving, around 2030–31, with rank, service history and courses. The aim is a case-specific assessment (possibly a Letter of Initial Assessment, which MSF 4275 mentions). Policy may have changed by then.

## 5. Nuclear-relevant credentials: transfer to civil nuclear, SQEP, radiation protection, skills passports and security clearance

### Takeaway
No public source shows RN submarine nuclear qualifications being formally accepted as equivalent to civil nuclear authorisations. SQEP is not a qualification; the employer or licensee judges and documents it role by role. RN nuclear courses do count as evidence for IMarEST registration. Security clearance is the most time-sensitive asset. Under Cabinet Office policy a clearance can be transferred to a new sponsor only if no more than 12 months pass between leaving one organisation and joining another, and the person has not lived overseas for more than 6 months. SC is reviewed after 10 years and DV after 7.

### Cited Findings
- SQEP "originated within the UK nuclear industry". It typically "requires a professional qualification and several years of experience". — [NAFEMS: What is SQEP](https://www.nafems.org/publications/resource_center/wt03)
- SQEP "is not a single standalone qualification... It is a competence standard, a judgement made by an organisation". It is determined by the duty holder or licensee and inspected by ONR (training provider's explanation). — [360 Nuclear Skills Training: SQEP](https://360nuclearskillstraining.com/about-us/sqep-nuclear-training)
- Government and industry (2015, old): most SQEP training "is done in-house". The Nuclear Skills Passport and integrated Competency Framework were "being aligned with the similar Competence Framework used in Defence". — [GOV.UK: Sustaining Our Nuclear Skills (2015, PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a74f2d6ed915d3c7d529339/Sustaining_Our_Nuclear_Skills_FINAL.PDF)
- NSAN Skills Passport is now described as an online tool where individuals "can access their own Skills Passport to record their competence via assessment and upload evidence". — [NSAN course brochure 2024 (PDF)](https://www.nsan.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/NSAN-Course-Brochure-2024-LR.pdf). NSAN's "GAIN" programme builds on the "Award for Nuclear Industry Awareness". — [Sizewell C Supply Chain: Skills](https://sizewellcsupplychain.co.uk/skills-training)
- IMarEST's Submarine routes use RN nuclear courses (NPSC, NPOC, NPSupC, Category A2/B Nuclear Watchkeeping) as evidence for EngTech and IEng. — [IMarEST RN application form (PDF)](https://www.imarest.org/static/c189b876-05e8-430d-a260379d5f2642db/3d60f3ff-9891-4456-b4bbc97a174ef8f4/RN-Application-Form-Marine-Engineer-26.pdf)
- Radiation protection: under IRR17 an RPS is appointed by the employer in writing. - Demand (Nuclear Institute jobs blog, July 2026, quoting government and industry figures): Sizewell C "around 17,000 jobs" at peak. Great British Energy – Nuclear signed with Rolls-Royce SMR in April 2026, with about 3,000 peak construction jobs for the first project. The Defence Nuclear Enterprise employs about 47,600 people, rising to about 65,000 by 2030. — [Nuclear Institute Jobs: UK nuclear jobs market 2026](https://jobs.nuclearinst.com/blog/uk-nuclear-jobs-market-2026-where-demand-is-growing)
- **Security clearance transfer rules (Cabinet Office/UKSV):** "The clearance must not be older than 10 years for SC or CTC, and 7 years for DV." "No more than 12 months between the individual leaving one organisation and joining another." "Individual must not have lived overseas for more than 6 months if there has been a break in service." MOD also requires that the clearance has not expired. - Examples of transfer given by government include "service personnel leave... HMF and... move on to a role in defence industry". "Security clearance is not owned by you as an individual, your sponsoring authority will own the security clearance." — [GOV.UK: UKSV applicant](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/united-kingdom-security-vetting-applicant)

### Inferences
- Job timing matters: a cleared civilian role (defence nuclear, Babcock, AWE, Rolls-Royce Submarines, MOD contractors) should start within 12 months of discharge. Avoid more than 6 months living abroad, for example a long overseas contract, in between. That keeps the clearance transferable instead of starting fresh DV vetting (published standard about 95 days and often longer). Using the 2-year resettlement window to secure an offer before leaving is the safest approach.
- Since SQEP is employer-assessed, the best "portable" nuclear evidence is: certified copies of RN nuclear course certificates (NPSC and similar, if releasable), a detailed experience log, EngTech (IMarEST lists nuclear courses as evidence), an RPS certificate, and an NSAN nuclear-awareness award. Several of these are cheap compared with the value of the clearance.
- An RPS certificate taken during service would be within the 5-year refresher window in 2032 if taken after about 2027. Its value is greatest close to leaving.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on whether civil licensees (EDF, Sellafield, Sizewell C, Rolls-Royce SMR) recognise specific RN nuclear qualifications. RN-to-civil-nuclear equivalence appears to be decided by each employer.
- The current status and uptake of the NSAN Nuclear Skills Passport in 2026 (beyond the 2024 brochure) was not confirmed.
- The exact clearance level held by ET(ME) SM ratings is not stated publicly. The DTS page mentions passing "a Security Check" before training; DV for reactor work comes only from a recruiter source.

## 6. Higher education while serving: HNC/HND, Open University, and study around deployments

### Takeaway
The OU BEng (Hons) costs £24,528 for 360 credits (about £4,088 a year part-time over 6 years, for modules starting before 31 July 2027). Students have up to 12 years to finish, and registration from BFPO addresses is accepted. An online HNC in Electrical and Electronic Engineering costs about £3,950 and accepts ELCs. A part-time HNC or OU Stage 1 is technically possible while serving. Funding while serving is thin (SLC £175 a year) until 6 years' service unlocks ELC. The cheapest route to a first degree is often after leaving, through PF FE/HE (section 7).

### Cited Findings
- OU BEng (Hons) Q65: three stages of 120 credits each. Stage 1 equals the CertHE in Engineering (T48). Part-time is about half the full-time rate; full-time is possible from Stage 2. "You have up to 12 years to finish." The fee is £24,528 (valid for modules starting before 31 July 2027). - OU Top-up BEng (Hons) Q78: one 120-credit stage, £8,176. It is accredited by IET, IMechE, IED and IOM3. - Solent University offers a BEng (Hons) Marine Engineering Top-Up for holders of a suitable FdEng or HND. — [Solent: Marine Engineering Top-Up](https://www.solent.ac.uk/courses/undergraduate/marine-engineering-top-up-beng-hons/2025/beng-hons-marine-engineering-top-up-1-year-full-time-june-2025)

### Inferences
- Feasibility at sea: submarine patrols mean long periods with no internet. The workable pattern is probably one module at a time timed for shore or maintenance periods, using the OU's 12-year limit and module-by-module structure. A self-paced online HNC (open learning) is likely more forgiving than fixed OU assessment dates.
- An HNC (Level 4) does several jobs: JIB Technician, IEng evidence and data-centre/HV engineer roles. If the RN FdEng (via LETQC/POETQC) is not reached by 2032, the HNC is the most useful single academic qualification to self-fund.

If the RN awards an FdEng, or the person self-funds 120+ credits at Level 4, free PF FE/HE degree funding may be lost (JSP 822 has a nuance for Service-accredited awards; see section 7). Check with the Education Officer before starting self-funded HE.

### Gaps
- No OU policy page on deferrals or extensions for deployed or submarine personnel was found.
- No source found on whether RN shipboard or submarine education support (Learning Centres, offline study) exists for submarine crews.
- Costs of a college-based part-time HNC Electrical (block release or evening) were not checked.

## 7. Funding: SLC, ELC (ELCAS), PF FE/HE, resettlement (IRTC/GRT) and what each can pay for

### Takeaway
In years 1–5 the only personal funding scheme is Standard Learning Credits: 80% of fees, up to £175 per financial year. ELC (Lower Tier, up to £1,000 a year for 3 years, or £3,000 aggregated), free first-degree funding through PF FE/HE, and the Core Resettlement Programme (IRTC £534, CTP courses, GRT) all need 6 or more years' qualifying service. "About six years" is therefore a hard financial threshold: leaving even a few weeks short of 6 full years loses all of them. ELC registration is not automatic and can be done once Phase 1 is complete, so it can be done now.

### Cited Findings
**Standard Learning Credits (SLC)**
- SLC pays "80% of fees, up to a maximum of £175 per FY" for part-time academic and vocational courses, exams and support. It "may not be claimed for... Service training... Civilian accredited Service training". "SLCs cannot be claimed for an S/NVQ gained only through submission of a portfolio of evidence. There must be evidence of fresh individual work". "Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) assessment fees leading to partial or complete qualifications are eligible." — [JSP 822 Vol 9 Elective Education (Nov 2026, PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf)
- RN Discover My Benefits: up to £175 a year; permission is needed before the course starts. SLC cannot pay for professional bodies and institutes, course material, or the City & Guilds Professional Recognition Awards. — [DMB Royal Navy: SLC](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/professional-development/standard-learning-credits). Unused SLC does not carry forward, and claims use MOD Form 1950 (secondary). — [Total Veteran: ELCAS and SLC](https://totalveteran.com/journal/elcas-standard-learning-credits)

**Enhanced Learning Credits (ELC)**
- "From 1 Apr 16, those completing Phase 1 training... are eligible to register for the scheme. Registration is not automatic". Lower Tier: "up to a maximum of 3 awards of up to £1k per FY", with 6 or more years' qualifying service (4 years if completed before 1 April 2017). Aggregated Lower Tier: "a single award of up to £3k", 6 or more years. Higher Tier: up to £2k per financial year, 8 or more years. A personal contribution of at least 20% is required, and study is off duty or on leave. One claim per financial year (1 April–31 March), for learning starting in that year. Level 3 or above only, with an approved provider. Leavers from 1 April 2016 have 5 years after discharge to start. No retrospective claims. Not for Service training or civilian accreditation of Service training. — [JSP 822 Vol 9 (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf)
- ELC is used for "educational purposes" throughout a career up to the last 24 months, and for resettlement in the last 2 years, but only once qualifying service is met. — [JSP 822 Vol 9 (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf)
- RN members find ELC providers through the [ELCAS provider search](https://www.enhancedlearningcredits.com/). Claims are submitted at least 25 clear working days before the course. — [DMB Army: ELC (same tri-service scheme)](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/army/professional-development/enhanced-learning-credits)
- Electrical courses on the ELCAS list: one provider lists the 2365 Level 3 Diploma, 2382, 2391, 2921 and Solar PV/Battery as eligible. It says "5357 [NVQ] is not ELCAS-approved at present" and that Level 2 courses are not eligible. — [Total Skills: ELCAS funding explained](https://www.totalskills.co.uk/guides/elcas-funding-explained). Able Skills lists 2391, 2377 (PAT) and the Level 4 2396 under ELCAS. — [Able Skills: ELC courses](https://www.ableskills.co.uk/blog/enhanced-learning-credits-courses); [The Guilds: ELCAS electrical](https://www.theguilds.co.uk/elcas-electrical-resettlement-courses)

**Publicly Funded FE/HE (PF FE/HE), the free first Level 3 or first degree**
- PF FE/HE supports "A first full Level 3 qualification" or a first HE qualification at Level 4–6, including an "Undergraduate Degree (including a Foundation Degree)" and "Higher National Certificate or a Higher National Diploma". "If the scheme is used to achieve a first full Level 3 qualification, or the SL has already achieved 120 credits at Level 4 or above..., it cannot be used to achieve a HE qualification." Eligibility needs active ELC membership and "At least 6 years' qualifying service", counted from "1 April 2000 or the date of enlistment, whichever is later". It requires 3 years' UK residence, is available for 5 years after discharge, and part-time study must be at least 25% of full-time. Fees are paid up to £9,535 a year for publicly funded providers and £6,000 for private ones. A leaver who gained a qualification "purely by dint of completing Service training courses and has undertaken no extra personal study... may still be eligible". — [JSP 822 Vol 9 (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf)

**Resettlement**
- RN Discover My Benefits: 0–4 years gets CTP Future Horizons only; 4–6 years adds the Employment Support Programme (digital job platform for 2 years and workshops); "6+ years of service or medically discharged" gets the Core Resettlement Programme. That includes "training grants, allowances, travel warrants, authorised resettlement absences... subsidised vocational training". The IRTC can buy CTP courses "using £26.70 per day from your IRTC grant" and can be combined with SLC or ELC. Maximum entitlements need 16 years. The (cash) Resettlement Grant needs 12 or more years. — [DMB Royal Navy: Resettlement (updated 6 Aug 2026)](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/professional-development/resettlement)
- The CRP is available from 2 years before discharge and for 2 years afterwards, "up to 35 days Graduated Resettlement Time... dependent upon length of Service". — [GOV.UK: Career Transition Partnership](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/career-transition-partnership)
- IRTC maximum is £534. In resettlement, IRTC may be used "to offset, or pay in full, the 20% personal contribution required when using ELC". — [JSP 822 Vol 9 (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf); [Royal British Legion: education support](https://www.britishlegion.org.uk/get-support/expert-guidance/employment/education-training-armed-forces-community)

**Professional body fees**
- ELC and SLC cannot pay professional body fees. IMechE says Armed Forces registration fees can be reimbursed annually by the MOD under DIN 2024DIN01-071. — [IMechE: Royal Navy](http://imeche.org/membership-registration/become-a-member/armed-forces-and-mod/royal-navy); [DMB RN: SLC](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/professional-development/standard-learning-credits)

**Navy-funded education**
- Service-provided qualifications include the Level 2 apprenticeship (entry), a Level 3 award at Leading Hand, the FdEng via LETQC and POETQC, and a funded BEng for Degree Top Up entrants (see section 1). No public RN page named "Personal Development Pathways" or "Defence Further and Higher Education" as a separate funding scheme. The tri-service elective-education rules are JSP 822 Vol 9 (SLC, ELC, PF FE/HE).

### Inferences
- **The 6-year cliff.** If enlistment was in 2026 (the person is in Phase 2 in November 2026), 6 years' qualifying service is reached in 2032. Discharge must fall on or after the sixth anniversary of enlistment to unlock: ELC Lower Tier (£1,000 a year × 3, or £3,000 once), PF FE/HE (a free first degree, HNC or HND up to £9,535 a year), the CRP with IRTC £534 and GRT, and CTP courses. Leaving at 5 years 11 months drops to the Employment Support Programme only. Confirm the exact qualifying-service date with the unit Education Officer and HR. Staying to 8 years would unlock Higher Tier ELC (£2,000 a year).
- ELC claims are one per financial year and must start within 5 years of discharge. To use three £1,000 claims, plan courses in three different financial years, for example 2032/33, 2033/34 and 2034/35. Alternatively take the aggregated £3,000 in one go for a single larger course, such as an HNC or an HV/CompEx package if the provider is ELCAS-listed.
- NVQ and AM2 funding: SLC excludes an NVQ gained only from a portfolio of earlier work, and ELC needs 6 years. The NVQ L3 completion and AM2 (about £885–£1,340 + VAT) are therefore likely self-funded. A possible exception is SLC's RPL assessment-fee clause, capped at £175; check with Education staff.
- Use SLC every year (£175 at 80% means a course of about £219 or more yields the full £175). Good targets: 2382-26 (from £395 + VAT), ECS HS&E (about £57), OU Access or Stage 1 modules, the 2921-34 EV course (£395), RPS online (£588).
- PF FE/HE versus an RN FdEng: if the RN FdEng is awarded through Service courses with no extra personal study, the JSP says the leaver "may still be eligible". That could keep a free first degree available after leaving. Self-funding 120 or more Level 4 credits (for example an OU CertHE) before leaving could, however, block PF FE/HE for a degree. Weigh this before starting OU study.

### Gaps
- GRT days for exactly 6 years' service (the "up to 35 days" depends on service length) were not found on a public page; the detail is in JSP 534 on Defnet.
- The ELCAS provider list for specific offshore tickets (BOSIET, GWO), CompEx and HV courses was not checked course by course. Note that ELC needs a regulated Level 3 or higher qualification, and many tickets (BOSIET, GWO, CCNSG) are industry certificates rather than RQF qualifications. Such tickets would probably have to use IRTC, SLC or self-funding (inference, not verified).
- The meaning of DIN 2024DIN01-071 (the professional fee reimbursement rules) is on Defnet and not public.

## 8. Recommended order: years 1–2, 3–4 and 5–6 of service

### Takeaway
Early years: lock in the electrician core and the Navy's own apprenticeship, at low cost (SLC). Middle years: get EngTech and the long-life technical awards (2391, Level 4 study), and keep BS 7671 current. Final 1–2 years, only after 6 years' service is secured: spend ELC, IRTC and GRT on sector-specific, time-limited tickets, and line up a cleared job to start within 12 months of leaving.

### Inferences (the suggested plan)
**Years 1–2 (late 2026 to 2028): Phase 2, Dolphins, first boat**
1. Register for ELC now (allowed once Phase 1 is complete; it is not automatic). Confirm the enlistment date that sets the 6-year qualifying date.
2. Join a professional body cheaply: IET student/apprentice membership is £36 for the course, or IMechE apprentice membership is free. Start a CPD and experience log; it will be needed for EngTech, IEng, SQEP evidence and the NVQ.
3. Finish the NVQ L3 and AM2 in leave periods if the NVQ evidence rules allow (AM2 about £885–£1,340 + VAT, 3 days, probably self-funded). Then take the ECS HS&E (online, about £47.50 + VAT) and apply for the Gold Card. Reason: it turns an "almost" electrician into a JIB Electrician and ECS Gold Card holder. It is the entry requirement for CompEx, solar PV, EV and 2391 courses, and it gives a Level 3 that supports EngTech.
4. Take 2382-26 (3 days, about £395–£525 + VAT; SLC can contribute up to £175). It is the current BS 7671 Amendment 4 qualification, and A4 covers batteries, ICT earthing and PoE, which matters for data centres.
5. Do not buy expiring offshore or hazardous-area tickets yet.

**Years 3–4 (2028 to 2030): Leading Hand stage**
1. LETQC (Level 3 award) and NPSC. Apply for EngTech as soon as you hold NVQ L3 or the LET submarine package. Use IMarEST (MarEngTech) or the IET (free application; IMechE SAP also has no fee and no interview). Reason: registration is a credential recognised across every target sector, and it is cheapest and easiest while serving.
2. Take 2391-52 (5–7 days, about £995–£1,300) in a shore period, before or around the 31 August 2028 last-registration date or its replacement. Reason: it is required for JIB Approved grade later and highly valued in maintenance, data-centre and contracting work.
3. Start Level 4 study at a pace that fits patrols, either an online HNC Electrical/Electronic (about £3,950) or OU modules. First check the PF FE/HE "first HE qualification" rule, because self-funding 120 Level 4 credits could block free degree funding later. Consider 2396 (Level 4, about £790) instead if aiming at JIB Technician.
4. Optional low-cost, long-life short courses using SLC: EV 2921-34 (2.5 days, £395), solar PV and battery 2922-34/2923-34 (5 days, about £995–£1,100 + VAT), an introductory PLC course (2 days, £675 + VAT).
5. Keep BS 7671 current: watch the IET updates page for any 19th Edition announcement (none as of October 2026).

**Years 5–6 (2030 to 2032): resettlement window (the last 2 years)**
1. Make sure the discharge date falls on or after 6 full years' qualifying service, which unlocks ELC, PF FE/HE, the CRP, IRTC £534 and GRT.
2. Choose a target sector by about 2031 and buy only that sector's time-limited tickets in the final 6–12 months:
   - Offshore oil and gas: BOSIET with CA-EBS (3 days, £750–£880, 4 years) plus OEUK medical and CA-EBS Fitness to Train; CompEx Ex01–04 (5 days, £870–£1,100, 5 years).
   - Offshore wind: GWO BST (5 days, about £1,015–£1,199, 24 months).
   - Onshore energy, power or petrochemical sites: CCNSG (2 days, about £200, 3 years) and EUSR SHEA Power (1 day, 3 years).
   - HV, utilities or data centres: an HV AP or SAP course (3–5 days, £845–£1,565 + VAT; the employer then appoints).
   - Nuclear: RPS (2 days, £588–£1,025 + VAT) and NSAN nuclear awareness.
   - Merchant Navy or ETO: email the MCA for a case review around 2030–31; HV Management (£1,350–£1,606) only if pursuing an ETO CoC.
3. Funding mix: from the 6-year point, ELC Lower Tier (one claim per financial year, up to £1,000 at 80%, or £3,000 aggregated) for ELCAS-listed Level 3+ courses, with IRTC paying the 20% contribution. IRTC, CTP courses and GRT cover non-RQF tickets where allowed. After discharge, PF FE/HE can fund a first degree, HNC or HND (for example the OU BEng, up to 360 credits) within 5 years.
4. Job timing: secure a cleared role (defence nuclear or contractors) to start within 12 months of discharge, without more than 6 months living overseas, so SC or DV can be transferred. Apply for IMechE or IET registration upgrades before the 12-month SAP window closes.
5. After leaving: work towards JIB Approved grade (2 years as an electrician plus 2391) and later Technician or IEng (HNC, FdEng or BEng plus experience).

### Gaps
- Promotion timing (whether Leading Hand by year 3–4 and PO by year 6 are realistic for ET(ME) SM) is not published, so the FdEng and IEng timing above is uncertain.
- Return-of-service obligations after RN courses, and notice periods, which could move the 2032 exit date, were not researched.
- Whether the NVQ L3 can practically be finished while serving: unresolved (see section 2).
