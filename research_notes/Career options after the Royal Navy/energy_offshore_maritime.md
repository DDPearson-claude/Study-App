# UK energy, offshore and maritime careers for an ex-RN submariner marine engineer and qualified electrician (exit around 2032)

Research date: 14 November 2026. All job adverts below were seen on 14 Nov 2026; the posting date shown is the one printed on the advert. UK only. Pay is gross. Where a figure comes from a recruiter or CV-writing site, not an employer, regulator or government body, it is marked "(secondary)".

---

## 1. Offshore wind: demand to the mid-2030s, roles, pay, rotations, hubs, entry tickets and veteran schemes

### Takeaway
Offshore wind has the clearest long-term demand of the four areas for an electrically qualified ex-marine engineer. About 17 GW is installed now. The record AR7 auction (8.4 GW, January 2026) has been held, AR8 is under way, and auctions are scheduled every year to 2030. The workforce has to roughly double by 2030, with wind turbine technicians the most-needed role. Staff pay is moderate (about £39k–£55k for a technician; about £55k–£75k plus allowances for an HV Senior Authorised Person). Most work is on 2-weeks-on/2-weeks-off service operation vessel rotations from east-coast and Scottish ports. Ørsted, RWE, SSE and Vestas all have public armed-forces commitments.

### Cited Findings

**Capacity targets and pipeline**
- Clean Power 2030 Action Plan (Jan 2025): 43–50 GW of offshore wind by 2030. Installed capacity at publication was 14.8 GW, and NESO scenarios ranged from 43 to 51 GW. — [GOV.UK Clean Power 2030 Action Plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/clean-power-2030-action-plan/clean-power-2030-action-plan-a-new-era-of-clean-electricity-main-report)
- AR7 results (14 Jan 2026): a record 8.4 GW of offshore wind, "Europe's biggest ever offshore wind auction". Fixed offshore cleared at an average £90.91/MWh in 2024 prices. — [GOV.UK press release, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/record-breaking-auction-for-offshore-wind-secured-to-take-back-control-of-britains-energy)
- AR7 detail: 8,245 MW fixed-bottom and 192.5 MW floating (total 8,437.5 MW). By nation: England 6,090 MW, Scotland 1,380 MW, Wales 775 MW. Projects: Dogger Bank South East and West (RWE, 1,500 MW each), Norfolk Vanguard East and West, Berwick Bank Phase B (SSE, 1,380 MW), Awel y Môr (775 MW), plus floating Erebus (100 MW) and Pentland (92.5 MW). Phase-1 delivery years run 2028/29–2030/31. — [GOV.UK AR7 results (accessible webpage), 10 Feb 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/contracts-for-difference-cfd-allocation-round-7-results/contracts-for-difference-allocation-round-7-results-accessible-webpage)
- RWE won about 6.9 GW of the AR7 offshore capacity. Westwood flags the concentration in one developer as a risk ("Ørsted's recent difficulties may be instructive"). — [Westwood Energy, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.westwoodenergy.com/news/westwood-insight/westwood-insight-analysing-the-record-breaking-ar7-offshore-wind-results)
- Westwood says AR7 "largely foreclose[s] any possibility" of 43–50 GW actually online by 2030. The target is defined to include under-construction and committed capacity, however, so it remains achievable on that looser basis. — [Westwood Energy](https://www.westwoodenergy.com/news/westwood-insight/westwood-insight-analysing-the-record-breaking-ar7-offshore-wind-results). ### Inferences
- Timing suits a 2032 exit. Projects from AR6, AR7 and AR8 deliver 2028–2032, so their 25+ year operations phases will be starting as the person leaves. Operations and maintenance (O&M) technician and HV/SAP demand should still be growing into the mid-2030s, even if the 2030 headline target slips.
- An NVQ Level 3 electrician with RN marine engineering experience (permits, isolations, sea-going life) matches the advertised requirements for technician and balance-of-plant roles. These roles also offer a clear ladder: AP, then SAP at 33 kV, then 66/132 kV. The SAP tier pays about £55k–£75k plus shift and SOV allowances.
- The headline money is below North Sea oil and gas. The trade-off is a growing sector, permanent staff contracts, and home-port living in East Anglia, the Humber or Scotland.
- Developer and supply-chain concentration is a real risk: RWE holds most of AR7, Ørsted has had financial strain, and Teesside has seen cutbacks.

## 2. Offshore oil and gas (North Sea): outlook, electrical/E&I roles, tickets, pay and rotations, and whether it is a sound bet from 2032

### Takeaway
The UK North Sea is in structural decline:
- no new licences, now being legislated through the Energy Independence Bill (introduced 4 Nov 2026)
- capital spending forecast to halve by 2028
- decommissioning spend expected to be double capital investment by about 2030
- NSTA projecting 90% (oil) and 97% (gas) falls in output from 2025 to 2050

Offshore electrical technician pay is the highest of the four areas (about £400–£540/day, or roughly £72k–£96k a year on rotation; secondary sources). Rotations are 2/2 or 2/3. Adverts commonly demand CompEx, BOSIET/CA-EBS, MIST and about 5 years' oil and gas experience. From 2032 the sector is a short-to-medium-term earner or a bridge into wind and CCS, not a long-term career bet.

### Cited Findings

**Policy and outlook**
- The 2025 North Sea Future Plan says the government will not issue new exploration or production licences. It introduces Transitional Energy Certificates for areas next to existing fields; existing licences are not revoked. The Energy Independence Bill was introduced on 4 Nov 2026 to implement this. — [House of Commons Library CBP-12212, 9 Nov 2026](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-12212/)
- The effective marginal tax rate is 78%, including the 38% Energy Profits Levy (EPL). The EPL runs to 31 March 2030 or until the price floor mechanism triggers. Prices and profits rose in 2026 because of the US/Israel–Iran conflict. — [Commons Library CBP-12212](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-12212/). The EPL will be replaced by a permanent Oil and Gas Price Mechanism. — [Commons Library CBP-10905 (PDF), 23 Jun 2026](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-10905/CBP-10905.pdf)
- North Sea production peaked in 1999 and is down by more than 70%. NSTA projections show output falling 90% (oil) and 97% (gas) between 2025 and 2050. — [Commons Library CBP-10905 (PDF)](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-10905/CBP-10905.pdf)
- A Commons motion to reopen licensing was defeated 297–108 on 24 March 2026. — [OEUK Magazine Issue 66, Jun 2026](https://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Offshore-Energies-Magazine-Issue-66-June-2026-OEUK.pdf)
- OEUK Business Outlook 2026, citing NSTA's November 2025 projections: capital investment "projected to halve" by 2028, and "by the end of the decade, expenditure on decommissioning is likely to be double capital investment". — [OEUK Business Outlook 2026 (PDF)](https://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Business-Outlook-Report-2026-Final.pdf)
- OEUK Economic Report 2026 (Sep 2026):
  - production held up in 2025–26 because of project delays and life extensions
  - the UK drilled no exploration wells last year, for the first time since production began
  - on current trajectories production falls by more than 40% by the end of the decade
  - oil and gas supported 180,800 FTE jobs in 2024: 28,200 direct, 93,700 indirect (supply chain), 58,900 induced
  - Source: [OEUK Economic Report 2026 (PDF)](https://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/2026/09/Economic-Report-2026-OEUK-u0dihi.pdf)
- OEUK Workforce Insight 2025 (Nov 2025) counts about 154,000 offshore energy jobs in 2024. In its October 2025 pulse survey, 55% of firms had cut staff in the past year, 49% had cut contractors, and 45% expected further cuts. — [OEUK Workforce Insight 2025 (PDF)](http://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Workforce-Insight-2025-OEUK.pdf); [OEUK news, 5 Nov 2025](https://oeuk.org.uk/oeuks-workforce-insight-a-blueprint-for-delivering-the-energy-jobs-of-the-future)
- Note that OEUK is an industry lobby body. Its "212,000–225,000 by 2030" growth scenario assumes reversing current policy. — [OEUK Workforce Insight 2025](http://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Workforce-Insight-2025-OEUK.pdf)
- Robert Gordon University (RGU): the UK oil and gas workforce fell from about 120,000 (2023) to about 115,000 (2024). Offshore renewables employment is expected to exceed oil and gas "from the late 2020s onwards". — [RGU ETI "Striking the Balance" 2025 (PDF)](https://www.rgueti.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/RGU_ETI_Report_2025.pdf)
- RGU 2026: the North East Scotland offshore energy workforce fell from about 56,000 (2016) to 42,200 (2024). It could fall by a further about 18,000, to about 24,400, by 2035, shifting from about 90% oil and gas to 30–45% oil and gas. — [RGU "Delivering Positive Energy" 2026 (PDF)](https://agcc.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/images/RGU-Delivering-Positive-Energy-2026.pdf)

**The transferable-skills narrative**
- OEUK: "Around 90% of skills in the oil & gas offshore workforce are transferable to renewables, hydrogen, and CCS." It names the Energy Skills Passport, Connected Competence and the ECITB/GWO Cross Skilling Programme as mobility tools. — [OEUK Workforce Insight 2025](http://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Workforce-Insight-2025-OEUK.pdf)
- RGU: over 90% of the UK Continental Shelf oil and gas workforce has "medium to high transferability" to offshore renewables, "although there may be an ongoing need for targeted retraining". Before 2027 renewables may have limited capacity to absorb oil and gas workers. — [RGU ETI 2025](https://www.rgueti.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/RGU-ETI-Report-2025-Full.pdf)
- The Energy Skills Passport launched on 22 Jan 2025, with four initial pathways into offshore wind. — [GOV.UK, 22 Jan 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/support-for-workers-to-benefit-from-thousands-of-clean-power-jobs). The Clean Energy Jobs Plan (Oct 2025) extends it to electricity networks. — [NAO, Sep 2026](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Upgrading-the-electricity-transmission-network.pdf)

### Inferences
- From 2032 the basin will be well into managed decline. Production will be roughly 40%+ below 2025 levels, decommissioning spend will exceed new capital, and no new fields will be licensed.
- Electrical and E&I technicians will still be needed on ageing assets kept running past their design life, and on decommissioning work. Day rates could stay high because the remaining pool is shrinking. Contract security, though, will be short (6–12 month PAYE contracts) and could end with an asset's cessation of production.
- The "5 years' oil and gas experience" and "CompEx essential" requirements are a real entry barrier for someone arriving from the RN without hydrocarbon-industry time. Submarine HV and propulsion electrical experience may partly offset this, but that is untested.
- Best use: a few years of high earnings and acquiring CompEx and BOSIET, which also open doors in CCS, hydrogen, FPSOs and some wind roles. It is not a 25-year career plan in the UK.

### Gaps
- I found no employer-posted 2026 UK offshore electrical technician day rate; agency adverts say "PAYE rates – get in touch". All day-rate figures above are from secondary salary guides.
- I did not find the ECITB workforce forecast numbers for oil and gas specifically.
- There is no reliable source on how many E&I roles decommissioning will need in the 2030s.

## 3. Electricity grid and networks: investment plans, roles, pay and ex-forces programmes

### Takeaway
Grid work has the most secure funding and the longest horizon of the four areas:
- **Transmission:** RIIO-T3 runs 2026–2031, with Ofgem expecting up to about £70bn of transmission spend.
- **Distribution:** RIIO-ED3 runs April 2028 to March 2033.
- **Beyond 2030:** NESO's plan recommends 43 projects and about £89bn of investment after 2030.

Direct network employment is forecast to grow from about 90,000 to about 117,000 by 2035, with about 71,000 new entrants needed. An NVQ Level 3 electrician fits straight into substation fitter and craft roles (about £45k–£54k). Ofgem-regulated firms then train people into AP/SAP roles (about £50k–£70k, then £60k–£90k at 132 kV). Work is mostly home-based on days with a standby rota. Several network operators run dedicated ex-forces schemes.

### Inferences
- Funding is locked in by regulation: T3 to 2031, ED3 from 2028 to 2033, and Beyond 2030 projects into the 2040s. That makes 2032 a good entry point, with ED3 delivery at its peak and many Beyond 2030 transmission schemes in construction.
- An NVQ Level 3 electrotechnical qualification meets or exceeds the stated craft entry bar (C&G 2365 or 2339, or an electrical apprenticeship). RN permit-to-work and HV switching experience is directly relevant to AP/SAP authorisation.
- The realistic path is fitter or craftsperson (about £45k–£54k), then AP/SAP at 11/33 kV (about £50k–£70k), then SAP at 132 kV or protection and commissioning (about £60k–£90k).
- Lifestyle is the main differentiator: mostly days, home most nights, standby rota. Contractor roles are the exception, involving nationwide travel and overnights.
- SPEN's EMPower eligibility (at least 4 years' service, no more than 2 years out) matches a roughly 6-year leaver, if the scheme still runs around 2032.

### Gaps

- Individual DNO pay charts sit inside the Prospect PDF as images and were not extractable; only the group ranges are recorded.

## 4. Maritime: Merchant Navy ETO and engineer officers, superyachts, and the Royal Fleet Auxiliary

### Takeaway
Officer demand is rising. The UK faces a growing shortfall of deck, engine and technical officers (about 2,600 by 2030 and about 3,500 by 2035), with the strongest growth expected in electro-technical roles. Converting RN experience to an MCA certificate of competency is covered elsewhere; this section assumes an ETO (STCW III/6) or engineer CoC is held.

| Option | Pay (2025–26) | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| UK ETO, Merchant Navy | about £45k–£70k (secondary) | deep-sea: typically 4-month contracts |
| Global ETO vacancies | median $6,500/month | typically 4-month contracts |
| UK ferry officers | e.g. £72.7k for a CalMac Second Engineer | often 1/1 or 2/2 weeks |
| Superyacht ETO, 60 m+ yachts | about €7,000–€10,000+/month | 2:2 rotation is standard for senior engineers |
| RFA qualified engineer or systems officer | starts about £43.5k–£45k as Third Officer, plus civil service pension | 21 days' voyage leave per month at sea |

The RFA has chronic crewing shortages, which mean long tours and less than one-for-one leave.

  - Sources: [RFA QSEO](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/rfa/how-to-join/qualified-systems-engineer-officer-rfa); [RFA listing](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/rfa)
- The RFA joining page states: "if you've served in the past you could still join our fleet". Qualified officers go through a Defence Aptitude Assessment, interview and Admiralty Interview Board. — [RFA joining process](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/services/royal-fleet-auxiliary/joining-options/rfa-joining-process)
- RFA pay deal agreed 28 July 2026: 4.5% from 1 Apr 2025 and 3.6% from 1 Apr 2026, improved voyage-leave accrual, a one-off leave buy-back and £250. MoD describes it as part of a 14.1% cumulative rise since July 2024. — [GOV.UK, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/pay-rise-for-royal-fleet-auxiliary-personnel-supporting-royal-navy-operations)
- Navy Lookout (4 Aug 2026):
  - the deal adds about 13 days' leave a year but still falls short of commercial one-for-one rotations
  - the RFA numbers about 1,750; voluntary outflow fell from about 15% (start of 2025) to below 10% (start of 2026)
  - shortages persist "at numerous grades", and sailors are often kept aboard beyond planned appointments
  - RFA Tiderace had been laid up for lack of crew
  - Fleet Solid Support ships will need hundreds more trained seafarers
  - Source: [Navy Lookout](https://www.navylookout.com/rfa-pay-deal-offers-breathing-space-but-the-personnel-crisis-is-far-from-over/)
- Older data (2022): RFA tours of 4 months followed by 84 days' paid leave, and Sponsored Reserve status with an allowance. — [Bachmann Group RFA campaign (2022)](https://bachmanngroup.com/electro-technical-officers)

### Inferences
- An RN ETME (submariner) who adds an NVQ Level 3 electrotechnical qualification is a natural fit for the ETO certificate. The government's own projections say ETO demand will grow fastest while UK technical-officer supply declines, so certificated ETOs should be in demand through the 2030s.
- Lifestyle varies widely:
  - UK ferries offer the most home time (home daily, 1/1, 2/2, or Stena's 24 weeks on/28 off) at lower or moderate pay.
  - Deep-sea and cruise work means 3–4 month trips. Foreign-going service may qualify for the Seafarers' Earnings Deduction.
  - Superyachts pay the most for ETOs on 60 m+ yachts (€7k–€10k+/month, roughly £6k–£8.5k at about 0.85 £/€; my conversion, not sourced). Contracts are owner-dependent with less job security and are often outside UK employment law.
  - SOV and installation-vessel ETO roles combine Merchant Navy certification with offshore wind tickets, on 2–4 week rotations.
- RFA is the least culture shock for an ex-RN engineer: naval operations, a civil service pension, and a starting salary in the mid-£40ks. Today's manpower crisis, though, means longer tours and leave below commercial norms, the same "away a lot" lifestyle a leaver may be trying to escape.

### Gaps
- Nautilus International pay data (for example its professional or pay surveys) was not publicly accessible in my searches.
- I found no RFA pay scales above Third Officer (Second Engineer, Chief Engineer, senior systems engineer).
- I did not open the Seafarers' Earnings Deduction rules on GOV.UK.
- I found no UK ferry ETO salary stated in an advert (Stena, Serco and others say "competitive").
- The Merchant Navy Training Board pages were not opened.

## 5. Fit verdict for an ex-submariner marine engineer and electrician (pros, cons, lifestyle)

### Takeaway
On demand security plus skills fit, the ranking from 2032 is:
1. **Grid and networks:** most secure, home-based, strong ex-forces schemes.
2. **Offshore wind:** growing, good fit for HV and SAP progression, 2/2 rotations.
3. **Maritime ETO/RFA:** shortage occupation, good fit, but most time away.
4. **North Sea oil and gas:** best day rates, declining and hard to enter.

The tickets overlap and can be combined across sectors: GWO, BOSIET/CA-EBS, CompEx, AP/SAP authorisations and an STCW III/6 ETO CoC. Grid SAP and wind SAP authorisations are especially close.

### Cited Findings
- Wind turbine technician is the single most in-demand offshore wind role to 2030 (about 21,000 needed by 2030). — [RenewableUK/OWIC 2025](https://www.renewableuk.com/media/jvsdey0k/ruk-owic-offshore-wind-skills-report_2025.pdf)
- Network direct employment needs about 71,000 new entrants over the decade to 2035. — [ENA, 18 Oct 2026](http://www.energynetworks.org/newsroom/the-new-electricity-sector-growth-plan-shows-network-investment-can-unlock-gbp-58bn-for-the-uk-economy-and-support-more-than-210-000-high-quality-well-paid-jobs)
- Oil and gas production is falling more than 40% by 2030 on current trajectories; capex is halving by 2028. — [OEUK Economic Report 2026](https://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/2026/09/Economic-Report-2026-OEUK-u0dihi.pdf); [OEUK Business Outlook 2026](https://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Business-Outlook-Report-2026-Final.pdf)
- UK technical-officer shortfall rising to about 3,500 by 2035, with ETO growth strongest. — [DfT Seafarer Projections Review 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4672fd1c8bd7ce25a5e9ea/dft-seafarer-projections-review.pdf)
- Resettlement caveat: the MoD Service Leavers Guide (June 2026) says the Core Resettlement Programme "is available to personnel who have served for more than six years", usable from two years before discharge, with up to 35 days' Graduated Resettlement Time. A leaver at about 6 years should check eligibility carefully. — [MoD Service Leavers Guide (PDF), Jun 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4e19cc09605a87f4354531/Service_Leavers_Guide_June_26.pdf). Separately, CTP-funded courses come first ("CTP First"), with IRTC/ELCAS or self-funding otherwise. — [GOV.UK CTP](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/career-transition-partnership)

### Inferences

**Offshore wind: strong fit**
- Pros:
  - growing through 2035 (AR7–AR12 auctions, 93 GW pipeline)
  - electrical, mechanical and hydraulic maintenance maps well from RN marine engineering
  - HV/SAP ladder to £55k–£75k plus allowances
  - pro-veteran developers (Ørsted, RWE Gold, SSE)
  - ELCAS-fundable GWO course
- Cons:
  - working at height in harsh weather, physically demanding
  - lower pay than oil and gas
  - developer and supply-chain volatility
  - must live near an east-coast or Scottish hub
- Lifestyle: 2/2 on an SOV (sea-going, familiar to a submariner but with far more home time), or daily CTV or onshore shifts.

**Grid and networks: strongest long-term fit**
- Pros:
  - regulated funding to 2033 and beyond
  - NVQ Level 3 electrician is a direct entry
  - paid training to SAP (National Grid £47k–£57k while training, plus £5k SAP allowance)
  - home most nights, Monday to Friday days plus standby
  - ex-forces programmes (SPEN EMPower, National Grid VERG, ENWL guaranteed interview, UKPN work experience)
- Cons:
  - lowest ceiling without moving into engineering or SAP management
  - standby call-outs
  - contractor roles involve travel and overnights
  - less adventurous work
- Lifestyle: closest to a normal civilian 9-to-5 with on-call duties.

**Maritime: good fit, lifestyle-dependent**
- Pros:
  - shortage of ETOs and technical officers
  - RN marine engineering closely matches the work
  - RFA offers naval continuity and a civil service pension
  - superyacht ETO pay is high
  - ferries can be home daily or week-on/week-off
- Cons:
  - deep-sea and RFA mean months away
  - RFA crewing shortfalls stretch tours
  - superyacht work is insecure and owner-driven
  - the CoC conversion is required first (covered elsewhere)
- Lifestyle: ranges from home daily (Red Funnel) to 4–6 months away (deep-sea or RFA).

**Oil and gas: weak long-term fit, useful short-term**
- Pros:
  - highest day rates (about £400–£540/day; HV AP up to about £620/day, secondary)
  - 2/2 or 2/3 rotations
  - CompEx and BOSIET carry over to CCS, hydrogen, FPSOs and some wind
- Cons:
  - no new licences
  - production and capex in steep decline by 2032
  - short PAYE contracts
  - adverts want about 5 years' oil and gas experience plus CompEx, a barrier for a direct entrant
  - no tax advantage on fixed installations
- Lifestyle: 2 or 3 weeks offshore, then 2 or 3 weeks fully off, travelling by helicopter from Aberdeen, Norwich or Blackpool.

**Combining sectors**
- A plausible hybrid is offshore wind HV/SAP, then onshore grid SAP. Both use the same safety-rules culture and AP/SAP authorisation progression, which gives an exit from rotations later in life.
- Holding an ETO CoC as well keeps SOV, WTIV and ferry options open.

### Gaps
- No source directly compares how well RN submariner (ETME) competencies are recognised by wind OEMs or network operators. Recognition seems to happen case by case under "recognise military skills" pledges.
- I found no data on how many of these ex-forces schemes (for example EMPower, Mission Renewable) are likely to still exist in 2032.
