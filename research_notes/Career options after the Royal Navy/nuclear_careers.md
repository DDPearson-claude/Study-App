# UK nuclear-sector careers (civil and defence), 2030–2035, for an ex-submariner marine engineer who is also a qualified electrician

Research date: 14 November 2026. All job adverts were seen in search results or on employer pages on 14 November 2026 unless stated otherwise; their posting and closing dates are recorded where shown. Figures are UK only. Older data and dates that have slipped before are flagged.

## 1. Demand outlook to the mid-2030s: workforce size and forecasts

### Takeaway
The combined civil and defence nuclear workforce was about 103,000 at 31 October 2025, up from 83,000 in 2023. Projected demand is about 119,000 in 2030 and about 123,000 through the mid-2030s, then rises to about 156,000 by 2043, driven mainly by defence. The Defence Nuclear Enterprise (DNE) alone supports over 47,000 jobs, with demand forecast at 65,000 by 2030. For someone leaving around 2032 the demand picture is strong, and it is strongest in defence.

### Cited Findings
- Nuclear Workforce Assessment (NWA) 2025, published November 2026: the workforce "now stands at approximately 103,000 people, up from around 83,000 in 2023". Over the same period, the gap between the workforce and the people needed by 2030 "has narrowed from 40,000 to 16,000". This is the first NWA produced under the Nuclear Skills Plan, with data as at 31 October 2025. — [Cogent Skills, NWA 2025](https://cogentskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Nuclear-Workforce-Assessment-2025-Final.pdf)
- NWA 2025 demand curve: about 119,000 in 2030, holding broadly level at about 123,000 through the mid-2030s, then climbing sharply to 156,000 by 2043. The sharp increase from the late 2030s "is driven primarily by Defence activity". The NWA says the flat early-to-mid-2030s profile "is likely to understate demand" because not all organisations gave forecasts beyond 5 to 10 years. — [Cogent Skills, NWA 2025](https://cogentskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Nuclear-Workforce-Assessment-2025-Final.pdf)
- NWA 2025 composition: engineering is now the largest functional area in both the workforce and recruitment, and people aged 20 to 29 make up 24% of the workforce. — [Cogent Skills, NWA 2025](https://cogentskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Nuclear-Workforce-Assessment-2025-Final.pdf)
- NWA 2024 (older data): workforce 96,000 in 2024, up 15% from 83,000, with 8,768 new hires and projected demand of 120,000 by 2030. — [Cogent Skills, NWA 2024](https://cogentskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Nuclear-Workforce-Assessment-2024-FINAL25.pdf)
- The NWA and the NIA Jobs Map "measure different things": the NWA covers civil and defence; the NIA Jobs Map estimates civil nuclear employment and its supply chain. — [Cogent Skills, NWA 2025](https://cogentskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Nuclear-Workforce-Assessment-2025-Final.pdf)
- NIA Jobs Map 2026 (civil only): a record 102,606 civil nuclear jobs in 2026, up 67% from 61,371 in 2021 and up 4,433 since 2025. 86% of jobs are outside London and the South East. South West: 34,084 jobs (up 163.6% since 2021, driven by Hinkley Point C). North West: 30,445. Wales: 2,210. The operating stations support around 5,000 jobs. — [NIA 2026 Jobs Map](https://www.niauk.org/industry/2026-jobs-map/); [NIA press release, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.niauk.org/uk-nuclear-jobs-hit-100000-for-first-time-as-britain-invests-in-energy-security/)
- DNE workforce: "more than 47,000 people", expected to grow "to around 65,000 by 2030" (MOD answer of 6 November 2026). — [UK Parliament written answer 39344](http://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-10-29/39344)
- DNE 2026 Annual Update (September 2026): early NWA indications are that the combined sector grew from 83,000 in 2023 to over 100,000 in 2025. In the first two years, Skills Plan members recruited over 2,000 graduates and over 4,000 apprentices, of whom about 1,300 graduates and 3,100 apprentices were in defence nuclear. "Eight indirect intervention contracts are now being mobilised to support mid-career switchers, improve cross-sector mobility, address regional challenges". — [GOV.UK, DNE 2026 Annual Update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament)
- "A National Endeavour" (MOD, updated August 2026): 47,600 jobs supported by the DNE; demand of 65,000 by 2030; a "DNE wage premium" of 20%; aims of 22,000 apprenticeships and 9,000 graduate roles in defence nuclear by 2034/35; 6,000+ UK suppliers; £100bn+ to be spent through UK suppliers in the next decade. It says the DNE "depends on skilled trades such as welders, electricians, and mechanical fitters". — [GOV.UK, A National Endeavour](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uks-nuclear-deterrent-a-national-endeavour/a-national-endeavour-nuclear-as-part-of-the-defence-engine-for-growth)
- DNE average salaries "reach £45,500 – 20% higher than the UK average" (government analysis, June 2025). — [GOV.UK, 18 Jun 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/1500-jobs-created-at-uk-nuclear-weapons-headquarters-as-sector-boasts-above-average-wages)
- Older baseline (March 2024, at the launch of the skills package): government and industry to invest at least £763m by 2030 to help "fill 40,000 new jobs by the end of the decade". The industry was "expected to need 123,000 people by 2030", and Royal Navy submariners were named among the target skill groups. — [GOV.UK, 24 Mar 2024](http://www.gov.uk/government/news/pm-announces-national-endeavour-to-strengthen-the-uks-nuclear-deterrent)
- The Nuclear Skills Plan was published by the Nuclear Skills Taskforce in May 2024 and is jointly delivered by MOD and DESNZ, backed by BAE Systems, Rolls-Royce Submarines, Babcock and EDF. — [GOV.UK, DNE 2026 Annual Update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament)

### Inferences
- The 2030 headline target (about 119,000 to 123,000) has stayed broadly stable since 2024, but the shortfall has fallen from 40,000 to 16,000. Entry in 2032 should therefore meet a less acute shortage than in 2024 to 2026. Demand still exceeds supply and is still rising, especially in defence from the late 2030s.
- Mid-career-switcher funding (the eight intervention contracts) suggests government-backed routes for experienced technicians will exist in the early 2030s. Their exact form is not yet public.

### Gaps
- I found no published NWA breakdown of demand by occupation (for example electrical technicians or operators) for 2030 to 2035. The NWA mentions separate "critical skills" research, which I did not locate.
- NSAN (National Skills Academy for Nuclear) material was not reached in this session.

## 2. Civil nuclear projects and their dates

### Takeaway
Hinkley Point C (HPC) Unit 1 is now scheduled for 2030; its start date has slipped repeatedly. Sizewell C reached its final investment decision (FID) on 22 July 2025 and financial close on 4 November 2025, with first power expected around 2038 to 2039. The first small modular reactor (SMR) project is Wylfa on Anglesey ("Gwyndod"), with three Rolls-Royce SMRs targeting the grid in the mid-2030s; its FID is still to come. All four generating AGR stations are due to close in March 2030. Sizewell B has a deal to run to 2055. Sellafield and the NDA group remain a large, stable employer (about 19,000 people).

### Cited Findings
**Hinkley Point C (Somerset)**
- In February 2026 EDF adjusted Unit 1's start of production to 2030, a 12-month slip from its 2029 best case, attributed to the mechanical, electrical and HVAC (MEH) works. Cost: £35bn in 2015 prices, against an initial £18bn. A further 12-month delay would add about £1bn (2015 prices). — [New Civil Engineer, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/latest/hinkley-point-cs-cost-climbs-to-35bn-with-confirmation-unit-1-will-power-up-in-2030-20-02-2026)
- History of slippage: originally end-2025; revised to 2027 in May 2022; in 2024 a 2030 base case with a 2029 to 2031 range. — [World Nuclear News, 28 May 2026](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/edf-highlights-hinkley-point-cs-positive-impacts); [Construction Enquirer, 22 Feb 2026](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2026/02/22/me-delay-pushes-hinkley-c-first-reactor-start-up-back-a-year)
- EDF: "The project is organised on Unit 1 becoming operational in 2030". In 2025 the project supported 26,000 jobs across Britain, Unit 1 civil construction was 95% complete, and MEH installation rates doubled in Q4 2025. — [EDF results release, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.edfenergy.com/media-centre/edf-backing-electric-britain-15bn-investment-planned-2026-28)
- Construction Enquirer gives the cost as £35bn in 2015 prices, "equivalent to £48bn today". — [Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2026/02/22/me-delay-pushes-hinkley-c-first-reactor-start-up-back-a-year)
- HPC has a 14,000-strong workforce, 34% of it local, and has trained 1,740 apprentices. — [EDF HPC Socio-economic Report 2026](https://www.edfenergy.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Hinkley%20Point%20C%20Socio-economic%20and%20Environmental%20Impact%20Report%202026.pdf); [EDF news, 26 May 2026](https://www.edfenergy.com/media-centre/new-report-measures-hinkley-point-cs-positive-impact-people-and-environment)
- Unit 2's reactor pressure vessel was installed on 1 June 2026. EDF says Unit 2 is being built 20 to 30% faster than Unit 1 (secondary source). — [Lechley programme page, read 12 Nov 2026](https://www.lechley.com/programmes/hinkley-point-c)
- Babcock/Cavendish Nuclear had 600+ people on HPC MEH work in 2025 and expected that team to "more than double in size". — [Babcock Annual Report 2025](https://www.babcockinternational.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Babcock-Annual-Report-and-Financial-Statements-2025.pdf)

**Sizewell C (Suffolk)**
- The FID was signed on 22 July 2025. The government is the largest shareholder alongside EDF, Centrica, La Caisse and Amber. The FID release cited 10,000 jobs at peak construction. — [GOV.UK, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sizewell-c-gets-green-light-with-final-investment-decision)
- Financial close was on 4 November 2025. Equity: UK government 44.9%, La Caisse 20%, Centrica 15%, EDF 12.5%, Amber 7.6%. Debt includes a National Wealth Fund term loan of up to £36.6bn. — [EDF Energy Holdings FY25 accounts](https://www.edfenergy.com/sites/default/files/2026-06/EEHL_FY25_Final_accounts_signed.pdf)
- NAO (May 2026): Sizewell C "is expected to start producing electricity from 2038". The FID came "at least 28 months later than originally planned". — [NAO, Sizewell C](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/sizewell-c.pdf)
- Sizewell C annual report: just under 9,000 direct and indirect jobs a year on average during construction, around 17,000 at peak, and about 7,900 working on site at peak. Its milestone timeline shows "J0" in 2029, followed by commissioning and operation. — [Sizewell C Annual Report 2025-26](https://www.sizewellc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Annual-Report-2025-26.pdf). Note that the 10,000 (FID release) and 17,000 (2026) peak figures differ; the 17,000 is direct plus indirect.
- July 2026 update: over 2,000 people on site each day, £4.89bn spent with 1,000+ UK suppliers, and 120 apprenticeships created. — [UK Parliament written statement HLWS262, 15 Jul 2026](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/detail/2026-07-15/hlws262)
- Cost estimates in secondary sources: "£38bn at 2024 prices" ([Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2026/02/22/me-delay-pushes-hinkley-c-first-reactor-start-up-back-a-year)); a critical policy brief cites a government central estimate of completion at the end of 2039 at £40.5bn (2024 money) ([Policy Brief, Univ. of Greenwich author](https://policybrief.org/briefs/to-fund-the-burnham-governments-priorities-abandon-hinkley-point-c-sizewell-c-and-wylfa)). These conflict; I did not verify the £40.5bn figure in a primary source.

**SMR programme: Great British Energy – Nuclear (GBE-N), Rolls-Royce SMR, Wylfa**
- Rolls-Royce SMR was named preferred bidder in June 2025. On 13 November 2025 the government named Wylfa (Anglesey) as the site for the first SMR station: an initial 3 units, a site that could host up to 8, about 3,000 jobs at peak construction, and power to the grid "from the mid-2030s". "GBE-N will start activity on the site in 2026." — [GOV.UK, 13 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/north-wales-to-pioneer-uks-first-small-modular-reactors)
- GBE-N and Rolls-Royce SMR signed the technology partner contract on 13 April 2026, starting site-specific design, regulatory engagement and planning "ahead of a future Final Investment Decision". £2.6bn was allocated in the 2025 Spending Review. — [GBE-N, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.greatbritishenergynuclear.uk/great-british-energy-nuclear-and-rolls-royce-smr-sign-contract)
- The contract has two stages: Stage One is site-specific design; Stage Two, the supply and installation of three units, needs a separate notice to proceed. Spending is managed within £2.6bn for the Spending Review period, and "additional funding requirements will be confirmed at future spending reviews". The National Wealth Fund will lend up to £599m. — [GOV.UK Technology Partner business case](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a5f794847af652afa0c8959/technology-partner-summary-business-case.pdf)
- GBE-N had awarded nearly £900m in contracts by June 2026, with at least 1.4GW from three reactors. — [GOV.UK, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-smr-programme-at-wylfa-hitting-target-for-british-contracts)
- The project is named "Gwyndod" (Gwyndod Power Station). It has three units of 498 MWe each, with first grid connection targeted for the mid-2030s. — [World Nuclear Association, UK outlook, 16 Oct 2026](https://world-nuclear.org/our-association/publications/world-nuclear-outlook-report/united-kingdom---world-nuclear-outlook-report)
- NIA: Gwyndod is expected to create 3,000 local jobs and 5,000 more across the UK at peak construction. — [NIA 2026 Jobs Map](https://www.niauk.org/industry/2026-jobs-map/)
- Generic Design Assessment (GDA) Step 3 began on 31 July 2024 and "is due to finish in December 2027". — [Rolls-Royce SMR GDA site](https://gda.rolls-royce-smr.com/gda-process). The Environment Agency is "targeting completing the GDA in 2027". — [UK Parliament HL15462, answered 20 Mar 2026](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-03-12/HL15462)
- ONR (June 2026): technical engagements specific to Wylfa were expected to start in FY2026/27, once the contract was signed. — [ONR FOI, 5 Jun 2026](https://www.onr.org.uk/publications/corporate-publications/foi-releases/2026/06/onr-wylfa-smr-project-communications)
- Critic's view (opinion, flagged): an FID "is not expected until 2029", and GDA could slip beyond July 2026 to December 2027 or later. — [Policy Brief](https://policybrief.org/briefs/to-fund-the-burnham-governments-priorities-abandon-hinkley-point-c-sizewell-c-and-wylfa)
- Other civil new-build: Centrica and X-energy propose Xe-100 reactors, with Hartlepool the preferred first site (12 units, 960 MWe). The design entered GDA in September 2026. GBE-N was asked to report by autumn 2026 on sites for a further large reactor. — [WNA UK outlook](https://world-nuclear.org/our-association/publications/world-nuclear-outlook-report/united-kingdom---world-nuclear-outlook-report); [GOV.UK, 13 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/north-wales-to-pioneer-uks-first-small-modular-reactors)

**Existing EDF fleet and life extensions**
- Heysham 1 (Lancashire) and Hartlepool (Teesside) are extended to March 2030 (decision of 22 July 2026; previously March 2028). This supports "more than 1000 jobs". Torness and Heysham 2 were not reviewed and remain at March 2030. — [EDF, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.edfenergy.com/media-centre/uk-energy-security-supported-further-nuclear-power-station-life-extensions)
- Slippage history: Heysham 1 and Hartlepool were due to close in March 2026 and were extended three times (Dec 2024 to 2027; Sep 2025 to 2028; Jul 2026 to 2030). — [EDF, 2 Sep 2025](https://www.edfenergy.com/media-centre/two-uk-nuclear-plants-generate-longer-supporting-energy-security); [S&P Global, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/072726-uk-reactor-extensions-to-cut-gas-demand-help-swing-to-exports-from-2028)
- EDF aims to run Heysham 2 "further, subject to plant inspections and regulatory approvals". Heysham 2 employs about 520 EDF staff plus 250+ contract partners. — [EDF, 8 Jan 2026](https://www.edfenergy.com/media-centre/heysham-2-takes-top-nuclear-generation-title). Torness employs about 550 plus 180 contractors (2024). — [BBC, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c33dvekx021o)
- Sizewell B is extended to 2055 under Heads of Terms for a 20-year Contract for Difference (£70.50/MWh, 2025 prices) from 2035. This supports about 900 jobs. The long-form contract is still to be finalised. — [GOV.UK, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sizewell-b-power-plant-given-lifetime-extension-to-2055)
- About £800m of Sizewell B refurbishment will be delivered over 15 years during outages every 18 months, replacing pipework, valves and pumps and adding new monitoring systems. — [EDF, 9 Jul 2026](https://www.edfenergy.com/media-centre/milestone-moment-sizewell-b-life-extension-agreement)
- EDF Nuclear Operations has about 5,000 permanent employees. EDF's nuclear family aims to hire 1,500 people in 2026. AGR extensions support about 2,000 staff jobs across four stations. 91 people moved from Nuclear Operations to HPC in 2025, and more than 600 over the last decade. — [EDF UK Nuclear Fleet Strategy Update 2026](https://www.edfenergy.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/FM11758-UK-Nuclear-Fleet-Strategy-Update-2026.pdf)
- Hunterston B transferred to the NDA's Nuclear Restoration Services (NRS) in April 2026, and Hinkley Point B was due to transfer in October 2026. — [EDF fleet update, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.edfenergy.com/media-centre/2025-fleet-update)

**Sellafield and NDA decommissioning sites**
- NDA group (October 2026): 19 sites, 19,000 people, 16,751 core employees. Sellafield Ltd has 10,808 core employees, NRS 4,065, Nuclear Waste Services 836, and Nuclear Transport Solutions 705. — [GOV.UK, NDA group People Report Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-nda-group-people-report/the-nda-group-people-report-october-2026)
- Sellafield Ltd employed an average of 11,817 people in 2025/26 (8,987 in projects and site operations). — [Sellafield Ltd ARA 2025/26](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ad61b3e0f7b53973e026916/Annual_Report___Accounts_2025_2026_accessible.pdf)
- Sellafield took on about 1,250 new starters in 12 months, including about 400 mid-career hires into front-line roles (Operations, Engineering, Health Physics). It is focused on growing Operations and Maintenance capability. (Evidence dated March 2025.) — [Parliament committee evidence, Mar 2025](https://committees.parliament.uk/publications/47440/documents/246109/default)
- The NDA's mission stretches "into the next century". — [NDA group careers](https://ndagroup.careers/about-the-nda-group/)

### Inferences
- By the 2032 exit, the generating AGR fleet will probably have closed (all four are due to close in March 2030), unless Heysham 2 or Torness win further extensions. AGR-era roles will then be mainly in defuelling and decommissioning, increasingly under NRS after transfer from EDF.
- In 2032 to 2035, the large civil operating opportunities are likely to be HPC operations (Unit 1 from 2030, Unit 2 after it), Sizewell B (to 2055), and Sizewell C's commissioning and operations build-up ahead of first power around 2038. Wylfa SMR commissioning and operations recruitment would fall in the mid-2030s if FID and construction proceed on time.
- HPC's 2030 date has slipped four times. Planning should allow at least 1 to 2 years of further slack for HPC and treat Wylfa's mid-2030s date as aspirational until FID.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source for the permanent operating headcount of HPC or Sizewell C once generating. Figures of around 900 per station are often quoted, but I did not verify them.
- No published FID date for Wylfa; no Wylfa operating headcount.
- The outcome of GBE-N's autumn 2026 report on sites for a further large reactor was not found.
- The Civil Nuclear Roadmap (January 2024, previous government) was not re-examined. WNA notes the 24 GW by 2050 goal has not been formally recommitted by the current government ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/our-association/publications/world-nuclear-outlook-report/united-kingdom---world-nuclear-outlook-report)).

## 3. Defence nuclear employers: growth outlook and ex-forces hiring

### Takeaway
Defence is the fastest-growing and best-funded part of the sector. Submarine build will double to one SSN-AUKUS boat every 18 months alongside Dreadnought. Rolls-Royce's Raynesway site is doubling in size (1,170 new roles), Barrow has grown to about 13,500 to 14,000 staff, Devonport has £4.4bn of investment over ten years, and Clyde has £250m+ of modernisation. All the main primes publicise veteran recruitment, and BAE Systems, Babcock and AWE hold the Armed Forces Covenant Employer Recognition Scheme (ERS) Gold award.

### Cited Findings
**Programme drivers**
- HMS Dreadnought is to complete sea trials and be "ready to enter service ... in the early 2030s". The £8.4bn Delivery Phase 4 includes a £5.9bn contract with BAE Systems. About 1,800 roles have been added at Barrow "under this government". — [GOV.UK, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/major-84bn-investment-advances-uk-nuclear-deterrent-and-backs-british-industry)
- SSN-AUKUS: steel cut on the first UK boat is planned for 2027, with the first into service in the "late 2030s". Decisions on total numbers are due before the end of the Detailed Design and Long Lead (D2L2) phase in 2028. The target is one submarine every 18 months, "doubling the current build rate". About £6bn is to be invested in Raynesway, Barrow and the supply chain over the Spending Review period. — [DNE 2026 Annual Update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament); [DNE 2026 PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa8f80d22b2d47f37ff2f6a/DNO-26-057_DNE_2026_Annual_Update_to_Parliament.pdf)
- The SSN fleet is to grow "from seven to up to 12 boats". — [GOV.UK, A National Endeavour](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uks-nuclear-deterrent-a-national-endeavour/a-national-endeavour-nuclear-as-part-of-the-defence-engine-for-growth)
- AUKUS "will create over 7,000 additional jobs", with "more than 21,000 personnel expected to be working on SSN-AUKUS in the UK at peak". — [Government response to Defence Committee AUKUS report](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5902/cmselect/cmdfence/520/report.html)

**Rolls-Royce Submarines (Raynesway, Derby)**
- Ground was broken on a new manufacturing facility (3 July 2026) as part of plans "to double the size of its entire Raynesway site", adding 100,000m² and "1,170 skilled roles". — [Rolls-Royce press release, 3 Jul 2026](https://www.rolls-royce.com/media/press-releases/2026/03-07-2026-rr-hits-key-milestone-in-major-site-expansion-to-support-uk-and-australia-defence-commitments.aspx)
- Rolls-Royce Derby grew from about 4,700 staff at the end of 2024 to about 5,600 in 2026, with about 200 further positions in Cardiff and Glasgow. — [Govt AUKUS response](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5902/cmselect/cmdfence/520/report.html). This conflicts with "4,000" in the National Endeavour site table ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uks-nuclear-deterrent-a-national-endeavour/a-national-endeavour-nuclear-as-part-of-the-defence-engine-for-growth)); the two probably use different scopes or dates.
- Australia is investing £2.4bn over ten years in Rolls-Royce infrastructure and SSN-AUKUS design. — [DNE 2026 Annual Update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament)
- Rolls-Royce also runs the Vulcan Naval Reactor Test Establishment (250 personnel) and a nuclear propulsion test facility in Caithness. — [GOV.UK, A National Endeavour](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uks-nuclear-deterrent-a-national-endeavour/a-national-endeavour-nuclear-as-part-of-the-defence-engine-for-growth)

**BAE Systems Submarines (Barrow-in-Furness)**
- Barrow grew from just under 11,000 in 2023 to 13,500 employed by BAE Systems, with regional DNE demand of around 16,500 by 2027. — [GOV.UK, A National Endeavour](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uks-nuclear-deterrent-a-national-endeavour/a-national-endeavour-nuclear-as-part-of-the-defence-engine-for-growth); [DNE 2025 Annual Update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2025-annual-update-to-parliament/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2025-annual-update-to-parliament)
- BAE Barrow headcount was about 12,000 in 2024 and about 14,000 in 2026. — [Govt AUKUS response](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5902/cmselect/cmdfence/520/report.html)
- The SMITE2 propulsion test facility concept design was completed in early 2026, and Ramsden Dock remediation is under way. — [DNE 2026 Annual Update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament/defence-nuclear-enterprise-2026-annual-update-to-parliament)
- Veterans: BAE holds ERS Gold, was a founding Covenant signatory, and was one of the first ERS Gold winners. It has a dedicated veteran recruitment team and the ForcesNet veteran network, and gives 15 extra days' leave for reservists. — [BAE Systems UK veterans page](https://www.baesystems.com/careers/locations/uk/veterans-reservists-and-armed-forces). Over 7,000 of its global team have served. — [BAE careers, veterans](https://careers.baesystems.com/life-at-bae-systems/veterans)

## 4. Roles that fit an ex-submariner marine engineer and electrician: pay, allowances and work pattern

### Takeaway
2026 adverts show a clear ladder:
- Time-served electrical craft roles: about £34,000 to £46,000 (Sellafield, Babcock, BAE).
- Contractor electricians at Sellafield under NAECI: about £23 to £26 per hour, often on a four-day week.
- AGR operations technicians: about £44,000 to £46,000 base, plus a shift allowance where applicable.
- HPC Nuclear Control Room Operator: £56,506 to £86,905, with Royal Navy experience explicitly named as relevant.
- HV Senior Authorised Person (SAP) roles: about £55,000 to £90,000 or more, plus shift and standby allowances.
Several better-paid technician-to-engineer roles ask for an HNC.

### Inferences
- The strongest fit for an ex-submariner ET(ME) who is also an NVQ L3 electrician is probably one of four routes, each with different pay and patterns:
  - Operations and control-room routes (EDF HPC, Sizewell B, later Sizewell C or Wylfa; Rolls-Royce Neptune and Vulcan; Babcock reactor engineer). These pay best (up to about £87,000 at HPC) and are mostly shift-based.
  - Electrical maintenance craft roles at Sellafield, Babcock or AWE. These pay about £34,000 to £48,000, mostly days or day and back shifts.
  - Commissioning roles at HPC, Barrow, Raynesway or Sizewell C, often on project contracts.
  - HV AP/SAP progression, which needs the HV authorisation training and experience that an NVQ L3 alone does not give.
- An HNC (electrical or mechanical) is a recurring shortlisting requirement for higher-paid roles (EDF CRO, BAE commissioning, Rolls-Royce Neptune and EC&I). Holding one, or a recognised equivalent, by 2032 would probably widen options considerably.
- Shift allowances in nuclear generation appear to be large. The older EDF Dungeness B figure of about £11,400 on about £38,000 base is roughly 30%, but it needs re-checking against current adverts.

- No current EDF advert stating a shift allowance amount was found. The figures above are from 2023-era adverts re-posted by aggregators.

## 5. How ex-forces, and especially ex-submariners, are recruited

### Takeaway
Named schemes are mostly employer-wide veteran offers rather than submariner-specific programmes:
- Babcock: guaranteed interview, ERS Gold, about 750 armed forces community joiners a year.
- BAE Systems: ERS Gold, dedicated veteran recruitment team, ForcesNet.
- AWE: ERS Gold.
- EDF: Forces Supporters Network and a Career Transition Partnership (CTP) link.
- Rolls-Royce SMR: Covenant signatory.
The clearest evidence that Royal Navy nuclear plant experience is valued is EDF's HPC Control Room Operator advert, which names "the Royal Navy" and "military experience" as relevant. Security clearance can be transferred if a new cleared role starts within 12 months of leaving, but it lapses when you leave, and clearance is not normally a prerequisite to apply.

### Cited Findings
- EDF partners with the Career Transition Partnership (CTP) and Forces Families Jobs. It runs a Forces Supporters Network and a talent community for service leavers. — [EDF Veterans careers](http://careers.edfenergy.com/veterans-careers-at-edf)
- The EDF CEO reaffirmed the company's Armed Forces Covenant commitment in May 2026 aboard HMS Belfast. — [EDF careers news, 2026](https://careers.edfenergy.com/news/2026-6/stronger-together-supporting-armed-forces-careers-at-edf)

- In March 2024 the government named "Royal Navy submariners" among the groups for which the skills package would create opportunities. — [GOV.UK, 24 Mar 2024](http://www.gov.uk/government/news/pm-announces-national-endeavour-to-strengthen-the-uks-nuclear-deterrent)
- Security clearance transfer criteria (UKSV):
  - The clearance must be no older than 10 years (SC or CTC) or 7 years (DV).
  - No more than 12 months may pass between leaving one organisation and joining another.
  - You must not have lived overseas for more than 6 months during a break in service.
  - For transfers into Defence, MOD also requires that the clearance has not expired.
  - "The security clearance will be lapsed upon submission of a Leaver notification, but may be eligible for reinstatement if the individual transfers to another security cleared role within 12 months."
  — [GOV.UK, UKSV aftercare and existing clearances](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dbs-national-security-vetting-aftercare-information/aftercare-and-existing-clearances)
- UKSV gives the explicit example of service personnel who leave and move "on to a role in defence industry or as a MOD civilian". The new sponsor requests the transfer, at the same or a lower level. — [GOV.UK, UKSV existing clearance holders](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/united-kingdom-security-vetting-existing-clearance-holders)
- Clearance is owned by the sponsor, not the individual. — [GOV.UK, UKSV applicant guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/united-kingdom-security-vetting-applicant)
- Government policy is that "individuals should not be expected to hold an existing security clearance in order to apply for posts ... that require vetting, except in exceptional circumstances". — [GOV.UK, HMG Personnel Security Controls](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hmg-personnel-security-controls/hmg-personnel-security-controls-html)
- DV is required for "frequent and uncontrolled access to Category I nuclear material". DV is reviewed at least every 7 years and SC every 10. — [GOV.UK, NSV clearance levels](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/united-kingdom-security-vetting-clearance-levels/national-security-vetting-clearance-levels)
- Employer clearance requirements in current adverts:
  - Rolls-Royce Submarines: SC without caveats, with Rolls-Royce supporting the application if you do not hold it, and UK nationality ([Rolls-Royce posting](https://engradar.com/jobs/workday:rollsroyce:/job/Derby/Test-Facility-Engineer---Submarines_JR6160469)).
  - BAE Barrow: BPSS minimum, with many roles needing higher vetting and 5 to 10 years' UK residency ([BAE](https://jobsearch.baesystems.com/job/senior-commissioning-engineer-electrical-00139210)).
  - Babcock Devonport: SC ([Careers in Defence](https://careersindefence.com/jobs/nuclear-facilities-electrical-engineer-babcock-devonport)).
  - EDF operations: CTC with 3 years' residency ([EDF](https://careers.edfenergy.com/job/operate-technician-in-morecambe-jid-1847)).
- A resettlement magazine advises recording your clearance level, grant date and sponsor before leaving, and describing it accurately on your CV (for example "SC, held until March, sponsored by MOD"). — [Pathfinder International, Oct 2026](https://pathfinderinternational.co.uk/security-clearance-guide-service-leavers/)
- A vetting firm's blog says nuclear DV roles may involve an extra ONR stage, giving a planning assumption of up to about 190 days. No transfer timescale has been published (secondary source). — [Scantec blog, Oct 2026](https://www.scantec.co.uk/blog/view/342/index2/how-long-dv-clearance-takes-2026)

### Inferences
- An existing SC or DV is most valuable if a new cleared employer is lined up so the gap after discharge is under 12 months. Leaving in 2032 and starting at a defence prime (Rolls-Royce, BAE, Babcock, AWE) within that window could avoid fresh vetting delays, which can run to months for DV.
- Recognition of RN nuclear experience appears to come mainly through accelerated training or higher starting points within pay bands (EDF CRO) rather than formal credit. Submarine reactor qualifications are not a civil licence.

### Gaps
- I found no named submariner-specific scheme at any employer, and no evidence of formal mapping between RN nuclear qualifications and civil competence (for example Sellafield's "SQEP" status, meaning suitably qualified and experienced person) or the ONR's view of such experience.
- The Sellafield Ltd and Cavendish Nuclear veteran offers and ERS status were not verified.
- The Nuclear Skills Plan's eight mid-career intervention contracts are not yet described publicly in detail, so I could not tell whether any target service leavers.

## 7. Risks: delays, funding and the end of the AGR fleet

### Takeaway
The main risks are:
- Further HPC slippage (the date has moved from 2025 to 2027, to 2029–2031, to 2030).
- Sizewell C's long timeline (power around 2038 to 2039).
- SMR funding beyond the current Spending Review and an FID still pending.
- The AGR fleet ending generation by March 2030, before a 2032 exit.
Defence demand is more stable, but SSN-AUKUS numbers are not fixed until 2028.

### Cited Findings
- HPC worst case: a further 12-month delay at about £1bn extra (2015 prices). HPC has been funded solely by EDF equity since the end of 2023. — [New Civil Engineer](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/latest/hinkley-point-cs-cost-climbs-to-35bn-with-confirmation-unit-1-will-power-up-in-2030-20-02-2026)

### Inferences
- For a 2032 exit, the lowest-risk civil options are probably Sizewell B (to 2055), HPC operations (once Unit 1 is online) and NDA/Sellafield decommissioning, which runs for decades. Sizewell C and Wylfa operations are mid-to-late-2030s prospects and depend on funding.
- Defence nuclear (Barrow, Derby, Devonport, Clyde, AWE) appears the most resilient to civil funding swings. It is underpinned by Dreadnought into service in the early 2030s, a submarine every 18 months, and £6bn of Spending Review-period investment. Its main risk is programme scale decisions in 2028.

### Gaps
- No independent assessment was found of the probability of further HPC slippage beyond EDF's own worst case.
- No official statement was found on the Spending Review treatment of SMR Stage Two funding or on a Wylfa FID date.
