# Leaving the Royal Navy voluntarily after about 6 years: rules, entitlements and timing traps for a Royal Navy rating (ET(ME) Submariner)

Scope note: UK and Royal Navy rules as published to November 2026. Primary sources are BRd 3(1) Naval Personnel Management (Version 8, effective June 2026), the Armed Forces Pay Review Body (AFPRB) reports for 2024, 2025 and 2026, JSP 822 (Elective Education, V8.1 October 2026, published on GOV.UK 2 November 2026), MOD Discover My Benefits (DMB), GOV.UK and AFPS 15 scheme guidance. Unofficial sources are labelled "(unofficial)". Rules can change before 2032, especially submariner pay (the Submarine Remuneration Review is still being rolled out) and State Pension age.

Access limits: the current JSP 534 (resettlement, v24, March 2025) is published only as an .odt file and could not be opened in this session; JSP 754 (pay and financial incentives, where the exact repayment terms for incentives sit) is on the MOD intranet only. Figures that depend on them come from older or secondary MOD material and are flagged.

## 1. Notice to leave (voluntary outflow / Early Termination) for RN ratings

### Takeaway
A trained rating can give 12 months' notice (Early Termination, also called voluntary outflow or VO) once they have served a minimum of 2.5 years after becoming a Gain to Trained Strength (GTS), and only if no training or financial Return of Service is still outstanding. That puts the earliest possible discharge at about GTS + 3.5 years. Notice can be withdrawn only by request, and career managers can refuse the request.

### Cited Findings
- **Earliest point to give notice:** "ratings and ORs must complete a minimum of 2.5 years' service from the point of becoming Gained to Trained Strength (GTS) before they are eligible to exercise their right to give 12 months' notice to leave the Service" (BRd 3(1) para 0506). Ratings become eligible to apply for Early Termination "2 ½ years after they have joined the Trained Strength; this is on completion of Phase 1 and Phase 2 training" — [BRd 3(1) v8, effective June 2026](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Notice is 12 months, subject to RoS:** "All ratings will be allowed to submit 12 months' notice to leave the Service, subject to a minimum Return of Service (RoS) after specified courses, or RoS associated in respect of promotion, training received or associated with the award of a Financial Retention Initiative (FRI). Ratings must complete 2½ years' service after completing their Standard Initial Training Period (SITP) before they may be released." — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Minimum Time to Serve (MTS):** for ratings, the MTS before notice can be submitted is "30 months from achieving GTS. Length of notice period is 12 months" (stated in an ET(ME) entry section; the same 30-month MTS is applied to other entry routes) — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Earliest discharge date is recorded on enlistment:** "An individual's earliest date of discharge will have been recorded on the S3049 which individuals sign on enlisting in the Service (a copy of which is given to individuals)." — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Submarine-specific example:** for Accelerated Apprentice submariners, the MTS starts once GTS is reached after the LETQC pipeline, "where AA(SM)s must deliver 30 months of service before they may submit their request for early termination (42 months total). The RoS for LETQC (24 months) will commence at the same time and working out of the liability will run concurrently." — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **For submarine ratings, GTS / first sea assignment is tied to SMQ:** "New entries are available for their first sea assignment (FSA) from the date of completion of Phase 2 training. For Submarine ratings, this will be when they have successfully completed the Submarine Qualification (SMQ) Dry." — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **No right to less than 12 months:** "there is no entitlement to notice of less than 12 months". Someone who wants to leave sooner "may then negotiate for earlier release with their employer and CM" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Leaving early through a non-standard route:** ratings may apply for non-standard Early Termination before the end of their MTS. Those who have finished their MTS but are still blocked by an outstanding RoS "for undertaking further training or receipt of financial remuneration" can ask for a RoS waiver. A RoS Board decides waivers case by case, and "will also consider the recovery of any costs for Training or other RoS" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Withdrawing notice:** "CMs may decide not to approve requests for VO withdrawal, drawing on advice from Strategic Workforce Planners. When necessary to avoid exceeding funded personnel levels, approval of requests for VO withdrawal may be restricted to those requests that would help relieve Workforce Capability Gaps (WCG) or for specialisations that are predicted to be significantly under borne." — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Statutory basis:** under the Royal Navy Terms of Service (Ratings) Regulations 2006, reg. 8(5), a rating may "give 12 months' notice in writing to his commanding officer of his desire to be discharged". Under reg. 8(6), anyone who "consents in writing to be restricted in the exercise of the right ... to give 12 months' notice, in consideration of being permitted to undergo a course of instruction or receiving any other benefit or advantage, shall not exercise such right before the expiration of the period specified in such consent." — [legislation.gov.uk SI 2006/2918](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2006/2918/body/made)
- The notice period was cut from 18 months to 12 months by the 2000 amendment regulations — [Royal Navy Terms of Service (Ratings) (Amendment) Regulations 2000](https://openlegislation.uk/royal-navy-terms-of-service-ratings-amendment-regulations-2000)
- **Effects of giving notice that matter for timing:**
  - Time spent ashore before termination depends on length of service: "Under 6 years 4 months (but 6 months post-LFS assignment)"; "6 years and above 6 months" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
  - Sea assignments are not normally shorter than 12 months, but "may be reduced to three months for shortage categories and personnel who have submitted 12 months' notice". Ratings "will normally be assigned to submarines only if they have at least three years to serve" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
  - If notice is given while at sea and the rating is moved ashore with more than 9 months before Terminal Leave starts, they should be put in a complement position and "GRT should be undertaken during the course of that final assignment" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
  - Time-based advancement and the acting higher rate apply only to ratings who have "not requested Early Termination". Submitting Early Termination is also a stated reason for removal from the Fast Track scheme — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
  - The Optional Exit Points that let submariners give more than 12 months' notice apply only to engagements accepted before 28 February 2014, so they are legacy rules — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- (Unofficial) A commercial explainer says Premature Voluntary Release (PVR) applications go through the chain of command and career manager, with "formal notification of Termination Date (12 months forward for most cases)" — [notice-period-calculator.uk (unofficial)](https://notice-period-calculator.uk/armed-forces-voluntary-release)

### Inferences
- Earliest possible discharge = GTS + 30 months (MTS) + 12 months notice = GTS + 42 months, or later if a training RoS is still running. For an ET(ME)SM whose GTS falls about 12 to 18 months after joining, the earliest exit is roughly 4.5 to 5 years after joining. A 6-year exit is therefore not blocked by the MTS. It is decided by the RoS attached to any LET qualifying course (see section 2) and by when the 12 months' notice is submitted.
- To discharge at or just after the 6-year anniversary of joining, notice has to go in about 5 years after joining. Because notice ends certain career benefits (time-based advancement and acting rate, Fast Track), submitting it is a one-way step in practice, and withdrawing it depends on the career manager's approval.
- Leaving at 6 years or more also gives 6 months ashore before termination instead of 4 months (subject to Service needs). That is more time for resettlement.

### Gaps
- The general Chapter 53/54 wording on whether the final year of any RoS can always overlap with the 12-month notice period was not extracted. The overlap is stated only for UGAS ("the last year of a RoS and the 12-month notice period can run concurrently") and for AA(SM) (LETQC RoS runs concurrently with the MTS). Check the general rule with the career manager.
- The exact GTS point for a standard (non-Accelerated Apprentice) ET(ME)SM entrant (end of Phase 2 versus SMQ Dry versus after the ET course) was not confirmed from the ET(ME)SM pipeline chapter.
- No current official procedure for withdrawing notice (form, deadline) was found in the public documents.

## 2. Return of Service (RoS) obligations and how they move the earliest leaving date

### Takeaway
A RoS stops a rating giving notice until it is served. For an ET(ME)SM the ones that matter are the qualifying courses for promotion: the LET(MESM) qualifying course carries a 24-month RoS, and the POET(MESM) qualifying course and the CPO-level Nuclear Propulsion Supervisor course carry 36 months. Financial Retention Incentives (FRIs) and golden hellos also carry RoS. Promotion itself does not, but the course needed for it does. Agreeing to a qualifying course after about year 4 can push the earliest exit past 6 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Why RoS exists:** "When RN and RM personnel undertake specific courses of training, studies or receive financial bonuses, the RN requires a return on its investment. The normal requirement is that individuals complete an effective period of service called a Return of Service (ROS)." Training RoS is split into initial training (before joining the trained strength) and further training (after it) — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Promotion versus course:** "A return of service applies for many courses in recognition of the investment made in the individual. Promotion does not carry any return of service. Chapter 53 contains detailed regulations. In view of the relatively frequent changes, it is recommended that recent DINs and RNTMs are also scrutinised." — [BRd 3(1) v6, effective November 2025](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd-3.pdf?rev=464d58dca353438d8aefbe3eacc19797). The v8 notice paragraph still says notice is subject to "RoS associated in respect of promotion" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780). The two wordings differ slightly. In practice the RoS comes from the qualifying course that is a pre-requisite for promotion (next three findings).
- **LET(MESM):** a pre-promotion requirement is "Minimum 24 months length of service remaining post career course to complete attached Return of Service (RoS)." (Table 74B-3) — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **POET(MESM):** "Minimum 36 months length of service remaining post career course to complete attached Return of Service (RoS)." (Table 74B-5) — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **CPOET(MESM):** "Minimum 36 months length of service remaining post NPSupC to complete attached Return of Service (RoS)." (Table 74B-9) — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **UGAS (Upper Graduate Apprenticeship Scheme) route:** the UGAS(MESM) LET Qualifying Course (ME176) carries a 24-month RoS (Table 73L-1). The scheme aims "to secure 36 months' qualified sea service" as a POET(MESM)/POET(WESM) before the BEng starts. Also: "the last year of a RoS and the 12-month notice period can run concurrently" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **When the RoS clock starts:** for WESM courses, the LET RoS "starts on completion of LETQC and all elements of the LET(WESM) training pipeline (inc equipment and platform specific specialist training)". The POET RoS starts on completion of POETQC and its pipeline (Table 53C note 10). For Fast Track and Intermediate Fast Track, the training RoS is "incurred from the start date of the course and will be applied, subject to Service needs irrespective of the final course outcome", although the RoS period itself starts on completion of LETQC/POETQC. Individuals "who are withdrawn from training, for whatever reason, or who effectively withdraw themselves from training, will have a period of TRoS applied." — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Signing away the right to give notice:** "Individuals who are eligible to apply for Early Termination will be required to waive that right for a period equal to the length of the training course (if there is no ROS attached to the course) or the length of the course plus any ROS required for volunteering to attend a further training course." The same 'ROS Declaration' applies to FRIs — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Extension of service:** personnel without enough time left on their engagement "must agree to request an extension of service ... to cover the length of course and its associated ROS" on Form S61 before training starts. Personnel without enough service left for the LETQC/POETQC/NPSupC RoS "may be offered an Extension of Service (EoS). Acceptance of the EoS will be required prior to start" of the course — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Joint and tri-Service courses:** RoS details for these are in JSP 750 Chapter 3, Annexes A and B. Table 53C covers RN-only further training — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- **Submarine service liability:** a rating compulsorily streamed to submarines can apply to transfer to General Service after "5 years from the point of initial submarine qualification" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780). This is a minimum tour in submarines, not a bar on leaving.
- **Older precedent for transferees (2016):** engineers transferring into the Submarine Service received a £5,000 Golden Hello on gaining Dolphins, and the transfer "comes with it a five year return of service once they become submarine qualified" — [Royal Navy news, 10 Feb 2016](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/news/2016/february/10/160210-engineers-for-submarines). This covered transferees in 2016 and may not apply to direct-entry ET(ME)SM in 2026.
- **Financial incentives carry RoS (examples):** RN catering retention payments are £10,000 at four years' service "attracting a three-year Return of Service" and £15,000 two years after promotion to OR4 with a further three-year RoS. Engineering Stabilisation Incentives include an "RN Air Engineer Technician Financial Retention Incentive (FRI) of £30,000 over two years for a three-year RoS" and "the re-profiling of RRP (Naval Service Engineer) ... for Marine Engineers and Weapon Engineers" — [AFPRB 54th Report 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/682f2121b33f68eaba9539a8/AFPRB_Report_2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Main timing trap:** if the LET(MESM) qualifying course is taken at about year 3.5 to 4, its 24-month RoS runs to about year 5.5 to 6. If the last year of the RoS can overlap with notice (stated for UGAS and AA(SM); confirm for the standard route), discharge at about 6 years is still possible. If no overlap is allowed, the earliest exit becomes about year 6.5 to 7. Taking LETQC later, at about year 4.5 or more, almost certainly pushes the earliest exit beyond 6 years.
- Starting the POET(MESM) qualifying course (36-month RoS) before about year 5 is incompatible with leaving at 6 years unless a RoS waiver is granted, and a waiver may involve recovery of training costs.
- Accepting any FRI or retention payment usually commits the rating to a set period, three years in the published examples. Accepting one in years 3 to 5 could push the exit to year 6 to 8.
- A RoS waiver is discretionary and may carry repayment, so it should not be relied on in planning.

### Gaps
- Table 53C (the full list of RN further-training RoS by course) was not extracted line by line, and the RoS (if any) for SMQ, Dolphins or the Nuclear Propulsion Operators Course (NPOC) for an LET/AB was not confirmed.
- The general rule on whether a direct-entry ET(ME)SM has a RoS attached to submarine qualification itself (unlike the 2016 five-year RoS for transferees) was not found.
- JSP 754 (exact FRI and golden hello repayment clauses) is on the MOD intranet only.

## 3. Submarine pay and incentives (2025–2026) and what has to be repaid

### Takeaway
Submariner pay is in transition. RRP (Submarine) is still a daily payment (Level 1 £17.33 a day from 1 April 2026, up to Level 5 £38.10). Nuclear Skills Pay replaced the nuclear RRPs from 1 January 2026. A single "Submarine Pay" that also replaces the Submarine Golden Hello is planned for 1 January 2027. The Royal Navy advertises a £5,000 Dolphins bonus and a £25,000 retention payment "after six years as a qualified submariner". The AFPRB describes the retention payment as payable "between eight and twelve years qualification". On either reading, someone leaving about 6 years after joining (about 4 to 5 years after qualifying) would not reach it. Repayment terms are not published.

### Cited Findings
- **Advertised extras (RN careers site):** "A £5,000 (£7,500 for Warfare Specialist Submariners) bonus on being awarded your Dolphins"; "Additional Submariner Pay is awarded upon completion of submariner training and starts at £6,106 raising to £10,690; depending on time served as a submariner"; "Receive a £25,000 bonus after serving six years as a qualified submariner" — [Royal Navy pay and benefits](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/why-navy/pay-and-benefits); also [royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/why-navy/pay](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/why-navy/pay)
- ET(ME)SM role page: starting salary "at least £27,282" and potential earnings "£102,000 per year as your career progresses (Not including Longer Separation Allowance)" — [RN ET(ME)(Submariner) role page](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/roles/engineering-technician-marine-submariner)
- The Medical Assistant (Submariner) page has the same wording: "£25,000 Retention Payment after serving 6 years as a Submariner" — [RN MA(SM) role page](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/roles-and-specialisations/services/submarine-service/medical-assistant-submariner)
- The Submarine Service page advertises "up to £27k joining bonus, £5k training bonus and up to £3,200 per month additional pay for time at sea". The £27,000 joining bonus appears on officer role pages (Engineer Officers), not on the ET(ME)SM rating page — [RN Submarine Service](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/submariners); [RN Marine Engineer Officer (SM)](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/roles/marine-engineer-officer-submariner)
- **Nuclear Skills Pay** is advertised (on the Degree Top Up rating role) as "ranging from £5 per day to £32.50 per day" — [RN Submarine Engineering Rating – Degree Top Up](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/careers/roles/submarine-engineering-rating---degree-top-up-scheme)
- **Submarine Remuneration Review (AFPRB 2024):** "Submarine Pay" to replace RRP (Submarine) "and the Submarine Golden Hello with transition commencing from 1 April 2026"; "Nuclear Skills Pay" to replace RRP (Nuclear Propulsion), RRP (Weapon Engineer Submarine) and RRP (Engineer Officers' Supplement) from 1 April 2025; a Submarine Environmental Allowance to replace RRP (Submarine Supplement) from 1 July 2024; and "a Retention Payment of £25,000 should be payable between eight and twelve years qualification as a submariner with effect from 1 April 2025" — [AFPRB 53rd Report 2024](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/66a790baab418ab055592ebf/AFPRB_53rd_Report_2024_Accessible.pdf)
- **Revised timetable (AFPRB 2026):** "Nuclear Skills pay would be introduced on 1 January 2026 to replace the various technical RRPs that are available to submariners" (including RRP (Naval Service Engineer)); "Submarine Pay would be introduced on 1 January 2027 as the final element of SMRR implementation"; "the new Submarine Environment Allowance would be delivered concurrently with Submarine Pay". MOD said "the first retention payment had been successful in stabilising and increasing the useable workforce". Recommendation 8 adds "a second retention payment from 1 April 2026 for OR7 submariners with between 14 and 16 years' service and OF3 submariners ... with 12 years' service". MOD had "originally intended that this second retention payment should incentivise retention beyond 12 years, this being the point at which personnel would be eligible to receive a resettlement grant." — [AFPRB 55th Report 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a27f8383b15d05a7ce31f52/AFPRB_55th_Report_2026.pdf)
- The government accepted the 2026 recommendations in full, including the second submariner retention payment and a 3.6% uplift to "Nuclear Skills Supplements" — [Written statement HCWS101, 9 June 2026](http://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/detail/2026-06-09/hcws101)
- **RRP (Submarine) daily rates:**
  - From 1 April 2025: Level 1 £16.73, Level 2 £21.74, Level 3 £25.92, Level 4 £29.29, Level 5 £36.78. RRP (Submarine Supplement): harbour £6.70, sea £20.08 — [AFPRB 2025, Appendix 3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/682f2121b33f68eaba9539a8/AFPRB_Report_2025.pdf)
  - From 1 April 2026: Level 1 £17.33 and top level £38.10 (Levels 2 to 4 cut off in the extract). RRP (Nuclear Propulsion) Category C £4.15, B2 £8.34, B1 £16.66, A2 £55.50, A1 Level 1 £16.49. All RRPs rose 3.6% — [AFPRB 2026, Appendix 3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a27f8383b15d05a7ce31f52/AFPRB_55th_Report_2026.pdf)
  - The Afloat Environmental Allowance replaced RRP (Submarine Supplement), Unpleasant Living Allowance (Seagoing) and the MCMV allowance — [AFPRB 2026, footnote 105](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a27f8383b15d05a7ce31f52/AFPRB_55th_Report_2026.pdf)
- **Not pensionable:** RRP "is subject to Tax and National Insurance but is not pensionable" — [DMB: RRP](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/your-pay-and-pension/recruitment-and-retention-payments). Financial incentives (FRI, Golden Hello, Re-joining Bounty and so on) are "not pensionable", and "Your career manager will have details of current schemes" — [DMB: Financial Incentives](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/your-pay-and-pension/financial-incentives)
- **Engineer FRIs:** a 2020 FOI reply says General Service engineer FRIs (RNTM 01-021/17) did not cover submarine engineers, whose pay instead came through RRP (SM), RRP (NSE), RRP (Nuclear Propulsion), RRP (WE SM) and the SM Golden Hello. "All General Service FRIs have now ceased" — [MOD FOI2020/04288](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65e6f9f92f2b3be8b07cd7a7/FOI2020_04288.pdf)
- In December 2025 the MOD said retention measures include "Targeted Financial Retention Incentives" and a pay supplement trial for critical engineering skills, with "a 53% increase in intention to remain" — [UK Defence Journal, 16 Dec 2025](https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/mod-points-to-early-gains-in-retaining-military-engineers)
- **RoS with incentives (pattern):** golden hellos and retention payments approved by the AFPRB normally carry RoS. Examples: the nurses' Golden Hello (£50,000 with a five-year RoS) and the RN catering retention payments (three-year RoS each) — [AFPRB 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a27f8383b15d05a7ce31f52/AFPRB_55th_Report_2026.pdf); [AFPRB 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/682f2121b33f68eaba9539a8/AFPRB_Report_2025.pdf). Financial RoS can be waived only by a RoS Board, which "will also consider the recovery of any costs for Training or other RoS" — [BRd 3(1) v8](https://sccd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/footer-pages-documents-and-images/brd3-version-8.pdf?rev=db6c1b85335141bd8ec65e3d06123780)
- (Unofficial) A payslip site repeats the RN figures (Dolphins £5,000; additional pay £6,106 to £10,690; £25,000 after 6 years) and says none of these is pensionable — [PayslipIQ (unofficial)](https://payslipiq.co.uk/guides/royal-navy-pay-structure-2026)

### Inferences
- The RN site's "£6,106 to £10,690" equals the April 2025 RRP (Submarine) Levels 1 to 4 × 365 days (16.73 × 365 = £6,106; 29.29 × 365 = £10,691). The advertised figure is therefore the 2025 RRP (Submarine) scale. At the 2026 Level 1 rate (£17.33) it is about £6,325 a year.
- **Conflict on the £25,000 retention payment:** RN careers pages say "after six years as a qualified submariner". The AFPRB 2024 recommendation says "between eight and twelve years qualification". Either way, a rating who qualifies (Dolphins) about 1 to 2 years after joining and leaves about 6 years after joining will have about 4 to 5 years qualified, which is short of both. Staying to about year 7 or 8 after joining could reach the "six years qualified" point if the RN-site wording is right. Accepting it would very likely carry a RoS (the pattern for AFPRB-approved retention payments), and that would push the exit later again.
- Leaving forfeits all ongoing submarine and nuclear payments (daily RRP / Nuclear Skills Pay / future Submarine Pay) from discharge, and none of them adds to the pension.
- The £5,000 Dolphins bonus (Submarine Golden Hello) is due to be folded into Submarine Pay from 1 January 2027. A trainee qualifying after that date may get a different structure, so check the terms at the time.

### Gaps
- No public source gives the RoS or clawback terms for (a) the Dolphins bonus / Submarine Golden Hello for direct-entry ratings or (b) the £25,000 first retention payment. Both sit in JSP 754 or RN DINs, which are intranet only.
- The final structure and rates of "Submarine Pay" (due 1 January 2027) and the eligibility levels of Nuclear Skills Pay are not published beyond the RN-site range of £5 to £32.50 a day.
- No current ET(ME)SM-specific FRI was found in public sources.
- Navy News, Navy Lookout and Rum Ration coverage of the retention payment's exact eligibility was not found.

## 4. Resettlement through the Career Transition Partnership (CTP)

### Takeaway
The resettlement package changes sharply at 6 years. Below 6 years (4 to 6 years' service) the rating gets only the Employment Support Programme (ESP), with no Graduated Resettlement Time (GRT) and no training grant. At 6 years or more it is the Core Resettlement Programme (CRP): 20 days' GRT, the £534 Individual Resettlement Training Costs (IRTC) grant and 4 travel warrants. At 8 years and more it is 25 days and 5 warrants. CTP support runs from 2 years before discharge to 2 years after. GOV.UK says CRP is for those who have served "more than six years", so leaving exactly on the anniversary is borderline.

### Cited Findings
- **Programmes by length of service (GOV.UK, last updated 1 October 2024):**
  - CRP "is available to personnel who have served for more than six years. It is available from two years prior to discharge", with "up to 35 days Graduated Resettlement Time (GRT) ... The number of days is dependent upon length of Service", and "Support from the CRP is then available for a further two years after discharge."
  - ESP "is available to personnel who have served for between four and six years. It can be accessed from two years prior to discharge and for up to two years post-discharge."
  - Future Horizons is for those who "discharge before completing four years of Service".
  - CTP Assist is for personnel on Recovery Duties and others "who face significant additional challenges ... Access is provided regardless of the time served."
  - CTP is run by the MOD and Reed in Partnership.
  - Source: [GOV.UK: Career Transition Partnership](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/career-transition-partnership)
- The same thresholds appear in [GOV.UK: Getting employment support when leaving the armed forces](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/getting-employment-support-when-leaving-the-armed-forces) and in the June 2026 [Service Leavers' Guide](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4e19cc09605a87f4354531/Service_Leavers_Guide_June_26.pdf)
- **RN Discover My Benefits (updated 6 August 2026):**
  - "4-6 years of service": Employment Support Programme, with access to the Digital Platform for up to two years after leaving plus briefings such as the Employment Support Workshop.
  - "6+ years of service or medically discharged": CRP, which provides "training grants, allowances, travel warrants, authorised resettlement absences, transition workshops, one-to-one career guidance ..., subsidised vocational training support, housing advice, financial briefs and job finding support. The maximum entitlements are available after 16 years". IRTC can be used on CTP courses at "£26.70 per day" and combined with SLC/ELC.
  - GRT means "you continue to earn your daily pay" and are "paid travel and subsistence".
  - Resettlement Grant of "up to £15,047" needs "at least 12 years in the Regular Armed Forces".
  - Process: on entering the last 2 years of full career service, JPA changes status to Service Leaver or Early Service Leaver. The Resettlement Advisory Brief is due "within 3 months of entering the final 2 years ... or, if you are an Early Service Leaver, within 1 month of giving notice or on notification of discharge".
  - Source: [DMB Royal Navy: Resettlement](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/professional-development/resettlement/)
- **CTP statistics wording:** "The CRP is offered to service leavers with at least six years' service at their time of exit"; ESP for "between four and six years at exit"; FH for "fewer than four years at exit" — [MOD CTP Background Quality Report 2024/25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/698c5aa29db95e3291140b7b/Background_Quality_Report_Career_Transition_Partnership_annual_statistics_UK_Regular_Service_Personnel_Employment_Outcomes__2024-25.pdf)
- **GRT and IRTC tables (MOD FOI reply, 2018; current tables not re-verified):** "To qualify for the full resettlement package a Service Leaver must have completed 6 or more years' Service (unless Medically Discharged)." "All Service Leavers entitled to GRT are entitled to receive the full IRTC grant (£534)." — [MOD FOI, resettlement schemes 2012–2018](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5bf3ed35ed915d1813d80e79/03786.pdf)

- IRTC "could contribute up to a maximum of £534" for those with six or more years or any medical discharge — [Royal British Legion (charity, Jan 2025)](https://www.britishlegion.org.uk/get-support/expert-guidance/employment/education-training-armed-forces-community)
- IRTC is non-taxable, and its detailed rules are in JSP 534 Section 4 — [JSP 752 v67.1, Oct 2026, Ch 13 Sect 2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6abe38b1f04a15f533722856/JSP_752_-_Tri-Service_Regulations_for_Expenses_and_Allowances_.pdf)
- JSP 534 v24 (21 March 2025) was "simplified policy and procedure documents ... The overall resettlement policy provision and CTP programmes have not changed." — [GOV.UK: JSP 534 page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tri-service-resettlement-manual-jsp-534)
- (Charity) The RNRMC Resettlement Directory 2026 says ESP "is accessed 6 months prior to discharge and includes a 1 day workshop and one-to-one guidance session, resettlement briefs, job-finding support ... and access (on a standby basis) to vocational training courses", with career consultant support up to 2 years after discharge. CRP includes a "3-day Career Transition Workshop" plus travel and subsistence — [RNRMC Directory of Resettlement 2026](https://www.rnrmc.org.uk/sites/default/files/documents/Directory%20of%20Resettlement%202026%201.pdf). This conflicts with GOV.UK, which says ESP is available from 2 years before discharge.
- Since October 2024 CTP has been delivered by Reed in Partnership, working with the Forces Employment Charity — [Service Leavers' Guide June 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4e19cc09605a87f4354531/Service_Leavers_Guide_June_26.pdf)

### Inferences
- At 5 years, a leaver gets ESP only: a workshop, the digital platform and job support, with no GRT and no IRTC. At 6 years and more they get CRP with 20 days' GRT, £534 IRTC and 4 warrants. At 8 years and more GRT rises to 25 days and warrants to 5, while IRTC stays at £534. 6 years is the cliff edge, and 8 years is a minor step up.
- **Wording trap:** GOV.UK and the Service Leavers' Guide say "more than six years", while DMB, the FOI and the CTP statistics say "6+", "6 or more" and "at least six". To be safe, set the discharge date (end of terminal leave) a little after the 6-year anniversary of entry, and get the Service Resettlement Adviser to confirm reckonable service in writing.
- A voluntary leaver on 12 months' notice effectively has about 12 months to use resettlement before discharge, not 2 years. BRd 3 directs GRT into the final shore assignment, and the Resettlement Advisory Brief is triggered within 1 month of notice. Giving notice earlier (if RoS allows) buys more preparation time.

### Gaps
- The GRT and travel-warrant tables and the £534 IRTC figure come from a 2018 FOI reply and a 2025 charity page. The current JSP 534 v24 (March 2025) could not be opened to confirm them, and DMB's £26.70-a-day rate suggests the system is unchanged. Confirm with the SRA.
- Whether "6 years" for CTP purposes means reckonable service from entry (including Phase 1/2) was not confirmed from JSP 534 text.

## 5. Learning credits: Standard Learning Credits (SLC), Enhanced Learning Credits (ELC) and Publicly Funded FE/HE

### Takeaway
ELC requires registration as a scheme member; it is not automatic. Eligibility opens on completing Phase 1, so register now. For someone joining in 2026 the Lower Tier needs 6 years' qualifying service: up to £1,000 a claim, three claims in separate financial years, or one aggregated £3,000 claim. The Higher Tier needs 8 years: up to £2,000 a claim, £6,000 in all. ELC covers at most 80% of fees and must be used within 5 years of discharge. Completing 6 years also unlocks the Publicly Funded FE/HE scheme (a fully funded first Level 3 qualification or first degree). SLC (£175 a year) can be used only while serving.

### Cited Findings
- **Registration (latest JSP 822, Chapter 8, V8.1 Oct 2026, published 2 Nov 2026):** "From 1 Apr 16, those completing Phase 1 training, as well as all currently serving SP/Veterans who are not already members of the ELC scheme, are eligible to register for the scheme. Registration is not automatic; SP/SL must contact their sS to complete registration process." Also: "New Regular and FTRS SP are eligible for scheme membership but must contact their sS education staff to initiate registration as this is not an automatic process." — [JSP 822 Ch 8 Elective Education, Oct 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf)
- **Award types:**
  - Lower Tier: "up to a maximum of 3 awards of up to £1k per FY. SP or SL must have completed 6 or more years' qualifying service".
  - Aggregated Lower Tier: "6 or more years of qualifying service on or after 1 Apr 16 ... a single award of up to £3k", not available to those who "previously accessed a Lower Tier award, or qualify for Higher Tier awards".
  - Higher Tier: "up to a maximum of 3 awards of up to £2k per FY ... 8 or more years of qualifying service".
  - Source: [JSP 822 Ch 8, Oct 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf); same text in [JSP 822 Vol 9 v4.0, Nov 2025](https://www.enhancedlearningcredits.com/uploads/documents/jsp_822_volume_9_elective_education.pdf)
- **Old 4-year rule:** "For Lower Tier claims, those who have completed 4 years of qualifying service before 1 Apr 17 are eligible. For those who have not completed 4 years of qualifying service before 1 Apr 17, 6 years of qualifying service is required." Only service after 1 April 2000 counts, and unpaid career breaks do not count — [JSP 822 Ch 8, Oct 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf)
- **Using the credits:** ELC can be used "during their Service and for up to five years after discharge". One claim per financial year (1 April to 31 March), "for learning that starts within that financial year". It funds "up to 80% of the total course cost subject to maximum tier limits", and the claimant pays at least 20%. Only Level 3+ (RQF), or SCQF Level 6+, with approved providers — [JSP 822 Ch 8, Oct 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf)
- **After leaving:** "you may submit a claim up to five years (if you left on/after 1st April 2016) or up to ten years (if you left before 1st April 2016) after you leave the service" — [ELCAS: Service Leaver Claiming ELC](https://www.enhancedlearningcredits.com/service-leaver/ex-service-guidance). "You must be a registered Scheme Member with sufficient accrued service before submitting an online claim." — [ELCAS home](https://www.enhancedlearningcredits.com/)
- **DMB (RN):** "£1,000 for a lower-tier claim or £2,000 for a higher-tier claim", or aggregate three lower-tier claims into "a single one-off £3,000 claim". Submit "at least 25 clear working days before the course starts". In resettlement, IRTC can fund the 20% contribution and GRT can be used to attend. SLC "may not be used simultaneously" with ELC on the same course — [DMB RN: Enhanced Learning Credits](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/professional-development/enhanced-learning-credits/)
- **Standard Learning Credits:** "You may claim 80% of fees ... up to a maximum of £175 per financial year, but you must have permission to claim prior to the start of the course", "while in Service or during your resettlement phase", applied for via MyNavy Learning & Development — [DMB RN: Standard Learning Credits](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/professional-development/standard-learning-credits). JSP 822 confirms SLC can be used for resettlement purposes in the last 2 years — [JSP 822 Ch 8, Oct 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf)
- **Publicly Funded FE/HE (PF FE/HE):** the claimant must "be an active member of the ELC scheme" and have "At least 6 years' qualifying service". It funds a first full Level 3 or a first HE qualification (Cert HE, Foundation Degree, undergraduate degree, HNC/HND). It must be applied for during the resettlement phase or "for up to 5 years after leaving Service". There is a UK residency requirement of 3 years (overseas Service counts) — [JSP 822 Ch 8, Oct 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aedae6092ed9cc41b7bd09c/Elective_education.pdf). DMB says tuition fees are claimable "up to the current tuition fee cap of £9,250 per year" (page dated January 2024) — [DMB RN: Publicly Funded FE/HE](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/professional-development/publicly-funded-higher-education/)
- **Conflicting secondary claims:**
  - (Unofficial) A training provider says the 2016 Amendment Order "removed the registration requirement" and members "are automatically enrolled ... once they have completed Phase 1 training" — [Course2Career (unofficial)](https://www.course2career.com/military-resettlement-funding-uk). This is contradicted by the October 2026 JSP 822 ("Registration is not automatic").
  - (Unofficial) Another site says "You have to register for the scheme while you're still serving. You can't register after discharge" — [Redeployable (unofficial)](https://redeployable.io/military-benefits-bank/enhanced-learning-credits-elc). JSP 822 says veterans who are not members are "eligible to register". Both agree registration is needed.
  - (Unofficial) An older provider page still quotes the 4-year lower tier and a 10-year post-service window — [NILC (unofficial, 2023)](https://www.nilc.co.uk/enhanced-learning-credits). This is out of date for post-2016 leavers and post-2013 joiners.

### Inferences
- **Registration trap:** the old pre-2016 "register early or lose it" rule has been softened. Registration is still a manual step with Education Staff, and several provider sites wrongly say it is automatic. The rating should register as soon as possible after Phase 1, keep written confirmation of the registration date, and check it before submitting notice.
- **Service threshold trap:** at 5 years 11 months the leaver gets no ELC (only historic 4-year rights apply, and those do not apply to a 2026 joiner) and no PF FE/HE. At 6 years they get the Lower Tier (£3,000 maximum) plus PF FE/HE (potentially a whole first degree's tuition). At 8 years the ELC maximum doubles to £6,000. Leaving at 7 years adds nothing to learning credits over 6 years.
- **Financial-year trap:** because only one claim per financial year is allowed and each must start in that year, using all three ELC claims needs courses starting in three separate April-to-March years within the 5-year post-discharge window.
- **Possible changes by 2032:** JSP 822 was restructured (Volume 9 became Chapter 8) in October 2026. The ELC rules sit in legislation but have changed before (in 2016 and 2017). Re-check tiers and amounts in about 2030 to 2031.

### Gaps
- No record of changes in the October 2026 chapter was available to say whether anything other than the structure changed in 2025 or 2026.
- The current PF FE/HE tuition cap figure on DMB (£9,250) may be out of date; it is dated January 2024.

## 6. Pension (AFPS 15) after about 6 years, and what else is lost or kept

### Takeaway
With about 6 years' service the rating leaves with a deferred AFPS 15 pension. AFPS 15 is a career-average scheme: 1/47 of each year's pensionable pay is added, then revalued with CPI. The deferred pension is payable from State Pension Age, or from 55 with a permanent actuarial reduction. No immediate pension, Early Departure Payment (needs 20 years' service and age 40) or Resettlement Grant (needs 12 years) applies at 5 to 8 years. Allowances, RRPs and incentives are not pensionable.

### Cited Findings
- **Accrual:** "Every scheme year (1 April to 31 March), the MOD adds 1/47th of your annual pensionable earnings into your CARE 'pension pot'." "You need to have at least 2 years of paid Service to qualify for most pension benefits." Leaving "after two years of qualifying service, but before the age of 60" gives "a deferred pension ... claimed at your State Pension age", which increases "annually in line with inflation (the Consumer Price Index)". From age 55 it can be paid early, "subject to an actuarial reduction ... This reduction is permanent." — [DMB: AFPS 15](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/army/your-pay-and-pension/armed-forces-pension-scheme-15)
- **No automatic lump sum:** AFPS 15 "does not automatically pay a fixed pension lump sum". Commutation is at 12:1 — [DMB: AFPS 15](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/army/your-pay-and-pension/armed-forces-pension-scheme-15)
- **Deferred pension age:** "If you leave the Service before age 60 ... you will become a deferred member and will be entitled to a deferred pension paid at State Pension Age", and you can ask for it from age 55 at a reduced rate — [AFPS 15: Your Scheme Explained (Jan 2024)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6596b527614fa2000df3a8ee/20240104_-_AFPS_15_Your_Scheme_Explained.pdf); confirmed in [AFPS Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a58b74f2cbe5d1c179a6653/Armed_Forces_Pension_Scheme_annual_accounts_2025_to_2026.pdf) and [MOD: Guide to Deferred and Preserved Pension Entitlement (2024)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/664dca9e4f29e1d07fadcc77/A_Guide_to_Deferred_and_Preserved_Pension_Entitlement.pdf)
- **Early Departure Payment (EDP 15) thresholds:** "reach age 40 or over (but before the age of 60) and serve as a Regular for at least 20 years ... the 20/40 EDP point". EDP pays a tax-free lump sum of 2.25 × the annual deferred pension plus monthly income of 34% of the deferred pension. A break in service of more than 5 years means serving another 20 years to qualify — [AFPS 15: Your Scheme Explained](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6596b527614fa2000df3a8ee/20240104_-_AFPS_15_Your_Scheme_Explained.pdf); also [DMB: AFPS 15](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/army/your-pay-and-pension/armed-forces-pension-scheme-15) and [Forces Pension Society help sheet, Sept 2026 (charity)](https://forcespensionsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FPS-Help-Sheet-AF-Pensin-Schemes_Eligibility_Claiming_220926-Update-1.pdf)
- **Rejoining:** a 2-calendar-year vesting period applies. If a gap in service is 5 years or less, service is "deemed to be continuous" and the pension account is "reactivated". If the gap is more than 5 years, the old pension stays deferred and a new account starts — [JSP 905 Part 1 (Aug 2020)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f88422ee90e074158ba8a46/15.10.2020_JSP_905_Part_1_V2.0_Aug_2020_-_CB_Final.pdf)
- **Resettlement Grant:** needs "12 years' reckonable service as an Other Rank from age 18 (or date of entry if later)" and no other immediate pension benefits — [Service Leavers' Guide June 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4e19cc09605a87f4354531/Service_Leavers_Guide_June_26.pdf); up to £15,047 — [DMB RN: Resettlement](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/professional-development/resettlement/)
- **Claiming:** in the 6 months before discharge, JPA prompts the Pens Form 1. The deferred pension must later be claimed — [Forces Pension Society help sheet (charity)](https://forcespensionsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FPS-Help-Sheet-AF-Pensin-Schemes_Eligibility_Claiming_220926-Update-1.pdf). The pension is not paid automatically — [AFPS 15: Your Scheme Explained](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6596b527614fa2000df3a8ee/20240104_-_AFPS_15_Your_Scheme_Explained.pdf)
- **Not pensionable:** RRPs and financial incentives are "not pensionable" — [DMB: RRP](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/your-pay-and-pension/recruitment-and-retention-payments); [DMB: Financial Incentives](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/royal-navy/your-pay-and-pension/financial-incentives)
- (Unofficial) The AFPS 15 deferred pension age follows State Pension age ("or 65 if that is higher"). State Pension age is legislated to rise to 67 between 2026 and 2028 and to 68 between 2044 and 2046. Uniformed service schemes are exempt from the rise in minimum pension age from 55 to 57 in April 2028 — [armedforcespensioncalculator.co.uk (unofficial)](https://armedforcespensioncalculator.co.uk/guides/armed-forces-pension-age). The Commons Library also confirms AFPS 15 deferred pensions are "payable at State Pension age" — [House of Commons Library CBP-10168, Oct 2026](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-10168/CBP-10168.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative only:** 6 years at an average basic (pensionable) pay of about £33,000 gives 6 × 33,000 / 47 ≈ £4,200 a year of deferred pension in today's money, CPI-linked, payable at State Pension age (likely 67 to 68 for this person). Each extra year adds about 1/47 of that year's basic pay (about £700 to £800 a year at LET-level pay). There is no pension "cliff" between 5 and 8 years, only steady accrual.
- Leaving at about 6 years means giving up EDP (20/40) and the Resettlement Grant (12 years). Neither is reachable at 5 to 8 years, so neither affects a choice between 5, 6, 7 and 8 years.
- If the person might rejoin, returning within 5 years keeps pension continuity, which matters for any future EDP qualification.

### Gaps
- An official RN pay figure for an LET(MESM) at a given increment was not pulled, so the pension illustration uses an assumed average.
- The person's own State Pension age depends on their date of birth and future legislation; check it at gov.uk/state-pension-age.

## 7. Other leaving entitlements and support (brief)

### Takeaway
Before leaving: Forces Help to Buy can be used only while serving, and any unpaid balance is taken from terminal benefits. After leaving: Veteran Card (issued on leaving), Op COURAGE (NHS mental health in England, open once a discharge date is set), Op FORTITUDE (homelessness referral line), Forces Employment Charity (lifelong, any length of service) and CTP Assist (for those facing significant barriers to work).

### Cited Findings
- **Forces Help to Buy (FHTB)** has been "an enduring policy on 1 January 2023". It lets personnel "borrow up to 50% of their salary, up to a maximum of £25,000 interest free" — [GOV.UK FHTB statistics 2025/26 (Aug 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/forces-help-to-buy-scheme-statistics-2025-to-2026/forces-help-to-buy-statistics-2025-to-2026); [GOV.UK announcement, Dec 2022](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/forces-help-to-buy-scheme-made-permanent-to-help-service-personnel-purchase-housing). It is "normally repaid through salary over a period of up to 10 years. If you leave the Services before the advance has been fully repaid, any outstanding balance will be recovered from your terminal benefits." — [DMB: Forces Help to Buy (July 2026)](https://discovermybenefits.mod.gov.uk/army/families/forces-help-to-buy-scheme)
- (Unofficial) FHTB applicants must have "more than six months left to serve when you apply", and "Veterans cannot apply" — [Total Veteran (unofficial)](https://totalveteran.com/journal/uk-veteran-housing-schemes.html)
- **Veteran Card:** "If you left the armed forces in 2019 or later, you should have received a Veteran Card when you left service". Uses include voting ID, GP registration, Op COURAGE and Op FORTITUDE access, "claim National Insurance contributions relief for your first civilian employer after service", and exemption from the local-connection test for social housing — [GOV.UK: Using your Veteran Card](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/using-a-veteran-card-as-a-service-leaver). A digital version became available on 17 October 2025 via the GOV.UK One Login app — [GOV.UK press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/digital-version-of-veteran-card-launched-for-quicker-and-easier-access-to-support)
- **Op COURAGE** (launched 4 March 2021) is an NHS veterans' mental health service that accepts self-referrals and supports "those in the process of leaving the armed forces". **Op FORTITUDE** (launched July 2023, run by Riverside) is a single referral pathway for homeless veterans — [House of Commons Library CBP-12261, Nov 2026](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-12261). Op FORTITUDE phone 0800 9520 774, with direct self-referral — [GOV.UK: Op FORTITUDE](https://www.gov.uk/veteran-support-organisations/op-fortitude). Op COURAGE is open to those "still serving but have a discharge date", with no minimum length of service — [Royal British Legion (charity)](http://www.britishlegion.org.uk/get-support/expert-guidance/physical-mental-health/op-courage)

### Inferences
- If FHTB is taken in years 3 to 5, a large unpaid balance at discharge will be taken from final pay and terminal benefits. Plan the exit budget around this.

## 8. Summary table: what applies if discharged at about 5, 6, 7 or 8 years' service

### Takeaway
6 years from date of entry is the main threshold. It unlocks CRP (20 days' GRT, £534 IRTC), ELC Lower Tier (£3,000 maximum) and PF FE/HE (a funded first degree or Level 3), and doubles shore time before termination to 6 months. 8 years doubles the ELC maximum to £6,000 and adds 5 days' GRT. Pension grows steadily with no cliff. Submariner retention money is out of reach for a 6-year leaver either way. The binding timing constraint is RoS from the LET (24 months) and POET (36 months) qualifying courses, plus 12 months' notice.

### Inferences
- **Best "a little earlier or later" comparison:**
  - Leaving at 5 years instead of 6 loses about £3,000 of ELC, a potentially fully funded first degree (PF FE/HE), 20 days' paid GRT, £534 IRTC, 4 warrants and 2 months' extra shore time. That is a large loss for about 12 months less service.
  - Leaving at 7 years instead of 6 adds only about one more year of pension accrual and pay, with no new entitlement.
  - Leaving at 8 years adds £3,000 more ELC (£6,000 in all) and 5 more GRT days, and might reach the submariner £25k retention payment if the RN-site "6 years qualified" rule holds. That payment would probably carry a further RoS.
- **Safe planning sequence (inference):**
  1. Register for ELC now and get written confirmation.
  2. Confirm the GTS date and the S3049 earliest discharge date.
  3. Before accepting LETQC, POETQC or NPOC, any FRI or the retention payment, work out its RoS end date against the target exit.
  4. Aim for discharge (the end of terminal leave) shortly after the 6-year anniversary of entry, because of the "more than six years" CTP wording.
  5. Submit notice about 12 months before that date. Ask in writing whether the final year of any RoS can overlap with the notice period.
  6. Book the Resettlement Advisory Brief within 1 month of giving notice, and plan GRT for the final shore assignment.

### Gaps
- Exact RoS lengths for every submarine course (Table 53C), and repayment terms for the Dolphins bonus and the £25k retention payment, are not public. Ask the career manager or the Divisional Officer for the extant RNTM or DIN.
- The GRT, warrant and IRTC figures need re-confirming against the current JSP 534 v24 (not readable here).
- Rules likely to change before 2032: Submarine Pay structure (due January 2027), FRIs (temporary by design), State Pension age, and possibly ELC values (unchanged at £1k/£2k for years; no indexation found).
