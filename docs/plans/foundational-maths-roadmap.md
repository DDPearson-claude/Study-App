## The five courses

### 1. Percentages (8 lessons)
- **Gives you:** percentages as hundredths, and the sums the job uses. For example, voltage-drop
  limits are 3% for lighting and 5% for other uses [1]: 6.9 V and 11.5 V on 230 V. Also
  efficiency and percentage error.
- **Draft lessons:**
  1. What a percentage is
  2. A percentage of an amount
  3. What percentage is it?
  4. Increase and decrease with multipliers (×1.2, ×0.85)
  5. Percentage change, and percentage points
  6. Reverse percentages
  7. Repeated changes (+20% then −20% doesn't get you back)
  8. Efficiency, percentage error and propeller slip
- **Visual:** yes. A 10 by 10 grid with squares shaded, and a percentage bar.
- **Builds on:** Fractions (equivalents, decimals, a fraction of an amount) and Negative numbers
  (a fall is a negative change).

### 2. Proportional reasoning (8 lessons)
- **Gives you:** ratios, rates and scaling, worked without algebra. That covers mixes, unit rates
  such as grams of fuel per kWh [2], scale drawings at 1:50 and 1:100, and the ratios inside
  machines: a transformer's turns ratio [3], gear reduction and a diesel's compression ratio
  [4].
- **Draft lessons:**
  1. Ratios as blocks
  2. Equivalent ratios (multiply, never add: adding is the most reported ratio error [5])
  3. Sharing in a ratio
  4. Unit rates
  5. Direct proportion
  6. Scale drawings
  7. Inverse proportion (Boyle's law: pressure × volume stays the same [6])
  8. Ratios in machines
- **Boyle's law needs absolute pressure.** Gauge readings give wrong answers. Lesson 7 says so and
  links back to Negative numbers lesson 3 (absolute = gauge + atmospheric). Its source comes when
  the lesson is written.
- **Visual:** yes. Bars of equal blocks, ratio tables and a double number line.
- **Builds on:** Fractions (equivalent fractions become equivalent ratios) and Percentages (a rate
  per 100). On your shelf this comes before Solving equations, so it's taught with tables and
  blocks, not cross-multiplying. Brilliant's Math Foundations list shows Solving Equations earlier
  [7], but that's the order of a list, not a stated prerequisite. Graphs of proportion
  move to The coordinate plane.

### 3. Solving equations (9 lessons)
- **Gives you:** the skill your college maths leans on most, rearranging a formula. SQA's HN
  Engineering Mathematics 1 says it helps to have "an ability to transpose engineering formulae"
  [8].
- **Draft lessons:**
  1. The balance
  2. One-step equations
  3. Two-step equations
  4. Brackets
  5. Unknowns on both sides
  6. Writing an equation from a situation
  7. Using formulas (V = IR, P = VI)
  8. Rearranging formulas I
  9. Rearranging formulas II (P = I²R, p1V1 = p2V2 with absolute pressures, the voltage-drop
     formula solved for the longest run)
- **Visual:** yes for lessons 1 to 6, with a balance and boxes, as Brilliant uses [9].
  Partly for lessons 7 to 9, whose SHOW screens carry a little more explanation.
- **Builds on:** Negative numbers, because in one study "the presence of negative numbers gave
  rise to many errors" [10]. Order of operations is taught in Negative numbers lesson 8, so
  this course relies on it rather than teaching it again. Also Fractions and Proportional
  reasoning. Inequalities and simultaneous equations wait for Linear equations and Linear systems.

### 4. The coordinate plane (8 lessons)
- **Gives you:** plotting and reading graphs. SQA's Technician 1 maths unit asks you to "interpret
  graphical information and construct appropriate graphs from given numerical data" [11].
- **Draft lessons:**
  1. Points on a grid
  2. Four quadrants
  3. Scales on axes
  4. Horizontal and vertical lines
  5. Plotting data from a table
  6. Reading a graph
  7. Straight lines through the origin (proportion, and a first look at steepness)
  8. Distance on the grid (leading into Pythagoras)
- **Visual:** yes. Points you drag. I'd drop Brilliant's level on inequality regions [12].
- **Builds on:** Negative numbers (the four quadrants) and Proportional reasoning (lesson 7).

### 5. Geometry fundamentals (10 lessons)
- **Gives you:** angles, perimeter, area, volume and Pythagoras. That covers a conductor's
  cross-section (πr²), a tank's capacity in m³ and litres, read from sounding tables [13],
  and the travel between conduit offset bends [14].
- **Draft lessons:**
  1. Angles
  2. Angles in triangles and shapes
  3. Perimeter and circumference
  4. Area of rectangles and triangles
  5. Area of a circle
  6. Square units and scaling (1 m² is 10,000 cm², not 100 [15])
  7. Pythagoras' theorem
  8. Pythagoras at work
  9. Volume
  10. Surface area
- **Visual:** yes. Unit squares, unit cubes, and squares drawn on a triangle's sides.
- **Builds on:** Proportional reasoning (scaling, π), Solving equations (a missing side) and The
  coordinate plane (grid distance). Brilliant's version also asks for Solving Equations first
  [16]. The trigonometry for conduit bends waits for the Trigonometry course.
- **Overlap:** see "Things to settle" item 3.

## How I'll build each course
The process that worked for Fractions, step by step, with what I need from you.

1. **Research.** Three strands:
   - Brilliant's course and other good courses
   - where each skill shows up on your route
   - how best to teach it, and the mistakes to catch

   Every source is kept with its exact words. *From you:* nothing.
2. **The brief,** like the Negative numbers one: the lesson list, the pictures, the mistakes and
   the cost, with a short version at the top. *From you:* read the short version on your phone,
   then say yes or what to change.
3. **The pilot.** Lesson 1 is built in full and put live, with a one-screen prototype of the
   riskiest new picture. No screen may scroll on a 360 × 640 phone. *From you:* try it on your
   phone. Tell me what's too wordy, too slow, too easy or confusing.
4. **The rest, overnight.** Lessons are written in order, each one knowing the ones before it.
   Each lesson goes through:
   - the build: write, critique and rewrite, then build the screens and test them on a phone and
     on a desktop, in light and dark
   - a fresh fact-check by a reader who wrote none of it
   - full process only: a teaching review, reading it as you would; a motion review, so every
     animation is soft and nothing pops in; fixes; and an independent confirm before it goes live

   The motion review and the confirm aren't in the RUNBOOK yet. Until a lesson passes, it shows
   "Coming soon". *From you:* nothing.
5. **Your review in the app.** *From you:* play it when you like. Tell me the lesson and screen of
   anything wrong, and I'll fix it and check it again.
6. **Done.** The course shows "Ready to browse", and its files are saved in the repo.

While one course builds, I can write the next one's brief, so there's one waiting for you in the
morning.
