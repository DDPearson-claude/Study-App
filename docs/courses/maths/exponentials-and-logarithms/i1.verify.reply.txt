   "now": "Some things grow by adding the same amount on each equal step. Others grow by multiplying by the same number on each step, and that is [[exponential]] change: the output is multiplied by the same factor every time the input goes up by one, and that multiplier is called the [[growth factor]].[^1] Start two columns of bars at 1. The left one adds 2 each step: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. The right one multiplies by 2: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. At first the doubling column is behind, 2 against 3 and 4 against 5, so here multiplying starts slowly. But each doubling adds as much as there already is, so its rises go 1, 2, 4, 8 while the left column's stay at 2. By step 10 the left column reads 1 + 10 × 2 = 21, and the right column, 1 doubled 10 times, reads 1,024. A slow start can hide it for longer. Say a count of 100 grows 9% a day. After a week it is about 183, close to the 163 that adding 9 a day gives, but after 30 days it is about 1,327 against 370.\n\nThat is the cause: each step's rise is a fixed share of what is there, so the more there is, the more it grows, and the rate of growth is proportional to the current value.[^2] Write the start as A and the factor as b. After one step you have A × b, after two A × b × b, and after x steps A multiplied by b x times, which is A × bˣ. That is a power, as in Powers and roots, but now the letter is in the power, counting the steps. A percentage change gives the factor just as a multiplier does in Percentages: + 5% is × 1.05, because you keep the whole and add 5%. Say a £1,000 pot grows 5% a year. After 10 years it is 1,000 × 1.05¹⁰ ≈ £1,628.89, more than the £1,500 that adding £50 a year gives, because each year's 5% is taken of a bigger amount. It doubles between years 14 and 15, as 1.05¹⁴ ≈ 1.98 and 1.05¹⁵ ≈ 2.08.\n\nA positive growth rate makes the growth factor larger than 1, and a negative one makes it less than 1,[^3] so the amount shrinks each step, which is called [[decay]]. A 25% cut is × 0.75, and halving is × 0.5. Halving from 400 goes 400, 200, 100, 50, 25, losing half of what is left each time, so the drops shrink too: 200, 100, 50, 25. Half of a positive amount is still positive, so the rule takes it ever closer to zero but never to zero. Between the steps the rule still works, with a fraction of a step in the power. Half a step multiplies by √b, because two half steps must make one whole step: √2 ≈ 1.414, and 1.414 × 1.414 ≈ 2. So the curve through the dots bends, rather than running straight from one dot to the next.\n\nThe course's capacitor, halving every 10 seconds from 400 V, is 400 × 0.5ˣ with x counting 10-second steps: 50 V after 30 seconds and 25 V after 40, so it passes 30 V somewhere between, and finding exactly when needs a way to undo a power. **Exponential change multiplies by the same growth factor on every equal step,[^1] so a start A becomes A × bˣ after x steps: it grows when b is larger than 1, as × 1.05 does for 5%, and shrinks when b is less than 1.[^3]**"
