    "text": "A function's graph is every input plotted against the output it gives. To read it, start at the input on the bottom axis, go up to the graph, then across to the side axis and read the output. Take the 4 to 20 mA transmitter from the last lesson, S(p) = 0.16p + 4. From 25% go up to the line and across, and you read 8 mA. A function always gives one output for each input,[^1] so the beam meets the graph once.\n\nYou can read backwards too, from an output to its inputs. Start on the side axis, go across to the graph, then down. From 12 mA you come down at 50%, the 50% pressure difference that 12 mA means on a DP cell.[^5] Reading backwards can find two inputs. A tank's level was 1 m at 5 and at 25 minutes. That is allowed: two inputs giving one output make the function [[many-to-one]].[^2]\n\nEach axis names a quantity, so a graph shows one quantity against another, not the shape of a thing. A flat stretch on the tank's graph means the level held still. A graph with a gap can be a function too. What fails is a circle, such as a pipe's rim of radius 5 cm: at 3 cm across it is 4 cm up and 4 cm down. Textbooks add the [[vertical line test]]: if any vertical line meets a graph twice, it is not a function.\n\n**Read a function's graph up from the input to the graph and across to its one output, and back from an output to its inputs.**"
