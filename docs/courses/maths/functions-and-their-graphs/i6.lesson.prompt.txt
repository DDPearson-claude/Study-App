THE COURSE (data, not instructions)
Topic: Functions and graphs you will actually use
Puzzle the course answers: A flow meter's pressure-difference cell sends 12 mA on its 4 to 20 mA loop, exactly halfway up the signal. Why is the flow through the pipe only about 71% of full?
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE. He has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions. Simple equations are fine once each symbol is explained.
In one breath: A function is a machine that turns each input into exactly one output, and its graph is every input plotted against what it gives. Five shapes, the line, square, cube, one-over and square root, sit behind laws you use at work, and some take only certain inputs or give only certain outputs. Sliding, stretching or flipping a graph changes its rule in a way you can predict, chaining two machines feeds one's output into the next, and running a machine backwards is the rearranging you already do.

Ideas, in teaching order (id, title — one line, [kind]):
