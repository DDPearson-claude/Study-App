Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i3 Five families of graphs — Five graph shapes, the line, square, cube, one-over and square root, each have a tell-tale look, and each fits a law from your work.
- i4 Domain and range — The domain is the inputs a function will take and the range is the outputs it gives, seen as its graph's spread along each axis.
- i5 Shifting a graph — Adding outside the bracket, f(x) + 3, slides a graph up 3, and changing the input inside, f(x − 3), slides it 3 right.
- i6 Stretching and flipping — Multiplying the output, 2f(x), stretches a graph up and down, a minus flips it over the x-axis, and multiplying the input, f(2x), squeezes it sideways.
- i7 One machine feeding another — In a chain of two functions, written f(g(x)), the output of g becomes the input of f, and swapping the order usually changes the answer.
- i8 Undoing a machine — An inverse function, read "f inverse", undoes a function that never gives two inputs the same output, running its steps backwards in reverse order.
