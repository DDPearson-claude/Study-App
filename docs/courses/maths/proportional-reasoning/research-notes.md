### 2.2 Transformer turns ratio and voltage ratio

Electronics Tutorials, All About Circuits and the worksheet all treat a transformer as a ratio problem. The order of the ratio matters, and a 10:1 turns ratio gives 10:1 voltage but 1:10 current, which is a built-in direct-then-inverse example.

- Transformer turns ratio is the same as the voltage ratio
  - Source: Transformer Basics and Transformer Principles, Electronics Tutorials, <https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/transformer/transformer-basics.html>
  - Quote: "the turns ratio of any given transformer will be the same as its voltage ratio."
- The order of a turns ratio matters (3:1 is not 1:3)
  - Source: Transformer Basics and Transformer Principles, Electronics Tutorials, <https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/transformer/transformer-basics.html>
  - Quote: "Note that the order of the numbers when expressing a transformers turns ratio value is very important"
- A 10:1 turns ratio gives 10:1 voltage and 1:10 current (inverse link)
  - Source: Step-up and Step-down Transformers, All About Circuits, <https://www.allaboutcircuits.com/textbook/alternating-current/chpt-9/step-up-and-step-down-transformers>
  - Quote: "Turns ratio of 10:1 yields 10:1 primary:secondary voltage ratio and 1:10 primary:secondary current ratio."

### 2.4 Compression ratio

Compression ratio is a ratio of two volumes. Note for build: CED Engineering is a continuing-education publisher reproducing a US Navy text; prefer Britannica for the definition.

- Compression ratio is a ratio of volumes
  - Source: Compression ratio, Britannica, <https://www.britannica.com/technology/compression-ratio>
  - Quote: "A compression ratio of six means that the mixture is compressed to one-sixth its original volume by the action of the piston in the cylinder."
- Compression ratio defined as the maximum volume divided by the minimum volume
  - Source: Compression ratio, Britannica, <https://www.britannica.com/technology/compression-ratio>
  - Quote: "It is defined as the maximum volume of the combustion chamber (with the piston farthest out, or bottom dead centre) divided by the volume with the piston in the full-compression position"

  - Source: Diesel Engine Fundamentals, CED Engineering, <https://www.cedengineering.ca/userfiles/M04-002%20-%20Diesel%20Engine%20Fundamentals.pdf>
  - Quote: "In a diesel engine, compression ratios ranging from 14:1 to as high as 24:1 are commonly used."

## 3. Teaching: common mistakes and visual models

### 3.1 Mistake: adding instead of multiplying (the additive error)

The additive error is well documented. The numbers for how common it is are not quoted here.

- The additive error is a documented tendency
  - Source: Evaluating Middle Years Students' Proportional Reasoning, ERIC, <https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED573239.pdf>
  - Quote: "a tendency to approach proportional situations additively instead of multiplicatively"
- Even older students think additively by default (NCETM)
  - Source: Introducing multiplicative thinking, NCETM, <https://www.ncetm.org.uk/features/introducing-multiplicative-thinking>
  - Quote: "Any secondary teacher will tell you that even older students often think additively by default."
- Students look at the difference first
  - Source: Introducing multiplicative thinking, NCETM, <https://www.ncetm.org.uk/features/introducing-multiplicative-thinking>
  - Quote: "they will often look first at the difference, even where the relative proportions are more relevant."
- Three misconceptions in sharing in a ratio, including additive reasoning
  - Source: Misconceptions on part-part-whole proportional relationships, Taylor & Francis, <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19477503.2018.1548222>
  - Quote: "regarding a ratio as a number, additive reasoning, and confusion about the whole."
- Common mistakes in ratio and proportion come from focusing on constant difference
  - Source: Second-Year Pre-Service Teachers' Responses to Proportional Reasoning Test Items, ERIC, <https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1015981.pdf>
  - Quote: "common mistakes in ratio and proportion are often the result of a focus on constant difference when multiplicative comparisons are more appropriate"

### 3.2 Mistake: part-to-part versus part-to-whole

Confusion about the whole was the most frequent misconception in one study of ratio sharing. Reading 1:4 as four parts instead of five is a likely form of it but is UNCONFIRMED: no checked source states it.

- Confusion about the whole was the most frequent misconception
  - Source: Misconceptions on part-part-whole proportional relationships, Taylor & Francis, <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19477503.2018.1548222>
  - Quote: "Confusion about the whole was the misconception that appeared most frequently across all grades."
- Teach part-to-part versus part-to-whole explicitly
  - Source: Ratios and Proportional Relationships, NCTM, <https://www.nctm.org/Handlers/AttachmentHandler.ashx?attachmentID=pG275lzaSZQ%3D>
  - Quote: "Ask students to explain part-to-part versus part-to-whole ratios."

### 3.3 Mistake: treating inverse as direct, and assuming every straight-line link is proportional

Inverse as direct is a listed error and students do worse on inverse tasks. The fix suggested by the sources is to build both from the same table, with the product constant for inverse.

- Treating an inverse situation as direct is a listed error
  - Source: Evaluating Middle Years Students' Proportional Reasoning, ERIC, <https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED573239.pdf>
  - Quote: "Treating an inverse situation as if it involved direct proportion"
- Students do worse on inverse than direct proportion
  - Source: The Deficits of Students' Orientation in Solving Proportion Problems, PMC, <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10659227>
  - Quote: "students were more successful on tasks that used direct proportions, and less successful on tasks concerning inverse proportions"
- Not every linear relationship is proportional
  - Source: Core Concept 8.1 Working with direct and inverse proportion, NCETM, <https://www.ncetm.org.uk/media/o1ub30ed/ncetm_ks4_cc_8_1.pdf>
  - Quote: "It is a common misconception that all linear relationships are proportional."
- Multiplying when the situation is additive or constant is also an error (a check that a situation is really proportional)
  - Source: Evaluating Middle Years Students' Proportional Reasoning, ERIC, <https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED573239.pdf>
  - Quote: "Applying a multiplicative strategy to an additive situation"

### 3.4 Mistake: muddling the order of a ratio

One electrical source says the order is "very important". No education source on order was kept.

- Order of the numbers in a ratio changes the meaning
  - Source: Transformer Basics and Transformer Principles, Electronics Tutorials, <https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/transformer/transformer-basics.html>
