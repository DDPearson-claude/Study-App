Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 Equivalent ratios and scaling — Multiply or divide both parts by the same number to keep a ratio, since adding the same number to both breaks it.
- i3 Sharing in a ratio — Add the parts to count the blocks, find one block, then each share: 40 litres in 3 : 2 is 24 and 16.
- i4 Unit rates — Turn a rate into an amount per one (per hour, per kWh, per turn) and scale from it, but only where the rate holds steady.
- i5 Direct proportion — Two amounts are in direct proportion when doubling one doubles the other, like a transformer's output voltage, and a fixed charge plus a rate is not.
- i6 Inverse proportion — One goes up as the other comes down with their product fixed: halve a gas's volume and its absolute pressure doubles.
