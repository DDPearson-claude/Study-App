- Picture: a squared grid with numbered axes, A and B, and only the piece of line between them (the long side). The step triangle has a run arrow straight across from A, a rise arrow up or down to B and a right-angle mark where they meet, with each arrow labelled with its signed size. On distance screens the long side is labelled "d = √(run² + rise²) = d" with the numbers filled in ("d = √((−6)² + (−8)²) = 10", to one decimal place when not whole). On midpoint screens, ticks mark halfway along the run and halfway up the rise. Dashed teal lines grow from those ticks to the midpoint M (a teal dot labelled with its coordinates), then on to the number scales, where the averages are written in teal and replace the numbers they would cover. The readout above the grid gives the rule (the distance formula, the midpoint average, or "B is the same step on from M as M is from A") and then fills in the working piece by piece. Every number is computed from the points as they move. A and B can be dragged on the SHOW screens except working back (whole-number points, arrow keys too).
