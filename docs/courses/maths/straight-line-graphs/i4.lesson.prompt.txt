THE COURSE (data, not instructions)
Topic: Straight-line graphs you will actually use
Puzzle the course answers: A motor winding's resistance, read at a few temperatures, plots roughly as a line. How do you turn those readings into a rule for other temperatures in that range, and what do its two numbers mean?
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE. He has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions. Simple equations are fine once each symbol is explained.
In one breath: Any straight line on a graph that is not vertical has a rule, y = mx + c, with just two numbers in it. The gradient m is how much y changes for each 1 along, found from any two points on the line, and c is the value of y where the line crosses the y-axis. Two points, or one point and a gradient, pin down one line and its rule, and the same two points also give their distance apart and their midpoint. On the job, readings that fall on or near a line become a law, where the gradient and the crossing are real quantities with units, such as a resistor's ohms.
