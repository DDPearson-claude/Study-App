To find it from two points on the line, divide the difference in their y-coordinates by the difference in their x-coordinates.[^1] Picture a step triangle between the two points. Its [[run]] is the arrow straight across, x₂ − x₁, and its [[rise]] is the arrow up or down, y₂ − y₁. For A (1, 3) and B (4, 9), the run is 4 − 1 = 3 and the rise is 9 − 3 = 6, so m = 6 ÷ 3 = 2. The line climbs 2 for each 1 along. The other way up, 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5 would say it climbs half a step for each 1 along, but the triangle climbs 6 in 3 steps along, which is 2 for each. So the change in y goes on top.\n\nAny two points on the same line give the same m. A straight line climbs the same amount for each 1 along wherever you are on it, because if the climb changed from one step to the next, the line would bend. So a run of 3 is three steps of 1 with three equal climbs, and dividing the rise by 3 gives back the climb for one step. Slide A and B along the line to (2, 5) and (3, 7), and the triangle shrinks to a run of 1 and a rise of 2, which is m itself. The gradient is a measure of how steep the line is,[^2] since a bigger rise or fall for each 1 along makes a steeper line.\n\nThe arrows' directions give the sign.
