Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- A best-fit line mimics the trend and may miss most plotted points; the idea is a line with equal numbers of points either side. [R1]
- A force against extension graph is a straight line through the origin only while the limit of proportionality is not exceeded. [R2]
- One student read the y intercept of a best-fit line as the work done against friction. [R3]
- Given two points on a line, find the slope between them, then solve for the y-intercept in y = mx + b. [R4]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The equation of a line can be found from its gradient and one point on it. [R5]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The gradient comes first, found by dividing the difference in y by the difference in x. [R6]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- On a plot of voltage against current for an ohmic device, the slope of the line is the resistance. [R7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Resistance is measured in volts per ampere, which is the ohm. [R8]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In many materials, resistivity depends approximately linearly on temperature, so a line fits. [R9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- On a force against extension graph for a spring, the gradient equals the spring constant. [R10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A marine engineering syllabus expects the law of a straight line graph in the form y = ax + b, with a and b defined. [R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In the linear model of resistance against temperature, α is the temperature coefficient of the material; i5 uses it to name gradient ÷ intercept of its example readings (no real α quoted). [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Ohm's law links voltage, current and resistance; the unit of resistance, the ohm, is one volt per ampere. [R8]  (whole topic)
- A plot of voltage against current for an ohmic device is approximately a straight line whose slope is the resistance. [R7]  (whole topic)
