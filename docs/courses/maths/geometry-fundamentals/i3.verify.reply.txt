BBC Bitesize works it the same way, 1 cm² = 10 mm × 10 mm = 100 mm², a conversion factor of 100.[^1] Cable sizes are areas in mm². Nexans lists conductor cross-sections of 1.5 mm², 2.5 mm², 4 mm² and up,[^2] and IEC 60228 is the standard that sets these nominal areas.[^3] So a 25 mm² conductor has a nominal area of 25 square millimetres, which is 25 ÷ 100 = 0.25 cm².\n\nA 1 m square is 100 cm each way, so it holds 100 rows of 100, and 1 m² is 100 × 100 = 10,000 cm². Bitesize warns of the slip of taking 100 cm² for 1 m² because 1 m is 100 cm,[^4] but 100 cm² is only the one row along the bottom. Going to a smaller unit you multiply, and going to a bigger one you divide. Units scale this way because area counts squares in two directions at once. Make every side of a square twice as long, a [[scale factor]] of 2, and 2 × 2 = 4 copies of the old square fit inside, so it covers 4 times the area. With every side 3 times as long, 3 × 3 = 9 copies fit.
