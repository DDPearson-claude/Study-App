Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 Circles — Circumference is 2πr and area is πr², both from the radius, so halve a diameter first; a ring is the big circle less the small.
- i3 Square units and scaling — Area scales by the square, so 1 cm² is 100 mm², 1 m² is 10,000 cm², and doubling a side makes four times the area.
- i4 Pythagoras — In a right-angled triangle the squares on the two shorter sides add up to the square on the hypotenuse; a square root undoes the square.
- i5 Pythagoras at work — A rectangle's diagonal, a straight run across a room, the distance between two grid points and a box's diagonal all come from Pythagoras.
- i6 Volume and litres — A box or cylinder holds its base area × its length, and a litre is a 10 cm cube, so a cubic metre holds 1000 litres.

Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- A rectangle can be split into squares and counted, and the same area comes from multiplying width and height. [R1][R2]
- A triangle is half of a rectangle: its base and perpendicular height are the rectangle's width and height, so the area is half of base times height. [R3][R4][R5]
- The base and perpendicular height of a triangle are at right angles to each other, so a sloping longest side is not used as the height. [R6][R7]
- Teachers watch for students who over-simplify to "add for perimeter" and "multiply for area", and who confuse the two ideas. [R8][R9]
- Shapes can have the same perimeter but different areas, which is how the confusion between the two is tackled. [R10]
- Cutting a 2D shape up and rearranging it leaves its area unchanged, though not every child grasps this. [R11]
- When converting between metric units of area and volume, the numbers to multiply or divide by are squared for area and cubed for volume. [R12]  (whole topic)
- A cable maker states conductor size as a cross-section in mm², and IEC 60228 is the standard that sets the nominal cross-sectional areas of conductors. [R13][R14]  (whole topic)
- The MCA General Engineering Science I syllabus includes the area of an annulus and the mass of an object from its volume and density. [R15][R16]  (whole topic)
