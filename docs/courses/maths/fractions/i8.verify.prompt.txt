Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Dividing one number by a fraction asks how many times that fraction fits into the number. [R1][R2][R3]
- 3 divided by a quarter can be read as how many quarters there are in 3, which is 12: a small piece gives a big count. [R4]
- The reciprocal of a fraction swaps numerator and denominator; a number times its reciprocal is 1, and the reciprocal says how many times the fraction fits into 1. [R5][R6][R7]
- How many of a fraction are in one is one divided by that fraction, so dividing any number by that fraction is that number times the reciprocal. [R8][R9]
- To divide fractions, multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second, which is easier than dividing by a fraction directly. [R10][R11]
- The word 'of' can be replaced by 'multiplied by': three-quarters times one-half is three-eighths. [R12][R13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators for the new numerator and the denominators for the new denominator, then simplify where necessary. [R14][R15]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Half of two-fifths is two-tenths, which simplifies to one-fifth: taking a fraction of a fraction gives a smaller piece. [R16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Area models (a rectangle cut both ways) are used to represent the multiplication of two fractions and to see why the rule multiplies numerators and denominators. [R17][R15]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In a fraction the top number (numerator) counts the parts you have and the bottom number (denominator) says how many equal parts the whole is divided into. [R18][R19][R20]  (whole topic)
- A fraction is a number with its own place on the number line, and the whole it is a fraction of can be a shape, a length, a number or a set of objects. [R21][R22][R23]  (whole topic)
- Fractions that look different can name the same number: they sit at the same point on the number line, and multiplying top and bottom by the same number does not change the value. [R24][R25][R26]  (whole topic)
- Comparing fractions is only valid when both fractions refer to the same whole. [R27]  (whole topic)
