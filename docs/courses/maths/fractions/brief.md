# Fractions: the course brief

Written 10 Oct 2026 for Dan, answering the course brief in tools/course/RUNBOOK.md section 0.

## 1. The overall topic
Reading fractions, telling which is bigger, and calculating with them: the fractions that turn
up on a tape, a drawing, a drum of cable, a circuit's load, a timesheet, a recipe or a price.
It stops at fractions; percentages and ratios have their own courses on the Maths shelf.

## 2. What is actually useful to teach, in the order it builds
| # | Lesson | The skill Dan leaves with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What a fraction is | Read any fraction as one number: the bottom names the piece, the top counts them, and it can go past 1. |
| 2 | Equivalent fractions | Rename a fraction (3/4 = 6/8 = 12/16) and simplify one back down (6/8 = 3/4). |
| 3 | Mixed numbers | Switch between 7/4 and 1 3/4, the way sizes like 1 3/8 inches are written. |
| 4 | Comparing fractions | Say which is bigger (3/8 or 5/16) by giving both the same piece, or as decimals. |
| 5 | Adding and taking away | Add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers (half an hour plus three quarters). |
| 6 | A fraction of an amount | Find 3/4 of 32 A or 2/5 of a 50 m drum: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top. |
| 7 | Multiplying fractions | Multiply fractions and mixed numbers (half of three quarters is 3/8). |
| 8 | Dividing fractions | Work out how many fit (6 m into 3/4 m tails is 8) and why you flip and multiply. |

Concept material is folded into lesson 1. Lesson 6 is the single most useful skill on the job,
so it gets its own lesson before the multiplying rules.

## 3. Can it be taught mostly visually, through interaction?
Yes. Every lesson is a steps lesson (`format: 'steps'`): about 12 screens alternating SHOW
(one to three plain sentences and a demonstrating picture, often animated) and DO (a puzzle with
Check, a one-line hint, Why?, Continue), ending with two or three mixed puzzles. The pictures are
bars and shapes cut into equal parts with the parts shaded, with a number line where position is
the point. Minimal writing.

## 4. What else
- **Benchmark.** Brilliant's Fractions is 27 lessons in five levels: Visualise, Equivalent
  fractions, Comparing, Adding, Multiplying. It has no mixed numbers, no fraction of an amount as
  its own skill, no decimals and no dividing; this course adds all four because Dan needs them.
- **Starting point.** Dan works with sizes, lengths, loads and ratings every day. Nothing about
  electrics is taught; the examples come from the job where they fit naturally.
- **The mistakes to catch, each with at least one puzzle and its own hint:**
  - 2: adding the same number to top and bottom (3/4 to 4/5); simplifying by subtracting.
  - 3: reading 1 3/4 as 13/4; turning 7/4 into 1 3 instead of 1 3/4.
  - 4: thinking a bigger bottom means a bigger fraction; comparing tops when the pieces differ.
  - 5: adding tops and bottoms (1/2 + 1/4 = 2/6); losing the whole number in a mixed sum.
  - 6: dividing by the top and multiplying by the bottom (32 ÷ 3 × 4).
  - 7: matching bottoms before multiplying; multiplying the parts of mixed numbers separately.
  - 8: flipping the wrong fraction; believing dividing always makes a number smaller.
- **Where each is used.** Drill bits and imperial fittings (2, 3, 4), drawings and tape readings
  (3), timesheets and lengths (5), circuit loads and cable drums (6), rating factors (7), cutting
  cable into tails (8).
- **Size and cost.** 8 lessons of about 12 screens. Lesson 1 is built. Each further lesson takes
  one builder agent, roughly 150k to 200k tokens, so about 1.2M for lessons 2 to 8.
- **Pilot first.** Lesson 1 was the pilot. Lesson 2 is built next for Dan's review, then 3 to 8.
