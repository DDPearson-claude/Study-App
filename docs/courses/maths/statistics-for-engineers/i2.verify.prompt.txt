Say a charger holds a battery bank at 27.00 V, and each reading has a random error of 0.04 V, its standard deviation. A single reading can easily land 0.08 V from the true value. Now make each reading the mean of four. Their random errors point different ways, so they partly cancel. They are random, so they never cancel fully. The spread of a mean of n readings is the spread of one reading divided by √n.[^3] Four readings halve it, to 0.02 V. Two readings cut it only to about 0.028 V. So to halve the spread again you need four times as many readings, not twice as many.\n\nThe same rule says how well the mean of fifty readings is pinned down.
