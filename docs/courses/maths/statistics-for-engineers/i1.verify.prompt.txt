This lesson: i1 "The middle and the spread" — Repeat readings pile up round a middle, the mean or median, and the standard deviation measures how far they typically sit from it. [quantity]
Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 How far to trust a reading — Averaging repeat readings shrinks random scatter but not a meter's steady offset, so a result is a value plus or minus its uncertainty.
- i3 The normal curve and honest outliers — Readings often pile into a bell, about 95% within two standard deviations of the mean, so counting standard deviations shows how unusual a reading is.
- i4 Drift or noise: trends over time — Plotted in the order taken, readings show a drift as a steady slope, while noise alone still makes a fitted line lean a little.
- i5 How often equipment fails: reliability — A steady failure rate sets the share of units still working over time, so only about 37% reach the MTBF, the mean time between failures.
