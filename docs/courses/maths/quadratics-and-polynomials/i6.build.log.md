  handles of the step on screen (`STEPS[at()]`) while the checks painted other steps, so `d.sl` was
  undefined. The sweep found no problems. The pictures showed one more fault. Step 1's end state (a = −1)
  showed only a small cap of the upside-down U at the bottom of the window [−5, 7, −12, 12], so the
  falling ends were cut off.
- Attempt 2 (i6.body2.html): body1 with three exact edits. Grips are placed only for the step on screen.

  settled by 3.8 to 4.4 s. The puzzles settle in under half a second.
- Pictures read (once each): attempt 1, all ten in lookAt. For attempt 2, only those that changed:
  360 light opening, 1280 end and the five contact sheets. The 360 moved, 360 end and dark moved were
  byte-identical to attempt 1's.
- Design: 13 screens. SHOW on 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9; DO on 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10; mixed puzzles 11, 12 and 13.
  Clue markers: an orange square for a y-crossing, a navy dot for a root and a blue diamond for the
  turning point, with a dashed line of symmetry labelled "x = …". A key sits inside each drawing.
  Labels are placed clear of each other and of the markers, and a check confirms that at every
  setting tried.
  - Screen 1 has an a handle (−3 to 3, skipping 0). The demonstration glides a from 1 to −1, so the U
    flips through a line. "Both ends rise/fall" and the x = ±100 working follow a.
  - Screen 3 runs a dot along the curve to (0, −8) and writes 0² − 2 × 0 − 8 = −8.
  - Screen 5 lights the roots −2 and 4 and the turning point (1, −9), then frees a, b and c
    (window [−10, 10, −30, 30], so every clue stays on the picture). The demonstration glides c from
    −8 to −3 and back. The curve itself also drags c.
  - Screen 7 draws amber steps between whole-number x under a table of x, y, step and change. The
    change row reads +2 each time.
  - Screen 9 lays y = mx + c across y = x², with m and c handles (−4 to 4). The readout says
    "m² + 4c = 1 + 8 = 9" and "it crosses twice / touches once / misses". The demonstration lowers c
    to −1 (it misses) and back.
- Puzzles hide their answer until Check (the curve, the line, the turning point, the crossings, the change row).
  After a right answer these arrive with lines of working. A wrong pick puts its own numbers back into
  the rule, in red, and is ringed where it is a point. Screen 11's three sketches are drawn from rules:
  2x² − 8x + 6 (right), 2x² + 8x + 6 (roots flipped; x = −1 gives 2 + 8 + 6 = 16) and −2x² + 8x − 6
  (upside down, y-crossing −6).
- Choices the lesson left open: screen 2's third option is "No turning point". Screen 13's third
  option is (0, 0) and (4, 0), the right x values with y left at 0 instead of put into the line.
  Screen 8 shows the steps row before Check and hides only the change row.
