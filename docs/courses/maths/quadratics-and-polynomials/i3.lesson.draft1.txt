    "text": "Factorising turns a quadratic into two brackets multiplied, and if the product of two numbers is zero then at least one of them is zero.[^1] So each bracket can be set to zero on its own: (x − 2)(x − 5) = 0 gives x = 2 or x = 5. These are the equation's [[root]]s, and they are where the [[parabola]] y = (x − 2)(x − 5) meets the axis.[^3]\n\nThe rule works only for zero. If (x − 2)(x − 5) = 10, the brackets can be −2 and −5, or 5 and 2, so neither has to be 10. Move the 10 across first: x² − 7x + 10 = 10, so x² − 7x = 0, which is x(x − 7) = 0, and x = 0 or x = 7. Never divide both sides by x: x² = 6x divided by x leaves x = 6 and loses x = 0. And x² = 9 has two roots, 3 and −3, since (x + 3)(x − 3) = 0.\n\n**Get zero on one side, factorise, and set each bracket to zero.**"
