   "now": "Multiplying two brackets can be pictured as finding the area of a rectangle whose sides are the two brackets. Picture a rectangle x + 3 wide and x + 2 tall.[^1] Cut each side where its x part ends and its number part begins, and lines from those splits cut the rectangle into four tiles. x by x is x², x by 2 is 2x, 3 by x is 3x and 3 by 2 is 6. The area can be written as the two brackets multiplied or as the four tiles added, and these are two ways of writing the same area, so they are equal.[^2] Writing it as the tiles added is expanding the brackets, as in Linear equations. Collect the two strips, 3x + 2x = 5x, and (x + 3)(x + 2) = x² + 5x + 6. At x = 4 the brackets make 7 × 6 = 42, and the tiles make 16 + 12 + 8 + 6 = 42.\n\nDrag the splits to other numbers a and b and the tiles keep the same pattern. The strips are ax and bx, which add to (a + b)x, and the corner is a × b, so (x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab. An expression like this, whose highest power of x is x², is what textbooks call a [[quadratic]]. When both sides are x + 5 the rectangle is a square, and its two strips are both 5x, so (x + 5)² = x² + 10x + 25. An easy slip is to write x² + 25, which leaves out both strips. With numbers, (1 + 4)² = 25, but 1² + 4² is only 17. The missing 8 is the two strips, 1 × 4 and 4 × 1. In letters, (x + y)² = x² + 2xy + y², and the 2xy is those two strips.\n\nA tile cannot have an area below zero, so a term like −4 has no tile, and the rectangle becomes a grid. The grid still gives the right answer, because the rule only multiplies each pair of terms and needs no lengths. Each cell takes its sign from the signs rule of Negative numbers, so −4 × x is −4x and −4 × 1 is −4. (x − 4)(x + 1) gives x², x, −4x and −4, which collect to x² − 3x − 4. At x = 5 the brackets make 1 × 6 = 6, and 25 − 15 − 4 = 6. In (x + 3)(x − 3) the two middle cells are 3x and −3x, which cancel, leaving x² − 9. A number in front of x works the same way, as 2x × x is 2x², so (2x + 1)(x + 3) = 2x² + 6x + x + 3 = 2x² + 7x + 3.\n\nIn the course puzzle, the load's power turns out to be a rule with the current squared in it, and later lessons find its peak by rewriting such rules between bracket form and expanded form, of which expanding is the first. **Two brackets multiply as the four tiles of a rectangle with those sides, every term in the first times every term in the second,[^3] so (x + 3)(x + 2) = x² + 5x + 6.**"
