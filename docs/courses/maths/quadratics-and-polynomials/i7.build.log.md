# i7 build log: Quadratics at work (One curve, three jobs)

- Attempt 1 (i7.body1.html): 11 of 11 checks passed and the sweep found nothing. The self-test reported one fault.
  The gap's letter "h" sat inside the amber area under the parabola, and on a slider screen the moving amber gap bar
  runs through it. In the dark theme its page-colour rim no longer matched the tinted area behind it, so the bar read
  as a line through the label. The pictures showed nothing else wrong.
- Attempt 2 (i7.body2.html): body1 with one edit. The gap's letter is drawn only on the puzzles, where no key row names
  the curve. It now sits at 0.45 of the peak height, clear of the teal curve. On the slider screens the amber key row
  "h / P / M, the gap" names the curve instead. Passed with 11 of 11 checks, no problems and no warnings.
- Pictures read (once each): all ten in attempt 1's lookAt list. For attempt 2 I read the 360 light opening, which was the
  only screenshot that changed (the 360 moved, 360 end, dark moved and 1280 end were byte-identical to attempt 1), and
  its five contact sheets.
- Film: five demonstration steps (0, 2, 4, 6, 8) settle by about 3.4 to 3.7 s. Steps 4 and 6 (the supply SHOW
  screens) keep changing up to about 5.3 to 5.5 s. That is by design: the current's dots flow round the circuit
  continuously, at a speed that follows the current. Puzzles settle in under half a second.
- Design: 13 screens. SHOW on 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9; DO on 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10; mixed puzzles 11, 12 and 13. Pills at the top
  show the job (Ball, Supply, Beam). Each graph draws the straight-line part (navy), the squared part (teal) and the
  gap as an upside-down U (amber, with a pale amber area). On slider screens the gap is marked twice: as a bracket
  between the two parts and as the same length stood up from the axis, with the value labelled. Three key rows under
  the graph show the two parts and the gap, worked out exactly from the rule (3 decimal places at most, exact on every
  slider step).
  - Ball: the ball sits beside the graph on the same height scale. A dashed navy ghost shows where it would be with no
    gravity, and a teal arrow shows gravity's pull. The time slider runs 0-4 s in 0.1 s steps.
  - Deck ball (screens 4 and 11): the pier, the water, and the rule and its parts drawn dashed before 0 s.
  - Supply: a battery casing holds the cell (12 V) and r (0.5 Ω), feeding a load. On the SHOW screens current dots flow
    at 2.5 × I units/s. Screen 7 adds the load's volts and resistance and a bar splitting 144 W into 72 W to the load
    and 72 W lost inside.
  - Beam: the beam is aligned with the graph's x axis, with the even load, supports and 6 kN pushes. Dragging the point
    along it shows the teal load on those x metres, with its 2x kN arrow at x ÷ 2.
- Puzzles hide the parabola and the answer marks until Check, except screen 8, where the curve and the 72 W peak are
  given. A right answer brings in the curve, the roots or peak, and the working; the ball glides to the answer time.
  A wrong pick is ringed in red at its own point, with a red line of working built from the rule.
- Choices the lesson left open: screen 4's third option is 1 s (back at deck height). Screen 8's third option is
  72 W (unchanged). Screen 11's third option is 2 s (the water). On screen 12 the 16 A option is written
  "16 A, 384 W" and ringed where the two parts meet.
- Token cost: not measured exactly. The session's token counter fell by about 0.37M over this build (two attempts,
  16 pictures read).
