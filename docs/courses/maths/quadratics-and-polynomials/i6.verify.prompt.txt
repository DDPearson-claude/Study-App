  "text": "A quadratic's graph is a parabola, a U-shaped curve,[^1] and three clues in its rule y = ax² + bx + c sketch it before you plot a single point. The sign of a says which way up it opens. c says where it crosses the y-axis. The roots, when it has them, say where it crosses the x-axis, and the turning point sits halfway between them.\n\nEach clue comes straight from the rule. Far enough from 0, either way, x² is a big positive number that outgrows bx and c: at x = 100, x² − 2x − 8 is 10,000 − 200 − 8, and at x = −100 it is 10,000 + 200 − 8. So out at the ends y takes the sign of a. If a is positive the parabola opens upward,[^2] a U with a lowest point; if a is negative both ends fall, and it is an upside-down U with a highest point. At x = 0, ax² and bx are both 0, which leaves y = c, so the curve crosses the y-axis at (0, c).[^3] On the x-axis y is 0, so the crossings are the solutions of ax² + bx + c = 0, the roots from lesson 3 or the formula in lesson 5. The line of symmetry from lesson 4 runs through the turning point, and the curve is the same either side of it, so a height of 0 on one side is matched by a height of 0 the same distance the other side: an upside-down U's highest point sits in the middle of its roots,[^4] and a U's lowest point does too. Take y = x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2). a is 1, so it is a U. It crosses the y-axis at (0, −8) and the x-axis at −2 and 4. Halfway is x = 1, and there y = 1 − 2 − 8 = −9, so the turning point is (1, −9), just what the completed-square form (x − 1)² − 9 gives.[^5] Setting y to 0 finds the roots, not the turning point: x² − 2x − 8 = 0 gives −2 and 4, where the height is 0, while the lowest point is at −9.\n\na is not a slope, though it sits where m sits in y = mx + c.
