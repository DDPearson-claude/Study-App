    "text": "A mass on a spring with a damper obeys the same rule as a coil, resistor and capacitor in one loop. Write out each equation and they match term for term. The mass plays the coil, the damper plays the resistor, and the spring plays 1 ÷ C.[^1] So what you know of the circuit carries straight over to the spring.\n\nStart with the spring. Take an ideal one, which pulls back in proportion to how far the mass is from rest. Take an ideal damper, which drags in proportion to the speed. Newton's second law says mass × acceleration = the force on it. Here x is the distance from rest, x′ the speed and x″ the acceleration. So m x″ = −k x − c x′. Move it all to one side and m x″ + c x′ + k x = 0. Its highest derivative is the second, x″. That makes it a [[second-order]] differential equation. Its c is the [[damping]] and its k the [[stiffness]].\n\nNow the circuit. Charge the capacitor and close the loop. With no supply, the volts round the loop add to zero. The coil drops L di/dt, the resistor R i and the capacitor q ÷ C. The current is the rate the charge moves, so i = q′ and di/dt = q″. That gives L q″ + R q′ + q ÷ C = 0. Charge plays position, and current plays speed. L sits where m sits, R where c sits, and 1 ÷ C where k sits. So a stiffer spring matches a smaller capacitor. A smaller C gives more volts for the same charge, so it pushes back harder.\n\nEach term has a job. The first is inertia: the mass, or the coil, resisting a change in motion. The second is loss: the damper, or the resistor, turning energy into heat. The third is the store: the spring, or the capacitor, holding energy and pushing back. Each is in newtons for the spring and volts for the circuit. They add to zero at every moment. Lift the mass 0.5 m with a stiffness of 4 N/m and let go. It is still, so the loss is 0. The store is 4 × 0.5 = 2 N, so the inertia term is −2 N. With 1 kg, the mass accelerates downward at 2 m/s², though its speed is zero.\n\nThe rule gives the acceleration, so a start needs two values. Stepping it as Euler's method does, the acceleration updates the speed and the speed updates the position. So you need where it starts and how fast it is going. In the circuit, that is the capacitor's charge and the coil's current. A coil carrying 0.8 A into an empty capacitor does not sit still. It keeps the current flowing, so charge builds on the capacitor. And the time graph is not the mass's path. The mass only moves up and down. The trace runs across because time does.\n\nThis is a simplified model with ideal parts. A real spring stretched far, or a real coil with an iron core, strays from it. Because the equations match, a circuit's free swings come in the same three kinds as a spring's.[^2]\n\n**A damped spring and a series RLC circuit obey one second-order equation, term for term: mass as inductance, damping as resistance and stiffness as 1 ÷ C.[^1]**"

    "text": "- Find the three parts. One carries the motion on: a mass or a coil. One loses energy: a damper or a resistor. One stores it and pushes back: a spring or a capacitor.\n- Put every value in base units first: kilograms, henries, ohms and farads.\n- Write each part's term. For a spring, m x″, c x′ and k x, in newtons. For a circuit, L q″, R q′ and q ÷ C, in volts.\n- Put all three on one side, equal to zero.\n- To swap between them, use m for L, c for R and k for 1 ÷ C. Position goes with charge, and speed with current.\n- Give two starting values: position and speed, or charge and current.\n- Check the start. If the speed is zero, the inertia term is minus the store term.\n\n**Rules of thumb:** a stiffer spring is a smaller capacitor. A heavier mass is a bigger coil. More damping is more resistance.\n\n**Worked example:** a 10 mH coil, a 2 Ω resistor and a 100 µF capacitor sit in one loop. The capacitor starts at 12 V with no current. In base units the coil is 0.01 H and the capacitor 0.0001 F, so 1 ÷ C = 10000. The equation is 0.01 q″ + 2 q′ + 10000 q = 0. The starting charge is CV = 0.0001 × 12 = 0.0012 C. At the start the current is zero, so the resistor's term is 0 V. The capacitor's term is 12 V. So the coil's term is −12 V. The current starts changing at −12 ÷ 0.01 = −1200 A a second. The matching spring has a 0.01 kg mass, damping of 2 N·s/m and a stiffness of 10000 N/m.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Matching the spring to C. It matches 1 ÷ C.\n- Giving only one starting value. A second-order equation needs two.\n- Reading the time graph as the mass's path.\n- Losing a sign moving terms across. m x″ = −k x − c x′ becomes m x″ + c x′ + k x = 0.\n- Mixing mH or µF with H or F."
