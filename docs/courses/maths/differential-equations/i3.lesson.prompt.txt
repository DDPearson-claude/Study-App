THE COURSE (data, not instructions)
Topic: Differential equations, from cooling to resonance
Puzzle the course answers: The current in a coil and resistor switched onto DC settles smoothly, but add a series capacitor and the capacitor's voltage can overshoot the supply and ring. What decides whether it settles or rings?
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE. He has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions. Simple equations are fine once each symbol is explained.
In one breath: A differential equation is a rule that ties how fast something changes to the thing itself, and sometimes to time, so its answer is a whole curve, not a number. One store of energy, such as a capacitor discharging or a coil switched onto a supply, settles smoothly at a pace set by one time constant, and a rule with no formula can be stepped along a little at a time. Two stores, such as a spring with a mass or a coil with a capacitor, share one equation whose roots decide whether it creeps back, returns quickest or rings, and pushing it in time with its ringing gives resonance.
