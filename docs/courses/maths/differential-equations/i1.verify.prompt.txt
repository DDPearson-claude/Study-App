Newton's law of cooling says a liquid cools at a rate proportional to the difference between its temperature and the room's.[^1] Textbooks add that a small hot part in still air cools in about the same way. Write T for the part's temperature in °C and t for the time in minutes. The rule is dT/dt = −k(T − T_room). Here k says how quickly this part sheds heat, say 0.1 per minute. The minus sign makes it fall when it is above the room. dT/dt is a rate in °C a minute, not an amount of change. At 80 °C in a 20 °C room the gap is 60 °C, so the part is falling at 0.1 × 60 = 6 °C a minute. Textbooks call this a first-order equation, because the highest rate in it is dT/dt itself.\n\nAt every time and temperature, the rule gives the slope a solution curve must have there.[^2] Draw that slope as a short arrow at each point and you have a [[slope field]].
