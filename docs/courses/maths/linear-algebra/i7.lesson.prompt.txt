Research notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Concentrations in linked brine tanks obey a linear system of differential equations solved through the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of its matrix. [1]
- The simple solutions of coupled oscillators are called normal modes, and any motion is a sum of them. [2]
- For two equal masses on three identical springs, the eigenvectors (1,1) and (-1,1) have eigenvalues k/m and 3k/m. [3]
- In the opposite-motion mode the masses move the same amount in opposite directions and the middle spring is not stretched or compressed. [4]
- An eigenvector is a direction a transformation only stretches or squishes, and its eigenvalue is the factor by which it is stretched. [5]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Non-trivial eigenvectors exist exactly when det(A − λI) = 0. [6]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The eigenvalues of a square matrix add up to the sum of its diagonal entries, the trace, which gives a check on the answer. [7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For i5: two masses coupled by springs have the eigenvectors (1, 1) and (−1, 1), each with its own eigenvalue; these are the system's simple ways to move, its normal modes. [3]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In a basis of eigenvectors, a matrix just scales each basis arrow by its eigenvalue, so applying it 100 times scales each by the 100th power of its eigenvalue. [8]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A matrix can be diagonalised when it has n linearly independent eigenvectors; a shear does not have enough eigenvectors to span all of space. [9][10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
