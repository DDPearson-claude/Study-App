    "text": "A line in space is a start point plus any amount of one direction.[^1] A plane is a start point plus one arrow standing square to it, at right angles, called its [[normal]].[^2] The plane is every point whose step from the start is square to the normal. That one fact gives its equation, ax + by + cz = d.\n\nWrite the start as p and the direction as v. A point on the line is r = p + t v, where t is any number. At t = 0 you are at the start. Each extra unit of t adds one more v, so the dot moves in a straight line at a steady pace. Take p = (0, 1, 0) and v = (1, 2, 0). At t = 2 the dot is at (0, 1, 0) + (2, 4, 0) = (2, 5, 0). The two parts do different jobs. p is a place, an arrow from the origin. v has no place of its own. It only says which way. Swap them and you get another line. (1, 2, 0) + t(0, 1, 0) runs parallel to the y-axis and never reaches (0, 1, 0). For the line through two points A and B, the direction is the step between them, B − A. A + t(B − A) is a fraction t of the way along, so t = ½ gives the midpoint.\n\nA plane needs a different kind of arrow. Take the start p = (1, 1, 3) and the normal n = (2, 1, 2). Any point r on the plane is reached from p by a step r − p that lies flat in the plane. A step flat in the plane is square to n. Arrows square to each other have a dot product of 0. So n · (r − p) = 0 for every point on the plane. Off the plane, the step has a part along n, so the dot product is not 0. Its sign says which side. It is positive on the side n points to and negative on the other. Multiply it out. 2(x − 1) + 1(y − 1) + 2(z − 3) = 0 becomes 2x + y + 2z = 9. The normal's parts sit in front of x, y and z, and 9 is n · p. So (2, 1, 2) is on the plane, as 4 + 1 + 4 = 9. The point (3, 2, 5) is not. It is p + n, a whole step along the normal, and it reads 9. The dot product is |n| times the part of the step along n. Here |n| = √(4 + 1 + 4) = 3, and the origin reads −9. So the origin is 9 ÷ 3 = 3 from the plane, on the side away from n.\n\nThe same plane can come from two directions lying in it. It is a span slid to the start, r = p + s u + t w. Its normal must be square to both, so it is u × w. u = (1, 0, −1) and w = (0, 2, −1) give (2, 1, 2), the same normal. To find where a line meets a plane, put the line's point into the equation and solve for t. For (0, 1, 0) + t(1, 2, 0), 2t + (1 + 2t) + 0 = 9, so 4t = 8 and t = 2. They meet at (2, 5, 0). Each unit of t changes the reading by n · v, here 4. If n · v is 0, the line runs parallel to the plane. It then misses it, or lies in it. Two planes that are not parallel meet in a line. Its direction lies in both planes, so it is square to both normals. 2x + y + 2z = 9 and x − y = 0 meet along (1, 1, 3) + t(2, 2, −3).\n\n**A line is r = p + t v, a start plus any amount of one direction. A plane is n · (r − p) = 0, every point whose step from the start is square to the normal, and it multiplies out to ax + by + cz = d with n = (a, b, c).**"
