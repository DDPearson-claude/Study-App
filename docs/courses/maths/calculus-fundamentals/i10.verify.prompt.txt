Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- The average value of a function on an interval is its definite integral divided by the length of the interval. [R1]
- RMS value: square the function, find the mean over a period, then take the square root. [R2]
- For a sine wave the RMS value is the peak divided by the square root of 2, about 0.707 of the peak. [R3][R4][R5]
- The mean of a sine wave over a whole cycle is zero, so its mean is taken over half a cycle, when it is 0.637 of the peak. [R6][R7]
- The RMS value is the steady DC value that would give the same energy to a circuit. [R8]
- The MCA Marine Electro-technology syllabus asks for the mean value and rms value of a sine wave. [R9]
- Integration is the inverse of differentiation; add one to the power and divide by the new power. [R10][R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Because inverse operations do the opposite in the opposite order, the power rule for integrals adds 1 to the power, then divides by it. [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The power rule for integrals cannot be used at n = −1 because it would divide by n + 1 = 0. [R13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The constant c appears because differentiating a constant gives zero; it is fixed from given conditions such as initial values. [R11][R14][R15]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- SQA examiners report that candidates make basic errors in dealing with the constant of integration. [R16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Adding the areas of rectangles under a rate graph approximates the total; the evaluation theorem then gets the exact area by evaluating an antiderivative at both ends and subtracting. [R17][R18]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- When a quantity changes, its new value equals its initial value plus the integral of its rate of change. [R19]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Integrating the current function over a time interval gives the charge transferred in that interval. [R20]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- By the evaluation theorem, any antiderivative of the integrand will do when evaluating a definite integral. [R21]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- An integral describes a quantity as a sum of many small pieces (MIT 18.01). [R22]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The definite integral can be taught as the area under a curve or as an accumulation of a rate.
