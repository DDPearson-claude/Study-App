  "text": "For any power of x there is a short rule for its derivative, so you can differentiate a whole formula without drawing a single chord. Last lesson the growing square showed why x² gives 2x: grow the side by a small step h and the area gains two strips, x by h, and a tiny corner, h by h; divide by h and shrink it, and only 2x is left. A cube shows the next power the same way. Grow every edge of a cube of side x by h and its volume, x³, gains three slabs, each x by x by h, three thin rods, each x by h by h, and one tiny cube, h by h by h. On the graph of y = x³, moving from x to x + h raises y by exactly that new volume, so the chord between them has Δy equal to the new pieces over Δx = h. Divide each piece by h: each slab leaves x², each rod leaves xh and the tiny cube leaves h², so the chord's slope is 3x² + 3xh + h². Shrink h and every piece with an h left in it shrinks away, so the slope settles on 3x². For a cube of side 2 the chord slopes for h = 1, 0.5, 0.1 and 0.01 are 19, 15.25, 12.61 and 12.06, settling on 12, which is 3 × 2².\n\nPut the results side by side: x gives 1, x² gives 2x and x³ gives 3x². Each time the power comes down in front and the power left on x is one less, and the reason is the same each time. xⁿ means n x's multiplied together, so growing x by h gives n brackets of (x + h) multiplied together. Multiply them out, and the pieces with a single h in them come from taking h from one bracket and x from all the rest, so there are n of them, each xⁿ⁻¹ × h. For the square those are the two strips, and for the cube the three slabs. Every other piece has h × h or more in it, so after dividing by h it still has an h and shrinks away. So the derivative of xⁿ is nxⁿ⁻¹, the [[power rule]]: bring the power down in front, then take one off the power.[^1][^2] The slabs show why for whole-number powers; the rule also holds for powers below zero and between whole numbers,[^1] and the Second Engineer syllabus asks for it with powers above and below zero.[^3] A number in front stays. 5x³ is x³ with every height 5 times bigger, so every rise between two points is 5 times bigger over the same step across, and every slope is 5 times as steep: the derivative of 5x³ is 5 × 3x² = 15x².[^4] At x = 2, x³ climbs at 12 and 5x³ at 60.
