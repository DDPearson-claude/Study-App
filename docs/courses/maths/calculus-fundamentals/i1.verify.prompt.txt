Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 The rate at an instant — Slide two points together and the slope of the line joining them settles on one number: the rate at that instant, written dy/dx.
- i3 The derivative as a function — Finding the rate at every point at once gives a new rule, the derivative f′(x), which for y = x² is 2x.
- i4 The power rule — The derivative of xⁿ is nxⁿ⁻¹, worked term by term, so 4x³ − 2x² + 7x − 9 gives 12x² − 4x + 7.
- i5 Rates on the job — Read in its units, a derivative is a rate on the job: current is the rate charge flows, i = dq/dt, and velocity is displacement's.
- i6 Highest and lowest — A smooth curve is flat where it turns at a top or bottom, so setting its derivative to zero finds the largest area or power.
- i7 Integration, the reverse — Integrating undoes differentiating: xⁿ (n not −1) becomes xⁿ⁺¹ ÷ (n + 1), plus a constant c fixed by a starting value.
- i8 Adding up: the definite integral — Adding up bars under a rate graph gives the total, such as charge from current, and the reverse rule, F(b) − F(a), gets it exactly.
- i9 Work from a pV diagram — The work a gas does is the area under its pV curve, which for pVⁿ = C is (p₁V₁ − p₂V₂) ÷ (n − 1).
- i10 Mean and RMS values — Mean value is area ÷ length; RMS is the root of the squared graph's mean, which for a sine wave is its peak ÷ √2.

On its graph, pick two points, P at 1 s and Q at 3 s, and join them with a straight line, a [[chord]]. Δ, the Greek letter delta, means \"change in\". From P to Q, Δx = 3 − 1 = 2 s and Δy = 12 − 4 = 8 m, so Δy ÷ Δx = 8 m ÷ 2 s = 4 m per s, the slope of the chord. On a straight line every chord gives the same answer, because each second adds the same 4 m, so Δy is 4 times Δx wherever the points sit. A second boat that started 10 m out, f(x) = 10 + 4x, has the same slope, 4 m per s.
