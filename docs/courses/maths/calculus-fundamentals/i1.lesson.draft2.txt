    "text": "An average rate of change says how much one thing changed for each unit of another, between two points. You already work these out. 120 km in 2 hours is 120 ÷ 2 = 60 km per hour, the change in distance divided by the change in time, and its unit comes straight from the division, km over hours.\n\nA boat leaving a buoy at a steady 4 metres a second is 4x metres away after x seconds. A rule like this, which takes one number and gives back one, is a [[function]], written f(x) = 4x and read \"f of x\". f(3) means the rule applied to 3, so f(3) = 4 × 3 = 12 m. On its graph, pick two points, P at 1 s and Q at 3 s, and join them with a straight line, a [[chord]]. Δ, the Greek letter delta, means \"change in\". From P to Q, Δx = 3 − 1 = 2 s and Δy = 12 − 4 = 8 m, so Δy ÷ Δx = 8 m ÷ 2 s = 4 m per s, the slope of the chord. On a straight line every chord gives the same answer, because each second adds the same 4 m, so Δy is 4 times Δx wherever the points sit. A second boat that started 10 m out, f(x) = 10 + 4x, has the same slope, 4 m per s. Dividing its y by its x at 5 s gives 30 ÷ 5 = 6 m per s, which is too much, because the 30 m includes the 10 m it had before the clock started. y ÷ x is the slope of the chord from (0, 0), so it answers a different question unless your first point is (0, 0).\n\nA boat pulling away from the quay speeds up, so its graph curves. Say it covers f(x) = x² metres in x seconds, where x² means x × x. It covers 1 m in its first second, 3 m in its second, 5 m in its third and 7 m in its fourth, more each second, so a chord further along rises more for the same Δx. From 0 s to 1 s the chord's slope is 1 m per s, and from 3 s to 4 s it is 16 − 9 = 7 m per s. The chords no longer agree, so the answer depends on which two points you pick. Δy ÷ Δx between them is the [[average rate of change]] over that stretch, the change in f divided by the change in x.[^1] It is the steady rate that would give the same change, because that rate times Δx gives back Δy. From 0 s to 4 s the curve and the steady boat's line both go from 0 m to 16 m, so both chords give 4 m per s, though one boat crawled at first and then went faster. An average does not show what happened between its two points. Calculus uses the slope of a chord like this to estimate the rate at which one quantity changes in relation to another.[^2]\n\nA falling graph gives a rate below zero. A tank draining from 200 L at 0 min to 50 L at 5 min has Δy = 50 − 200 = −150 L, because the later amount is the smaller, so the rate is −150 ÷ 5 = −30 L per min, and the minus says it is emptying. Take both changes in the same order, or the sign comes out wrong. In the course's puzzle a gas's pressure falls as its volume grows, and Δy ÷ Δx, in pressure per unit of volume, is the first way to say how fast. **The average rate of change between two points is Δy ÷ Δx, the change in y divided by the change in x,[^1] the slope of the chord joining them, in units of y per unit of x.**"
