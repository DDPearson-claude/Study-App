Topic: Calculus you will actually use
Puzzle the course answers: Expanding gas pushes a piston with a pressure that keeps falling, so no single force times distance will do. How do you get the work it does from its pressure–volume diagram?
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE. He has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions. Simple equations are fine once each symbol is explained.
In one breath: Calculus is the maths of things that change, and it has two halves that undo each other. The derivative is a rate, how fast one thing changes per unit of another at a single instant, like current as the rate charge flows; one rule for powers finds most of them, and a slope of zero finds the largest or smallest value. The integral adds a rate up into a total, like charge from a current graph or work from a pV diagram, and the same rule run backwards gets it exactly, right through to mean and RMS values.
