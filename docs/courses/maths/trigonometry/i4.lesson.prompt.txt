Research notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- The law of cosines says the square of any side of a triangle equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides minus twice their product and the cosine of the included angle. [1]
- The cosine rule can be remembered as the Pythagoras theorem with something extra so that it works for all triangles. [2]
- The cosine rule finds a missing side or angle when two sides and the included angle, or all three sides, are known. [3]
- The sine rule finds a side from another side and two angles, or an angle from two sides and a non-included angle. [4]
- With the sine rule the second possible angle is found by subtracting the first answer from 180 degrees, and exists only if the angle sum of the triangle stays within 180 degrees. [5]
- Sine, cosine and tangent are ratios of sides of a right triangle: sine is opposite over hypotenuse, cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse, tangent is opposite over adjacent. [6]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In similar right-angled triangles the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent sides stay in the same proportion, so adjacent over hypotenuse is the same however much the triangle is enlarged. [7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For a right-angled triangle, trigonometry is used when a question involves two sides and an angle. [8]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In a conduit offset bend, the offset depth is the side opposite the bend angle, the distance between bends is the hypotenuse and the horizontal run is the adjacent side. [9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
