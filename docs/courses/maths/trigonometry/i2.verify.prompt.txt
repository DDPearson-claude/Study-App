Remember the 150 mm offset with 300 mm between the bends. The offset depth is the opposite side and the distance between bends the hypotenuse,[^3] so sin θ = 150 ÷ 300 = 0.5. Which angle has a sine of 0.5? The calculator's sin⁻¹ key answers that: sin⁻¹ 0.5 = 30°. It is read [[inverse sine]], because it undoes sin. An inverse takes the output and does the opposite to get back to the input:[^4] sin turns 30° into 0.5, and sin⁻¹ turns 0.5 back into 30°. cos⁻¹ and tan⁻¹ undo cos and tan in the same way. Choose the key as you chose the ratio in lesson 1, from the two sides you have: sine is opposite over hypotenuse, cosine adjacent over hypotenuse, and tangent opposite over adjacent,[^5] so those pairs take sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ and tan⁻¹. A slope is the last kind. As in Straight-line graphs, its gradient m is rise ÷ run. The rise is the side opposite the slope's angle and the run is the side next to it, so tan θ = m, and the line y = 0.5x, drawn with the same scale on both axes, climbs at tan⁻¹ 0.5 = 26.6°. In a right-angled triangle θ is between 0° and 90°, and that is the angle these keys give; a later lesson finds a second angle with the same sine. You need an inverse key only when you want an angle.
