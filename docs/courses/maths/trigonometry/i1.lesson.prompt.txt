Research notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Sine, cosine and tangent are ratios of sides of a right triangle: sine is opposite over hypotenuse, cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse, tangent is opposite over adjacent. [1]
- In similar right-angled triangles the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent sides stay in the same proportion, so adjacent over hypotenuse is the same however much the triangle is enlarged. [2]
- For a right-angled triangle, trigonometry is used when a question involves two sides and an angle. [3]
- In a conduit offset bend, the offset depth is the side opposite the bend angle, the distance between bends is the hypotenuse and the horizontal run is the adjacent side. [4]
- The offset multiplier is the cosecant of the bend angle, 1 over sin: sin 30 degrees is 0.5 giving 2.0, and sin 45 degrees is 0.7071 giving 1.414. [5]
- The multiplier method is an approximation because it does not account for the arc of the bend itself (the source says the difference is negligible for most field work up to about 30 degrees). [6]
- Scientific calculators used for trigonometry should be in degrees mode, according to Bitesize. [7]
- UK low-voltage supplies are declared at a frequency of 50 hertz and a voltage of 230 volts between the phase and neutral conductors at the supply terminals, unless otherwise agreed in writing. [8]  (whole topic)
- Low voltage in building supplies is described as 400 V three-phase and 230 V single-phase; these are nominal voltages. [9]  (whole topic)
