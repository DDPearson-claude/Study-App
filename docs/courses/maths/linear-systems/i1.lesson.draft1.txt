    "text": "Two facts about the same two unknown numbers each draw a straight line, and the pair of numbers that fits both facts is the point where the lines cross.[^1] Start with one fact, y = 2x + 1. Pick any x, work out its y, and you have a pair that fits, such as (0, 1), (1, 3) or (2, 5), and every point between them fits too. One equation in two unknowns has endless answers,[^2] and plotted they make the rule's line, as in Straight-line graphs. Drag the test point onto line 1 and its readout ticks. Move it off and the readout shows ≠, because a point off the line has a y the rule does not give for its x.\n\nNow add a second fact about the same x and y, x + y = 7, drawn as the dashed line 2. Its own answers fill its own line, such as (0, 7), (3, 4) and (7, 0). You want the pair that makes both equations true at once,[^4] so it must be on both lines. Two equations solved together like this are [[simultaneous equations]], and a pair that fits them both is a [[solution]]. Two straight lines with different tilts meet at one point, so here there is one solution, the [[crossing point]] (2, 5): 2 × 2 + 1 = 5 and 2 + 5 = 7. Solving each equation alone does not find it. (3, 7) fits y = 2x + 1, but 3 + 7 = 10, not 7, so it is on line 1 and misses line 2.\n\nTurn line 2 and the crossing point moves. Written as y = −x + 7, line 2 has m = −1 against line 1's 2. Turn it to y = 1.5x + 7. At x = 0 it is 6 above line 1, and each 1 along line 1 climbs 0.5 more than line 2, so the gap shuts by 0.5 a step and closes at x = 12, off the grid. The solution is still there, and the grid just does not show it. Turn line 2 to the same tilt, y = 2x + 7, and the gap stays 6 all the way along, so the lines are parallel, never cross, and there is no solution.[^1] Slide its c down to 1 as well and line 2 lies on top of line 1, so every pair on it fits both, and there are endless solutions.[^3] So for two lines written as y = mx + c, read m and c before you draw. Different m's give one crossing point. The same m with different c's gives none. The same m and the same c give one line.\n\nA graph gives a reading, which is not always exact. y = 2x + 1 and x + y = 5 cross between the tenths, and the nearest the test point gets, (1.3, 3.7), fits x + y = 5 but gives 2 × 1.3 + 1 = 3.6, not 3.7.
