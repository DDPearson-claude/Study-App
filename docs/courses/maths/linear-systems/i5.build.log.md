## The body
- 12 screens. SHOW 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, 9; DO 2, 4, 6, 8, ending with two mixed puzzles 10 and 11.
  0 the circuit (12 V with 1 Ω, 12 V with 2 Ω, a 2 Ω load), each loop's route traced; 1 the junction law,
  dials turning and I₃ following, both junctions ringed; 2 the load's current (5 A, 3.5 A, so 1.5 A; slip 8.5);
  3 the ring walks loop 1 at 1 A and 1 A to 7 V; 4 walk loop 1 at 3 A and 1 A (1 V; slip 23, adding the drops);
  5 the walk in letters, tidied to 3I₁ + 2I₂ = 12; 6 loop 2's equation (2I₁ + 4I₂ = 12; slips 4I₂ = 12 and
  2I₁ + 2I₂ = 12); 7 the (I₁, I₂) grid, loop 1 solid and loop 2 dashed, the dials' dot going to (4, 0) and then
  the crossing (3, 1.5); 8 which pair fits both (slips (4, 0) and (2, 2), each walked, with the grid);
  9 battery 2 swapped to 4 V, solved to I₂ = −1.5 A, the arrow turning round in ink; 10 the 10 V and 8 V
  batteries with 1 Ω everywhere ((4, 2); slip (5, 0) leaves loop 2 at 3 V); 11 I₁ = 4 A and I₂ = −1 A at the
  junction (3 A; slip 5 A, the minus dropped).
- Picture: one circuit, two tracer rings (one per loop) dragged round their own loops (or moved with the
  arrow keys). Each part a ring passes writes its change beside it (+12 V, −1 V, −4 V, or the letter form on
  the letter screens), and each loop shows its running total and "start / so far / full loop". Two dials
  (I₁ and I₂, −2 A to 6 A in 0.5 A steps, − and + buttons and a slider) sit under the circuit on the SHOW
  screens. A current below zero turns its arrow round and shows its size. Every number is computed from
  the rule: the walk changes, totals, junction, the elimination rows, the check walks, each wrong pick's walk.
- The dials are native range inputs with K.buttons, not K.control, because the lesson lists only "step" as a
  control; the rings are a custom drag on the loop wires (pointer capture, touch-action none on the wires only,
  so the page still scrolls elsewhere).
- Checks cite OpenStax 21.3 (junction rule; loop rule; one equation per rule, as many as unknowns; a wrong
  direction comes out negative, no harm done), OpenStax 19.1 (Ohm: drop proportional to current) and
  OpenStax 10.1 (terminal voltage falls as current rises), each restating its quote; the rest are known
  answers worked by hand (every listed number), the puzzle structure, the drawn states and the timing.
