    "text": "Each fact a story gives about two unknowns becomes one equation, so two separate facts give two equations, and the pair that solves them is the answer that fits the whole story. Take a cinema receipt: 2 drinks and 1 bag of popcorn cost £8. Call a drink's price d and a bag of popcorn's p, both in pounds. The receipt can check any guess, by working out 2 × d + p and comparing it with £8. It accepts a £2 drink with £4 popcorn, because 4 + 4 = 8. It accepts £3 and £2, and £1 and £6, too. On the grid of guesses, with d across and p up, every guess it accepts lands on one straight line, the line of 2d + p = 8. One fact about two unknowns allows endless answers,[^4] so on its own it cannot tell you the prices.\n\nA second receipt says 1 drink and 2 bags of popcorn cost £7, so d + 2p = 7. Each item's count times its price, added up, makes the total. Its accepted guesses, such as £1 and £3 or £5 and £1, make a second line. Only the crossing point, a £3 drink and £2 popcorn, passes both receipts: 2 × 3 + 2 = 8 and 3 + 2 × 2 = 7. That pair is the solution, because it makes both equations true.[^6] Dragging guesses about finds it, but one guess at a time, so work it out instead. The first receipt gives p = 8 − 2d. Swap that into the second: d + 2(8 − 2d) = 7, so d + 16 − 4d = 7, −3d = −9 and d = 3. Then p = 8 − 6 = 2.\n\nThe steps are the same for any story. Say what you are looking for, and give each unknown a letter,[^1] written down with what it stands for and its unit. Turn each fact into one equation.[^1] Solve the pair by substitution or elimination. Check the answer in the story's own words, and say what it means, with units. The unknowns need not be prices. In Straight-line graphs you read a lifting machine's law, P = aW + b, off a graph, where P is the effort and W the load. Two readings give it by algebra instead. Say a load of 100 N needs an effort of 15 N, and 300 N needs 35 N. Now a and b are the unknowns, so each reading gives one equation: 100a + b = 15 and 300a + b = 35. Take the first away from the second: 200a = 20, so a = 0.1, and b = 15 − 10 = 5. The law is P = 0.1W + 5. Each extra newton of load needs 0.1 N more effort, and 5 N is the effort with no load on. The Second Engineer exam's maths syllabus asks for just this: equations of two unknowns built from the data in a question, then solved.[^2]\n\nThe second fact must say something new. A receipt for 4 drinks and 2 bags of popcorn at £16 is the first receipt doubled, so it accepts every guess the first accepts: a £1 drink and £6 popcorn make 4 + 12 = 16. Its line lies on top of the first, and there are endless answers.[^5] At £15 it would disagree with the first receipt. Its line would run parallel, and no pair would fit both.[^3] **Two separate facts about the same two unknowns give two equations,[^1] and when their lines cross, the pair that solves them, checked in the story, is the one answer that fits both facts.**"

Two unknowns need two separate facts.\n\n**Worked example:** two orders from a wholesaler, with example prices. Order 1 is 10 m of 2.5 mm² cable and 10 m of 1.5 mm², for £20. Order 2 is 10 m of 2.5 mm² and 20 m of 1.5 mm², for £28. Let x be the price of the 2.5 mm² and y the price of the 1.5 mm², both in £ a metre. Then 10x + 10y = 20 and 10x + 20y = 28. Take the first away from the second: 10y = 8, so y = 0.8. Put it back: 10x + 8 = 20, so 10x = 12 and x = 1.2.
