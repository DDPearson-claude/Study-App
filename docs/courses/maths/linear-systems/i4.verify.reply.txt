Its two axes are the two unknowns of a story, such as a drink's price across and a bag of popcorn's price up, in pounds, or a and b in a machine's law P = aW + b. The guess is a ring you drag anywhere on the grid. It snaps to set steps, 50p on most price screens. Beside the grid, each fact in the story is a receipt card. It puts the guess into its own rule, such as 2 × d + p for 2 drinks and 1 bag of popcorn, and shows the total that guess would make. It shows ✓ when that matches the real total, and too much or too little when it does not. Each guess a receipt accepts leaves a small mark. The marks line up, because every pair that makes a rule come to its total lies on that rule's straight line, so the receipt's line is drawn through them, receipt 1's solid and receipt 2's dashed. Where two lines cross, only the crossing point is on both, so only that guess gets two ticks. A receipt that is another one doubled lays its line on top of the first. One whose total does not fit runs parallel and never crosses it. Beneath the grid the working runs line by line: the letters and what they stand for, one equation per fact, the solve, the check in the story, and what the pair means, with units.
