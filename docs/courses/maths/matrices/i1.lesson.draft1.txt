    "text": "A [[matrix]] is a grid of numbers in rows and columns, and you can read it two ways. Across, each row holds the numbers of one equation.[^1] Down, each column is an arrow. Multiplying the grid by a column of amounts mixes those arrows, so many of the first plus so many of the second.\n\nA grid's size is its rows, then its columns, so a grid of 2 rows and 3 columns is 2 by 3. Each number in it is an [[entry]], named by its row first, then its column. In [4 7 1; 2 5 3], row 2, column 1 is 2, while row 1, column 2 is 7. Now take the equations x + 3y = 7 and 2x + y = 4. Their numbers make the grid [1 3; 2 1], one row for each equation. The unknowns x and y stand in a column beside it, and 7 and 4 in a column after the equals sign. To read row 1 back, multiply across the row and down the unknowns together, then add. That gives 1 × x + 3 × y, so row 1 says x + 3y = 7.\n\nNow read the same grid down its columns. Column 1 is the arrow (1, 2), and column 2 is the arrow (3, 1). Every x in the equations multiplies a number from column 1, so all the x parts together are x lots of column 1. All the y parts are y lots of column 2. So the grid times (x, y) is x lots of column 1 plus y lots of column 2, laid nose to tail. That mix is a [[linear combination]] of the columns.[^2] With x = 2 and y = 1, two lots of (1, 2) reach (2, 4), and one (3, 1) on the end lands on (5, 5). Row 1 adds the across parts, 1 × 2 + 3 × 1 = 5, and row 2 adds the up parts, 2 × 2 + 1 × 1 = 5. Take the rows as the arrows instead and you land on (4, 7), a different point.\n\nTurn it round and you have simultaneous equations. To land on (7, 4), the across parts must make 7 and the up parts 4. That is x + 3y = 7 and 2x + y = 4, which give x = 1 and y = 2. A circuit's loop equations fit a grid the same way, with the loop currents as the column of unknowns.[^3] So finding a circuit's currents is finding the amounts that hit a target.\n\n**A matrix times a column of amounts is that many of each of its columns, added nose to tail, and each row of the grid is one equation for that sum.**[^2]"
