   "now": "A 3 by 3 matrix moves all of space at once. Its three columns are where the arrows i, j and k land. Its determinant does for volumes what ad − bc did for areas. You find it from the top row: each entry times a signed 2 by 2 determinant, all added up. With i, j and k in the top row, the same recipe gives the cross product.\n\nStart from the unit cube, with edges i, j and k. Its volume is 1. Keep i and j where they are, so the floor stays the unit square, and move only k. The cube leans into a slanted box. A box's volume is its floor's area times its height. The floor's area is 1, so the volume is the height, which is k's up part. Sliding k's tip sideways leans the box but leaves its height alone, so the volume stays the same. That is the shear you met in the plane, one size up. Textbooks add that every 3 by 3 matrix works this way: its determinant's size is the factor it scales every volume by. Drop k's tip to the floor and the box is flat, so the determinant is 0. All of space then lies on one plane, and no move can lift it back. Push the tip below the floor and the box comes out mirrored. A move that flips space like that has a negative determinant.[^1]\n\nHere is the recipe for any 3 by 3 grid. Take a top-row entry and cover its row and its column. A 2 by 2 grid is left, and its ad − bc is that entry's [[minor]]. Do the same for the other two entries. Give the three minors the signs +, −, +, and they become [[cofactor]]s. Multiply each top-row entry by its cofactor and add the three.[^2] Try it on a box whose floor is not square. Send i to (2, 1, 0), j to (1, 3, 0) and k to (1, 0, 2). The top row is 2, 1, 1, and the minors are 6, 2 and 0. So the determinant is 2 × 6 − 1 × 2 + 1 × 0 = 10. Now check it as a box. The floor is the parallelogram of i and j, with area 2 × 3 − 1 × 1 = 5. The height is 2. So the volume is 5 × 2 = 10, and the two agree. Forget the middle minus and you get 14, which fails the check. That is a check on one box, not a proof for every grid.\n\nThe cross product comes from the same recipe. Put i, j and k in the top row as if they were numbers, a in row 2 and b in row 3.[^3] Here a and b sit in rows, not columns. Textbooks add that turning a grid's rows into columns leaves its determinant the same, so either way works. The three cofactors are then a × b's i, j and k parts. Take a = (2, 1, 0) and b = (1, 3, 0), the floor above. The i and j parts come out 0, and the k part is 2 × 3 − 1 × 1 = 5. So a × b = 5k. It stands square to the floor, and its length is the floor's area. Swap rows 2 and 3 and every part changes sign, so b × a = −5k. Check the order on i × j, which must give k.\n\n**Along the top row, each entry times its cofactor, with signs + − +, added up, gives a 3 by 3 determinant: the volume scale factor, 0 when space is squashed flat. With i, j and k in the top row, the same recipe gives a × b.**"
