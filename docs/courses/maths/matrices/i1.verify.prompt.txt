This lesson: i1 "What a matrix is" — A matrix is a grid of numbers, read row then column; times a column of amounts, it mixes its own columns, each row one equation. [concept]
Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 Moving the whole plane — A 2 by 2 matrix moves every point of the plane at once, and its two columns are where the arrows i and j land.
- i3 One move after another — Multiplying two matrices makes one move: the right-hand one first, then the left, so swapping their order usually gives a different move.
- i4 How areas change: the determinant — The determinant, ad − bc, is the number a 2 by 2 move scales every area by; negative means flipped, zero means squashed flat.
- i5 Undoing a move: the inverse — The inverse matrix plays a move backwards, so it solves a two-loop circuit's equations, and it exists only when the determinant is not zero.
- i6 Three by three: the determinant and the cross product — Expanding along the top row by cofactors, small 2 by 2 determinants with signs, gives a 3 by 3 determinant and the cross product's formula.
- i7 Elimination: three unknowns, three loops — Gaussian elimination's row moves step three equations, such as a three-loop circuit's, into a staircase solved from the bottom up.
