# Percentages: the course brief

Written 11 Oct 2026 for Dan, answering the course brief in tools/course/RUNBOOK.md section 0. Dan
asked for a short course: the core skills only. Sources and quotes are in `research-notes.md` beside
this file.

## The short version
**Five lessons**
1. What a percentage is
2. What percentage is it?
3. Up or down by a percentage
4. Percentage change
5. Back to the original

**The main picture:** a percentage bar: one bar for the whole (100%) with the amount underneath,
so a percentage and its amount always line up (Oak calls this a double number line).

**Questions for you**
1. Are these five the right core? (Lesson 5 is the one to cut if you want four.)
2. Fine to go straight to all five on the new settings, with no separate pilot? Fractions and
   Negative numbers set the style.

## 1. The overall topic
Percentages as "out of a hundred", and the four jobs they do: find a percentage of an amount, say
what percentage one amount is of another, raise or cut by a percentage, and work out a change or
an original. It builds on Fractions (fractions, decimals, a fraction of an amount) and Negative
numbers (a fall is a negative change). Compound interest, percentage points in depth, and
efficiency as physics are left for later courses.

## 2. The lessons, in the order they build
| # | Lesson | The skill Dan leaves with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What a percentage is | Read 15% as 15 out of 100, as 0.15 and as a fraction, and find a percentage of an amount (10%, 1%, then any %, by the calculator's × 0.15). Percentages over 100% fold in here. |
| 2 | What percentage is it? | Say what percentage one amount is of another (part ÷ whole × 100): a drop in volts, an efficiency, a tolerance. |
| 3 | Up or down by a percentage | Raise or cut in one step with a multiplier (+20% is × 1.2, −15% is × 0.85). |
| 4 | Percentage change | Change ÷ original × 100, always from the original; a fall is negative. One screen tells percent from percentage points. |
| 5 | Back to the original | Find the amount before a rise or cut by dividing by the multiplier; and why up 20% then down 20% doesn't get you back. |

Brilliant spends 21 lessons on this; the benchmark courses agree on the same core (research notes 1).
Efficiency, error and slip, a lesson of their own in the roadmap, fold into lessons 2 and 4 as
worked contexts instead.

## 3. Can it be taught mostly visually, through interaction?
Yes. Steps lessons, as before, minimal writing:
- **The percentage bar** carries every lesson: drag a marker along 0-100% and watch the amount move
  underneath; for a rise, the bar grows past 100%.
- **A 10 by 10 grid** opens lesson 1 only (100 squares, shade 15), to make "per hundred" visible.
- **The multiplier** shown as a stretch or squeeze of the bar (× 1.2, × 0.85).
- Colour as before: no red for a fall (red means a mistake); a fall is shown by direction.

## 4. What else
- **Benchmark.** Brilliant Percents (21 lessons, 5 levels) and Khan, BBC Bitesize, Oak Year 8 and
  NCETM all share this core; repeated change appears only in Brilliant and Bitesize.
- **Starting point.** Dan uses percentages daily (VAT, discounts, cable sizes and limits), so the
  course moves quickly through meaning and spends its time on the slips.
- **His aim.** The MCA General Engineering Science syllabus asks him to "Solve problems on ratio,
  proportion and percentage" and on "work, power, energy and efficiency".
- **Where it is used (each cited before a lesson uses it).** Voltage-drop limits (3% and 5% of
  230 V are 6.9 V and 11.5 V; the limits are confirmed only from IET forum posts, so no clause
  number is printed), resistor tolerance bands, motor efficiency on a nameplate, relative humidity,
  VAT at 20% (to re-confirm from GOV.UK).
- **The mistakes to catch, each with a puzzle and its own hint.**
  - 1: dividing by 10 instead of 100 (3% as 0.3); "to find 20%, divide by 20".
  - 2: dividing the wrong way round (whole ÷ part).
  - 3: adding the percentage as a number (+20% as + 20).
  - 4: working the change from the new amount instead of the original.
  - 5: taking the percentage off the new amount to go back (no publisher names this slip, so it is a
    check rather than a sourced mistake); up 20% then down 20% gives 96, not 100.
- **Size and cost.** 5 lessons of about 12 screens on the agreed settings: about 1M tokens a lesson
  measured on Negative numbers 5-9, so about 5M, plus about 0.5M for the course plan and research.
- **Gaps to close at build time.** The BS 7671 wording, the VAT 20% quote, and an HN maths unit line.
