    "text": "Multiplying two complex numbers works like multiplying out two brackets, with one extra step: wherever j × j appears, put −1. On the plane, the answer is the first arrow stretched and turned.\n\nStart with what you know. Times a positive number stretches an arrow: 2 × (2 + j1) = 4 + j2, both parts doubled, so it points the same way and is twice as long. Times −1 turns it through 180 degrees.[^4] Times j turns it a quarter turn anticlockwise.[^3] Here is why, part by part: j × (2 + j1) = j2 + j × j × 1 = −1 + j2. The real part 2 has become the j part j2, so it now points up, and the j part j1 has become −1, so it now points left. Each part has turned a quarter turn, so the whole arrow has. Its parts are the same sizes, 2 and 1, swapped, so by Pythagoras it is just as long.\n\nNow two brackets, as the four tiles in Quadratics: (3 + j2)(1 + j2). The tiles are 3 × 1 = 3, 3 × j2 = j6, j2 × 1 = j2 and j2 × j2 = j × j × 4 = −4. That last tile started as two j parts multiplied, but j × j = −1 makes it a real number, so it moves to the real pile with its sign changed. The rules are the ones for ordinary brackets, plus j² = −1.[^1] Real: 3 − 4 = −1. j: 6 + 2 = 8. The answer is −1 + j8. Take j × j as +1 and you get 7 + j8, a different arrow. This is the difference from adding: adding keeps real with real and j with j, while multiplying moves a piece across.\n\nWhy is the answer a stretch and turn? Split the second bracket: z × (c + jd) is c lots of z plus d lots of jz. The first is z stretched; the second is z turned a quarter turn and stretched, laid on its tip. Multiply 1 by c + jd the same way and you get c lots of 1 plus d lots of j, which is just c + jd. So whatever turn and stretch takes 1 to c + jd, the same turn and stretch takes z to the answer. For (2 + j1)(1 + j1), z + jz = (2 + j1) + (−1 + j2) = 1 + j3, while 1 goes to 1 + j1. By Pythagoras 1 + j1 is √2, about 1.41, long, and 2 + j1 grows from √5, about 2.24, to √10, about 3.16, which is 2.24 × 1.41: the same stretch. A later lesson turns this into a rule with lengths and angles.[^2] **Multiply out the brackets and put −1 for j × j;[^1] the answer is the first arrow stretched and turned.**"
