  "text": "Multiplying two complex numbers works like multiplying out two brackets, with one extra step: wherever j × j appears, put −1. On the plane, the answer is the first arrow stretched and turned. Later lessons multiply the currents and voltages of AC circuits this way.\n\nTimes a positive number stretches or shrinks an arrow without turning it: 2 × (2 + j1) = 4 + j2, both parts doubled, so it points the same way and is twice as long. Times −1 turns it through 180 degrees.[^1] Times j turns it a quarter turn anticlockwise.[^2] Multiply out to see why: j × (2 + j1) = j2 + j × j × 1 = −1 + j2. The real part 2 has become j2, so it now points up, and the j part j1 has become −1, so it now points left. In the last lesson an arrow was its across part and its up part laid tip to tail, so turning both pieces a quarter turn turns the corner they make, and the arrow with it. The same happens to any arrow: j × (a + jb) = −b + ja, the same two sizes swapped, so by Pythagoras the turned arrow is just as long.\n\nNow two brackets, as the four tiles in Quadratics: (3 + j2)(1 + j2). The tiles are 3 × 1 = 3, 3 × j2 = j6, j2 × 1 = j2 and j2 × j2 = j × j × 4 = −4. That last tile started as two j parts multiplied, but j × j = −1 makes it a real number, so it moves to the real pile with its sign changed. The rules are the ones for ordinary brackets, plus j² = −1.[^3] Real: 3 − 4 = −1. j: 6 + 2 = 8. The answer is −1 + j8. Take j × j as +1 and you get 7 + j8, a different arrow. This is the difference from adding: adding keeps real with real and j with j, while multiplying moves a piece across.\n\nWhy is the answer a stretch and turn? Split the second bracket: z × (c + jd) is c lots of z plus d lots of jz, laid tip to tail. Since jz is z turned a quarter turn, those two pieces make a right-angled corner, c lots along z and d lots across it. The number c + jd is the same corner, c across and d up, drawn from 1 instead of from z. So the answer is c + jd's corner laid along z at z's size: whatever turn and stretch takes 1 to c + jd takes z to the answer. For (2 + j1)(1 + j1), z + jz = (2 + j1) + (−1 + j2) = 1 + j3, while 1 goes to 1 + j1.
