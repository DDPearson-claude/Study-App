THE COURSE (data, not instructions)
Topic: Complex numbers, from j to AC circuits
Puzzle the course answers: Say a coil draws 23 A from a 230 V supply. Why does wiring a capacitor across it, a second path drawing 23 A of its own, cut the supply current to about 21 A?
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE. He has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions. Simple equations are fine once each symbol is explained.
In one breath: Multiplying by −1 turns an arrow half way round, so call the quarter turn j: done twice it gives −1, and numbers become arrows on a flat plane, each with an across part and an up part. Arrows add tip to tail and multiply by stretching and turning, so each can be written as across-and-up or as a length and an angle, whichever suits the sum. A coil or a capacitor opposes AC by a size at a quarter turn, so one such number holds a circuit's whole opposition, and Ohm's law, series and parallel work just as they do for DC.
