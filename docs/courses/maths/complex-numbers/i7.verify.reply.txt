The difference is direction: on the complex plane the resistor's opposition lies along the real axis, the coil's points straight up and the capacitor's points straight down.[^1] Laid tip to tail, they make one complex number, Z = R + jX, called the [[impedance]]: the AC version of resistance, still in ohms.[^3][^4]\n\nWhy the quarter turns? A resistor's voltage rises and falls in step with its current, so its opposition has no j part: 8 Ω is 8 + j0.[^5] A coil is different. Remember from Calculus fundamentals that a coil's voltage follows how fast its current changes, v = L di/dt. The current wave changes fastest as it crosses zero and stops changing for an instant at its peak. So the coil's voltage is biggest as the current crosses zero, and zero as the current peaks: its wave is the current's wave a quarter cycle earlier. Draw each wave as its turning arrow from Trigonometry. The arrows turn anticlockwise, so the voltage's arrow, a quarter cycle ahead, is the current's arrow turned a quarter turn anticlockwise and resized. The first lesson showed that a quarter turn anticlockwise is multiplying by j. So the coil turns its current into its voltage by multiplying by j and by its size in ohms, X_L, and its opposition points up: +jX_L. A capacitor is the other way round. Its current follows how fast its voltage changes, i = C dv/dt, so its current peaks a quarter cycle before its voltage. Its voltage's arrow is the current's arrow turned a quarter turn back, clockwise, which is multiplying by −j, so its opposition points down: −jX_C. The size, X_L or X_C in ohms, is called [[reactance]]. A calculator's sum gives only that size; you put on the +j or −j from the kind of part.\n\nThe sizes depend on the frequency f. Double f and the current wave climbs twice as steeply, so a coil's voltage doubles for the same current: its reactance is X_L = 2πfL, growing in step with f.[^6] For a capacitor, a voltage wave twice as steep drives twice the current, so its opposition halves: X_C = 1 ÷ (2πfC),[^7] using ω = 2πf from Trigonometry, with L in henries and C in farads. At very high frequencies a capacitor's reactance tends to zero, and it barely holds back the current at all.[^8]\n\nWith no coil or capacitor, X is 0 and Z is just R, your DC resistance.
