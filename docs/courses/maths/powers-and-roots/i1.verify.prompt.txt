This lesson: i1 "Powers and the three laws" — A power counts copies of the base multiplied together, so with one base 9³ × 9⁵ = 9⁸, 8⁵ ÷ 8³ = 8² and (a⁴)³ = a¹². [concept]
Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 Zero and negative powers — Each step down in power divides by the base, so a negative power means one over, never a negative number: 4⁻² is 1/16.
- i3 Roots and surds — A square root is the side of a square of that area, and leaving it as a surd, such as √3 m, keeps it exact instead of rounded.
- i4 Fractional powers — A fractional power is a root, with the bottom number the root and the top the power, so 8^(1/3) = 2 and 27^(5/3) = 243.
- i5 Powers of ten and prefixes — Multiplying by a power of ten slides the digits, and each multiple of 3 has a prefix, so 0.0047 A is 4.7 × 10⁻³ A, or 4.7 mA.
- i6 Units with powers — A power on a unit takes its prefix too, so 1 mm² = (10⁻³ m)² = 10⁻⁶ m² and 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³.
- i7 Powers in formulas — When one quantity changes by a factor, the result changes by that factor raised to the formula's power, as in P = I²R and the inverse square law.
