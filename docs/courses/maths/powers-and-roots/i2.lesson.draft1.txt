Start from the tiles of lesson 1. 4³ is three 4s multiplied, 4 × 4 × 4 = 64. Take one 4 out of the multiplying and you have 4² = 16, because taking out a factor of 4 divides by 4. So walking down the staircase of powers, 4³, 4², 4¹, gives 64, 16, 4, dividing by 4 at every step.[^1]\n\nNow keep walking, dividing by 4 each time.[^1] 4¹ is 4, and 4 ÷ 4 = 1, so 4⁰ = 1, one whole square. The dividing law from lesson 1 agrees: 4² ÷ 4² is 16 ÷ 16 = 1, and taking the powers away gives 4²⁻² = 4⁰. So any base to the power of zero equals 1,[^2] as long as the base is not zero, because the step down divides by the base and you cannot divide by zero. One more step: 1 ÷ 4 = 1/4, so 4⁻¹ = 1/4. The next is 1/4 ÷ 4 = 1/16, so 4⁻² = 1/16. Since 16 is 4², that is 1 over 4². This is the rule: a negative power gives the [[reciprocal]], one over the number with the matching positive power.[^3] The dividing law agrees again: 4² ÷ 4⁴ is 16/256 = 1/16, and 2 − 4 = −2.\n\nDividing a positive amount by 4 makes it smaller but never takes it below nothing, so the minus in the power does not make the answer negative.[^4] It is a common slip to think it does.[^4] A negative base, as in (−2)⁻¹ = −1/2, can give a negative answer, but that minus comes from the base, not the power.
