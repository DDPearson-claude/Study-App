    "text": "A fraction as a power means a root. The bottom number of the fraction says which root to take and the top number says what power to raise it to, so 8^(1/3) is the cube root of 8, which is 2, and 27^(5/3) is the cube root of 27, which is 3, raised to the power 5, which is 243.\n\nStart from lesson 1's adding law. On the power ladder, rungs 1, 8, 64 and 512 are 8⁰, 8¹, 8² and 8³, and each step up multiplies by 8. Now cut one step into three equal small steps. Each small step is a third of a power, 8^(1/3), and three of them make one whole step, because multiplying adds the powers: ⅓ + ⅓ + ⅓ = 1, so 8^(1/3) × 8^(1/3) × 8^(1/3) = 8¹ = 8.[^4] So 8^(1/3) is the number that, used three times in a multiplication, makes 8. That is 2, because 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, so 8^(1/3) = ∛8 = 2, the cube root of lesson 3. The power-of-a-power law agrees: (8^(1/3))³ = 8^(⅓ × 3) = 8¹, so a power that is a fraction has to be a root.[^6][^1] Cut a step into two instead and 4^(1/2) × 4^(1/2) = 4¹, so 4^(1/2) = √4 = 2. Cut it into four and you get the [[fourth root]]: 16^(1/4) = 2, because 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16. The bottom of the fraction counts the equal small steps in one whole step, so it is the root.\n\nThe top number counts the small steps you climb. 8^(2/3) is two small steps of × 2, so 8^(2/3) = (∛8)² = 4. A power like this is a [[fractional power]]: the numerator tells you the power and the denominator tells you the root.[^2] 27^(5/3) is five small steps of × 3, so it is 3⁵ = 243. You can take the power first instead: 27⁵ = 14,348,907, and its cube root is 243 as well, because both ways multiply the powers to 5/3.[^6] Root first keeps the numbers small. A minus still means one over, as in lesson 2: 8^(−2/3) = 1/8^(2/3) = 1/4, a small amount, not a negative one.[^9] And the laws still add powers when you multiply: x^(1/2) × x^(3/2) = x^(1/2 + 3/2) = x². Two slips are worth seeing fail. 8^(1/3) is not 8 × ⅓ ≈ 2.67, because three steps of × 2.67 come to about 19, not 8. And the root goes on the bottom: 64^(2/3) = (∛64)² = 16, while (√64)³ = 512 is 64^(3/2). This lesson keeps to positive numbers; Exponentials and logarithms takes powers further.\n\n**A fractional power is a root: the bottom number of the fraction is the root and the top number is the power, so 8^(1/3) = 2 and 27^(5/3) = 3⁵ = 243.[^1][^2]**"
