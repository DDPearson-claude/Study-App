Multiply the numbers and the units separately. The numbers are powers of the same base, so their indices add,[^4] and −3 add −3 makes −6, giving 10⁻⁶. The units multiply too, and m × m is m². So 1 mm² = 10⁻⁶ m², a millionth of a square metre.\n\nZoom into a square metre and you can count it. Along one side 1000 mm fit, so the [[length factor]], how many small units fit along one metre, is 1000. The square fills with 1000 rows of 1000 small squares, so 1 m² = 1,000,000 mm², which is 10⁶ mm². You met the same count for 1 cm² = 100 mm² in Geometry fundamentals, 10 rows of 10; the zoom does it for any prefix. The length factor counts in two directions, across and down, so an area conversion squares it, and a cube adds a third direction, depth, so a volume conversion cubes it.[^5] Centi is 10⁻²,[^2] so 1 cm³ = (10⁻² m)³ = 10⁻⁶ m³: 100 cm along each edge of a cubic metre, and 100 × 100 × 100 = 1,000,000 small cubes.\n\nTwo easy slips come from using the length factor only once.
