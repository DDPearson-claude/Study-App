  "text": "When a result is one quantity raised to a power, multiplied or divided by things that stay the same, changing that quantity by some factor changes the result by the factor raised to the same power. So twice the current through the same resistor gives 2² = 4 times the heat, because the heat goes with the square of the current.[^1]\n\nHere is why. The heat per second in a wire goes with its resistance times the square of the current,[^1] which you write as P = I²R. I² is I × I, the current used twice. Double the current and both copies double, so their product grows by 2 × 2 = 4. On the screen the old heat is one tile, and the new heat is a square whose two sides are the two doubled copies, two tiles by two. Three times the current makes both copies three times as big, so the heat grows by 3² = 9, not 3 × 2 = 6. A whole-number power says how many copies of the factor multiply together. Textbooks add that a centrifugal pump with no height to lift against takes power that goes with the cube of its speed, the speed used three times. So twice the speed takes 2³ = 8 times the power, and 0.8 of the speed takes 0.8³ = 0.512 of it, about half.\n\nAn [[inverse square law]] puts the quantity underneath, as 1 ÷ d², which Zero and negative powers taught you to write as d⁻².
