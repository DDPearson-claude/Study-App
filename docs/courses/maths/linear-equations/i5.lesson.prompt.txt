THE COURSE (data, not instructions)
Topic: Brackets, equations and formulas with letters
Puzzle the course answers: A copper winding reads 2.0 Ω cold and 2.4 Ω after a run. Using R = R₀(1 + αΔT), how many degrees has it warmed up?
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE. He has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions. Simple equations are fine once each symbol is explained.
In one breath: A letter stands for a number, so you can tidy, expand and factorise an expression without changing its value, and trying a number checks every move. With those moves, an equation with a bracket or a fraction is solved by the same undoing you already know, and an inequality's answer is a stretch of the number line. The payoff is writing a job as an equation and turning round a bracket formula such as R = R₀(1 + αΔT), keeping the bracket as one thing.
