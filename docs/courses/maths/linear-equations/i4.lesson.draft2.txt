    "text": "An equation with a bracket is solved by the same undoing you already know. Work out what was done to x, in order, and undo it from the last step back. In 3(x − 2) = 27, x has 2 taken away, and then the whole of x − 2 is multiplied by 3, because the bracket keeps it together. As a flow diagram that is x, then − 2, then × 3, giving 27. Undo it from the right: 27 ÷ 3 = 9, so x − 2 = 9, and 9 + 2 = 11, so x = 11. You can check the solution by putting it back into the equation:[^2] 3(11 − 2) = 3 × 9 = 27.\n\nExpanding first works too. Expanding does not change the value of an expression,[^7] so 3(x − 2) = 27 is the same equation as 3x − 6 = 27. Add 6 to both sides to get 3x = 33, then divide by 3 to get x = 11 again. Which route is quicker depends on the equation's structure, so look at it before you start.[^1] Dividing first suits 3(x − 2) = 27, as 27 divides by 3. In 3(x + 4) + 2x = 37 the 3 multiplies only the bracket, not the 2x, so dividing both sides by 3 would turn the 2x into 2x ÷ 3. Expand first instead and collect like terms: 3x + 12 + 2x is 5x + 12. Then 5x + 12 = 37, 5x = 25 and x = 5. A minus before a bracket takes away the whole bracket. It works as a factor of −1, so it is multiplied into every term inside.[^8] 8x − (6x − 5) = 21 becomes 8x − 6x + 5 = 21, which is 2x + 5 = 21, so x = 8. Keeping the −5 gives 2x − 5 = 21 and x = 13, and the check shows the slip: 8 × 13 − (78 − 5) = 31, not 21.\n\nA fraction bar holds everything above it together, the way a bracket does, so in (x + 4) ÷ 3 = 5 the whole of x + 4 is divided by 3. The flow diagram is x, then + 4, then ÷ 3, giving 5. Undo it from the right: 5 × 3 = 15, and 15 − 4 = 11, so x = 11. With two fractions, as in x ÷ 2 + x ÷ 3 = 10, multiply both sides by the [[least common denominator]], the smallest number both bottom numbers divide into.[^3] Here that is 6. 6 × (x ÷ 2) = 3x, 6 × (x ÷ 3) = 2x, and the 10 is multiplied too, making 60. So 3x + 2x = 60, 5x = 60 and x = 12. Check: 12 ÷ 2 + 12 ÷ 3 = 6 + 4 = 10.\n\nSometimes the x terms cancel. 2(2x + 3) = 4x expands to 4x + 6 = 4x, and taking 4x from both sides leaves 6 = 0, which is never true. Whatever x is, the left side is 6 more than the right, so no number works and the equation has [[no solution]]. **Undo what was done to x from the last step back, treating a bracket or a fraction bar as one thing, or expand first and collect like terms, then put the answer back in to check it.[^2]**"

    "text": "- Look at the structure first.[^1] If a bracket is the whole of one side and the other side divides by the number outside it, divide first.\n- Otherwise expand every bracket. After a minus, change every sign inside.\n- Collect like terms on each side.\n- Undo the steps on both sides until x is on its own.\n- With fractions, multiply every term on both sides by the least common denominator first.[^3]\n- Put your answer back into the original equation.[^2]\n\n**Rule of thumb:** A fraction bar works like a bracket. Everything above it is divided together.\n\n**Worked example:** 2(x + 5) − (x − 4) = 20. Expand: 2x + 10 − x + 4 = 20. Collect like terms: x + 14 = 20, so x = 6. Check: 2(6 + 5) − (6 − 4) = 22 − 2 = 20.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Multiplying only the first term, so 2(x − 5) = 14 becomes 2x − 5 = 14 and x = 9.5. Check: 2(9.5 − 5) makes 9, not 14.\n- Keeping the last sign after a minus, so 8x − (6x − 5) = 21 gives x = 13. Check: it makes 31, not 21.\n- Undoing in the wrong order, so (x − 2) ÷ 4 = 3 gives 3 + 2 = 5, then 20.
