   "now": "A bracket keeps what is inside it together as one number, so 3(x + 5) means 3 lots of the whole of x + 5. [[Expanding]] the bracket writes it without the bracket, and the number outside, the [[factor]], multiplies each term inside, not just the first.[^1] Picture x + 5 as an x-bar with 5 squares on its end. Stack three of them and you have a rectangle 3 high and x + 5 long. Its left part is 3 rows of x, which is 3x, and its right part is 3 rows of 5, which is 15. So 3(x + 5) = 3x + 15. Expanding brackets does not change the value of an expression, as it is two ways of writing the same one,[^2] so trying a number checks it. At x = 4, 3(4 + 5) = 3 × 9 = 27, and 3x + 15 = 12 + 15 = 27.\n\nAn easy slip is to multiply the x and forget the 5, writing 3x + 5. The rectangle shows what that loses, as its right part holds 15 squares, not 5. At x = 2, 3x + 5 makes 11, while 3(x + 5) makes 21. A minus inside works the same way. 5(x − 2) is five x-bars, each with a dashed 2 cut off its end, so 5x with 10 cut off, 5x − 10. A letter outside works the same way too. R = R₀(1 + αΔT) gives the resistance R after a temperature change ΔT, where R₀ is the original resistance.[^3] Its bracket expands the same way,[^1] to R = R₀ + R₀αΔT. The first part, R₀ × 1, is the original resistance. The second part, R₀αΔT, is the change the temperature change makes, extra resistance when a metal warms. For a winding of 2.0 Ω cold and 2.4 Ω after a run, that second part is 2.4 − 2.0 = 0.4 Ω.\n\nA minus in front of a bracket takes away the whole of what is inside. 6x − 5 is 5 less than 6x, so taking it away takes away 5 less than taking away 6x would. That is −6x, then 5 back, so −(6x − 5) = −6x + 5. Each term inside has been multiplied by −1, so every sign inside has changed. The rectangle cannot show this, because an area cannot be below zero, so try a number instead. At x = 2, 6x − 5 makes 7, so −(6x − 5) makes −7, and −6x + 5 makes −12 + 5 = −7. The slip −6x − 5 makes −17. In the same way x − (a − b) is x − a + b, because the minus must be multiplied into both terms in the bracket.[^4]\n\n**Expanding multiplies the factor outside by every term inside the bracket,[^1] so 3(x + 5) = 3x + 15 and R₀(1 + αΔT) = R₀ + R₀αΔT. A minus in front changes every sign inside,[^4] and trying a number checks the result.**"
