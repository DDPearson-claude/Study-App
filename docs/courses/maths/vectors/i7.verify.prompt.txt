  "text": "A phasor is drawn like a vector and adds like one. But its angle marks a moment in time, not a direction in space. And because it is a complex number, it can be divided.\n\nStart with the force arrow. A 3 kN pull on a ring points somewhere real. Its angle says which way the rope runs. Now take an arrow that turns round its tail once every cycle. Its height at each instant traces the sine wave of a voltage. Freeze it and you have the fixed arrow you write as r∠θ. On a 50 Hz supply one turn takes 20 ms. So a current 90° behind the voltage peaks a quarter turn later, 5 ms after it. The angle measures that delay. Nothing in the cable points that way.\n\nTurn two arrows together and their nose-to-tail sum turns with them. So the sum's height is the two heights added, at every instant. That is why phasors add nose to tail, exactly as forces do.[^1] 6 A in step with the voltage and 8 A a quarter turn behind give √(6² + 8²) = 10 A, not 14 A. This needs every arrow to turn at the same speed. A 150 Hz arrow turns three times as fast as a 50 Hz one. So the angle between them changes from one instant to the next, and they cannot share a diagram.[^2] Being a number gives a phasor one thing a vector lacks. You can divide phasors, but you cannot divide one vector by another.[^3] Divide the sizes and take away the angles. So 230∠0° V ÷ 10∠−30° A gives an impedance of 23∠30° Ω.
