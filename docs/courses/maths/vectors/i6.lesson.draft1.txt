    "text": "The cross product of two vectors is a new vector. Its size is |a||b| sin θ, and it points square to both. A spanner's moment and the force on a motor's wire are both cross products.\n\nYou already work out a moment as the push times its distance from the bolt, with the push square to the handle. Now push at a slant. Call the arrow from the bolt to your hand r, and your push F. Let θ be the angle between them. Only the part of F square to the handle turns the bolt. The part along the handle just pulls on the bolt. The square part is |F| sin θ, the side opposite the angle. So the moment's size is |r||F| sin θ.[^1] That is also the area of the parallelogram r and F span. Its base is |r| and its height is |F| sin θ. A 150 N push, 0.3 m out and square on, gives 0.3 × 150 = 45 N m. At 30° to the handle it gives 45 × 0.5 = 22.5 N m. Along the handle θ is 0°, and nothing turns. Cos would give the part along the handle, which turns nothing.\n\nThe moment also has a direction. The bolt turns about its own line, so the arrow lies along the bolt. The [[right-hand rule]] finds which way. Point your fingers along r and curl them toward F. Your thumb then points along the [[cross product]], r × F.[^2] On a standard right-hand thread, the arrow points out of the nut when you undo it and in when you do it up. Swap the order and the arrow flips, so F × r = −(r × F). The axes follow the same rule: i × j = k, j × k = i and k × i = j. Turn any pair round and you get the minus, so j × i = −k. An arrow crossed with itself gives nothing, since sin 0° = 0. The dot product is a plain number, but the cross product is an arrow.[^4] Its size alone is only half the answer.\n\nThe force on a motor's wire is a cross product too. A wire of length l carries a current I across a magnetic field B. The force on it has size IlB sin θ, where θ is the angle between the wire and the field.[^6] It acts square to both the current and the field.[^5] Point your fingers along the current and curl them toward the field, and your thumb gives the force. 10 A in 0.2 m of wire, square across 0.8 T, feels 10 × 0.2 × 0.8 = 1.6 N. Line the wire up with the field and sin θ is 0, so there is no force.\n\n**The cross product, |a||b| sin θ, is an arrow square to both vectors, pointed by the right-hand rule.**[^1]"

    "text": "- Draw r from the pivot to where the force acts. Your thumb gives the answer.\n- Check that the answer is square to both arrows. Then write it with its direction, such as 45k N m.\n\n**Rule of thumb:** Square on gives the most. Lined up gives nothing. Swap the order and the arrow flips.\n\n**Worked example:** Say you push 200 N on a spanner, 0.45 m from the bolt, at 60° to the handle. Square on, the moment would be 0.45 × 200 = 90 N m. At 60° it is 90 × sin 60° = 77.94 N m. On a motor, a wire carrying 20 A with 0.3 m square across a 0.6 T field feels 20 × 0.3 × 0.6 = 3.6 N.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Giving only the size. A moment or a force needs its direction too.\n- Using cos in place of sin.
