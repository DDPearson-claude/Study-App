Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 Components: i, j and k — Split a vector into parts along the axes, written 3i + 4j, with cos on the side next to the given angle, and k for height.
- i3 Adding: the resultant and the equilibrant — Vectors add nose to tail or part by part, giving the resultant, and the equilibrant is the equal and opposite force that balances it.
- i4 Taking away: displacement and relative velocity — Taking a vector away means adding its reverse, so a displacement is end minus start, and another vessel's relative velocity is its velocity minus yours.
- i5 The dot product: how much lies along — The dot product, |a||b| cos θ or a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃, is a plain number saying how much of one vector lies along another, as in work.
- i6 The cross product: turning and the motor force — The cross product has size |r||F| sin θ and points square to both arrows by the right-hand rule, giving a spanner's moment and a motor's force.
- i7 Vectors and phasors — A phasor adds and takes away like a vector, but its angle is a shift in time, not a direction in space, and it can be divided.
