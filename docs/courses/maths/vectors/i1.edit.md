# i1 "What a vector is": plain-English edit

The facts, numbers, units, formulas and [^n] citations are all unchanged. In every field, the numbers and citation marks are the same before and after; the only exception is a repeated "2a" that names the subject of a split sentence. Both files were edited the same way: `i1.lesson.verified.json` and `i1.lesson.final.json` now hold the same explain, analogy, practice, brief, whatAmILookingAt and ignores text. `validate.mjs lesson ... --final` passes on both.

About 46 sentences were rewritten. Most were split so that each one carries one idea. Others were turned to lead with the point.

## Five before-and-after examples

1. **Explain, scalars**
   - Before: "A quantity with a size and no direction, such as mass, energy or temperature, is a [[scalar]]."
   - After: "A [[scalar]] has a size but no direction. Mass, energy and temperature are scalars."

2. **Explain, unit vector**
   - Before: "That is its [[unit vector]], 0.6 across and 0.8 up, and it holds the direction alone."
   - After: "That is its [[unit vector]]: 0.6 across and 0.8 up. It holds the direction alone."

3. **Explain, three-phase example**
   - Before: "Because direction is part of every vector, two of them cannot be added just by adding their sizes.[^5] Each line current has its own phase angle too, which is why 10 A, 10 A and 4 A on a three-phase board need not put 24 A on the neutral."
   - After: "Direction is part of every vector. So you cannot add two vectors just by adding their sizes.[^5] Each line current has its own phase angle too. That is why 10 A, 10 A and 4 A on a three-phase board need not put 24 A on the neutral."

4. **whatAmILookingAt, the readout**
   - Before: "A readout beside it gives its two moves, how many squares across and how many up from tail to tip, with left and down as minus, and its size, the arrow's length."
   - After: "A readout beside it shows the arrow's two moves: squares across and squares up, from tail to tip. Left and down count as minus. The readout also shows the size, which is the arrow's length."

5. **Analogy, where it breaks**
   - Before: "And a real force pushes at one place on a body, which matters when it turns it, though the vector itself is still only its size and direction."
   - After: "Also, a real force pushes at one place on a body. That place matters when the force turns the body. The vector itself is still only its size and direction."
