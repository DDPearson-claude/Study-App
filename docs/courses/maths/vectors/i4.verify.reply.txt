   "severity": "fix",
   "now": "- Say the order first. The other vessel relative to you is the other's velocity minus yours. The move from A to B is b − a.\n- Write both vectors as i and j parts. Measure both from the same thing, both over the ground or both through the water.\n- Turn round the one you take away. Flip the sign of every part.\n- Add part by part.\n- Sketch it tail to tail. The answer runs from the nose of the one you took away to the nose of the other.\n- Check it by adding your answer back on. You should get the first vector again.\n\n**Rule of thumb:** End minus start, and theirs minus yours. Steam meeting a moving turbine blade works the same way. The blade meets the steam's velocity minus its own.\n\n**Worked example:** Say you want to cross a 200 m channel straight over. Your boat makes 2.5 m/s through the water. The stream runs at 1.5 m/s. Aim upstream, so your upstream part is 1.5 m/s. Your across part is √(2.5² − 1.5²) = 2 m/s. The heading is sin⁻¹(1.5 ÷ 2.5) = 36.87° upstream of straight across. The crossing takes 200 ÷ 2 = 100 s. Now say you pointed straight across instead. You would cross in 200 ÷ 2.5 = 80 s. In that time the stream carries you 1.5 × 80 = 120 m downstream.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Adding when you mean to take away. Two vessels head-on at 12 knots each close at 24 knots, not 0.\n- Getting the order backwards. b − a and a − b are the same size and point opposite ways.\n- Flipping the sign of only some of its parts.\n- Pointing straight at the target. The stream carries you downstream of it.\n- Mixing frames. Take one velocity over the ground and the other through the water, and the answer means nothing.\n- Calling points collinear because two steps are parallel. The steps must share a point too."
