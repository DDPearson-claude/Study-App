    "text": "A [[vector]] is a size with a direction.[^1] A force, a velocity and an acceleration are all vectors. You draw one as an arrow. Its length is the size and the way it points is the direction. A quantity with a size and no direction, such as mass, energy or temperature, is a [[scalar]]. A pump's 500 kg mass is a scalar. Its weight is a vector, because weight is a force and it pulls down.\n\nOn a grid, the arrow called a runs 3 squares across and 4 up from its tail to its tip. Its size is its length, found by Pythagoras: √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5. That is written |a| = 5. Nothing else belongs to the arrow, so where it is drawn is not part of it. Two arrows with the same size and direction are the same vector, wherever they sit.[^2] Slide a anywhere on the grid and it still runs 3 across and 4 up. An arrow from point A to point B is the vector AB. Going backwards along a vector makes it negative,[^3] so BA = −AB. Printed, a vector's letter is bold, and by hand it is underlined.\n\nA number in front scales a vector by multiplying both its moves. 2a runs 6 across and 8 up, the same way and twice as long, so its size is 10. A negative number also turns the arrow round, because making a vector negative reverses its direction.[^4] So −2a runs 6 left and 8 down. Its size is still 10, since a length is never negative. Every multiple of a lies along the same line, so they are all parallel. Dividing a by its own size leaves an arrow of length 1 the same way, its [[unit vector]]. a ÷ 5 runs 0.6 across and 0.8 up.\n\nBecause direction is part of every vector, two of them cannot be added just by adding their sizes.[^5] Line currents carry an angle too, so 10 A, 10 A and 4 A on a three-phase board need not put 24 A on the neutral.\n\n**A vector is only its size and its direction, so its arrow can be drawn anywhere,[^2] and a number in front stretches or shrinks it and, if negative, turns it round.**"

    "text": "- Read the moves from tail to tip: across first, then up. Left and down are minus.\n- Find the size by Pythagoras on the two moves.\n- To compare two vectors, compare their moves, never where they sit.\n- To reverse a vector, change the sign of both moves. To scale it, multiply both moves by the number.\n- For the unit vector, divide both moves by the size, then check its size comes out as 1.\n\n**Rule of thumb:** Same moves, same vector. A minus in front turns it round.\n\n**Worked example:** A boat's velocity v is 3 m/s east and 4 m/s north. Its speed is the size, √(3² + 4²) = 5 m/s. At half speed on the same heading, ½v is 1.5 m/s east and 2 m/s north, a speed of 2.5 m/s. Turned right round at the same speed, −v is 3 m/s west and 4 m/s south, and its speed is still 5 m/s. The unit vector, v ÷ 5, is 0.6 east and 0.8 north: the heading with the speed taken out.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Treating a moved arrow as a new vector. If the moves match, it is the same vector.\n- Writing BA = AB. They are the same size but point opposite ways, so BA = −AB.\n- Forgetting that a minus number turns the arrow round. −2a points the other way from a.\n- Giving a negative size. −2a has size 10, not −10.\n- Making a unit vector by dividing by the moves added together. For 6 across and 8 up, dividing by 14 gives a size near 0.71, not 1. Divide by the size, 10."
