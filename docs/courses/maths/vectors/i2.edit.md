## Five before-and-after examples

1. **Explain, going back to the size**
   - Before: "Going back, the size is Pythagoras on the parts, not the parts added."
   - After: "To go back from the parts to the size, use Pythagoras. Never just add the parts."

2. **Explain, the sign check on tan⁻¹**
   - Before: "For −3i + 4j a calculator's tan⁻¹(4 ÷ −3) gives −53.13°, which points right and down."
   - After: "Take −3i + 4j. A calculator's tan⁻¹(4 ÷ −3) gives −53.13°. That points right and down."

3. **Analogy, the power triangle**
   - Before: "The reactive power is the side opposite: 10 × 0.6 = 6 kvar, since sin φ is 0.6 when cos φ is 0.8. Pythagoras takes you back to 10 kVA, and 8 + 6 = 14 means nothing."
   - After: "When cos φ is 0.8, sin φ is 0.6. The reactive power is the side opposite, so it is 10 × 0.6 = 6 kvar. Pythagoras takes you back to 10 kVA. Adding them, 8 + 6 = 14, means nothing."

4. **whatAmILookingAt, the bearing switch**
   - Before: "A switch moves the angle marker from off the x-axis to a bearing, measured clockwise from north. The arrow and its parts stay put. The angle now sits next to the north side, so the cos label jumps to that side."
   - After: "A switch moves the angle marker from off the x-axis to a bearing. A bearing is measured clockwise from north. The arrow and its parts stay put. But the angle now sits next to the north side, so the cos label jumps to that side."

5. **Practice, signs and a common mistake**
   - Before: "Give each part its sign. Left and down are minus, and so are west and south." / "Writing the parts the wrong way round. 4i − 2j is 4 across and 2 down, not −2i + 4j."
   - After: "Give each part its sign. Left, down, west and south are all minus." / "Writing the parts the wrong way round. 4i − 2j is 4 across and 2 down. It is not −2i + 4j."
