    "text": "Taking a vector away means adding its reverse. To work out a − b, turn b round and add it nose to tail.[^1] It works because a − b is the arrow you add to b to reach a. Take a = 5i + 2j and b = 1i + 3j. Turned round, b is −1i − 3j. Across, 5 − 1 = 4. Up, 2 − 3 = −1. So a − b = 4i − 1j, and the parts take away separately, just as they added.[^10] Check it: b + (4i − 1j) = 5i + 2j, which is a. Draw a and b from one tail and the same arrow runs from b's nose to a's nose. Adding instead gives 6i + 5j, which points somewhere else entirely.\n\nA move from one point to another is a [[displacement]]. Call the arrow from the origin to buoy A, a, and the one to buoy B, b. Go out along a, then make the move AB, and you land on B. So a + AB = b, which makes AB = b − a: where you end minus where you started.[^2] Say A sits at 1i + 2j km and B at 6i + 5j km. Then AB = 5i + 3j km, and BA is −5i − 3j km, the same move backwards. Taking away also tells you when three points lie in one line. Take A, B and C at 1i + 1j, 3i + 2j and 7i + 4j. AB = 2i + 1j and BC = 4i + 2j, which is 2 × AB, so the two steps point the same way. They share B, so they lie on one line, and the points are [[collinear]].\n\nVelocities take away the same way. A boat's heading and speed through the water is one velocity, and the stream moves the water. Each second the boat makes its own step through the water while the water carries it on by the stream's step, so over the ground its velocity is the two added. Head straight across at 4 m/s in a 3 m/s stream and you make √(4² + 3²) = 5 m/s over the ground, 36.87° downstream of your heading. To land opposite, aim upstream until your upstream part is 3 m/s and cancels the stream. Your across part is then √(4² − 3²) = 2.65 m/s, at a heading 48.59° upstream of straight across. Now look from your own boat. To you it sits still, so you see your own velocity taken away from everything. The bank slides past at minus your velocity, and another vessel moves at its velocity minus yours, its [[relative velocity]].[^3] Say you make 6i knots, due east, and a second vessel 8j knots, due north. Seen from you it moves at 8j − 6i = −6i + 8j, which is √(6² + 8²) = 10 knots.\n\n**Taking a vector away means adding its reverse. A displacement is end minus start, and a relative velocity is the other's velocity minus yours.**[^3]"

The move from A to B is b − a.\n- Write both vectors as i and j parts, measured from the same thing: both over the ground, or both through the water.\n- Turn round the one you take away by flipping both its signs.\n- Add part by part.\n- Sketch it tail to tail. The answer runs from the nose of the one you took away to the nose of the other.\n- Check it by adding your answer back on. You should get the first vector again.\n\n**Rule of thumb:** End minus start, and theirs minus yours. The same sum works for steam on a moving turbine blade: what the blade meets is the steam's velocity minus the blade's.\n\n**Worked example:** Say you want to cross a 200 m channel straight over. Your boat makes 2.5 m/s through the water and the stream runs at 1.5 m/s. Aim upstream so your upstream part is 1.5 m/s. Your across part is √(2.5² − 1.5²) = 2 m/s, and the heading is sin⁻¹(1.5 ÷ 2.5) = 36.87° upstream of straight across. The crossing takes 200 ÷ 2 = 100 s. If you pointed straight across instead, you would cross in 200 ÷ 2.5 = 80 s and drift 1.5 × 80 = 120 m downstream.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Adding when you mean to take away.
