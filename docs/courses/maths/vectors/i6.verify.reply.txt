Now push at a slant. Call the arrow from the bolt to your hand r, and your push F. Let θ be the angle between them. Split F into two parts. The part along the handle runs on a line straight through the bolt. It has no distance from the bolt to turn about, so it turns nothing. Only the part square to the handle turns the bolt. That part is |F| sin θ, the side opposite the angle. So the moment's size is |r||F| sin θ.[^1] It is also the area of the parallelogram r and F span. Its base is |r| and its height is |F| sin θ. A 150 N push, 0.3 m out and square on, gives 0.3 × 150 = 45 N m. At 30° to the handle it gives 45 × 0.5 = 22.5 N m. Cos would give 45 × cos 30° = 38.97 N m. With the push along the handle, θ is 0° or 180°, and the moment is 0.\n\nThe moment also has a direction. r and F lie in the plane the spanner turns in. An arrow square to both must stand along the bolt. The [[right-hand rule]] says which way. Point your fingers along r and curl them toward F. Your thumb then points along the [[cross product]], r × F.[^2] Undoing a standard right-hand thread turns it anticlockwise, seen from your side. Curl your right fingers anticlockwise and your thumb points at you. So the arrow points out of the nut to undo it, and in to do it up. Swap the order and the arrow flips, so F × r = −(r × F).
