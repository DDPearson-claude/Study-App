Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Forces on one object balance when their sum is zero; with no net force an object stays at rest or keeps a constant velocity. [R1][R2]
- In a two-leg sling each leg tension has a vertical part that carries the load; the two vertical parts together must equal the weight, so each leg pulls harder than half the weight. [R3]
- The load in each sling leg increases as the angle between the legs increases, and low angles are called extremely hazardous. [R4][R5]
- The extra tension appears without any extra load being lifted, and the sling itself is no weaker: only the force in each leg grows. [R6][R7]
- Load angle factors show the rise: leg tension times 1.000 at 90 degrees, 1.155 at 60, 1.414 at 45 and 2.000 at 30, with the angle measured from horizontal (some calculators use vertical). [R8][R9]
- Work is force times the distance moved in the direction of the force, and one joule is the work done when one newton moves something one metre, so work is also newton-metres. [R10][R11]  (whole topic)
- A moment is measured in newton metres, force times perpendicular distance, the same unit product as work but a different quantity. [R12][R11]  (whole topic)
- Power converts to watts only with speed in radians per second, so a speed in rpm must be converted first. [R13]  (whole topic)

The two legs lean the same amount in opposite directions. So their sideways parts are equal and opposite, and cancel. They lift nothing. As the legs spread, each leg leans further over.
