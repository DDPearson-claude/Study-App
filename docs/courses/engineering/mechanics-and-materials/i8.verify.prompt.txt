Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Natural frequency is the frequency at which a system would oscillate with no driving or damping, and resonance occurs when the driving frequency equals it. [R1][R2]
- Away from resonance, as the driving frequency rises above the natural frequency the response gets smaller. [R3]
- Natural frequency can be changed through stiffness or mass: lower stiffness lowers it. [R4][R5]
- The rotor speed at which a rotating machine resonates is its critical speed, and machines are designed to run at speeds away from it. [R6][R7]
- Unbalance force grows with the square of rotating speed, and an unbalance can form a couple that rocks the axis and vibrates the rotor in its bearings. [R8][R9]
- One guideline for torsional critical speeds is to keep operating speed at least 10 percent clear of them where feasible. [R10]
- A rigid rotor can be balanced at two end planes, while a flexible rotor needs balancing in several planes. [R11]
- Misalignment is another source of machine vibration, noise and early bearing or shaft failure. [R12]
- A flywheel with a higher moment of inertia, meaning more mass or mass further from the centre, stores more energy at the same speed. [R13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Rotational power is torque times angular velocity, with the speed in radians per second. [R14]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A flywheel evens out the speed variations an engine's fluctuating torque would cause, as on a diesel generator (added for i6 at review). [R15]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For a gear train the gear ratio is the input speed divided by the output speed; for two gears it matches the teeth or radius ratio. [R16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A belt that does not slip moves at the same speed as the edge of both pulleys. [R17]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- When a gearbox reduces speed it raises torque in proportion, and a real gearbox loses some power to friction so output power is smaller than input. [R18]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Marine diesel engines commonly use a reduction gearbox so the propeller shaft turns slower than the engine. [R19]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Shaft misalignment causes vibration, noise and premature bearing, coupling or shaft failure. [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Shaft misalignment has no measurable effect on motor efficiency; its harm is vibration, heat and wear (for i7). [R20]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Work is force times the distance moved in the direction of the force, and one joule is the work done when one newton moves something one metre, so work is also newton-metres. [R21][R22]  (whole topic)
- A moment is measured in newton metres, force times perpendicular distance, the same unit product as work but a different quantity. [R23][R22]  (whole topic)
- Power converts to watts only with speed in radians per second, so a speed in rpm must be converted first. [R14]  (whole topic)

That outward pull points at the spot wherever it is, so it turns with the shaft. The speed counts twice.
