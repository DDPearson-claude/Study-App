Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- A flywheel with a higher moment of inertia, meaning more mass or mass further from the centre, stores more energy at the same speed. [R1]
- Rotational power is torque times angular velocity, with the speed in radians per second. [R2]
- A moment is the turning effect of a force about a pivot, and its size is force times the perpendicular distance from the pivot. [R3][R4][R5]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For a body to be balanced the clockwise moments must equal the anticlockwise moments about the pivot. [R6]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The moments balance about any point, so one can pick a support as the pivot; that support's own force then has no moment and drops out. [R7][R8][R9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A body keeps its motion at constant velocity unless a net external force acts, so constant velocity means zero net force, not a force pushing it along. [R10][R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Acceleration is proportional to the net external force in the same direction and, as the full law states, inversely proportional to mass, so a net force changes velocity. [R12][R13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Work is force times the distance moved in the direction of the force, and it measures the energy transferred. [R14][R15]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Power is work done per unit of time, so the same work done more slowly takes less power. [R16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- One joule is the work of one newton moving through one metre. [R17]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Rotational power is torque times angular velocity, and the speed must be in radians per second for the result in watts. [R2]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Work is force times the distance moved in the direction of the force, and one joule is the work done when one newton moves something one metre, so work is also newton-metres. [R14][R17]  (whole topic)
- A moment is measured in newton metres, force times perpendicular distance, the same unit product as work but a different quantity. [R4][R17]  (whole topic)
- Power converts to watts only with speed in radians per second, so a speed in rpm must be converted first. [R2]  (whole topic)
