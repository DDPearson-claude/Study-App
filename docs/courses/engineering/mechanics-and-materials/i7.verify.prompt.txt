Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- For a gear train the gear ratio is the input speed divided by the output speed; for two gears it matches the teeth or radius ratio. [R1]
- A belt that does not slip moves at the same speed as the edge of both pulleys. [R2]
- When a gearbox reduces speed it raises torque in proportion, and a real gearbox loses some power to friction so output power is smaller than input. [R3]
- Marine diesel engines commonly use a reduction gearbox so the propeller shaft turns slower than the engine. [R4]
- Shaft misalignment causes vibration, noise and premature bearing, coupling or shaft failure. [R5]
- Work is force times the distance moved in the direction of the force, and it measures the energy transferred. [R6][R7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Power is work done per unit of time, so the same work done more slowly takes less power. [R8]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- One joule is the work of one newton moving through one metre. [R9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Rotational power is torque times angular velocity, and the speed must be in radians per second for the result in watts. [R10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A flywheel with a higher moment of inertia, meaning more mass or mass further from the centre, stores more energy at the same speed. [R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Rotational power is torque times angular velocity, with the speed in radians per second. [R10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A flywheel evens out the speed variations an engine's fluctuating torque would cause, as on a diesel generator (added for i6 at review). [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Work is force times the distance moved in the direction of the force, and one joule is the work done when one newton moves something one metre, so work is also newton-metres. [R6][R9]  (whole topic)
- A moment is measured in newton metres, force times perpendicular distance, the same unit product as work but a different quantity. [R13][R9]  (whole topic)
- Power converts to watts only with speed in radians per second, so a speed in rpm must be converted first. [R10]  (whole topic)
