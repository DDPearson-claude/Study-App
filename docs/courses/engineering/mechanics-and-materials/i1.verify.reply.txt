Each force on it has a giver: something touching the part, or something acting from a distance, like the Earth on its weight. Take the crane hook. The crane wire pulls it up, and each sling leg pulls it down along its own line. The hook stays still, so those three pulls laid nose to tail close a triangle.\n\nNow the sling. Each leg pulls along its own line, but only its vertical part holds the load up.[^4] With the load's weight centred between them, two legs share it equally, so each vertical part is half the load's weight. The two legs lean the same amount in opposite directions. So their sideways parts are equal and opposite, and cancel. They lift nothing. As the legs spread, each leg leans further over. The same pull then has a smaller vertical part, so the pull must grow to keep its half of the load up. Each leg now pulls 9.81 kN, the whole load, and 8.50 kN of that pull is sideways. It is the ring you balanced in Vectors: two equal pulls 120° apart balance one pull the same size.\n\nThe number 1 ÷ sin θ is the [[load angle factor]]. It is 1.155 at 60°, 1.414 at 45° and 2.000 at 30° from the horizontal.[^1] Each leg carries half the load times that factor.
