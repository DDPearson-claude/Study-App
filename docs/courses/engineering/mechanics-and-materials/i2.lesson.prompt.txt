Research notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- A moment is the turning effect of a force about a pivot, and its size is force times the perpendicular distance from the pivot. [1][2][3]
- For a body to be balanced the clockwise moments must equal the anticlockwise moments about the pivot. [4]
- The moments balance about any point, so one can pick a support as the pivot; that support's own force then has no moment and drops out. [5][6][7]
- Forces on one object balance when their sum is zero; with no net force an object stays at rest or keeps a constant velocity. [8][9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In a two-leg sling each leg tension has a vertical part that carries the load; the two vertical parts together must equal the weight, so each leg pulls harder than half the weight. [10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The load in each sling leg increases as the angle between the legs increases, and low angles are called extremely hazardous. [11][12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The extra tension appears without any extra load being lifted, and the sling itself is no weaker: only the force in each leg grows. [13][14]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Load angle factors show the rise: leg tension times 1.000 at 90 degrees, 1.155 at 60, 1.414 at 45 and 2.000 at 30, with the angle measured from horizontal (some calculators use vertical). [15][16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Static friction matches the push up to a limit, F = μN; once the object slides, kinetic friction is a little smaller, so it is easier to keep it moving than to start it. For lesson i1's crate. [17]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Lubrication makes both the static and the kinetic coefficient of friction considerably smaller. For lesson i1's crate. [18]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Work is force times the distance moved in the direction of the force, and one joule is the work done when one newton moves something one metre, so work is also newton-metres. [19][20]  (whole topic)
- A moment is measured in newton metres, force times perpendicular distance, the same unit product as work but a different quantity. [2][20]  (whole topic)
- Power converts to watts only with speed in radians per second, so a speed in rpm must be converted first. [21]  (whole topic)
