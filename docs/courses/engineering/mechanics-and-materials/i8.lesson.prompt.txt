In one breath: Mechanics is about what forces do. Held still, forces balance, in their pushes and in their turning effects, and they load the parts that carry them, which stretch a little and, loaded too far, bend for good or break; unbalanced, they change how things move, and a force moved through a distance does work, at a rate called power. Research notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Natural frequency is the frequency at which a system would oscillate with no driving or damping, and resonance occurs when the driving frequency equals it. [1][2]
- Away from resonance, as the driving frequency rises above the natural frequency the response gets smaller. [3]
- Natural frequency can be changed through stiffness or mass: lower stiffness lowers it. [4][5]
- The rotor speed at which a rotating machine resonates is its critical speed, and machines are designed to run at speeds away from it. [6][7]
- Unbalance force grows with the square of rotating speed, and an unbalance can form a couple that rocks the axis and vibrates the rotor in its bearings. [8][9]
- One guideline for torsional critical speeds is to keep operating speed at least 10 percent clear of them where feasible. [10]
- A rigid rotor can be balanced at two end planes, while a flexible rotor needs balancing in several planes. [11]
- Misalignment is another source of machine vibration, noise and early bearing or shaft failure. [12]
- A flywheel with a higher moment of inertia, meaning more mass or mass further from the centre, stores more energy at the same speed. [13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Rotational power is torque times angular velocity, with the speed in radians per second. [14]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A flywheel evens out the speed variations an engine's fluctuating torque would cause, as on a diesel generator (added for i6 at review). [15]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For a gear train the gear ratio is the input speed divided by the output speed; for two gears it matches the teeth or radius ratio. [16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A belt that does not slip moves at the same speed as the edge of both pulleys. [17]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- When a gearbox reduces speed it raises torque in proportion, and a real gearbox loses some power to friction so output power is smaller than input. [18]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Marine diesel engines commonly use a reduction gearbox so the propeller shaft turns slower than the engine. [19]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Shaft misalignment causes vibration, noise and premature bearing, coupling or shaft failure. [12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Shaft misalignment has no measurable effect on motor efficiency; its harm is vibration, heat and wear (for i7). [20]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Work is force times the distance moved in the direction of the force, and one joule is the work done when one newton moves something one metre, so work is also newton-metres. [21][22]  (whole topic)
- A moment is measured in newton metres, force times perpendicular distance, the same unit product as work but a different quantity. [23][22]  (whole topic)
- Power converts to watts only with speed in radians per second, so a speed in rpm must be converted first. [14]  (whole topic)

- control: a setting Dan changes; one the interactive picks itself (the weight it starts with) is stated as its choice;
- computed: worked out from the rule in whatAmILookingAt; a peak or threshold between slider steps is "near 85%", never "at 85%";
- constant: a fixed real-world value: with "source": n when a source above states it, otherwise a textbook-standard value you are certain of, presented as one, labelled with nothing its quote lacks; whatAmILookingAt says what it physically is. If rounding it makes a real, documented case come out in a way no source says, pick a value inside the stated range that keeps it true, and say so;
- assumed: a value chosen for the example and worded as chosen ("say a £1,000 pot", "drawn 10 times bigger"). A sketched curve's shape is assumed too: listed in numbers, faithful to every shape fact in RESEARCH, called a sketch in whatAmILookingAt;
- date: a historical date or documented fact, with "source": n when a source above states it.
No other number appears. Write each number as its readout shows it, in metric units ("tonnes", not "tons"), with no false precision. When your model's result and a cited real value differ, say why in one clause and use the real value in explain and practice.
