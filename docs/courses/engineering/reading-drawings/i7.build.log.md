- Design choices beyond the brief:
  - Flow is traced, not typed in. Each pump's lit path is every route through open parts from its outlet back to its
    suction, visiting no part twice. A non-return valve passes one way. A stopped pump passes both ways. TV1 joins its
    inlet to the cooler, the bypass or both. On the fuel sheet the paths run from an open storage tank through the
    running P3 to the day tank. Isolate mode spreads out from the picked part through pipes, non-return valves and pumps
    to the first hand valve on each side. A pipe stays red while that valve is open.
  - Valves, pumps, TV1, S1 and HX1 (S2 and P3 on the fuel sheet) are tapped on the drawing itself, with 30-36 unit hit
    targets that the keyboard can reach. They are not kit buttons, so the still guide's pictures show no controls. The
    kit buttons are only the Sheet tabs (Cooling, Fuel) and the Isolate and Plant switches. They appear on screens 8,
    10 and 12, and on puzzles 9, 11, 13 and 14 once answered.
  - Screen 1 keeps the pump curve after the answer. The dot slides along the curve to no flow and 30 m. The loop first
    appears on screen 2, so the curve Dan has just solved stays in view while he reads Why?.
  - Screen 5 draws P2's branch as a dotted line until it is answered. The legend calls it "Not traced yet". The rest of
    P1's path is lit, so neither "lit" nor "dark" gives the answer away. Screens 3, 7 and 13 show no flow until answered.
  - Screen 9 hides "cut off on every side" until Check. Before that the sheet says only "P2 is picked for isolation."
    The red pipes stay visible, as screen 8 left them.
  - The plant sketch sits beside the drawing on a laptop and below the say line on a phone. Its valves have tag plates
    and handwheels. The extra valve on the header tank's line has no plate and is drawn shut. After screen 11 a dashed
    red ghost marked "missing" appears on the drawing's header tank line.
  - Screen 14 shows no red and no rings until it is answered, because the count is the answer. After Check, F1, X1 and
    F3 are ringed.
  - The say line on screen 2 names the tags: HX1 is the cooler and HT1 the header tank. The instrument bubble is drawn
    without letters and called the temperature sensor that sets TV1 (fix round, 13 Nov: its letters are UNCONFIRMED).

- Symbols drawn, and what each was checked against. No maker's symbol sheet is held in the research, and this fix round
  had no web access, so none is checked against two makers' sheets yet. Each is a common P&ID form, drawn by eye.
  - Centrifugal pump: a circle with its outlet marked by a triangle at the edge. DOE's rule that the arrow's point
    touches the circle in a pump (research 11) is the only quoted check.
  - Hand valve (gate or globe): two triangles point to point, hollow when open and filled when shut. DOE says gate and
    globe valves are often drawn alike (research 23). Unchecked against a maker's sheet.
  - Non-return valve: a hollow arrow-shaped body against a seat bar, pointing the way it passes. Unchecked.
  - Three-way valve with a diaphragm actuator: three triangles meeting, a stem and a half-round dome. Unchecked.
  - Strainer: a diamond with a dashed mesh line. Unchecked.
  - Heat exchanger: a circle with a zigzag through it. Unchecked.
  - Instrument bubble: a plain circle on a stub, with a dashed signal line. Unchecked; drawn without letters.
  - To close: check each against two makers' public sheets (ISO 10628-2 forms) and list them here.
