In a pumped system like this, water flows only along a path that is open all the way through a running pump: from where it draws, through the pump, to where it goes. In this closed loop, where it goes is round and back to the pump's own suction. A line carrying flow is bold with arrows, and a line with none is pale. Unless the sheet says otherwise, it shows each valve in its normal position.[^1] Here P1 runs and P2 is stopped with both its valves open, so P2 stands by, ready to start.\n\nThe positions of every valve at one time are the [[valve lineup]], and you trace from it. Water leaves P1, goes through the cooler and the equipment, and comes back to P1's suction. Why does none run back through P2? P1's discharge sits at a higher pressure than the suction, so any open way between them would carry water back. A stopped pump does not push, but water can still pass through its casing. A centrifugal pump then runs against the shut valve at full head, with no flow. The three-way valve TV1 is different. It sends the water through the cooler, round it through a bypass, or some each way. It picks a path, so the water keeps moving whichever way it is set.\n\nTo isolate a part, follow every pipe from it to its first valve, on both sides, and shut that valve.
