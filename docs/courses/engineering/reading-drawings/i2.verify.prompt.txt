The sources can be generators, a shore supply and battery banks.[^2] So you read it from each source down to each load, and you ask one question of every load. Which closed paths join it to a source?\n\nThe incoming power usually sits at the top, and the drawing works its way down.[^3] On a three-phase system, one line stands for a bus or cable with all three of its phases.[^1] Each [[bus section]] is a thick line, and every outgoing breaker hangs from it. A load has supply while an unbroken path of closed breakers joins it to a live source. Open a breaker and the path through it breaks. So everything below it goes dead, unless another path still reaches it. A path can pass through a transformer or a charger, because power passes on through them at a new voltage. On the example board, -T1 steps 440 V down to 115 V, and charger -U1 makes 24 V DC. Open -Q12 and both loads on the 115 V board lose supply, and nothing else does. Not every source sits at the top. The battery here sits on the DC board, so opening the charger's breaker leaves that board live.\n\nThis drawing marks two breakers N.O., normally open, because they stay open in normal running. One is the [[bus-tie]] -Q4, which joins the two bus sections. Kept open, it leaves each section on its own source. Close it and one source feeds both, so a dead section comes back live. With both sections live from different sets, the sets must be synchronised before the tie closes. The other is pump -M3's [[alternate feed]], -Q23 from section B, beside its normal feed -Q13 from section A. An interlock lets only one of the two close.
