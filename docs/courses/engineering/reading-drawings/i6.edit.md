# i6 "Tracing a hydraulic circuit, then a pneumatic one": plain-English edit

91 sentences in i6.lesson.verified.json were rewritten. Most were long sentences chained with "and", "so", "while"
or "which", now split into short ones with one idea each and the point first. The edits cover explain, analogy (text
and breaks), practice, the brief, ignores, and the interactive's whatAmILookingAt: the kit description, the step
words, the read sentences, the hints and the whys. The read sentences are now full sentences ("-Y1 is on. Oil runs
...") rather than lists of phrases, so they read cleanly as stills. The explain text now has nine paragraphs instead of four, split at the
drawn state, the fixed pump and relief valve, -Y2, the stored-energy warning and the key rule. Every number, unit, rule, tag,
[[term]] and [^n] citation is unchanged; a token check confirmed it. Sources, ids, numbers and structure are
untouched. The lesson has no checks, predict or say.

i6.lesson.final.json got the same rewrite (90 sentences). Where it still has its pre-fact-check wording (no
"unless a set of sheets shows other phases", "more than the flow control lets through", "stays under pressure"
without "for a long time", no "usually" in the drawn-state step), it kept its own facts and was only reworded the
same way.

The brief keeps the form "The one thing you should see is ___ when you ___". The SHOW screens' words run from 33 to
50 words, well under 90. The validator passes on both files with `--final`.

## Five before-and-after examples

1. Three clauses chained with "and" and "so" (whatAmILookingAt, -Y1 on)
   - Before: "With -Y1 on, oil runs from P to A into the piston side, and from the rod side through the flow control and
     B to T to the tank, so the ram comes down."
   - After: "With -Y1 on, oil runs from P to A into the piston side. Oil from the rod side runs through the flow
     control and B to T, to the tank. The ram comes down."

2. A read sentence made of phrases turned into plain sentences (screen 12)
   - Before: "Read: the pump stopped, both solenoids off, the gauge at zero, and the line from A red, labelled still
     under pressure, with the ram still on the work."
   - After: "Read: the pump has stopped, both solenoids are off and the gauge reads zero. The line from A is red,
     labelled still under pressure. The ram is still on the work."

3. Point first, the exception after (explain, the drawn state)
   - Before: "Unless a set of sheets shows other phases, the sheet shows each part in its normal, or neutral,
     condition.[^1]"
   - After: "The sheet shows each part in its normal, or neutral, condition, unless a set of sheets shows other
     phases.[^1]"

4. Point first on a show screen (screen 6 words)
   - Before: "The flow control lets the rod side's oil out slowly, so it sets the ram's speed, and the pump's spare oil
     goes over the relief valve."
   - After: "The flow control sets the ram's speed. It lets the rod side's oil out slowly. The pump's spare oil goes
     over the relief valve."

5. Passive voice made active, and one fact per sentence (screen 13 why)

     the work. Release that pressure and prove that part at zero before the hose is opened."
   - After: "Why: the middle box blocks A. So the oil between A and the cylinder is shut in, and it still holds the ram
     on the work. Release that pressure and prove that part at zero before you open the hose."
