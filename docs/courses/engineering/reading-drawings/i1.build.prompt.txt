## The explanation Dan reads after playing (use the same words for the same things)
A system diagram is a map of what is joined to what. It is not a picture of the plant. So you read it by following its lines, not by where things sit. Take a small pump set. It has a supply, an isolator, a starter, a motor and the pump it drives, with a pipe to a tank. In the room, the isolator might be on a wall and the motor across the room. On the diagram they might sit side by side. A circuit diagram does not show what the circuit looks like, or how long its connections are. A diagram is drawn to show how the system works. It does not show where the parts really are. So parts go wherever their lines are easy to follow. Some diagrams are laid out roughly like the plant. But nothing on the sheet promises it.

So the meaning lives in three things: the symbols, the lines, and where the lines meet. A line joins the parts at its ends. It joins another line where they meet at a dot. It also joins where one line ends on another in a T. Drag a symbol across the sheet and its lines stretch to follow. Every joint stays the same, so the meaning is unchanged. Two parts drawn side by side are not joined unless a line runs between them. Parts drawn in a row are in series only if one path runs through each in turn. ## The house kit (complete API reference)
You write the BODY of one page: HTML, an optional `<style>`, then one inline `<script>` ending
with `K.ready()`. It runs in a sandboxed iframe on Dan's phone (about 340 px wide, touch) and
laptop, with the global `K` and the kit's stylesheet. No network or storage: no web
addresses, `fetch`, `localStorage`, `alert` or `prompt`.

### Page shape that works
1. `<p class="lead">`: one line saying what is drawn (the app adds title and action).
2. The main visual with its controls right under it: `K.stage('#scene', '#controls')` keeps the
   pair on one phone screen. A choice that re-sorts the picture may sit above it.
3. `<p class="say">`: one sentence that changes with the state and says why. Then
   `<div class="k-readouts">` for numbers the drawing does not label.
4. Secondary figures and anything that grows go below.
5. `<p class="caption">`: one short sentence, at most 20 words: where the numbers come from ("A
   sketch, not measured data") or the rule in the explanation's words. No new claim, no law or
   term the explanation does not teach. The app shows what the model leaves out.

### Keep the answer hidden until Dan moves
Dan predicts before he plays: open on a setting the question is not about, and hide whatever
gives the answer away until his first move:
- class `k-after-move` on any element (a mark, an arrow, a sentence): hidden, its space kept.
  Hide the answer, not the controls or a whole figure (a blank hole fails the test).
- `K.readout({..., afterMove: true})` shows "?" until then; plot series and marks take `afterMove: true` too.
- `K.moved` is true from then on: in `K.update`, write `K.moved ? answer : question`.
- `K.afterMove(fn)` runs `fn` once at that moment; `K.reveal()` marks it from your handlers.

### The pipeline
Controls register themselves; their values form `params` = `{id: value}`.
```
K.model(fn)        fn(params) -> outputs {name: number | string}. Pure: no DOM, no lists or objects.
K.update(fn)       fn(params, outputs): draw everything here. More than one is allowed.
K.at(over)         -> outputs of the model at those params (others take each control's starting value)
K.check(label, fn, {source?})   fn() must return exactly true
K.ready()          call once, last: draws the opening state and runs the checks
K.complete()       at the lesson's end
K.params()  K.outputs()  K.refresh()   (refresh reruns the pipeline after you change your own state)
```
A readout whose id is an output key updates itself.

### Controls
Every control takes `into` (a selector or element). Ids are unique; use the lesson's ids exactly.
```
K.control({id, label, min, max, step, value, unit?, prefix?, fmt?, log?, snap?, hint?, into}) -> {el, get(), set(v)}
K.choice({id, label, options: [value | {value, label}], value, hint?, into}) -> {el, get, set}
K.toggle({id, label, value: false, hint?, into}) -> {el, get, set}
K.stepper({id, steps: [title | {title, text}], value: 0, compact?, label?, into}) -> {el, get, set, setSteps(list)}
K.button({label, press, secondary?, into}) -> {el, press(), setLabel(text)}
K.drag(el, {control, toValue(x, y)}) -> {el}
```
`control` is a big slider with − / + buttons. Keep `unit` short; `fmt(v)` returns the whole text;
`log: true` spans decades; `snap: [values]` lands a drag exactly on a setting the prediction or a
check names. `choice`: 2-8 short named options; `value` is an option's value or its 0-based
index. `stepper` walks a process with Back / Next (`compact: true` when your diagram shows the
content). `button` is an action (Send it in): use it, not plain buttons. `drag` lets Dan move
the thing itself: `toValue` maps the pointer (viewBox units; px from `el`'s top-left for HTML)
to the control's value; the first touch counts as his move.

### Outputs

- `marks`: `[{x, y, label?, color?, guides?, afterMove?}]`: a dot with a label pill; `guides` adds dashed lines to the axes.

- 3-5 `K.check` known answers worked out by hand: an edge case, a shape fact (rises, halves,
  always last), and one answer from outside your formula. `source` may ONLY be a URL from the
  lesson's own sources, and only on a check whose label restates what that source's quote says;
  a result of your rule, or a fact about your drawing, takes none. Compare numbers with
  `K.near(a, b, tol)`: tolerance about a millionth of the size, a thousandth where rounding meets roots.
- `K.anim` for motion, never timers: soft, unhurried, nothing popping in. Parts arrive about 0.6
  s apart by `K.fade(el, K.arrive(T, t0))`; travel and growth use `K.ease`; a demonstration takes

- Ideas without numbers (mechanism, process, structure, history, concept): prefer a timeline

  drawing. Checks assert order and structure facts.
- A timeline runs forward: a slider rising left to right, its `fmt` showing the date (−500 as

<script>
  // Source [1] of the lesson: the halogens, alkali metals and noble gases.
  const SOURCE = 'https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halogen';

  // A fair set: the first members of three families in order (no hand-picking to make one sort
  // look good), with hydrogen left out because it is not an alkali metal.
  const ELEMENTS = [
    { sym: 'Li', name: 'Lithium', column: 1, state: 'Solid', mass: 6.94, does: 'metal' },
    { sym: 'Na', name: 'Sodium', column: 1, state: 'Solid', mass: 22.99, does: 'metal' },
    { sym: 'K', name: 'Potassium', column: 1, state: 'Solid', mass: 39.10, does: 'metal' },
    { sym: 'F', name: 'Fluorine', column: 17, state: 'Gas', mass: 19.00, does: 'salt' },
    { sym: 'Cl', name: 'Chlorine', column: 17, state: 'Gas', mass: 35.45, does: 'salt' },
    { sym: 'Br', name: 'Bromine', column: 17, state: 'Liquid', mass: 79.90, does: 'salt' },
    { sym: 'I', name: 'Iodine', column: 17, state: 'Solid', mass: 126.90, does: 'salt' },
    { sym: 'He', name: 'Helium', column: 18, state: 'Gas', mass: 4.00, does: 'inert' },
    { sym: 'Ne', name: 'Neon', column: 18, state: 'Gas', mass: 20.18, does: 'inert' },
    { sym: 'Ar', name: 'Argon', column: 18, state: 'Gas', mass: 39.95, does: 'inert' },
  ];
  // Three equal kinds of behaviour: categorical colours, each with its own shape too.
  const DOES = {
    metal: { text: 'Soft metal that fizzes in water', role: 'cat1', mark: '●' },
    salt: { text: 'Joins with sodium to make a salt', role: 'cat2', mark: '▲' },
    inert: { text: 'Hardly ever reacts', role: 'cat3', mark: '■' },
  };
  // Each way of sorting: the group an element goes in, and the groups in display order.
  const SORTS = {
    state: { groupOf: (e) => e.state, order: ['Solid', 'Liquid', 'Gas'] },
    mass: { groupOf: (e) => e.mass < 30 ? 'Lighter atoms' : 'Heavier atoms', order: ['Lighter atoms', 'Heavier atoms'] },
    column: { groupOf: (e) => 'Column ' + e.column, order: ['Column 1', 'Column 17', 'Column 18'] },
  };
  const groupsFor = (by) => SORTS[by].order.map((g) => ({ name: g, members: ELEMENTS.filter((e) => SORTS[by].groupOf(e) === g) }));
  // Pairs of group-mates, and how many of them share a behaviour.
  function pairs(by) {
    let all = 0, alike = 0;
    groupsFor(by).forEach((g) => g.members.forEach((a, i) => g.members.slice(i + 1).forEach((b) => { all++; if (a.does === b.does) alike++; })));
    return { all, alike };
  }

  // The lesson's control: named options, opening on the first (index 0).
  K.choice({ id: 'by', label: 'Sort them by', value: 0, into: '#controls', options: [
    { value: 'state', label: 'Solid, liquid, gas' },
    { value: 'mass', label: 'Weight' },
    { value: 'column', label: 'Table column' },
  ] });
  K.readout({ id: 'alike', label: 'Group-mates alike', unit: '%', decimals: 0, into: '#outs' });
  K.readout({ id: 'mixed', label: 'Mixed groups', into: '#outs' });

  // The pure model: a way of sorting -> how well its groups predict behaviour.
  K.model((p) => {
    const n = pairs(p.by), groups = groupsFor(p.by);
    return {
      alike: n.all ? 100 * n.alike / n.all : 0,
      mixed: groups.filter((g) => new Set(g.members.map((e) => e.does)).size > 1).length,
      groups: groups.length,
    };
  });

  const key = document.getElementById('key');
  Object.keys(DOES).forEach((k) => key.appendChild(K.el('span', null, K.el('i', { style: { color: K.color(DOES[k].role), fontStyle: 'normal' } }, DOES[k].mark), DOES[k].text)));

  K.update((p, o) => {
    const box = document.getElementById('groups');
    box.textContent = '';
    groupsFor(p.by).forEach((g) => {
      const kinds = new Set(g.members.map((e) => e.does)).size;
      const verdict = g.members.length < 2 ? 'on its own' : kinds === 1 ? 'all behave alike' : 'mixed: ' + kinds + ' kinds';
      box.appendChild(K.el('div', { class: 'group' },
        K.el('div', { class: 'group-head' }, K.el('b', null, g.name + ' · ' + g.members.length), K.el('span', null, verdict)),
        K.el('div', { class: 'cards' }, g.members.map((e) => K.el('div', { class: 'card' },
          K.el('i', { style: { color: K.color(DOES[e.does].role) }, 'aria-hidden': 'true' }, DOES[e.does].mark), K.el('b', null, e.sym), K.el('span', null, e.name))))));
    });
    box.setAttribute('aria-label', groupsFor(p.by).map((g) => g.name + ': ' + g.members.map((e) => e.name + ' (' + DOES[e.does].text.toLowerCase() + ')').join(', ')).join('. '));
    // The sentence uses the readout's own rounding (whole percent), so the two always agree.
    const pct = K.fmt(o.alike, { decimals: 0 }) + '%';
    document.getElementById('say').textContent = !K.moved ? 'Which way of sorting will keep elements that behave alike together? Make your guess, then try each one.' : {
      state: 'Sorted by state, only ' + pct + ' of group-mates behave alike: bromine sits alone as the only liquid, and the gases mix elements that react eagerly with ones that hardly react at all.',
      mass: 'Sorted by weight, just ' + pct + ' of group-mates behave alike: lithium shares a group with neon, potassium with bromine. Weight alone predicts almost nothing here.',
      column: 'Sorted by column, ' + pct + ' of group-mates behave alike. Knowing the column tells you how an element reacts: a natural grouping, and the reason the periodic table is laid out this way.',
    }[p.by];
  });

  // Known answers, counted by hand from the table above.
  K.check('By column, all 12 pairs of group-mates behave alike (100%)', () => K.near(K.at({ by: 'column' }).alike, 100) && K.at({ by: 'column' }).mixed === 0);
  K.check('By state, 7 of 16 pairs behave alike (43.75%)', () => K.near(K.at({ by: 'state' }).alike, 43.75));
  K.check('By weight, 5 of 20 pairs behave alike (25%)', () => K.near(K.at({ by: 'mass' }).alike, 25) && K.at({ by: 'mass' }).mixed === 2);
  K.check('At room temperature bromine is the only liquid; fluorine and chlorine are gases, iodine a solid', () =>
    groupsFor('state')[1].members.map((e) => e.sym).join() === 'Br' &&
    ['F', 'Cl'].every((s) => ELEMENTS.find((e) => e.sym === s).state === 'Gas') && ELEMENTS.find((e) => e.sym === 'I').state === 'Solid', { source: SOURCE });
  K.check('A fair test: every sort places each of the ten exactly once', () =>
    Object.keys(SORTS).every((by) => groupsFor(by).reduce((n, g) => n + g.members.length, 0) === 10 &&
      ELEMENTS.every((e) => groupsFor(by).filter((g) => g.members.includes(e)).length === 1)));

  K.ready();
</script>
