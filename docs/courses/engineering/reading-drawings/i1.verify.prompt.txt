Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 Single-line diagrams: from each source to each load — Trace a single-line diagram, a board's power map, from each source to each load, seeing what every breaker feeds and which loads have two feeds.
- i3 Control schematics: coils, contacts and cross-references — A control schematic draws each contact apart from the coil that moves it; the cross-reference under each coil finds its contacts, wherever they are drawn.
- i4 Fluid power symbols — Hydraulic and air symbols are built from simple marks that each mean one thing, so you can read one you have never met.
- i5 Valves in fluid power — A valve that steers oil or air is drawn as one box per position, and only the box against the ports shows where it flows.
- i6 Tracing a hydraulic circuit, then a pneumatic one — For each valve position, trace the supply to one side of a cylinder, and the other side out to the tank or, in air, the exhaust.
- i7 Piping and system diagrams — On a piping diagram, flow runs only along open paths from a running pump, so you can find which valves to shut to isolate a part.
- i8 Engineering drawings — An engineering drawing shows one object in several square-on views, laid out by a fixed rule, so you can rebuild its shape in your head.
- i9 Reading one you have never seen — Read a diagram you have never seen in four steps: legend, sources, the state it is drawn in, then one path at a time.
