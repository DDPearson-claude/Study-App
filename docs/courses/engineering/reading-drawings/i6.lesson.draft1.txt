  "text": "To read a fluid power circuit, take one valve position at a time and trace two paths. One runs from the supply to one side of the cylinder. The other runs from the cylinder's other side back to the tank or, in air, out through an exhaust. The side that is fed and the side that is emptied tell you which way it moves.\n\nStart with the job, then the parts. This press brings a ram down onto the work, squeezes it and lifts it again. A motor drives a pump, which draws oil from the tank through a filter. A relief valve and a gauge sit on the pump's line. A 4/3 valve with solenoids -Y1 and -Y2 steers the oil to the cylinder. On the line to the rod side, a flow control sits beside a check valve. Next read the drawn state. The sheet shows each part in its normal, or neutral, condition.[^7] Here the springs hold the valve's middle box against the ports. That box joins P to T and blocks A and B, so with the pump running its oil goes back to the tank at low pressure and the ram holds.\n\nNow energise -Y1. The left box joins P to A, so the pump's oil fills the piston side. It also joins B to T. While one side is fed, the other is lined up to drain back to the tank.[^2] That second path matters as much as the first. Oil barely squeezes, so the ram can come down only as fast as the rod side's oil gets out. That oil leaves through the flow control, because the check valve beside it lets oil pass only into the rod side. So the flow control sets the ram's speed, and the pump's spare oil goes over the relief valve. Once the ram stops on the work, all of the pump's oil goes that way, and the relief valve's setting decides how hard the ram presses. Energise -Y2 instead and the crossed box joins P to B and A to T. Oil reaches the rod side through the check valve and the piston side drains, so the ram goes back up. The route one position gives the oil, from the pump through the cylinder and back to the tank, is its [[flow path]].\n\nThe same job in air uses a 5/2 valve. Energise its solenoid and the supply reaches the piston side while the rod side is exhausted, so the rod comes out.[^1] The used air leaves through an [[exhaust port]] on the valve, into the room, so it never comes back. Air also squeezes, so a cylinder holding a load on shut-in air gives as the load grows. Last, stopping the pump does not empty the circuit. Oil shut in by a centred valve can still hold the ram on the work, so it is [[stored energy]] to release before anyone opens a line. **For each valve position, trace the supply to one side of the cylinder and the other side out to the tank or exhaust: the side fed and the side emptied together decide what moves.**"

  "text": "- Safety first. A stopped pump does not mean a dead circuit. Oil shut in by a centred valve, or holding a raised load, stays under pressure. Lower or prop the load, isolate the pump, then release the pressure by the plant's procedure. Prove the part you will open reads zero, not just the pump's gauge.\n- Read the job: what the circuit is for and what moves.\n- Name the parts. Find the supply, and the tank or the exhausts.\n- Read the drawn state: which box sits against the ports with nothing operating the valve.\n- For each position, slide its box in. Trace the supply to one side of the cylinder, then the other side out to the tank or exhaust. Say what moves and which way.\n- For each position, find what sets the speed, what limits the pressure and what holds the load.\n- Find the solenoids by their tags on the control schematic, to see what energises them.\n\n**Rule of thumb:** every position has two paths, one in and one out. If you cannot find the way out, an oil cylinder cannot move.\n\n**Worked example:** the press. As drawn, the middle box joins P to T, so the pump unloads and the ram holds. With -Y1 on, oil goes from P to A into the piston side, and the rod side's oil goes through the flow control and B to T to the tank. The ram comes down at the flow control's speed. On the work it stops, and the relief valve sets how hard it presses. With -Y2 on, oil goes from P to B through the check valve into the rod side, the piston side drains from A to T, and the ram goes up quickly.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Reading the drawn position as the working one.\n- Following only the fed side and missing the way out.\n- Expecting a check valve to pass oil both ways.\n- Looking for a return line on an air circuit.\n- Trusting the pump's gauge to say the whole circuit is at zero."
