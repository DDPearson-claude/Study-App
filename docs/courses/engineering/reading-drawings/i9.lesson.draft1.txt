    "text": "A sheet you have never seen is read in four steps. Find the legend, then every source, then the state it is drawn in. Then trace one path at a time. With those done, you can ask what one change does: what stops, and what still runs?\n\nStart with the legend and the notes, because conventions change from sheet to sheet. One sheet fills in a shut valve. Another draws every valve alike and writes N.C., normally closed, beside the shut ones. The notes and legend can also say what a symbol alone cannot.[^14] Next, find every source, wherever it is drawn. A single-line diagram usually starts at the top, with the incoming supply.[^4] A sheet you have never seen may not. Read it from the top left, and you may start at a load and never see a second way in. A source is anywhere supply can come in: a generator, a feeder from another board, a battery, a pump or a compressor. A store counts too. An [[air receiver]] is a tank of compressed air. With both compressors stopped, it still feeds the line until its pressure falls.\n\nThen find the state it is drawn in. Each symbol shows its part in one state, normal or at rest, unless the sheet says otherwise.[^3] A caption may also say how the plant is run, such as which generator is stopped. A generator drawn on the sheet feeds nothing while it is stopped. Last, trace one path at a time, from a source to a load. On a control sheet, trace from the supply line to a coil, then find each contact by its tag. One part can act on several paths, and each path needs its own trace. On the hoist, a switch near the top is opened by the hook when it gets there. It is the top [[limit switch]], -B11. It sits only in the path to the Up contactor, -K11. So when it opens, Up stops, but Down still works.\n\nAn engineering drawing is the odd one out. It has no sources, and nothing flows through it. So read its title block and issue, then its projection symbol, then follow one feature at a time across the views. Whatever the sheet, check it against the plant before you rely on it. Every isolation point on the plant should match the drawing.[^13] A pipe fitted after the sheet was drawn is a way in that the sheet does not show. A starter that stays live after you lock off its breaker has another way in too. Then prove dead, or vent, at the point of work.\n\n- Read the title block. Check the drawing number and its issue are the current ones.\n- Read the legend and the notes. Do not carry another sheet's symbols over.\n- Find every source, wherever it is drawn: generators, feeders from other boards, batteries, pumps, compressors, and stores such as an air receiver.\n- Find the state it is drawn in. Read every note or caption on how the plant is run.\n- Trace one path at a time, from a source to a load. On a control sheet, trace from the supply line to a coil, and follow each contact by its tag.\n- Change one thing at a time. For every load, not just the one you expect, ask whether it stops or still runs.\n- On an engineering drawing, read the title block and the projection symbol, then follow one feature across the views.\n\n**Rule of thumb:** start at the sources, not at the top left. Count the ways in before you count the ways out.\n\n**Worked example:** the second switchboard, with bus-tie -Q8 tripped. Legend: plain breaker symbols, with a caption for the state.
