- Design choices beyond the brief:
  - One 3D model drives everything. The bracket is faces painted back to front. Faces on the outside of the whole part
    paint last, because nothing can hide them. The views are line sets in each view's own two sizes. Each view lies on
    its glass wall, and the wall swings about its hinge on the front view's wall. The unfold (Unfold, 3.6 s) turns the
    eye to square-on while the walls swing, so the flat sheet is the box seen at the end of its swing. The same swing
    plays at square-on when a view arrives: the end view on screen 4, the third-angle sheet on screen 5, and the change
    of sides on screen 6 once answered.
  - Hidden lines were worked out by hand for this bracket: which edges are visible, hidden or coincident in each view.
    A centre line is drawn under a hidden edge, and a hidden edge under a visible one.
  - Section A-A is computed from the plane's position. The base has a gap across the slot, the upright a gap across the
    hole. The hatch rectangles leave out the gaps and the rib. A rib cut along its length shows as an outline, the same
    line as a rib beyond the plane. Arrows point along +x, from the left end, so A-A takes the left end view's place.
  - The projection symbol keeps one cone, wide end on the left. First angle puts the circles on that side, so the wide
    end faces them. Third angle puts them beside the narrow end. ISO 5456-2 is not in the research (UNCONFIRMED), so the
    layout follows from the lesson's own rule and the projection, not from a quoted figure. Check it against a
    public copy of the symbol before release.
  - Screen 5 (both sheets) drops the small turnable bracket so the two sheets fit. Every other sheet screen keeps it
    above the sheet. Taps on lines light the 3D edge in amber on that copy.
  - Sizes: 40 ± 0.2 and 60 are vertical dimensions, with text turned to read from the right as on real drawings. The
    title block has no digits beyond the lesson's numbers: "No. BKT-R", "Issue C", "Units: mm", "Scale 1 : 2".
  - Part B (a round hole down through the base) and Part C (a round peg on the base) share the same view from above.
    Only the front view tells them apart, as the brief's Why says.
