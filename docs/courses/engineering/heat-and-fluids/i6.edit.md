## Five before-and-after examples

1. Saying where something comes from (explain)
   - Before: "Most sources put smooth flow below about 2000 and eddies above about 4000. They do not agree on
     one switch point between, and in that band a flow can go either way."
   - After: "As a rule, flow is smooth below about 2000 and eddies above about 4000. There is no one agreed
     switch point between them. In that band a flow can go either way."

2. "Textbooks add" dropped, and a long aside split into one idea each (practice)
   - Before: "Textbooks add that an orifice plate, a flat plate with a hole in it, works the same way more
     cheaply, but loses more of the drop for good."
   - After: "An orifice plate is a flat plate with a hole in it. It works the same way and costs less, but it
     loses more of the drop for good."

3. Lead with the point, and a footnote moved off a mid-sentence comma (explain)
   - Before: "Water going through a narrower part of a pipe speeds up, and its pressure drops while it is
     there." ... "When water flows into a narrower part, its speed and its kinetic energy go up,[^3] and that
     energy comes out of the pressure."
   - After: "Water speeds up where a pipe narrows. While it is there, its pressure drops." ... "As water flows
     into a narrower part, its speed and its kinetic energy go up.[^3] That energy comes out of the
     pressure."

4. A screen's question with its choices after a colon, made to read as plain sense (interactive)
   - Before: "11 asks which flow is most likely to run in smooth layers: thick, cold oil creeping through a
     small pipe, seawater at 2 m/s in a 50 mm pipe, or the same seawater at the same speed in a 150 mm pipe."
   - After: "Screen 11 asks which flow is most likely to run in smooth layers. Is it thick, cold oil creeping
     through a small pipe? Seawater at 2 m/s in a 50 mm pipe? Or the same seawater at the same speed in a
     150 mm pipe?"

5. A chain of clauses split, and the reference to an earlier lesson made plain (explain)
   - Before: "Textbooks add a third share for a pipe that climbs, the ρgh from Pressure in liquids. ... In
     seawater, at 1025 kg/m³,[^1] that is ½ × 1025 × (8² − 2²) = ½ × 1025 × 60, about 30.8 kPa."
   - After: "A pipe that climbs adds a third share, the ρgh you met with pressure in liquids. ... Seawater is
     1025 kg/m³.[^1] So here the drop is ½ × 1025 × (8² − 2²) = ½ × 1025 × 60, about 30.8 kPa."
