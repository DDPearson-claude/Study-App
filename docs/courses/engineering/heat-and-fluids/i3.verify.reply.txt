Halve the space and they strike each bit of wall twice as often. Warm them and they strike harder and more often.\n\nA pressure gauge open to the air reads zero. Yet the air is pushing on it at about 1 bar. So a gauge reads how far you are above the atmosphere, or below it. That is [[gauge pressure]]. Add the atmosphere and you get [[absolute pressure]], counted from no push at all.[^1] You met Boyle's law in Proportional reasoning. Hold a gas's temperature and halve its volume, and its pressure doubles.[^2] It is the absolute pressure that doubles. A gauge reading 1 bar means the gas pushes with 2 bar in all. The gauge just leaves out the atmosphere's share. Halve the volume and the gas pushes with 4 bar absolute. So the gauge now reads 3 bar, not 2.\n\nTemperature has the same trap. Lock the piston so the volume cannot change, then warm the gas. Its pressure climbs in a straight line. Carry that line on to colder and colder gas, and it reaches zero pressure at about −273 °C. That is absolute zero, where the kelvin scale starts. So with the volume held, the pressure is in proportion to the kelvin temperature, not the Celsius one.[^3] Warm a locked cylinder from 20 °C to 40 °C.
