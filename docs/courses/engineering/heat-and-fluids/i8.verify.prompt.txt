Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- A centrifugal pump settles at the operating point, where its curve of head against flow crosses the system's curve. [R1]
- Closing a discharge valve changes the system resistance curve. [R2]
- With the discharge valve closed, a centrifugal pump builds maximum head, and the power the motor needs is at or near its minimum. [R3]
- As the valve opens and flow rises, the motor's current rises (source is a pump maker's start-up guide; it covers centrifugal pumps generally). [R4]
- Net positive suction head (NPSH) is the minimum absolute suction pressure needed to avoid cavitation. [R5]
- The energy needed to change a material's temperature depends on its mass, its specific heat capacity and the temperature change wanted. [R6]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For water, 4,200 J raises 1 kg by 1 °C. [R7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Latent heat is energy that enters or leaves during a phase change without any temperature change, so melting or boiling happens at a steady temperature. [R8]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Water's heat of fusion is 334 J/g and its heat of vaporization is 2260 J/g, so boiling away a kilogram takes about 2,260 kJ. [R9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For a fixed amount of gas at constant volume, pressure is proportional to absolute (kelvin) temperature. [R10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- At constant temperature, halving a fixed amount of gas's volume doubles its pressure. [R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Absolute pressure is gauge pressure plus atmospheric pressure. [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A larger air receiver prevents pressure fluctuation and frequent compressor motor starts. [R13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The atmosphere pushes with about 10^5 Pa, 100 kPa (the weight of 10 m of seawater); 1 bar is 100 kPa (i3, a cited constant). [R14]  (from an idea this one builds on)
