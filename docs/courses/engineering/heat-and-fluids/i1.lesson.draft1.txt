    "text": "Temperature tells you how hot something is. Heat is energy moving into or out of it, and the two are not the same thing. The energy needed to warm something depends on its mass, its material and how far its temperature must rise.[^1] The material's part is its [[specific heat capacity]], the energy to warm 1 kg by 1 °C. For water it is 4,200 J per kg per °C.[^2] Multiply the three together and you have the energy, Q = mcΔT. A 0.25 kg cup of water at 80 °C cooling to a 20 °C room gives out 0.25 × 4,200 × 60 = 63,000 J, which is 63 kJ. A 100 kg tank at 30 °C cooling to the same room gives out 100 × 4,200 × 10 = 4,200,000 J, which is 4.2 MJ. The cup is hotter, but the tank gives out over 60 times as much.\n\nNow put 1 kg of melting ice on a heater and plot its temperature against the energy put in. For the first 334 kJ the line stays flat at 0 °C.[^4] The energy is melting the ice, not warming it. Energy that goes into changing a solid to a liquid, or a liquid to a gas, without changing the temperature is called [[latent heat]].[^3] Once the ice has melted, the line climbs. Each kJ warms 1 kg of water by 1 ÷ 4.2, about 0.24 °C, so the slope is 1 ÷ (mc), and twice the mass climbs half as steeply. From 0 °C to 100 °C takes 420 kJ. At 100 °C the line goes flat again for 2,260 kJ while the water turns to steam.[^4] That is over five times the energy that warmed it. A 3 kW heater would take 112 s to warm 1 kg from 20 °C to 100 °C, and about 12.6 minutes more to boil it away.\n\nA [[kelvin]] is the same size as a degree Celsius, but its zero is absolute zero. Textbooks add that absolute zero is −273.15 °C. So ice at 0 °C is at about 273 K, and it still holds plenty of energy. A flow works the same way, a second at a time. Seawater's specific heat capacity is about 3,850 J per kg per °C.[^5] If 2 kg of seawater passes through a cooler each second and warms by 10 °C, it carries away 2 × 3,850 × 10 = 77,000 J each second, which is 77 kW. This is a simplified model, and a real heater also warms the air and the pan. The pump puzzle is an energy question too, counted in the same joules and watts.\n\n**Warming takes mass × specific heat capacity × temperature rise,[^1] and melting or boiling takes in energy with no change of temperature,[^3] so a temperature alone does not tell you how much energy went in or will come out.**"

    "text": "- Find the mass in kg, and the specific heat capacity of the material.\n- Find the temperature rise or fall, end minus start. Never the end temperature on its own.\n- Ask whether the material melts or boils on the way. Boiling it away takes over five times what warming it from 0 °C to 100 °C did.\n\n**Worked example:** Say a 3 kW immersion heater warms 150 kg of water in a tank from 15 °C to 60 °C. The rise is 45 °C. The energy is 150 × 4,200 × 45 = 28,350,000 J, about 28.35 MJ. At 3 kW, that takes 28,350,000 ÷ 3,000 = 9,450 s, about 2.6 hours, with no losses.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Treating a hotter thing as holding more energy.
