## Five before-and-after examples

1. Source narration removed (explain)
   - Before: "Textbooks add that heat moves from hotter to colder."
   - After: "Heat flows from hotter to colder."

2. Two footnotes in one sentence split (explain, bold summary)
   - Before: "**Warming takes mass × specific heat capacity × temperature rise,[^4] and melting or boiling takes in energy with no change of temperature,[^5] so a temperature alone does not tell you how much energy went in or will come out.**"
   - After: "**Warming takes mass × specific heat capacity × temperature rise.[^4] Melting or boiling takes in energy with no change of temperature.[^5] So a temperature alone does not tell you how much energy went in or will come out.**"

3. Long definition broken up, with the point first (explain)
   - Before: "Energy that goes in or out as a material changes between solid, liquid and gas, with no change of temperature, is [[latent heat]].[^5] One way to picture it: temperature follows how fast the molecules jiggle, and melting and boiling spend the energy on loosening their hold on each other instead."
   - After: "This is called [[latent heat]]. It is energy taken in or given out as a material changes between solid, liquid and gas, with no change of temperature.[^5] Here is one way to picture it. Temperature follows how fast the molecules jiggle. Melting and boiling spend the energy on loosening their hold on each other instead."

4. Source narration and a clause chain (explain, kelvin)
   - Before: "0 °C is only where ice melts, a handy mark, and plenty of things are colder. Textbooks add that the kelvin scale starts at absolute zero, −273.15 °C."
   - After: "0 °C is just where ice melts. It is a handy mark, and plenty of things are colder. The kelvin scale starts at absolute zero, −273.15 °C."

5. Screen-by-screen notes made into whole sentences (interactive.whatAmILookingAt)
   - Before: "9 asks what happens when a 3 kW heater runs 5 minutes on 1 kg of water at 100 °C: still 100 °C with about 0.4 kg boiled away, near 314 °C, or all boiled away."
   - After: "Screen 9 asks what happens when a 3 kW heater runs 5 minutes on 1 kg of water at 100 °C. It could stay at 100 °C with about 0.4 kg boiled away. It could reach near 314 °C. Or it could all boil away."

Kept as it was: the brief keeps the validator's required shape, "The one thing you should see is ___ when you ___."
