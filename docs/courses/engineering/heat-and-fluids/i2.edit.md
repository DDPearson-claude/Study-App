# i2 "How heat moves": plain-English edit

Fields rewritten: explain.text, analogy.text, analogy.breaks, practice.text, interactive.whatAmILookingAt,
interactive.ignores. The brief stays as it was because the validator needs its fixed form ("The one thing
you should see is ___ when you ___."). Every number, unit, rule, [[term]] and [^n] citation is unchanged,
and a script checked that. About 135 sentences were rewritten. i2.lesson.final.json now holds the same
text, so it also picks up the fact-check fixes. Both files pass `validate.mjs lesson --final`.

## Five before-and-after examples

1. Named the source of a fact (explain)
   - Before: "Textbooks add that metal passes heat far more easily than wood."
   - After: "Metal passes heat far more easily than wood."

2. Pointed at another lesson by name (explain)
   - Before: "How much it takes to warm something, the mc from Temperature and heat, is another."
   - After: "How much heat it takes to warm something, its mc, is another."

3. One long sentence split into one idea each, with the source tag removed (explain)
   - Before: "Textbooks add that heat moves in three ways: conduction through solids, convection as warmed air
     or water carries it away, and radiation."
   - After: "Heat moves in three ways. Conduction carries it through solids. Convection carries it away in
     warmed air or water. The third way is radiation."

4. An instruction to act changed to a sentence that still makes sense without controls (interactive)
   - Before: "Press Play to switch the current on and run the clock." / "Switch the current off and the trace
     falls back to the room along the same kind of curve."
   - After: "Play switches the current on and runs the clock." / "With the current switched off, the trace
     falls back to the room along the same kind of curve."

5. A chain of clauses split up, with the main point first (analogy)
   - Before: "Treat the losses as a current source, the copper's rise as a voltage, the thermal resistance as
     a resistor and the heat capacity as a capacitor across it."
   - After: "Think of it as a circuit. The losses are a current source. The copper's rise is a voltage. The
     thermal resistance is a resistor, and the heat capacity is a capacitor across it."
