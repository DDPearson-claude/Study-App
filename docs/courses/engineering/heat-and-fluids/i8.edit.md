## Five before-and-after examples

1. Saying where something comes from (explain)
   - Before: "That is [[cavitation]], and textbooks add that it rattles like gravel and can pit the impeller."
   - After: "That is [[cavitation]]. It rattles like gravel and can pit the impeller."

2. Talking about other lessons by name (explain and practice)
   - Before: "It is the crossing from Linear systems, with curves in place of lines." ... "The power goes
     with the speed cubed, the pump law from Powers and roots."
   - After: "It is the same sum as finding where two lines cross, with curves in place of lines." ...
     "The power goes with the speed cubed. These are the pump laws."

3. Lead with the point, one idea per sentence (explain)
   - Before: "A pump's [[net positive suction head]] required, NPSH for short, is the least absolute
     pressure it needs at its suction to avoid it.[^5] What the suction offers is the atmosphere's push on
     the tank, plus the tank's height above the pump, less the suction pipe's friction and the water's
     boiling pressure."
   - After: "Each pump needs a least absolute pressure at its suction to avoid it. That is its [[net
     positive suction head]] required, or NPSH for short.[^5] What the suction offers starts with the
     atmosphere's push on the tank. Add the tank's height above the pump. Take off the suction pipe's
     friction and the water's boiling pressure."

4. A sentence that started with a number (interactive, screen 6)
   - Before: "The hint: Q goes in m³/s. 3.6 kW comes from scaling 3.13 kW by the head alone, and 3.1 kW
     from expecting no change."
   - After: "The hint: Q goes in m³/s. Scaling 3.13 kW by the head alone gives the wrong 3.6 kW. Expecting
     no change gives 3.1 kW."

5. A clipped fragment turned into a plain rule (practice, reading the gauges)
   - Before: "Outlet gauge up, with flow and current down, usually means something on the discharge side
     is choking the flow."
   - After: "If the outlet gauge rises while the flow and current fall, something on the discharge side
     is usually choking the flow."
