## Five before-and-after examples

1. Saying where something comes from (explain)
   - Before: "How big could the work be? Textbooks add that no engine taking in heat at a hot temperature
     and giving it out at a cold one can beat the [[Carnot limit]], 1 − T cold ÷ T hot."
   - After: "How big could the work be? No engine that takes in heat at a hot temperature and gives it out
     at a cold one can beat the [[Carnot limit]], 1 − T cold ÷ T hot."

2. Naming another course, and a sum strung into one sentence (explain)
   - Before: "On a pressure–volume chart it is the area under the gas's line, as in Calculus fundamentals.
     At a steady 200 kPa, moving the piston out by 1 L, 0.001 m³, takes 200 kPa × 0.001 m³ = 200 J."
   - After: "On a pressure–volume chart, it is the area under the gas's line. Say the gas holds a steady
     200 kPa and moves the piston out by 1 L, which is 0.001 m³. The work is 200 kPa × 0.001 m³ = 200 J."

3. A colon and a chain of clauses split into one idea each (explain)
   - Before: "One way to picture it: to go round again, the gas must be squeezed back. Squeezed while still
     hot, it pushes back as hard as it pushed out, and takes back all its work."
   - After: "Here is one way to picture why. To go round again, the gas must be squeezed back. Squeeze it
     while it is still hot, and it pushes back as hard as it pushed out. The squeeze then takes back all
     the work."

4. An instruction to act made to read as plain sense with no controls (interactive)
   - Before: "Drag the work past it and it stops at the line, in red. Lower the line below the work, and
     the work is pushed down with it, the difference going to the exhaust."
   - After: "Work dragged past the line stops there, in red. If the line drops below the work, it pushes the
     work down too. The difference goes to the exhaust."

5. A worked example led by its point, one step a sentence (practice)
   - Before: "A generator set gives 400 kW of electrical output at 40% efficiency, fuel to electrical
     output. So the fuel brings 400 ÷ 0.4 = 1,000 kW, and 600 kW is rejected."
   - After: "A generator set gives 400 kW of electrical output. Its efficiency, from fuel to electrical
     output, is 40%. So the fuel brings 400 ÷ 0.4 = 1,000 kW. That leaves 600 kW rejected."
