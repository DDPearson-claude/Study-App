## Five before-and-after examples

1. Takeaway (explain): one long chain of clauses becomes three short sentences.
   - Before: "**A field turning past fixed coils makes three phases 120° apart,[^2] one cycle for each pole pair that passes, so the frequency is locked to the speed, while the field current changes the voltage the coils make, not the frequency.**"
   - After: "**A field turning past fixed coils makes three phases 120° apart.[^2] Each pole pair that passes makes one cycle, so the frequency is locked to the speed. The field current changes the voltage the coils make, not the frequency.**"

   - After: "A generator set's alternator is the simple turning-coil generator, turned inside out. ... One turn makes one full cycle."

3. Screen 9 read sentence: the opening clause stack is now a set-up sentence, then the result.
   - Before: "With nothing connected and the field unchanged, slowing a 4-pole set 5%, from 1800 to 1710 rev/min, makes its waves both lower and less frequent: 57 Hz and 418 V."
   - After: "Take a 4-pole set with nothing connected and the field unchanged. Slowed 5%, from 1800 to 1710 rev/min, its waves get both lower and less frequent: 57 Hz and 418 V."

4. Screen 10 why: one idea per sentence.
   - Before: "Frequency = pole pairs × rev/min ÷ 60, and the field is nowhere in it. So only the speed changes it. Speeding up raises the voltage a little too, and the field current can then trim that."

5. Analogy: the sensor and its rule are now split into separate steps.
