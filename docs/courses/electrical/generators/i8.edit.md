Scope: the brief, the ignores, the screen words, questions, slips, hints and whys in
whatAmILookingAt, then the explain, the analogy and its breaks, and the practice. Every fact,
number, unit, rule, formula, [[term]] and [^n] citation is unchanged. Sources, ids, numbers,
controls and structure are untouched. The builder's spec paragraphs (the picture, the controls,
the rule it computes) are untouched too. The brief keeps its template ("The one thing you should
see is ___ when you ___."). The lesson has no checks list.

The text already named no sources. The edit splits clause chains into one idea per sentence,
puts the point first, removes mid-sentence asides ("an example," between commas) and turns
passive questions active. The read sentences still fit their screens. Screen 2 still ends on
"Press Start to watch the drop.", and screen 8 still ends on "Switch the start method to compare."

## Five before-and-after examples

1. Screen 13 why: a passive clause chain becomes two active steps.
   - Before: "If A pushes past the whole load, B is driven as a motor, and its reverse-power relay trips it."
   - After: "If A pushes past the whole load, it drives B as a motor. B's reverse-power relay then trips it."

2. Screen 8: an aside wedged between commas moves to the front.
   - Before: "Here it holds the start to half, an example, 300 kVA."
   - After: "In this example, it holds the start to half, 300 kVA."

3. Explain: one sentence carrying two ideas becomes two.
   - Before: "Starting loads one after another, not together, cuts the starting kW needed, by 40% in one worked case.[^5]"
   - After: "Starting loads one after another, not together, cuts the starting kW needed. In one worked case it cut it by 40%.[^5]"

4. Practice: a tangled warning becomes a plain instruction.
   - Before: "A voltage that does not recover, or a motor that does not run up, means stop and find out why."
   - After: "If the voltage does not recover, or the motor does not run up, stop and find out why."

5. Screen 11 question: a passive set-up becomes something Dan does.
   - Before: "So a second, identical set is synchronised onto the board first."
   - After: "So you synchronise a second, identical set onto the board first."
