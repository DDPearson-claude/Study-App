## Five before-and-after examples

1. Explain: a sentence that told Dan where the idea comes from now just teaches it.
   - Before: "Textbooks add that loss-of-field protection watches for signs such as the inward kVAr, and trips the set, ideally before it slips."
   - After: "Loss-of-field protection watches for signs such as the inward kVAr. It trips the set, ideally before it slips."

2. Explain: a narrating lead-in and a sentence with an aside in the middle become plain steps.
   - Before: "Textbooks explain the start this way. The rotor's windings resist any sudden change in their flux, as any coil resists a sudden change in its current. So for the first few cycles only a small reactance, the sub-transient reactance, holds the current back."
   - After: "Here is why it starts so high. The rotor's windings resist any sudden change in their flux. Any coil resists a sudden change in its current in the same way. So for the first few cycles, only a small reactance holds the current back. It is called the sub-transient reactance."

3. Explain: an example figure tucked in with commas becomes three short sentences.
   - Before: "On two sets sharing 400 kW, A might draw 50.4 kW, an example figure, and B then carries 450.4 kW."
   - After: "Say two sets share 400 kW. As an example, A might draw 50.4 kW. B then carries 450.4 kW."

4. Screen 10 words: a value with its "an example" tag and its effect hung off commas is split.
   - Before: "Here the current settles at 3 times rated, an example, 2480 A, and the breaker trips.[^5]"
   - After: "In this example the current settles at 3 times rated, 2480 A. The breaker trips.[^5]"

5. Practice worked example: one long sentence carrying the method is split into steps.
   - Before: "So check each breaker against the set's current at the breaker's own trip time, read off its decrement curve, not only against the first current."
   - After: "So check each breaker against the set's current at the breaker's own trip time. Read that current off the decrement curve. Do not check only against the first current."
