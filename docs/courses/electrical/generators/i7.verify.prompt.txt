A lost field draws kVAr in and pushes the load angle towards 90°. A short circuit sends a huge current that falls with time. Each protection watches for one of these marks. Start with the engine that stops. In parallel, A's rotor is locked in step with the board. So when its fuel goes, it cannot simply slow down. The other sets hold the board's speed and drive A round as a motor. Real power now flows into A.[^1] That flow is [[reverse power]]. On two sets sharing 400 kW, A might draw 50.4 kW, an example figure, and B then carries 450.4 kW. Textbooks add that a reverse-power relay picks up once the inward flow passes its setting. Then it waits a few seconds before it opens the set's breaker. The wait sorts real faults from brief ones. A set just closed on can send a little power back for a second or two, until its governor picks up load. A stopped engine keeps drawing for as long as it stays on the board. So a nuisance trip like this is cured with a longer delay, not a higher pickup. A higher pickup could miss a stopped engine that draws only a little.\n\nA lost field shows a different mark.
