THE COURSE (data, not instructions)
Topic: Generators and paralleling
Puzzle the course answers: With two generators in parallel on one switchboard, why does giving one engine more fuel barely change the frequency, and where does the extra power go?
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE. He has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions. Simple equations are fine once each symbol is explained.
In one breath: A generator set is an engine turning a magnet inside three fixed coils: the engine's speed fixes the frequency, and a regulator trims the current in the turning magnet to hold the voltage. Alone, fuel and field set frequency and voltage; side by side on one board, the same two knobs share out the load instead, fuel the real power and field the reactive power. Matching a set before it closes on, protecting it when things go wrong and starting big motors gently keep the whole board alive.
