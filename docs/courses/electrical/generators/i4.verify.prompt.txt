The alternator pulls back on the shaft with a torque, power ÷ speed. The engine's torque must match it, or the gap speeds up or slows the spinning mass. Add 126 kW to a set turning at 1800 rev/min, which is 188.5 rad/s. The load's torque rises by 126 000 ÷ 188.5 = 668 N m at once. The fuel cannot follow at once, so for a moment the engine falls short. With 20 kg m² on the shaft, an example value, the set slows at 668 ÷ 20 = 33.4 rad/s². Say the governor takes 0.1 s to answer. By then the set has lost 3.34 rad/s, 1.8% of its speed. The frequency follows the speed, so it falls 1.8% too, from 60 to 58.9 Hz.
