## Five before-and-after examples

1. Explain: a sentence that told Dan where the idea comes from now just teaches it.
   - Before: "Textbooks call a board so big that no one set can move its frequency or voltage an [[infinite bus]]."
   - After: "A board so big that no one set can move its frequency or voltage is called an [[infinite bus]]."

2. Explain: the paragraph opened with a signpost ("Start with why...") and then a long sentence with a trailing clause. It now leads with the point.
   - Before: "Start with why more fuel cannot speed one paralleled set away from the others. Its rotor is locked in step with the board, held there by the same pull that brought it into step as it closed."
   - After: "More fuel cannot speed one paralleled set away from the others. Its rotor is locked in step with the board. The same pull that brought it into step as it closed holds it there."

3. The controls note: one long list held together by colons and semicolons becomes short sentences.
   - Before: "In the kW view: a Governors choice, Droop or Isochronous; A's setting and B's setting, 58.0 to 64.0 Hz in steps of 0.1, both opening at 62.4 Hz; a Droop slider, 1 to 6% in steps of 1, opening at 4%, the same on both; ..."
   - After: "The kW view has a Governors choice, Droop or Isochronous. A's setting and B's setting run from 58.0 to 64.0 Hz in steps of 0.1. Both open at 62.4 Hz. A Droop slider runs from 1 to 6% in steps of 1 and opens at 4%. It is the same on both sets. ..."

4. Screen 12 words: a cross-current sentence with its value and its effect hung off commas becomes three steps.
   - Before: "It is a cross current, 30 000 ÷ (√3 × 440) = 39.4 A here, adding to A's current and its heating."
   - After: "It is a cross current. Here it is 30 000 ÷ (√3 × 440) = 39.4 A. It adds to A's current and its heating."

5. Screen 10 words: an example-setting tag and a "with ... at ..." opener become their own sentences.
   - Before: "Each AVR lets its voltage fall 1 V for every 30 kVAr it carries, an example setting. That gentle fall is voltage droop. With both set to 447.5 V at no kVAr, the lines cross at 440 V, 225 kVAr each."
   - After: "Each AVR lets its voltage fall 1 V for every 30 kVAr it carries. That is an example setting. This gentle fall is voltage droop. Both are set to 447.5 V at no kVAr. So the lines cross at 440 V, with 225 kVAr each."
