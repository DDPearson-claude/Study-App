# i4 plain-English edit: The governor: fuel, speed and droop

Scope: whatAmILookingAt (picture notes, rule, screen words, questions, hints, whys and read
sentences), ignores, explain, analogy and its breaks, and practice. Every fact, number, unit, rule,
formula and [^n] citation is unchanged. Sources, ids, numbers and structure are untouched. The brief
keeps its required template sentence ("The one thing you should see is ___ when you ___."), so it
stays as it was. Where a SHOW screen's words and its read sentence said the same thing, both were
changed together, so they still match.

`i4.lesson.final.json` carries the same edits. Two of its sentences already differed in content from
the verified file ("On a 60 Hz set with 4% droop…" and "…above its rated frequency" with no
"usually"). They keep their own content and are reworded the same way.

About 61 sentences were rewritten, in 52 places in each file. Every SHOW screen stays under 90
words (84, 77, 71, 85 and 73). `node tools/eval/validate.mjs lesson ... --final` passes on both files.

## Five before-and-after examples

1. Explain: a sentence that narrated where a term comes from now just teaches it.
   - Before: "Textbooks call a governor that brings the speed exactly back to its set point, at any load, [[isochronous]]."
   - After: "A governor that brings the speed back exactly to its set point at any load is called [[isochronous]]."

2. Explain: a definition hung off a colon becomes its own sentence, so the new term lands first.
   - Before: "That fall is its [[droop]]: the change in speed, as a percentage, that takes the governor through its full travel, from no load to full load.[^2]"
   - After: "That fall is its [[droop]]. Droop is the percentage change in speed that takes the governor through its full travel, from no load to full load.[^2]"

3. Screen 4 words: a sentence with its speed squeezed in as an aside, and a three-part chain, become short steps.
   - Before: "At 1800 rev/min, 188.5 rad/s, the load's torque rises by 126 000 ÷ 188.5 = 668 N m at once. The fuel has not moved yet. So the load's arrow outgrows the engine's, the flywheel slows and the trace dips."
   - After: "The set turns at 1800 rev/min, which is 188.5 rad/s. So the load's torque rises by 126 000 ÷ 188.5 = 668 N m at once. The fuel has not moved yet. So the load's arrow outgrows the engine's. The flywheel slows and the trace dips."

4. Screen 11 question: a bracketing aside in the middle of the question moves out into its own sentence.
   - Before: "With 4% droop, you want this set at exactly 60 Hz with 378 kW, 75% of its rating. What set point, its no-load frequency, do you choose?"
   - After: "This set has 4% droop. You want it at exactly 60 Hz with 378 kW, 75% of its rating. Its set point is its no-load frequency. What set point do you choose?"

5. Screen 12 hint: a garbled sentence that ran two facts together now says each one plainly.
   - Before: "Work out the line. 4% of 60 Hz is 2.4 Hz below the no-load frequency."
   - After: "Work out the line. 4% of 60 Hz is 2.4 Hz. Full load sits that far below the no-load frequency."
