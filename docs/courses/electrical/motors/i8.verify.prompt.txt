- In a cumulative compound motor the series and shunt fields work together, with fluxes in the same direction; in a differential one they oppose. [R2][R3][R4]
- A shunt coil added to a series motor limits the top speed at light load, and a cumulative compound motor has a definite no-load speed and can run safely with no load. [R5][R6][R7]
- A cumulative compound motor has more starting torque than a shunt motor and steadier speed than a series motor, but not as steady as a shunt motor. [R8][R9][R10]
- A differential compound motor is rarely used because it can run unstably when the load changes; because the series flux opposes the shunt flux, flux falls with load and the speed can rise with load. [R4][R11][R12][R13]
- A long-shunt connection puts the shunt winding across the supply with the series winding between it and the armature; a short-shunt connection puts the shunt winding across the armature only. [R14][R15]
- Compound motors suit presses, shears, crushers and reciprocating compressors. [R16]
