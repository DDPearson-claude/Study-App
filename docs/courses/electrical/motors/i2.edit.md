# i2 plain-English edit: the DOL starter

Only the wording changed. Every number, unit, rule, part number and [^n] citation is the same. A script checked that the footnote markers are unchanged and that every distinct number still appears, in both files.
The lesson narrated no sources, and its footnotes already sat on figures worth looking up. So no citation needed moving.
The pass split comma chains, colon joins and ", and ..." tails into short sentences. It replaced the builder's word "opening" (for a control's starting value) with "starts", which read as a contradiction in "a switch, opening closed". It turned passive and fragment forms into active ones. It said the example motor once, as an assumption.
About 49 sentences were rewritten in i2.lesson.verified.json, through 43 replacements. They are in the brief, the screen walk-through with its quoted screen words and quick-check feedback, the "what it leaves out" note, the explain and its bold rule, the analogy and its limits, the practice, and the summary card's "for" and rule lines.
The same edits went into i2.lesson.final.json wherever it holds the same text. Where the fact-check left the two files worded differently, the same kind of split was made to each file's own wording: the worked example's starting current line and the coil-voltage rule line. The final file's supply-cut line was already clean and stays as it was.
The brief keeps the required "The one thing you should see is ___ when you ___." form. Checks are empty in this lesson. The trap cite fields, number labels, sources and ids are untouched.
`validate.mjs lesson ... --final` passes on both files, with no problems, soft points or warnings.

## 1. Screen 2: the controls, "opening" meant "starts at"
Before: "Isolator (2): a switch, opening closed." ... "Latching contact worn: a fault switch, opening off."
After: "Isolator (2): a switch that starts closed." ... "Latching contact worn: a fault switch that starts off."

## 2. Screen 2: the example values, a stacked caveat
Before: "The 7 and the 4 seconds are example values, drawn so the surge can be watched. A real start draws usually 6 to 8 times full-load current, sometimes more than 10 times, for as long as the load takes to run up."
After: "The 7 times and the 4 seconds are example values, chosen so you can watch the surge. A real start usually draws 6 to 8 times full-load current, sometimes more than 10 times. It lasts as long as the load takes to run up."

## 3. Screen 5: size, a colon list of three rules of thumb
Before: "The supply sets how big a motor can start this way, not the starter. ... Rules of thumb differ: under 4 kW, up to 5 kW, and some supply companies restrict DOL from 5.5 kW."
After: "The supply, not the starter, sets how big a motor can start this way. ... Rules of thumb differ. Some say under 4 kW, others up to 5 kW. Some supply companies restrict DOL from 5.5 kW."

## 4. Screen 8: quick-check feedback, dangling participles
Before: "Tripped, it would stop the motor starting at all, even with Start held." / "Stuck open, the coil could never be fed, so the motor would not start."
After: "If it had tripped, the motor would not start at all, even with Start held." / "If Stop were stuck open, the coil could never be fed. The motor would not start."

## 5. Explain: the bold rule, a clause list inside the sentence
Before: "**A contactor with a latching contact across Start gives no-volt protection. Once its coil circuit opens, by Stop, the overload or a power cut, only Start makes it again.**[^13]"
After: "**A contactor with a latching contact across Start gives no-volt protection. Stop, the overload or a power cut can open its coil circuit. After that, only Start makes it again.**[^13]"
