The currents peak in turn, so the strongest point of the field moves from winding to winding. The field turns at a steady speed, the [[synchronous speed]], ns = 120 × f ÷ p.[^13] Here f is the supply frequency and p the number of poles. On 50 Hz a two-pole field turns at 3000 rpm and a four-pole one at 1500 rpm.\n\nThe rotor is a cage of bars, shorted together by a ring at each end. Now picture the rotor at the field's own speed. The field would not move past the bars, so nothing would be induced. No current means no torque. So the rotor always runs a little slower than the field. That shortfall, as a fraction of ns, is the [[slip]]: (ns − n) ÷ ns. Put load on and the rotor slows a little. The field then sweeps past the bars faster, so more current flows, and the torque rises until it matches the load. Take load off and the rotor speeds up until the slip is small again.
