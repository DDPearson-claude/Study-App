A DOL starter switches a motor straight onto the full supply through a contactor. Start pulls the contactor in. A contact across Start then keeps the coil fed, so the motor runs on after you let go. Anything that opens the coil's circuit drops it all out. It stays out until someone presses Start again.

## The house kit (complete API reference)
You write the BODY of one page: HTML, an optional `<style>`, then one inline `<script>` ending
with `K.ready()`. It runs in a sandboxed iframe on Dan's phone (about 340 px wide, touch) and
laptop, with the global `K` and the kit's stylesheet. No network or storage: no web
addresses, `fetch`, `localStorage`, `alert` or `prompt`.

### Page shape that works
1. `<p class="lead">`: one line saying what is drawn (the app adds title and action).
2. The main visual with its controls right under it: `K.stage('#scene', '#controls')` keeps the
   pair on one phone screen. A choice that re-sorts the picture may sit above it.
3. `<p class="say">`: one sentence that changes with the state and says why. Then
   `<div class="k-readouts">` for numbers the drawing does not label.
4. Secondary figures and anything that grows go below.
5. `<p class="caption">`: one short sentence, at most 20 words: where the numbers come from ("A
   sketch, not measured data") or the rule in the explanation's words. No new claim, no law or
   term the explanation does not teach. The app shows what the model leaves out.

### Keep the answer hidden until Dan moves
Dan predicts before he plays: open on a setting the question is not about, and hide whatever
gives the answer away until his first move:
- class `k-after-move` on any element (a mark, an arrow, a sentence): hidden, its space kept.
  Hide the answer, not the controls or a whole figure (a blank hole fails the test).
- `K.readout({..., afterMove: true})` shows "?" until then; plot series and marks take `afterMove: true` too.
- `K.moved` is true from then on: in `K.update`, write `K.moved ? answer : question`.
- `K.afterMove(fn)` runs `fn` once at that moment; `K.reveal()` marks it from your handlers.

### The pipeline
Controls register themselves; their values form `params` = `{id: value}`.
```
K.model(fn)        fn(params) -> outputs {name: number | string}. Pure: no DOM, no lists or objects.
K.update(fn)       fn(params, outputs): draw everything here. More than one is allowed.
K.at(over)         -> outputs of the model at those params (others take each control's starting value)
K.check(label, fn, {source?})   fn() must return exactly true
K.ready()          call once, last: draws the opening state and runs the checks
K.complete()       at the lesson's end
K.params()  K.outputs()  K.refresh()   (refresh reruns the pipeline after you change your own state)
```
A readout whose id is an output key updates itself.

```

K.choice({id, label, options: [value | {value, label}], value, hint?, into}) -> {el, get, set}
K.toggle({id, label, value: false, hint?, into}) -> {el, get, set}
K.stepper({id, steps: [title | {title, text}], value: 0, compact?, label?, into}) -> {el, get, set, setSteps(list)}
K.button({label, press, secondary?, into}) -> {el, press(), setLabel(text)}
K.drag(el, {control, toValue(x, y)}) -> {el}
```
`control` is a big slider with − / + buttons. Keep `unit` short; `fmt(v)` returns the whole text;
`log: true` spans decades; `snap: [values]` lands a drag exactly on a setting the prediction or a
check names. `choice`: 2-8 short named options; `value` is an option's value or its 0-based
index. `stepper` walks a process with Back / Next (`compact: true` when your diagram shows the
content). `button` is an action (Send it in): use it, not plain buttons. `drag` lets Dan move
the thing itself: `toValue` maps the pointer (viewBox units; px from `el`'s top-left for HTML)
to the control's value; the first touch counts as his move.

### Outputs
```

```
One rounding everywhere a number appears: `decimals` matching the lesson text, and the `.say`
sentence built with `K.fmt(v, {decimals})` or `readout.text()`. Labels under 30 characters.

```
K.plot(target, opts) -> {draw(opts), x(v), y(v), invert(px, py), el, canvas}
```
- `x`, `y`: `{min, max, label, log?, prefix?, unit?, fmt?, ticks?}`. Leave out `y.min`/`y.max` to fit the data.
- `series`: `[{fn: x => y | points: [[x, y], ...], label?, color?, dash?, width?, fill?, dots?, gaps?, afterMove?}]`
- `shade`: `[{between: [a, b], label?, color?, x0?, x1?}]`: fills between two series (by label), a
  series and a constant, or two functions.
- `marks`: `[{x, y, label?, color?, guides?, afterMove?}]`: a dot with a label pill; `guides` adds dashed lines to the axes.
- `regions`: `[{x0, x1, label?} | {y0, y1, label?}]` (a band); `lines`: `[{x | y, label?}]` (a dashed line).
- `label`: the text alternative; `height`; `after(ctx, plot)` draws extras using `plot.x()`, `plot.y()`.

`draw(opts)` redraws with `opts` over the creation options: call it in `K.update`. Values must be
finite (`gaps: true` only where a gap is real). Series are navy, then teal; `color: 'muted',
dash: true` for a baseline.
```
K.bars(target, {max?, unit?, prefix?, decimals?, fmt?}) -> {draw(items)}      items: [{label, value, color?}], values >= 0
K.anim({step(dt, t), reset?, label?, autoplay?, button?, into}) -> {el, play, pause, toggle, reset, playing()}
```
`anim` adds a Play button (text `label`); `reset` adds Reset. When a switch, button or drag
already starts the motion, pass `button: false` and call `play()` from that control: one control
per action. In `step` advance your state (`dt` in seconds) and redraw; return `false` to stop. To
drive a slider, keep the exact value yourself (the slider moves in whole steps).

### Rules for a great interactive
- One idea. The answer to Dan's prediction shows within about 3 seconds of his first move (add
  a K.check that it does). Include something Dan causes and a case that surprises. One or two
  controls, three at most.
- Phone first: nothing wider than 340 px, no fixed widths over 300 px, rows wrap, no hover-only
  information, tap targets at least 44 px. Size tiles and grid columns in rem (`minmax(6rem,
  1fr)`): text grows a quarter at Text size XL, and no word may split across two lines.
- Every number shown is a control, computed by the rule shown, a constant from the lesson, an
  example value named once in the lead, or a date from the lesson. Never invent data, rates or chances.
- The `.say` sentence describes what is on screen now, plainly and warmly, and reads right at the
  extremes ("none of the 100"). Never claim Dan did something ("When you slid it to 20"):
  describe the state, or write conditionally ("Push it past 20 and…").
- 3-5 `K.check` known answers worked out by hand: an edge case, a shape fact (rises, halves,
  always last), and one answer from outside your formula. `source` may ONLY be a URL from the
  lesson's own sources, and only on a check whose label restates what that source's quote says;
  a result of your rule, or a fact about your drawing, takes none. Compare numbers with
  `K.near(a, b, tol)`: tolerance about a millionth of the size, a thousandth where rounding meets roots.
- `K.anim` for motion, never timers: soft, unhurried, nothing popping in. Parts arrive about 0.6
  s apart by `K.fade(el, K.arrive(T, t0))`; travel and growth use `K.ease`; a demonstration takes
  3-5 s. No `vh` units, `position: fixed` or `height: 100%`.
- Ideas without numbers (mechanism, process, structure, history, concept): prefer a timeline
  slider, a named choice, a sorter or a drag; use `K.stepper` only when every step changes the
  drawing. Checks assert order and structure facts.
- A timeline runs forward: a slider rising left to right, its `fmt` showing the date (−500 as
  "500 BC"), or a stepper of named events in order. A lesson's calendar-year control keeps its
  range, its `fmt` showing the year; years after the first date are for new controls. Each dated
  event appears as he reaches its date.

### The self-test
Before Dan sees it, the app loads your body 340, 720 and 1040 px wide, runs every check, moves
every control, button, animation and drag, reveals the after-move parts, and looks again at Text
size XL and in the other theme. It fails on an exception, NaN or undefined on the page, an update
over 150 ms, sideways overflow, text cut off or over other text, SVG text under 11 px on a phone,
a line or arrowhead through a label, text under 3:1 contrast with what is behind it, a colour
name, a blank hole until a move, or no `K.ready()`.

  const g = 9.81;

<script>
  // Source [1] of the lesson: static friction matches the push, up to a limit.
  const SOURCE = 'https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friction';
  const M = 20, G = 9.81, GRIP = 0.5;          // the example box and floor (assumed values)
  const MOST = GRIP * M * G;                   // the most the floor can grip with: 98.1 N
  // A simplification, said in the caption: the grip stays at MOST while the box slides (real
  // sliding friction is usually a little lower than the most a floor grips a box at rest).
  const S = 0.75;                              // drawing units per newton, the same for both arrows
  const N = (f) => K.fmt(f, { decimals: Number.isInteger(f) ? 0 : 1 }) + ' N';
  // Sliding, what is left of the push after the grip speeds the box up (a negative one slows it).
  const accel = (push) => (push - MOST) / M;

  // The cause Dan controls: his push, by dragging the handle or the slider (they move together).
  // A drag near the limit lands on it exactly (snap).
  const push = K.control({ id: 'push', label: 'Your push', min: 0, max: 160, step: 1, value: 40, unit: 'N', snap: [MOST], into: '#controls' });
  K.stage('#scene', '#controls');
  const handX = (f) => 136 - f * S;
  K.drag('#hand', { control: 'push', toValue: (x) => (136 - x) / S });   // its first touch reveals, like a slider move

  // The pure model, for a box that starts at rest: the floor pushes back as hard as the push, up to its limit.
  K.model((p) => ({ grip: Math.min(p.push, MOST), slides: p.push > MOST ? 'yes' : 'no' }));

  // The box's own state while it slides. The motion runs whenever the push beats the grip, and
  // stops when the box stands still again: no Play button, the push is what starts it.
  let speed = 0, slid = 0;
  const slide = K.anim({ button: false, step: (dt) => {
    speed = Math.max(0, speed + accel(push.get()) * dt);
    slid += speed * dt;
    draw(K.params(), K.outputs());
    return speed > 0 || push.get() > MOST;
  } });

  const svg = document.getElementById('scene');
  // A straight arrow from x1 to x2 at height y: a line and a filled head.
  function arrow(g, x1, x2, y, cls) {
    g.textContent = '';
    const dir = x2 > x1 ? 1 : -1;
    if (Math.abs(x2 - x1) < 12) return null;
    const line = K.svg('line', { class: cls, x1, y1: y, x2: x2 - dir * 9, y2: y });
    g.append(line, K.svg('path', { class: cls, d: `M${x2} ${y}l${-dir * 11} -7v14z` }));
    return line;
  }
  document.getElementById('most').setAttribute('x1', 232 - MOST * S);

  function draw(p, o) {
    // The floor's marks drift back while the box slides (the view follows the box).
    const off = (slid * 40) % 20, ticks = document.getElementById('ticks');
    ticks.textContent = '';
    for (let x = 10 - off; x < 350; x += 20) ticks.appendChild(K.svg('line', { class: 'tick', x1: x, y1: 101, x2: x - 8, y2: 111 }));
    const hx = handX(p.push), grip = speed > 0 ? MOST : o.grip;
    document.getElementById('hand').setAttribute('cx', hx);
    const yours = arrow(document.getElementById('yours'), hx + 10, 148, 70, 'yours');
    const gripLine = arrow(document.getElementById('grip'), 232, 232 - grip * S, 122, 'grip');
    // Labels move with their arrows; K.labels keeps them off the arrows, the box and the limit line.
    K.labels('#labels', [
      { x: (hx + 148) / 2, y: 56, text: p.push ? 'push ' + N(p.push) : 'no push', class: 'lab' },
      { x: 152, y: 126, text: 'grip limit ' + N(MOST), anchor: 'end', class: 'lab limit' },
    ].concat(K.moved && grip ? [{ x: 232 - grip * S / 2, y: 148, text: 'floor\'s grip ' + N(grip), class: 'lab' }] : []),
      { avoid: [yours, gripLine, '#most', '#box', '#hand'].filter(Boolean) });
    svg.setAttribute('aria-label', 'A 20 kg box on a floor, pushed with ' + N(p.push) + (K.moved ? '; the floor grips back with ' + N(grip) + (speed > 0 ? ', and the box is sliding.' : ', and the box stays put.') : '.'));

    let text;
    if (!K.moved) text = 'The hand pushes with ' + N(p.push) + ' and the box stays put. How hard is the floor pushing back? Make your guess, then drag the handle.';
    else if (p.push > MOST) text = 'The push (' + N(p.push) + ') is more than the floor can grip with (' + N(MOST) + '), so the box slides and keeps speeding up: ' + K.fmt(speed, { decimals: 1 }) + ' m/s now.';
    else if (speed > 0) text = 'The push is less than the grip again, so the grip slows the sliding box down: ' + K.fmt(speed, { decimals: 1 }) + ' m/s now.';
    else if (p.push === 0) text = 'No push, so the floor pushes back with nothing: its grip only ever answers a push.';
    else text = 'The floor pushes back with exactly ' + N(o.grip) + ', as hard as the hand pushes, so the box stays put. It can grip with up to ' + N(MOST) + '.';
    document.getElementById('say').textContent = text;
  }
  K.update((p, o) => {
    if (p.push > MOST && !slide.playing()) slide.play();
    draw(p, o);
  });

## Refresher lessons: typed answers, written answers, the case file, the summary

A refresher course's steps lesson (a ready-made course built in Claude Code) has four more kit
pieces. The app passes the page two pieces of checked data, as it passes the theme: the course's
case file (`K.caseFile`) and the lesson's own `{written, summary}` (`K.lesson`). Name what you want
by id; the kit draws the checked words, so never retype a figure, a point or a summary line.

```
K.figure(id) -> {id, label, value, unit, text, part, lesson, rule, work, practice}   a given or worked figure; print f.text ("23.08 A"), f.work its working; practice is true for a practice value
K.scenario({into, open?, title?, text?})                         the case's scenario panel (folds); a lesson may give its own
K.carried({parts: ['1a'], into})  -> {el, refresh()}             earlier Section A parts at the course's figures
K.answer({id, label?, unit, answer, decimals?, tolerance?, accept?, slips?, into, onCheck?, onEnter?})
    -> {el, input, check(), result, right(), missed(), reveal(), reset(), set(text)}
K.answer.grade(text, spec) -> {state: 'right'|'wrong'|'empty'|'unreadable', value, accepted?, slip?, hint?}
K.written({id, into, onMark?}) -> {el, id, stage(), answered()}
K.summary({into, kept?})
```

**Typed answers** (Section A, where a C&G paper has him write a figure). A decimal-keypad field;
your Check button calls `a.check()` (Enter does too) and you write the feedback from the result.
`answer` is the course's figure, each line worked from the rounded figures shown; it is accepted
within half a unit in its last place (`decimals`, default 2). `accept: [13.32]` adds the answer
worked from unrounded figures; `slips: [{value: 50, hint: 'You left out √3.'}]` names a usual slip
and its hint (`r.hint`). A unit typed after the number ("13.33 A") is fine. Give the part's
case-file id as the answer's id ('1b'), so `K.carried` knows when he missed it: a part got wrong
and never right, or shown with `a.reveal()`, is carried forward at the course's figure and says so.
After two wrong tries, show the working and `reveal()`; Continue once `right()`.
The self-test grades every answer: its figure and each accepted one right (with the unit too), a
near miss and each slip refused, and the figure typed into the field and checked on its step.

**Written answers** (Section B: "Explain why…", "State two…"). `K.written({id: 'b1', into})` shows
`lesson.written.b1`'s question, a box for his words and Check my answer. Claude marks them against
the lesson's points through the app (never from the page): each point comes back with an ink tick
or a dashed ring, a statement that is wrong named in red, one line on what he did well and one
nudge; one more go, then the model answer. Without Claude, the points are a checklist he ticks.
No marks, no grades, nothing to score. `onMark(r)` runs when a result is drawn; let him Continue
once `answered()`.

**The summary** (Keep this, the last step). `K.summary({into: '#keep'})` draws `lesson.summary`
as a card (what it is for, the rule, the traps, the case's figures so far, where it lives) and says
it is the first page of the chapter in his Book. A figure resting on a practice value is tagged so; the
traps' and points' cites stay as data and are never shown. Then the Finish button calls `K.complete()`.

**STEPS entries** (tools/course/guide.mjs reads them for the Book and the read-through): a
typed-answer step keeps `want` (the answer as printed, '13.33 A'); a written step is `{kind:
'written', written: 'b1', name, task}`; the summary step is `{kind: 'summary', name: 'Keep this'}`;
the recall opener is a DO step like any other. The read-through (and the Book) shows each step as
it looks finished, as a still picture with no controls, and its words in its place. Three fields
make that read as plain sense (Dan, 20 Oct: "some of the text doesn't make sense… the images are
blank"):
- `read` on a SHOW step: its sentence for reading, when its own words ask him to act ("Drag…",
  "Work each step yourself first, then show it"). Say what the finished picture shows.
- `read` on a DO step only when its task asks him to act on the screen: the same question, asked
  for reading. Never the answer: the read-through shows the question, the choices, the answer
  and why on their own (guide.mjs keeps the task and warns when a `read` gives the answer away).
- `still`: the state the picture shows, when the step's end needs his moves: `{control id: value}`
  (`{h: 35}`), or a function that sets it (`still: () => runTo(SPLIT)`, cards placed in their
  phases; `still: () => pick(...)`, a second question on the step answered too). The state is the
  one its sentence ends on. `still: false` leaves the picture out. guide.mjs leaves out a still
  that would show an empty box (a lane with no cards), and warns about it and about choices on
  the screen that nobody chose, so give those steps a `still`.
The guide drops a sentence that tells him to drag, tap, press or choose, a "Right." or "Still
there." verdict, and the scenario after its first showing; write the step so it still reads
whole without them.
