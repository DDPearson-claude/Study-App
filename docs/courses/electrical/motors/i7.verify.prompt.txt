Its current is set by the supply voltage and its own resistance, not by the load. That leaves more room for Ia Ra, so more armature current flows. Torque goes with flux times current, so the torque rises. The speed settles where the torque matches the load. It does not climb back to where it was. The fall is small because the flux holds steady. On the picture's 600 V motor, with its example no-load current, the back-emf falls only from 598.8 V running light to 575.8 V at rated load.
