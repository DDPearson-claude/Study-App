What happens when 2 and 3 are operated?
The isolator (2) when closed allows current to flow into the
contactor. The start button (3) is normally open, when this is
closed the current flows into the coil (4) energising it. The coil
magnetises the main contacts and pulls them closed and holds
them in place, enabling the motor to connect to the supply and
become energised.
What happens when 8 is operated?
8 is a normally closed stop button. It works on the opposite
principle of the start button. The start button 'makes' the
circuit, the stop button 'breaks' the circuit. Once operated, the
stop button disconnects the circuit which de-energises the coil,
releasing the contacts and isolating the motor from the supply.
What is the purpose of 9 and 10?
9 and 10 work together to disconnect the circuit in the
same way as the stop button. The thermal overload trips
are simply bi-metallic strips which will bend when too
much heat is passed through. The bend in the strips
causes the overload release contact to break the circuit,
which de-energises the coil, releasing the contacts and
isolating the motor from the supply.
What is the purpose of 4?
Inside the contactor coil is a solenoid. Current energises
the coil which magnetises the solenoid, which in turn
pulls the latching contacts closed. If the current is
interrupted (from either 8 or 9/10) the could de-
energises, the magnetic effect disappears and the
latching contacts release.
Component list:
1) 3-phase and neutral supply
2) 4 pole isolator
3) Normally open (N/O) start button. This is 'sprung' meaning it'll return to
N/O once released.
4) 230V contactor coil (only connected to one phase and neutral meaning only
230V is present.
5) Main contacts. These join the supply to the load once 'pulled' in and held.
6) Delta connected load.
7) Latching (maintaining) contacts. These 'pull' and 'hold' the main contacts in
place, these are pulled into place by the magnetic field created by the
energising of (4).
8) Normally closed (N/C) stop button
9) Thermal overload trip
10) Overload release contact
Where are these starters used and what are their limitations?
1) Generally used for the starting and stopping of small machinery
up to 5kW.
2) Compressors, pumps and fans are typically controlled by DOL
starters.
3) Due to the nature of the starter, a high in-rush of current can be
tolerated, unlike a traditional switch.
4) DOL starters have thermal overloads, which can mean 'overuse'
or regular stopping and starting can cause these to operate.
