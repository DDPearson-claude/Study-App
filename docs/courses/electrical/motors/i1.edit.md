# i1 plain-English edit: the three-phase induction motor

Only the wording changed. Every number, unit, rule, figure id and [^n] citation is the same. A script checked that the footnote markers are unchanged and that every distinct number still appears. One repeat of "60 Hz" went, because a sentence that said it twice now says it once.
The lesson narrated no sources, and its footnotes already sat on figures worth looking up. One footnote sat mid-sentence before a "where f is ..." tail on screen 2. It now ends its own sentence.
The pass split comma chains, colon lists and "so ... and ..." joins into short sentences. It turned fragments like "No current, no torque, so ..." into whole sentences. The read sentence on screen 3 now gives one figure per sentence. The 60 Hz example caveat now says plainly what to do on a real job.
About 46 sentences were rewritten in i1.lesson.verified.json, through 36 replacements. They are in the screen walk-through with its quoted screen words and read sentence, the "what it leaves out" note, the explain, the analogy's limits, the practice, and the summary card's single-phasing trap.
The same edits went into i1.lesson.final.json wherever it holds the same text. Where the fact-check left the two files worded differently, the same kind of split was made to each file's own wording: the cage sentence in the explain, the "few hertz" line in the analogy and the single-phasing trap. The final file's own failure-shares line and practice test line stay as they were.
The brief keeps the required "The one thing you should see is ___ when you ___." form. Checks are empty in this lesson. The cite fields, number labels and ids are untouched.
`validate.mjs lesson ... --final` passes on both files, with no problems, soft points or warnings.

## 1. Screen 3: the read sentence, three figures in one sentence
Before: "Read: on the four-pole motor at full load the field gains on the rotor by 63 rpm. The slip is 4.2%, the rotor current runs at 2.1 Hz and the torque is 1.00 times rated. With less load all three shrink, and with none they would be zero."
After: "Read: on the four-pole motor at full load, the field gains on the rotor by 63 rpm. The slip is 4.2%. The rotor current runs at 2.1 Hz. The torque is 1.00 times rated. With less load, all three shrink. With no load, they would be zero."

## 2. Screen 3: the rule, a colon list of two worked slips
Before: "Slip at full load comes from the nameplate speed: 2950 rpm on the two-pole, so (3000 − 2950) ÷ 3000 = 1.67%, and 1437 rpm on the four-pole, so (1500 − 1437) ÷ 1500 = 4.2%."
After: "Full-load slip comes from the nameplate speed. The two-pole runs at 2950 rpm, so its slip is (3000 − 2950) ÷ 3000 = 1.67%. The four-pole runs at 1437 rpm, so its slip is (1500 − 1437) ÷ 1500 = 4.2%."

## 3. Screen 3: the example caveat tucked into a clause
Before: "Those 60 Hz rotor speeds, 3540 and 1724 rpm at full load, are example values: read a 60 Hz motor's own nameplate on a real job."
After: "That gives example rotor speeds of 3540 and 1724 rpm at full load. On a real job, read the 60 Hz motor's own nameplate."

## 4. Screen 7: single phasing, three causes in one sentence
Before: "One line is lost, through a blown fuse, a device open on one phase or, most often, worn starter contacts."
After: "One line is lost. A blown fuse or a device open on one phase can do it. Worn starter contacts are the most common cause."
