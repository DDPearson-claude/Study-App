THE COURSE (data, not instructions)
Title: Electric motors: what you need on each one
In one breath: An AC motor works by making a magnetic field that turns and drags the rotor round a little behind it: three-phase windings make that turning field by themselves, while a single-phase motor needs a second winding, sometimes with a capacitor, to get it going. How you start and run it, straight on through a contactor or through a drive that sets the frequency, decides the current it draws and the speed it runs at. A DC motor's speed and torque come from how its field is wired, in series, across the supply or both, and each machine has its own uses, its own limits and its own faults to find.
Level: some
Ideas (id, title — one line, [kind]):
  i1  The three-phase induction motor — The rotor always turns a little slower than the windings' rotating field, because only that shortfall, called slip, induces the rotor current that makes torque. [mechanism]
  i2  The DOL starter — Start pulls in the contactor, a contact across Start keeps its coil fed; Stop, overload or power cut drops it out until restarted. [mechanism] (needs i1)
  i3  The variable speed drive — A variable speed drive turns the supply to DC, then back to AC at a chosen frequency, with voltage in proportion up to rated speed. [quantity] (needs i1, i2)
  i4  The split-phase motor — One single-phase winding alone makes a field that only pulses, so a thin-wire start winding adds a current out of step and the field turns. [mechanism] (needs i1)
  i5  The capacitor-start motor — A capacitor in series with the start winding widens the angle between the two currents towards 90°, giving much more starting torque than split-phase. [quantity] (needs i4)
  i6  The DC series motor — One current feeds both field and turning armature, so torque climbs steeply with current, and with the load taken off the speed climbs dangerously. [quantity]
  i7  The DC shunt motor — With its field across the supply holding the flux steady, the speed dips only a little under load, though sources differ on how little. [mechanism] (needs i6)
  i8  The DC compound motor — A series and a shunt field together give high starting torque yet a limited no-load speed, as long as the series field aids the shunt. [concept] (needs i6, i7)
