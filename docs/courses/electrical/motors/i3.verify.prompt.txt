Topic: Electric motors: what you need on each one
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE: he has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions.
This lesson: i3 "The variable speed drive" — A variable speed drive turns the supply to DC, then back to AC at a chosen frequency, with voltage in proportion up to rated speed. [quantity]
Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i4 The split-phase motor — One single-phase winding alone makes a field that only pulses, so a thin-wire start winding adds a current out of step and the field turns.
- i5 The capacitor-start motor — A capacitor in series with the start winding widens the angle between the two currents towards 90°, giving much more starting torque than split-phase.
- i6 The DC series motor — One current feeds both field and turning armature, so torque climbs steeply with current, and with the load taken off the speed climbs dangerously.
- i7 The DC shunt motor — With its field across the supply holding the flux steady, the speed dips only a little under load, though sources differ on how little.
- i8 The DC compound motor — A series and a shunt field together give high starting torque yet a limited no-load speed, as long as the series field aids the shunt.

[R191][R192][R193]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Star-delta puts each winding on 1/sqrt(3) of the line voltage while starting; the changeover can briefly open the supply and give a current spike, which on one generator maker's figures can be as high as 5 x run kVA if it is badly timed, but ABB says is typically lower than a DOL start. [R194][R195][R196]  [CONTESTED: Sources differ on the size of the star-to-delta changeover spike: ABB says typically lower than a DOL start; a generator maker warns it can reach 5 x run kVA if the changeover is not set well.]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The overload relay is set to the motor's rated current, not its trip current: no trip at 1.05 times the setting, trip at 1.2 times; it also protects against phase failure, and it has no power contacts so it can only work through the contactor. [R197][R198][R199]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- After an overload trip the relay will not reset until it has cooled, which lets the motor cool before it is restarted. [R200]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The contactor coil must have the right voltage: the usual limits are 0.85 to 1.1 times its rating; too low a voltage can fail to close it, make it chatter and melt the coil, and too high a voltage can burn it out. [R201][R202][R203][R204]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A buzzing or chattering contactor points to low or unstable coil voltage, a poor control connection, a wrong coil, dirty or unseated magnet faces, or a damaged shading ring; check the voltage across the coil terminals while it is closing. [R205]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A contactor can hold line voltage even when its coil circuit is off, so isolate and prove dead before touching a starter: prove all supply conductors dead, with a voltage indicator that is proved before and after use. [R206][R207][R208][R209][R210]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Several Stop buttons go in series (any one stops the motor) and several Start buttons in parallel with the holding contact (any one starts it). [R211][R212][R213]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Some supply utilities restrict direct-on-line starting for motors rated at 5.5 kW (7.5 hp) or higher. [R172]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In a star-delta starter only 1/sqrt(3) of the motor current flows through the main (line) contactor, so an overload relay mounted on it is set to 0.58 times the motor current. [R214]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The synchronous speed of the turning field is 120 f / p rpm for f hertz and p poles; an induction motor's shaft runs a little slower than it. [R82][R5]  (whole topic)
- In a series motor the same current flows through field and armature, and its speed can climb to dangerous levels if it runs without a load. [R215][R216]  (whole topic)
- A motor that is turning also acts as a generator; the emf it makes is called back emf and it opposes the supply. [R217][R218]  (whole topic)
