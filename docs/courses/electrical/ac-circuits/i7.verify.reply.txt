   "now": "- Safety first: near resonance the coil and the capacitor can each carry far more than the supply's voltage,[^10] so before a coil and a capacitor in series go live, check each one's voltage rating against at least Q times the supply, not the supply alone,[^9] and √2 times that if it is rated in peak volts. A 6 Ω resistor with 20 Ω of coil reactance and 12 Ω of capacitor reactance on 230 V mains[^1] draws 23 A, and puts 23 × 20 = 460 V across the coil. Isolate, prove dead and discharge any capacitor before handling either part.\n- Each reactance: X_L = 2πfL[^6] and X_C = 1/(2πfC),[^7] with f in hertz, L in henries and C in farads (10 µF is 0.00001 F).\n- The net reactance: X = X_L − X_C. If it comes out positive the circuit is inductive and the current lags; negative, capacitive, and the current leads.\n- The impedance: Z = √(R² + X²).[^5]\n- The current: I = V ÷ Z. Each part's voltage is then I times its own R, X_L or X_C.\n- The resonant frequency: f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)). There Z = R, the current is V ÷ R[^8] and Q = X_L ÷ R.\n\n**Rule of thumb:** With a coil and a capacitor in series, take the smaller reactance from the larger first, then use Pythagoras with R. Near resonance, when X_L is more than R, expect the coil's and the capacitor's meters to read more than the supply's.\n\n**Worked example:** Lesson 4's coil and lesson 5's capacitor in series with 2 Ω, on a 10 V test supply at 50 Hz: X_L = X_C = 10 Ω, so X = 0, Z = 2 Ω and I = 5 A, in step with the supply. Each part reads 5 × 10 = 50 V, and Q = 10 ÷ 2 = 5. Move the same circuit to 60 Hz, the frequency most common on board ship:[^2] X_L rises to 12 Ω and X_C falls to 8.33 Ω, so X = 3.67 Ω, inductive. Z = √(2² + 3.67²) = 4.18 Ω, I = 10 ÷ 4.18 = 2.39 A, lagging, and the coil reads 2.39 × 12 = 28.7 V, still nearly three times the supply.\n\nLesson 8 turns to power, where the angle between the current and the voltage decides how much is really used.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Using lesson 6's formula with one reactance, leaving the coil or the capacitor out.\n- Adding X_L and X_C, or putting both under Pythagoras with R. They point opposite ways, so take one from the other first.\n- Putting C in as ohms or in microfarads. 100 µF is not 100 Ω; convert it to farads and work out X_C.\n- Thinking no part in series can read more than the supply. True of resistors, or of R with just a coil or just a capacitor, but not of a coil and a capacitor together, above all near resonance.\n- Thinking R moves the resonant frequency. It changes the peak's height and sharpness, not where it sits."
