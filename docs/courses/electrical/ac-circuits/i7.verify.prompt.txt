Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- In a series RLC circuit the current is greatest at the resonant frequency, where impedance is minimum and X_L equals X_C. [R1]
- At resonance the voltage across L or C in a series circuit is Q times the applied voltage, which can be dangerously high. [R2][R3]
- In a pure inductor the voltage leads the current, that is, the current lags behind the voltage. [R4]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Inductive reactance is X_L = 2πfL, in ohms, and is directly proportional to the frequency of the alternating current. [R5][R6]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Inductors oppose changes in current through them by dropping a voltage directly proportional to the rate of change of current, where resistors drop one proportional to the current. [R7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A 10 mH inductor has a reactance of 3.7699 ohms at 60 Hz, 7.5398 ohms at 120 Hz and 157.0796 ohms at 2500 Hz. [R8]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- High power electric motors are inductive loads (Matrix electrical installation Level 3 teacher guide, SECONDARY). [R9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A City & Guilds 2366 sample question asks for the inductive reactance of a 20 mH inductor on a 50 Hz supply (6.28 ohms, computed from X_L = 2πfL). [R10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A capacitor opposes changes in voltage by drawing or supplying current as it charges or discharges to the new voltage level. [R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In a capacitor the current leads the voltage, so the voltage lags behind the current. [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Capacitive reactance is X_C = 1/(2πfC) and decreases as frequency rises, the opposite of inductive reactance. [R13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For i5: the memory aid ELI the ICE man: the emf (E) leads the current (I) in an inductor (L), and the current leads the emf in a capacitor (C) (screen 9's rows and the practice). [R14]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The phasors for a resistor and a reactance are 90 degrees apart, so the series impedance comes from Pythagoras' law. [R15]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Voltages 90 degrees apart do not add arithmetically: a 6 V source and an 8 V source at 90 degrees give 10 V. [R16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Inductive reactance is X_L = 2πfL, so a measured reactance at a known frequency gives the inductance. [R5]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- UK domestic supply is nominally 230 V, 50 Hz, kept within 216 V to 253 V by the Electricity Safety, Quality and Continuity Regulations. [R17]  (whole topic)
- Ships mostly run 60 Hz, because higher frequency lets motors and generators run faster and so be smaller for a given power. [R18]  (whole topic)
