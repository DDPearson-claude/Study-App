Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Capacitors and inductors absorb energy during one half-cycle and return it in the other, so the power wave goes below zero. [R1]
- True power is measured in watts, reactive power in volt-amps reactive (VAR), and their combination is apparent power in volt-amps. [R2][R3]
- Power factor is true power divided by apparent power, which equals the cosine of the phase angle; average power is V I cos φ with rms values. [R4][R5]
- Any power factor less than 1 means the wiring must carry more current to deliver the same true power. [R6]
- In a resistor, voltage and current are in phase: both curves reach maxima and minima together. [R7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The product of rms voltage and rms current in a resistor equals the average power. [R8]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The instantaneous power in a resistor oscillates at twice the frequency of the voltage and never goes negative. [R9][R10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The phasors for a resistor and a reactance are 90 degrees apart, so the series impedance comes from Pythagoras' law. [R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Voltages 90 degrees apart do not add arithmetically: a 6 V source and an 8 V source at 90 degrees give 10 V. [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Inductive reactance is X_L = 2πfL, so a measured reactance at a known frequency gives the inductance. [R13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- UK domestic supply is nominally 230 V, 50 Hz, kept within 216 V to 253 V by the Electricity Safety, Quality and Continuity Regulations. [R14]  (whole topic)
- Ships mostly run 60 Hz, because higher frequency lets motors and generators run faster and so be smaller for a given power. [R15]  (whole topic)
