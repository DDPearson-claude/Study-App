Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- A capacitor opposes changes in voltage by drawing or supplying current as it charges or discharges to the new voltage level. [R1]
- In a capacitor the current leads the voltage, so the voltage lags behind the current. [R2]
- Capacitive reactance is X_C = 1/(2πfC) and decreases as frequency rises, the opposite of inductive reactance. [R3]
- A single coil turned at constant speed in a uniform magnetic field produces an emf that varies sinusoidally in time. [R4]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The induced emf depends on how quickly the flux through the coil is changing, so a faster-turning coil gives a larger voltage. [R5][R6]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For i1: a real a.c. generator holds its terminal voltage by adjusting its d.c. field current (screen 12's Why? and the explain). [R7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In a resistor, voltage and current are in phase: both curves reach maxima and minima together. [R8]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The product of rms voltage and rms current in a resistor equals the average power. [R9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The instantaneous power in a resistor oscillates at twice the frequency of the voltage and never goes negative. [R10][R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- In a pure inductor the voltage leads the current, that is, the current lags behind the voltage. [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Inductive reactance is X_L = 2πfL, in ohms, and is directly proportional to the frequency of the alternating current. [R13][R14]  (from an idea this one builds on)

- UK domestic supply is nominally 230 V, 50 Hz, kept within 216 V to 253 V by the Electricity Safety, Quality and Continuity Regulations. [R19]  (whole topic)
- Ships mostly run 60 Hz, because higher frequency lets motors and generators run faster and so be smaller for a given power. [R20]  (whole topic)
