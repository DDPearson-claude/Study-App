Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i3 Resistance in AC: power, moment by moment — Multiplying a resistor's voltage and current instant by instant gives a power wave at twice the frequency, averaging rms volts times rms amps.
- i4 Inductance: the coil that pushes back — A coil pushes back against changing current, so its current lags the voltage by a quarter cycle and its reactance, X_L = 2πfL, rises with frequency.
- i5 Capacitance: the store that fills and empties — A capacitor draws current as it fills and empties, so its current leads the voltage by a quarter cycle and its reactance, X_C = 1/(2πfC), falls with frequency.
- i6 Series RL and RC: impedance you can measure — In series, the resistor's and the reactance's voltages add as arrows at right angles, so three meter readings give the impedance triangle and an unknown coil's inductance.
- i7 Series RLC and resonance — In series, a coil's and a capacitor's reactances subtract and cancel at one frequency, where the current peaks and each can carry more voltage than the supply.
- i8 Real, reactive and apparent power — Out of step, the power wave dips below zero, so watts used, vars sloshing back and volt-amps supplied form a triangle whose ratio is the power factor.
- i9 Power factor correction — Size a capacitor across an inductive load from the power triangle, so it supplies the load's vars locally and cuts the supply current while the watts stay put.
- i10 Three-phase: star and delta — Three windings 120° apart connect in star, where line voltage is √3 times phase voltage, or delta, where line current is; power is √3 V_L I_L cos φ.
