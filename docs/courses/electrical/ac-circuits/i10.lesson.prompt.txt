Research notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- A three-phase generator has three coils of wire placed at 120 degrees to each other and rotated in a magnetic field. [1]
- The line voltage is found by taking the phasor sum of the voltages in two phases, which gives the factor root 3. [2]
- In balanced star circuits line voltage is root 3 times phase voltage and line current equals phase current; in balanced delta the roles reverse. [3]
- Three-phase power equals the product of line-to-line voltage, line current, root 3 and the power factor. [4]
- The neutral conductor carries no current in a balanced three-phase network. [5]
- Triplen harmonic currents add within the neutral of a 4-wire Y-Y system, so 'balanced means no neutral current' holds for sine-wave loads. [6]
- A single coil turned at constant speed in a uniform magnetic field produces an emf that varies sinusoidally in time. [7]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The induced emf depends on how quickly the flux through the coil is changing, so a faster-turning coil gives a larger voltage. [8][9]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For i1: a real a.c. generator holds its terminal voltage by adjusting its d.c. field current (screen 12's Why? and the explain). [10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The phasors for a resistor and a reactance are 90 degrees apart, so the series impedance comes from Pythagoras' law. [11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Voltages 90 degrees apart do not add arithmetically: a 6 V source and an 8 V source at 90 degrees give 10 V. [12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Inductive reactance is X_L = 2πfL, so a measured reactance at a known frequency gives the inductance. [13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Capacitors and inductors absorb energy during one half-cycle and return it in the other, so the power wave goes below zero. [14]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- True power is measured in watts, reactive power in volt-amps reactive (VAR), and their combination is apparent power in volt-amps. [15][16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Power factor is true power divided by apparent power, which equals the cosine of the phase angle; average power is V I cos φ with rms values. [17][18]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Any power factor less than 1 means the wiring must carry more current to deliver the same true power. [19]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- For i8: a generator is rated in kVA at a stated power factor, with a kW figure as well, so it is checked against both: 100 kVA supplies 80 kW at 0.8, and a 60 Hz diesel set's data sheet reads 630 kVA and 504 kW at 0.8. [10][20][21]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- UK domestic supply is nominally 230 V, 50 Hz, kept within 216 V to 253 V by the Electricity Safety, Quality and Continuity Regulations. [22]  (whole topic)
- Ships mostly run 60 Hz, because higher frequency lets motors and generators run faster and so be smaller for a given power. [23]  (whole topic)
