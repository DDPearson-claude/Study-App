    "text": "An insulation monitor tells you there is an earth fault, but not where. It reads every feeder at once, in parallel. So you find the fault live, while the system runs. Then you isolate the circuit it leads you to and test that circuit dead.\n\nThe first way is to open feeders one at a time, with permission and with essential services covered, until the reading recovers.[^3] Opening a healthy feeder leaves the fault connected, so the reading barely moves. Opening the faulty feeder takes the fault off the system, so the reading jumps. The trap is a load that switches itself. A pump on a pressure switch can stop just as you open the wrong feeder. Its contactor lifts the faulty motor off the system, and the reading recovers for the wrong reason.[^7] So close the feeder again. If it holds the fault, the alarm comes straight back.\n\nThe second way keeps everything running. A leakage clamp goes round all three cores of a feeder, like the toroid in an RCD. Load current goes out and back through it, so it cancels. The clamp reads only current that leaves the cores for the hull. At 60 Hz, that includes charging current. Once a fault shifts the hull to one line, every healthy feeder passes three times its healthy charging current. The faulty feeder carries all of it home through the fault. In an ideal system that makes it read the most. But a long healthy feeder can read nearly as much, so a short faulty feeder is hard to pick out.[^5]\n\nA fault locator removes that doubt. It joins the live cores to the hull for a moment, through a current limit. Its pulse flows out on the cores, through the fault, and back through the hull.[^2] The pulse is slow, so the cables' capacitance barely disturbs it.[^6] So the pulse shows on the clamps along the fault's path, and next to nothing elsewhere. Follow it from the feeder to the sub-board, then to the circuit. This is a simplified picture, with one solid fault and ideal clamps. **Find an earth fault live, by opening feeders or by following a slow injected pulse with a clamp. Then isolate that circuit and test it dead.**"

Treat every core as live until you have proved it dead.\n- Note the monitor's reading and the time. It must recover when you open the feeder, and return when you close it.\n\n**Worked example:** Say a board has five feeders, each 1 MΩ when healthy, and a 15 kΩ fault on one.
