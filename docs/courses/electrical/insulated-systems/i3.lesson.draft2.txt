The bigger the system, the bigger that current.\n\nTake one line of a feeder. Its copper and the hull are two plates, with the insulation between them. On AC the plates charge and discharge 60 times a second. So a current flows in the line, even though no charge crosses the insulation. That is the line's [[charging current]]. A capacitor passes ω × C × V. Here ω = 2π × 60 = 377, C is the capacitance and V the voltage across it. Say each line has 1 µF to the hull on a 440 V supply. Healthy, each line sits 254 V from earth, so it passes 377 × 0.000001 × 254 = 96 mA. The three currents sit 120° apart, like their voltages. Laid head to tail they close into a triangle. L1's capacitance now has no voltage across it and passes nothing. L2 and L3 each sit 440 V from the hull, 1.73 times their healthy voltage. So each passes 1.73 × 96 = 166 mA.
