Topic: Insulated systems and earth faults

This lesson: i2 "The first earth fault: the triangle shifts" — On an insulated three-phase supply, a solid earth fault on one phase pulls that phase to earth and lifts the other two to line voltage to earth. [quantity]
Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i3 Earthed through capacitance — Cables and windings are capacitors to earth, so a first fault passes three times one phase's healthy charging current, growing with the system's size.
- i4 The second fault — A second earth fault on a different phase makes a phase-to-phase short through the hull, driven by line voltage round both circuits' loops.
- i5 Watching the insulation live — An insulation monitor injects a small DC or slow signal to read the system's total resistance to earth; guidance disagrees on where to alarm.
- i6 Insulated DC: reading the two poles — On an insulated DC board the two pole-to-earth voltages share the bus voltage, and the pole with the lower insulation resistance reads lower.
- i7 Finding the fault — An earth fault is found live by opening circuits or injecting a slow pulse and clamping each feeder, then the circuit is tested dead.
- i8 Arcing faults, drives and safe working — Experts disagree whether arcing faults can ratchet an insulated supply's voltage up, and drive filters and touching a floating supply bring their own dangers.
