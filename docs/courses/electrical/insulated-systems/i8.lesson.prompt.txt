Research notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Eaton reports that measured line-to-ground voltages of 1200 V or higher in its cases of intermittent ground faults on ungrounded systems are not uncommon. [1]  [CONTESTED: Eaton says arcing ground faults on ungrounded low-voltage systems give measured line-to-ground voltages of 1200 V or more, while the IMarEST paper says such overvoltages are not a problem on marine systems because of heavy damping.]
- Industry has many documented cases of multiple equipment failures, such as motors, across an entire 480 V ungrounded system while a ground fault was being found. [2]
- The IMarEST paper says such overvoltages are not a problem on marine systems because of their higher losses and heavy damping. [3]
- A drive's internal EMC filter capacitors connect the supply network to ground potential. [4]
- An IT system with a latent first fault behaves exactly like a grounded system. [5]
- When an arcing earth fault's current reaches zero, the arc goes out, and because the current leads the voltage by 90 degrees, the phase-to-earth capacitance is at its peak voltage at that moment. [6]
- Once the arc is out, nothing charges or discharges the capacitance, so the phase-to-earth voltage stays where it was when the arc went out. [7]
- Half a cycle after the arc goes out, the phase's voltage to earth reaches twice its first value. [8]
