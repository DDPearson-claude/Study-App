On a fixed supply, more turns mean less flux, and a lower frequency means more. A coil's inductance comes from the same law. Put the last lesson's Φ = N I ÷ S into Faraday's law, and its own emf is (N² ÷ S) × the current's rate of change. So L = N² ÷ S. Iron raises it, a gap lowers it, and saturation collapses it.\n\n**Worked example:** A primary of 300 turns is on 440 V at 60 Hz. Φm = 440 ÷ (4.44 × 60 × 300) = 440 ÷ 79,920 = 0.0055 Wb, or 5.5 mWb. Put the same winding on 440 V at 50 Hz. Φm = 440 ÷ (4.44 × 50 × 300) = 0.0066 Wb, or 6.6 mWb. That is 20% more flux, closer to the knee.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Blaming the field. A strong steady field makes no voltage. Only its change does.\n- Reading \"against the change\" as \"against the field\". With the flux falling, the induced field points the same way as the main one.\n- Expecting more primary turns to give more flux on a fixed supply. The voltage sets the flux, so more turns give less.\n- Leaving out the turns. Every turn adds its own emf.\n- Mixing rms and peak. E = 4.44 f N Φm takes the peak flux and gives the rms voltage."
