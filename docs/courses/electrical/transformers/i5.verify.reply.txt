   "now": "**Safe way first:**\n- In the open-circuit test, treat the open secondary as live. It sits at its full rated voltage.\n- In the short-circuit test, start the supply at zero and bring it up slowly, watching the ammeter. Stop at rated current. Full voltage on a shorted secondary is a dead short.\n\n**Steps:**\n- Get the rating in kVA, the iron loss from the open-circuit test, and the copper loss at full load from the short-circuit test.\n- Work out the load fraction x, the load in kVA ÷ the rating.\n- Copper loss = full-load copper loss × x².\n- Output = rating × x × power factor.\n- Efficiency = output ÷ (output + iron loss + copper loss).\n- Peak load = √(iron loss ÷ full-load copper loss).\n\n**Rules of thumb:** The iron loss runs whenever the transformer is energised, even with nothing connected. The copper loss quarters when the load halves. Efficiency peaks where the two are equal. A lower power factor gives the same losses for fewer watts, so the efficiency falls.\n\n**Worked example:** A 10 kVA transformer tests at 100 W open circuit and 400 W short circuit. At full load and unity power factor, it loses 100 + 400 = 500 W, so its efficiency is 10,000 ÷ 10,500 = 95.2%. At half load the copper loss is 400 × 0.5² = 100 W, so 5000 ÷ 5200 = 96.2%. That is the peak, since √(100 ÷ 400) = 0.5. At full load and 0.8 power factor, it gives 8000 W for the same 500 W: 94.1%. Left energised with no load all day, it still burns 100 W × 24 h = 2.4 kWh.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Expecting a heavy load to raise the iron loss. The supply sets the flux, not the load.\n- Scaling the copper loss straight with the load. It goes with the square.\n- Taking the short-circuit test's reading as the total loss. It is the copper loss at rated current only.\n- Forgetting the iron loss at light load. It is why efficiency falls away as the load drops."
