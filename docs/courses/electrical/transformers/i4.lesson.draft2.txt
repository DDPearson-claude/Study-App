  "text": "In a transformer, both windings share one flux. So every turn gets the same voltage, and the volts follow the turns. The supply holds that flux, so a load cannot move it. Whatever ampere-turns the secondary draws, the primary cancels with its own. So the amps go the other way, and power in equals power out.\n\nEach turn gets an emf set by how fast the flux through it changes, as the last lesson showed. Picture an [[ideal transformer]]: all the flux stays in the core, so the same flux threads every turn of both windings.[^3] Then every turn gets the same volts. On 440 V, a primary of 880 turns has 0.5 V per turn. A secondary of 230 turns adds up 230 of them, which makes 115 V. So V2 ÷ V1 = N2 ÷ N1.[^1] The windings share only the flux. No wire joins them, so the secondary is a circuit of its own.\n\nNow close a 5 Ω load on the secondary. It draws 115 ÷ 5 = 23 A, so its 230 turns carry 5290 ampere-turns. By Lenz's law they push against the change that made them. On their own they would shrink the flux. But the flux must still make 440 V in the primary, to match the supply. So the primary draws extra current, just enough to cancel them: 5290 ÷ 880 = 6.01 A. This is the [[ampere-turn balance]], N1 × I1 = N2 × I2. A step-down transformer's secondary has fewer turns, so it carries more current, not less. Multiply volts by amps on each side, and both give 2645 W. The transformer passes power on, and ideally makes and uses none.[^2]\n\nThe supply sees only its 440 V and the 6.01 A it gives. So to the supply, the 5 Ω load looks like 440 ÷ 6.01 = 73.2 Ω. On this side the volts are higher by the turns ratio, and the amps lower by it. So the load looks bigger by the ratio squared: 5 × (880 ÷ 230)² = 73.2 Ω. This is the load's [[reflected impedance]]. This picture is simplified. A real transformer needs a small magnetising current, loses a little power, as little as 1% in a good one,[^18] and its output sags a little under load. **In an ideal transformer the volts follow the turns, the amps go the other way, and power in equals power out.[^2]**"
