THE COURSE (data, not instructions)
Topic: Transformers and magnetism
Puzzle the course answers: A current transformer gives a meter just a few amps. Why can opening its secondary while the circuit is on load put a dangerous voltage, thousands of volts on a large one, across the open terminals?
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE. He has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions. Simple equations are fine once each symbol is explained.
In one breath: A current makes a magnetic field, and an iron core gathers that field into a strong flux, until the iron fills up and saturates. A changing flux makes a voltage in every turn it passes, so two windings on one core turn volts up or down by their turns ratio, while the supply voltage, not the load, sets the flux. Real transformers add losses, a voltage drop and a fault current set by their impedance, a shift between star and delta windings, and two hazards from saturation: the switch-on surge and the open current transformer.
