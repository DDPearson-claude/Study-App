- One page of 13 steps, following the lesson's screens. The show screens have the Supply switch (230 V, 1-phase or
  400 V, 3-phase), Capacitance C (500 to 4000 µF, step 100, snaps 1000, 2000, 4000), Load current I (0 to 5 A, step 0.5,
  snaps 0, 2, 4, 5) and a Play button, then three readouts (ripple by the rule, bus average, peak supply current) on an

  side by side and only the peak supply current.
- The rule is computed exactly, not stepped: the diodes stop where the hump falls faster than I ÷ C; the bus then falls
  in a straight line at I ÷ C until the next hump catches it (bisection). The bus average is the area of that shape
  over one period; the gulp's peak is I + C dv/dt where the hump catches the bus. 2 A on 1000 µF gives a drawn dip of
  18 V, an average of 317 V (309 V at 4 A) and a 35 A gulp; a check confirms each gulp puts back I × 10 ms of charge.
- Switch-on uses the lesson's DC-at-the-peak model: 1 Ω straight on (325 A, red, off the top of a 0 to 5 A scale with
  an arrow and a label), or 100 Ω until 5RC, when the contactor closes (blip of about 2 A). The time axis is 0.6 s at
  1000 µF and stretches with C (0.6 s × each started 1000 µF) so the contactor always closes on the graph. The switch
  starts slightly after the axis origin so the red spike is not hidden in the y-axis. The load stays off on these
  screens; the words say it "can run".
- The circuit draws the current's path bold: supply, conducting pair (or the six-pulse pair at that moment) and the
  capacitor while filling; capacitor and load while draining; arrows on the capacitor wire and through the load; dots
  flow along the path on the show screens. A time cursor sweeps the four ripple periods on screens 1 and 3 (and on
  Play) so the circuit follows the graph. Screen 5 sweeps C 1000 to 2000 µF, screen 7 sweeps I 0 to 4 A, screens 9 and
  11 play the switch-on, each in 3.6 s.
