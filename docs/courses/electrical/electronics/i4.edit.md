Only the wording changed. Every number, unit, rule, formula, [[term]] and [^n] citation is the same. A script
checked each field before and after. Sources, ids and structure are untouched.

81 sentences were rewritten in i4.lesson.verified.json. That covers the brief, the picture, the screen words, the
read sentences, the puzzle questions, hints and whys, interactive.ignores, the explain text, the analogy and where it
breaks, and the practice. The same edits were made in i4.lesson.final.json wherever it holds the same sentence

now starts with its main point where it did not. The explain text now has five shorter paragraphs instead of four

## 1. Screen 1: the heat rule comes first
Before: "A transistor is a switch you work with a small signal on a third lead. Here one feeds a 30 Ω load from
600 V DC, close to a 440 V drive's DC link. Fully off, the whole 600 V sits across it, but almost no current flows.
… The heat in a switch is the voltage across it times the current through it. So fully on, this one makes about
2 × 20 = 40 W."
After: "A transistor is a switch you work with a small signal on a third lead. The heat in a switch is the voltage
across it times the current through it. Here one feeds a 30 Ω load from 600 V DC. That is close to a 440 V drive's
DC link. Fully off, the whole 600 V sits across it. But almost no current flows. … So fully on, it makes about
2 × 20 = 40 W."

## 2. Screen 9: lead with why switching helps
Before: "Now flip the switch between fully off and fully on, on half the time. … During it, the switch sweeps
through half on, so each edge costs a burst of heat, about 1 mJ. Two edges a cycle at 10 kHz make 20 W. This
switching loss is the energy per switching times the frequency.[^1]"
After: "Flipping the switch fully on and off makes far less heat. It is on half the time. … During it, the switch
sweeps through half on. So each edge costs a burst of heat, about 1 mJ. This switching loss is the energy per
switching times the frequency.[^1] Two edges a cycle at 10 kHz make 20 W."

## 3. Screen 11: the coil's rule first, and the diode in two sentences
Before: "Many loads are coils, such as a contactor coil or a motor winding. A coil keeps its current going, as
v = L di/dt says. … So di/dt is huge, and the coil's voltage spikes far above the supply, across the switch. A diode
across the coil, fitted the way that blocks the supply, gives the current a path round."
After: "A coil keeps its current going, as v = L di/dt says. Many loads are coils, such as contactors and motor
windings. … So di/dt is huge. The coil's voltage spikes far above the supply, across the switch. A diode across the
coil gives the current a path round. It is fitted to block the supply."

## 4. Explain: the point before the reason
Before: "Now both are large at once, so it makes 300 × 10 = 3000 W. At each end one of them is near zero, so the
heat peaks at half on."
After: "Now both are large at once. So it makes 300 × 10 = 3000 W. The heat peaks at half on, because at each end one

The opening paragraph also now says what fully on or off buys before what half on costs: "In a converter, it is
kept fully on or fully off. That way it controls the load while making little heat.[^5] Held half on, it would turn
far more of the power into heat."
