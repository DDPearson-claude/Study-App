Harmonics. The top plot shows one cycle of the current in one line of a six-pulse bridge. It is drawn as a dashed ideal block. A large coil in the DC link, chosen, holds the DC current steady. Each line conducts forward for 120°, rests for 60°, conducts backward for 120° and rests again. That is because each line sits in two of the six 60° diode pairs each way. The block's height is the DC current, drawn as 1. A picker, Sines added, has eight settings. The first is Fundamental only and the last is Every harmonic. Between them come Up to the 5th, Up to the 7th, Up to the 11th, Up to the 13th, Up to the 17th and Up to the 19th. The solid line is the sum of the sines added. The fundamental runs at the supply frequency. Its peak is 1.10 times the block's height, 2√3 ÷ π. Each harmonic h runs at h times the supply frequency. It is 1/h as tall as the fundamental, with the sign the block needs. These are the block's exact Fourier sines. With Every harmonic the sum is the block.

Below, a spectrum shows a bar for each harmonic added, as a share of the fundamental. The 5th is 20%, the 7th 14.3%, the 11th 9.1%, the 13th 7.7%, the 17th 5.9% and the 19th 5.3%. Empty slots stand at the even orders and the multiples of 3. A switch, 50 Hz or 60 Hz, relabels the bars in hertz. At 50 Hz they read 250, 350, 550, 650, 850 and 950 Hz. At 60 Hz they read 300, 420, 660, 780, 1020 and 1140 Hz. The readout, THD of the sum, is the root of the added harmonics' squares over the fundamental. In order it reads 0%, 20%, 25%, 26%, 27%, 28%, 28% and 31%. Beside it, three range bars show the current THD of real drives. A 6-pulse drive draws 25% to 40%, a 12-pulse one 9% to 11% and an 18-pulse one 3% to 4%.

After isolation. DC link voltage against time since isolation. Both drives start at 540 V. That is about what a three-phase bridge gives from 400 V, 1.35 × 400 V.[^1] Each drains through a 27 kΩ bleed resistor, chosen. Drive A has 2200 µF and drive B 6600 µF, both chosen. Each follows V = 540 × e^(−t ÷ RC). Drive A's RC is 59 s, about 1 minute. Drive B's is 178 s, about 3 minutes. Each curve has a dotted mark at about five time constants, at 5 and 15 minutes. Both marks show about 3 V. A time slider runs from 0 to 20 minutes in steps of 0.5 minutes. A picker chooses Drive A or Drive B. The readout is the chosen drive's link voltage then.

Leakage. A 400 V, 50 Hz supply feeds a drive. The drive's EMC filter sits at its input. It is three 100 nF capacitors, chosen, one from each line to a point bonded to earth. Each capacitor's reactance is X_C = 1 ÷ (2π × 50 × 100 nF) = 31.8 kΩ. A small phasor diagram shows each capacitor's current and their sum. The sum is the readout, the current through earth. A picker, Case, has five settings.

Earthed, balanced. The supply's star point is earthed, and the three lines pass through an RCD, drawn as a ring. Each line is 230 V from earth, so each capacitor passes 230 ÷ 31.8 kΩ = 7.2 mA. The three are 120° apart and add to 0 mA. Earthed, a phase lost. L1's fuse has blown, and its capacitor carries nothing. The other two, 120° apart, add to 7.2 mA. It returns through earth to the star point, outside the RCD's ring. So the RCD sees it.

Insulated, healthy. The star point is not earthed. An insulation monitor sits between the system and earth. Each capacitor still passes 7.2 mA. With no path back from earth, the three only circulate among the lines. The current through earth is 0 mA. The filter carries a label: Disconnect on an insulated supply. Insulated, L1 earth fault. A red link joins L1 to earth. The L2 and L3 capacitors now each see 400 V and pass 12.6 mA. Their currents are 60° apart and add to 21.8 mA. That current flows through the fault, drawn red. Insulated, fault, filter out. The filter is disconnected, and the fault draws 0 mA. In both fault cases the monitor's alarm lamp lights.

Three readouts sit in a row. They are free to wrap onto two lines on a narrow phone.

What the model leaves out (the app shows this in its own panel; do not repeat it on the page): The blocks are ideal, with square corners and a perfectly steady DC current. Real ones have sloped edges. The current takes time to pass from one diode to the next. Without a large coil, the capacitor makes a drive draw two gulps each half cycle. Those gulps carry harmonics too. The supply is drawn as a perfect sine. In truth, harmonic currents bend it a little. Phase-shifting transformers let 12 and 18-pulse rectifiers cancel harmonics. That is left for Transformers. The discharge curves use one bleed resistor and nothing else. Real links also drain into control circuits. A failed bleed resistor can leave the charge there far longer. The filter is three plain capacitors. Real filters also pass high-frequency current from the switching. They are never perfectly balanced, either. With a phase lost, the lost line is drawn carrying nothing. In a real drive it floats. The insulated supply's own cable capacitance to earth is left out. So its fault currents read zero rather than small.

## How to fix the usual problems
- A thrown error (also from a K.button press or a K.anim step): go to the body line it names; check element ids, variable names and the kit call signatures.
- NaN or Infinity: guard the maths at the ends of every control's range (division by zero, log of 0, square root of a negative), or start the range where the rule makes sense.
- A model output that is a list or object: K.model returns single numbers or short strings; keep lists in your own variables.
- Too wide at 340 px: let rows wrap (flex-wrap), use width:100% and max-width:100%, give SVG a viewBox with width 100%, no fixed widths over 300 px, shorter labels.
- Text cut off: shorten it or let it wrap, or give it room (a wider box, a bigger viewBox); never cut it off or hide the overflow. SVG labels printed over each other, even by a letter or two: move one, or place them with K.labels. A plot's axis title too long for a phone: shorten it (about 30 characters). A word split across two lines (often at Text size XL): size its grid columns or tiles in rem, not px (minmax(6rem, 1fr)), so they reflow to fewer, wider columns; or use a shorter word.
- SVG text too small on a phone: font-size 13-16 in a drawing about 340 wide. A line or arrowhead through a label: place the label with K.labels and pass that line in avoid, or stop the line short of it. More labels than room (K.labels says avoid cannot be honoured): label fewer things, or move the names into a key under the drawing or a list beside it.
- A readout's value printed elsewhere (a label, an aria-label or a plot label that follows it): while Dan answers a check the app hides only the readout and the .say line, so show the number in the readout alone and name the thing elsewhere.
- Text hard to read (contrast) or a colour name: ink, muted, accent2, accent, warn or good (on-accent2 on navy), as var(--k-…) or K.color set in K.update, so it follows the theme.
- A blank hole before Dan moves: hide only the answer (a line, a mark, a sentence), never a whole figure.
- The first control far below the main figure (phone layout): put the visual and its controls together with K.stage(visual, controls), secondary figures below.
- K.sound: frequencies 20 to 20,000 Hz (200 to 2,000 for anything Dan must hear on a phone), dur above 0 and at most 10 s, gain 0 to 1, a listed type; play it from a K.button press.
- Navigation, links out, connections or frames: everything stays on this page (no <a href> or forms, no location changes, no WebSocket, XMLHttpRequest or iframes); name sources in words.
- A failing check: work the expected value out again by hand. Fix whichever is wrong, the model or the check. Never delete a correct check or change its expected answer to pass. A tolerance that was unrealistically tight (a millionth, where square roots or small steps meet rounding) may be widened to a sensible one (a thousandth of the size).
- Slow updates: sample curves less densely and do not rebuild large parts of the page on every change.
- Missing ids: use exactly the ids listed; an output must be a key of the object K.model returns.
- An unlisted web address: delete it, and the {source} that held it.
- A target out of reach: make the model follow the lesson's rule with the lesson's ranges and opening values, so moving that one control brings the output to the target.

### The pipeline
Controls register themselves; their values form `params` = `{id: value}`.
```

K.at(over)         -> outputs of the model at those params (others take each control's starting value)

```
