  "text": "Power electronics brings three side effects with it. A rectifier draws its current in blocks, not as a sine, and that puts extra frequencies on the supply. A DC link stays charged after you isolate it. And a drive's input filter leaks current to earth. Each one follows from something you already know.\n\nStart with the current. A six-pulse bridge with a large coil in its DC link draws a steady current. Each line carries it in blocks, 120° forward and 120° back. That is because each line sits in two of the six 60° diode pairs each way. Any wave that repeats each cycle is a sum of sines at whole multiples of the supply frequency. These are its [[harmonics]]. For ideal blocks, harmonic h is 1/h as tall as the fundamental. Only the orders 6n ± 1 appear, the 5th, 7th, 11th, 13th and on.[^1] Even ones cannot, because the two halves mirror. Multiples of 3 cannot, because the three lines have no neutral to return them. [[Total harmonic distortion]], THD, is the harmonics' combined rms over the fundamental's rms.[^9] Perfect blocks give 31%. Real six-pulse drives draw 25% to 40%.[^7] These currents flow back through the generator and cables. Their reactance rises with frequency, so each harmonic current makes a harmonic voltage. That bends the voltage every other load sees. So a ship's distribution system must keep its THD within 8%.[^6]\n\nIsolation stops the supply, but it does not empty the DC link. A bleed resistor drains the link on the RC curve, so the drive's own R and C set the wait. One maker asks for 5 minutes.[^2] Another asks for at least 15.[^14]\n\nAn [[EMC filter]] stops a drive's fast switching spreading back into the supply. It puts small capacitors from each line to earth, and each passes V ÷ X_C. On a balanced earthed supply the three currents are 120° apart, so they cancel. Lose a phase, and they no longer cancel, so current flows through earth past the RCD. A fault inside a drive can send current with DC in it to earth, which is why Type B suits drives.[^16] On an insulated supply nothing returns from earth, so a first fault draws almost nothing.[^20] The filter would give that fault a return path, so it is disconnected.[^4] The rectifier, the DC link and the filter that let a drive's switches make smooth AC bring all three effects with every drive. This is a simplified picture, with ideal blocks, one bleed resistor and plain capacitors. **A converter draws harmonics, holds charge after isolation and leaks current to earth, so you measure, wait and choose the protection to suit.**"
