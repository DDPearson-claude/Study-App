# i3 plain-English edit: Smoothing and the DC link

Only the wording changed. Every number, unit, rule, formula and [^n] citation is the same. Sources, ids and
structure are untouched.

70 sentences were rewritten in i3.lesson.verified.json. That covers the brief, the picture, the screen words, the
read sentences, the puzzle whys, interactive.ignores, the explain text, the analogy and where it breaks, and the
practice. The same edits were made in i3.lesson.final.json wherever it holds the same sentence. Four of final's own
older sentences are not in verified and were left alone: "The rule errs a little high.", "Precharge takes about five
time constants…", "It just lasts longer." and the explain's "After five time constants it is over 99% full…".
`validate.mjs lesson ... --final` passes on both files with no problems or warnings.

Most edits split a sentence that joined two ideas with "and", "so", "which" or a colon. Each puzzle's why now starts
with the answer. Each SHOW screen now starts with its main point. The SHOW screens are 82, 89, 71, 88, 89 and 77 words.
The brief keeps its "The one thing you should see is ___ when you ___." form. The three readouts and the stacked
puzzle answers keep their sentences about wrapping at 360 px.

## 1. Screen 1: start with the point, not the setup
Before: "Take a bridge rectifier on 230 V. … So the capacitor alone feeds the load until the next peak. This smoothing
capacitor turns the humps into smoother DC.[^1]"
After: "A smoothing capacitor turns a bridge's humps into smoother DC.[^1] Take a bridge rectifier on 230 V. …
The capacitor alone feeds the load until the next peak."
The same sentence also changed from "the hump falls away faster than the slowly draining capacitor" to "the hump
falls away faster than the capacitor drains".

## 2. Screen 6's why: the answer first, then the rule split in two
Before: "The bus falls at I ÷ C. … So in the same 10 ms it falls twice as far, about 40 V. The load and the ripple
frequency set the ripple, as well as the capacitor."
After: "It doubles, to about 40 V. The bus falls at I ÷ C. … So in the same 10 ms it falls twice as far. The capacitor
does not set the ripple alone. The load and the ripple frequency set it too."

## 3. Screen 9: the short-circuit point comes first
Before: "Press Switch on with the capacitor empty. … The supply sees an empty capacitor as a short circuit.[^5] …
Switched on at the 325 V peak, all 325 V falls across that 1 Ω, so 325 A flows."
After: "The supply sees an empty capacitor as a short circuit.[^5] Press Switch on with the capacitor empty. …
Switch on at the 325 V peak. All 325 V falls across that 1 Ω, so 325 A flows."

## 4. Explain: a trailing "which" clause becomes its own sentence
Before: "After the peak the hump falls away faster than the capacitor, which drains only slowly through the load."
After: "After the peak the hump falls away faster than the capacitor can drain. It drains only slowly, through the load."
The worked precharge sentence lost its "say" aside the same way. Before: "Through a 100 Ω resistor, say, the first current
is about 3 A." After: "Take a 100 Ω resistor. The first current is then about 3 A."

## 5. The picture: a sentence that listed asides now gives one idea each
Before: "With precharge off, the empty capacitor meets the supply through 1 Ω of supply and wiring, a value chosen for
the example. The first current is the peak ÷ 1 Ω, drawn in red, running off the top of the graph."
After: "With precharge off, the empty capacitor meets the supply through 1 Ω of supply and wiring. That value is chosen
for the example. The first current is the peak ÷ 1 Ω. It is drawn in red and runs off the top of the graph."
