A DC link stays charged after you isolate it. And a drive's input filter leaks current to earth.\n\nStart with the current. A six-pulse bridge with a large coil in its DC link draws a steady current. Each line carries it in blocks, 120° forward and 120° back. That is because each line sits in two of the six 60° diode pairs each way. Any wave that repeats each cycle is a sum of sines at whole multiples of the supply frequency. These are its [[harmonics]]. For ideal blocks, harmonic h is 1/h as tall as the fundamental. From a balanced supply, only the orders 6n ± 1 appear, the 5th, 7th, 11th, 13th and on.[^3] Even ones cannot appear, because the two halves mirror. Multiples of 3 cannot, because the three lines have no neutral to return them. [[Total harmonic distortion]], THD, is the harmonics' combined rms over the fundamental's rms.[^5] Sines of different frequencies multiply to an average of zero, so their rms values add as squares. Perfect blocks give 31%. Real six-pulse drives often draw 25% to 40%.[^6] These currents flow back through the generator and cables. Their reactance rises with frequency, so each harmonic current makes a harmonic voltage. That bends the voltage every other load sees. So ship rules cap a distribution system's voltage THD at 8%,[^16] unless all its equipment is built and documented for more.\n\nIsolation stops the supply, but it does not empty the DC link. A bleed resistor drains the link on the RC curve, so the drive's own R and C set the wait. One maker asks for 5 minutes.[^17] Another asks for at least 15.[^8]\n\nAn [[EMC filter]] limits how much of a drive's fast switching spreads back into the supply. It puts small capacitors from each line to earth, and each passes V ÷ X_C. On a balanced earthed supply the three currents are 120° apart, so they cancel. Lose a phase, and two are left. Two equal currents 120° apart add to one of the same size. That current flows through earth, past the RCD. A fault in a three-phase converter can also carry DC. Type B protects against that.[^19] Type A detects only sinusoidal AC and pulsating DC.[^13] On an insulated supply there is no planned path back from earth, so a first fault draws almost nothing.[^9] The filter would give that fault a return path, so it is disconnected.[^10] Most drives have a diode rectifier, a DC link and an EMC filter. So most bring these three effects with them. This is a simplified picture, with ideal blocks, one bleed resistor and plain capacitors. **A converter draws harmonics, holds charge after isolation and leaks current to earth, so you measure, wait and choose the protection to suit.**"
