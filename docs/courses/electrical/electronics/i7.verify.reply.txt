   "severity": "fix",
   "now": "Isolate and prove dead before you work on a drive or a UPS. Its DC link holds charge after isolation. A UPS battery can keep the link live with the mains off.\n\n- Find the DC link: about 1.35 × the line voltage from a three-phase bridge.[^1]\n- At full depth, plain sine-triangle gives 0.612 × the link between lines.\n- Depth needed = the voltage you want ÷ that maximum. The output frequency is the reference's.\n- Choose the carrier as a trade: faster gives a smoother current, slower gives cooler switches.\n\n**Rules of thumb:** the reference sets the output, and the carrier only sets how fine the pulses are. Below rated speed, a motor’s voltage and frequency move together. Dead time must cover the switches' turn-off, and little more.\n\n**Worked example:** a 440 V, 60 Hz motor must run at 30 Hz. Its drive is on a 440 V supply. The link is about 1.35 × 440 = 594 V. Full depth gives 0.612 × 594 = 364 V between lines. At 30 Hz the motor needs 440 × 30 ÷ 60 = 220 V. So set the reference to 30 Hz, and the depth to 220 ÷ 364, about 60%.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Thinking the carrier sets the output frequency. It sets the sawtooth and the switching heat.\n- Reading a zero on the reference as the switches off. It is 50% on, the middle of the sine.\n- Thinking no dead time gives the cleanest wave. It shorts the link through the leg.\n- Expecting a sine on a scope across the motor lines. You see pulses at the full link voltage. Their average is the sine.\n- Thinking an online UPS in double conversion changes over when the mains fails. Its inverter feeds the load all the time.[^7]"
