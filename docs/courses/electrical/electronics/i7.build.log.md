## The model, and one thing for the reviewer
- PWM is naturally sampled. The carrier has a trough at each k × Tc, and the top switch is on while the reference is
  above it. Each cycle's on-share matches D = 50% + 50% × depth × sin within 1%, and a check holds this.
- The current comes from v = L di/dt + R i, stepped exactly. The drawn current is the average model, carrying the
  rule's dead-time loss against the current, plus the switching sawtooth. The sawtooth is the switched current without
  dead time minus its own smooth average. A fully switched model was tried first, in Node. At the slowed 1 kHz carrier
  its 13.5 A sawtooth spans zero, so dead time flattened the tops, not the zeros. The rule's averaged loss is the
  real-carrier behaviour, so the page draws that.
- Checked against the rule:
  - The sawtooth at 3 kHz is 0.334 of its height at 1 kHz.
  - Line current at 3 kHz stays within 3% of a sine.
  - The drawn pulses lose 108.9 V at 100 µs and 22.2 V at 20 µs (rule: 108 V, 22 V).
  - At 30% depth and 100 µs the current stalls at zero for 28% of each cycle.
- At 20 µs there is no stall. The current only bends: its slope after each zero is about 30% shallower. So screen
  12's read, "The current has a small flat near each zero", is a slight bend in the picture. If the reviewer finds it
  unconvincing, the fix is in the lesson's words ("bends a little near each zero"), not the build. The page's own
  .say at 20 µs reads "The current flattens a little near each zero."
