Finding why a DOL starter won't start, won't stop or won't hold.

The bench

Three jobs on one starter. Each starts with a report. You choose the tests and say what you expect. Then read the meter. Name the fault and prove it fixed. Theory turns up when a reading surprises you.

Job 1 · Coached

No click

  1. Make it safe
    1. Identify the right circuit
    2. Isolate it from every supply
    3. Lock off and post a notice
    4. Prove the tester on a known supply
    5. Test dead where you'll work
    6. Prove the tester again
  2. ContinuityP0 to P3Over range: open
    The break is in the top half: the fuse, the panel Stop or the pump's Stop loop.
  3. P0 to P2Near zero: a path
    Fuse and panel Stop are whole. It's in the pump's Stop loop.
  4. R1 to R2Near zero: a path
    The pump's Stop closes, so a core is open.
  5. P2 to R1Over range: open
    The core out to the pump is open.
  6. Fix it, then restore
    Check nothing will start by surprise. Locks removed, people told, supply back on.
  7. Operate it
    Press Start
    It pulls in and runs.
    Start, then let go
    It keeps running.
    Press the panel Stop
    It stops.
    Press the pump's Stop
    It stops.

Job 2 · Coached

The Stop that won't stop

  1. Make it safe
    Isolate and prove dead, as before.
  2. LookThe joint, opened
    Both cores sit in one connector. The pump's Stop is joined out.
  3. ContinuityR1 to R2Hold the pump's Stop pressedNear zero: a path
