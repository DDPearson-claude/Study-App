control circuit is cut off from its supply, so a dead meter sees only this circuit. When the
contactor is out, the hold-in contact is open. Nothing on the bench closes the contactor by hand.

The jobs use f1 to f4. The others make sure every reading has to be earned, so the solver cannot
treat a test as wasted. **Change:** f8 and f9 are added to the design's six, so that the short route's
tests 3 and 4 are needed by the fault list as well as by the places.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| f1 | `holdin` | open: the wire is out of terminal 14 | Warm-up; job 3's second fault | Pump 2 only runs while someone holds Start. |
| f2 | `coreA1` | open: core A between P2 and the joint | Job 1 | Pump 2 won't start. No click. Overload not tripped. |
| f3 | joint | short: `Ja` joined to `Jb`, both cores in one connector, so the pump's Stop is bridged | Job 2 | The Stop at the pump doesn't stop it. |
| f4 | `coil` | wrong rating: a coil rated 400 V AC `[example]` on this 230 V circuit | Job 3's first fault | It buzzes when Start is pressed and won't run. |
| f5 | `fuse` | open | distractor | Pump 2 won't start. No click. |
| f6 | `ol` | open, with the flag not showing a trip, as a loose wire at 95 would leave it | distractor | Pump 2 won't start. No click. Overload not tripped. |
| f7 | `coil` | open winding | distractor | Pump 2 won't start. No click. |
| f8 | `rstop` | open: the contact has failed | distractor | Pump 2 won't start from either place. |
| f9 | `coreB1` | open: core B between the joint and P3 | distractor | Pump 2 won't start. No click. |

Job 3 is a **staged pair**: f4 and f1 together. f1 cannot show until f4 is fixed, because the coil
never seats. The bench credits either order. If Dan looks at terminals 13 and 14 first, he finds f1
early, and that is a good route.

`bench.mjs` must allow faults that read the same on every **meter** test when a **Look** separates
them. f1 and f4 read the same as a healthy circuit on every dead test. Only a Look tells them apart:
the terminals for f1, the rating plate for f4. That is the lesson: some faults only show to the eye or
to the proof.

## 2. The rules

Each rule's `words` is the rule line of a note card. `bench.mjs` computes the table from these rules.

### R1 `continuity` — dead, any two targets, with the toggles as set

Build the circuit as a graph: the closed parts are zero-ohm links, the coil is a winding, and open
parts are left out. `start` is closed only while held. `holdin` is always open when dead.
`rstop` is open while held. f3 adds a link `Ja`–`Jb`.
- A path of links only reads **near zero**.
- If the only path passes through the coil, it reads **a coil's reading**: a class, never a number.
- No path reads **over range**.

Words: "A whole wire or a closed contact reads near zero. A break reads over range. So does a healthy
normally open contact, unless it is held in. The coil reads in between." Sources: `[NVQ-FF p.4]`
gives a low resistance ohmmeter for open circuits, `[M42]` and `[M52]` the open contacts. No coil
resistance value is used: none is sourced.

### R2 `voltageToN` — live, Start held

A node joined to L by links reads **230 V** `[M60]`. A node joined to N only through the coil reads
**0 V**, because the coil ties it to neutral. A node joined to neither is **floating**. No job offers a
floating node: live tests are offered only with Start held, and no single fault leaves one floating
then.

### R3 `voltageAcross` — live, Start held

It is the difference of R2's two node readings. Across an open part in an otherwise whole rung:
230 V. Across a closed part: 0 V. Across the coil when the rung is whole: 230 V.

### R4 `coilBand` — whether the contactor pulls in

The coil seats only when its voltage is within a band around its rating. The example band is 85% to
110% `[M245]`, one maker's figure and always said "for example". A general source is
`[TBS: contactor operating limits]`. The ratio is 230 ÷ 400 = 0.575, about 58% `[computed]`. Below the
band, it buzzes and doesn't seat `[M248]`. **For the fact-checker:** whether a coil at about 58%
buzzes or stays silent depends on the make. The report sets it as buzzing, and [M248] supports
buzzing as one way it shows.

### R5 `halving` — the note card's numbers

With n places, a test across half of them clears about half, whichever way it reads. The worst case
is ⌈log₂ n⌉ tests. Six places take 3 tests at most `[computed]`. Piece by piece, one part at a time,
the worst case is n − 1 tests, because the last part is known once the others pass: 5 for six
places `[computed]`. **Change:** the plan's note says "Piece by piece can take six tests". Use five.

In job 1, the six suspects treat the pump's Stop loop as one place. Once the first two tests show
the break is in that loop, the loop becomes a new set of three: core A, the Stop and core B. Halving
three takes 2 tests at most. So the short route is 2 + 2 = 4 tests, and that matches the "short
route: 4" line.

Source for the method and its name: `[TBS: half-splitting in a public troubleshooting text]`. It is
not in the NVQ slides.

### R6 `look` — what the eye shows, under each fault

| Look at | Shows under f1 | f3 | f4 | Every other fault and healthy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Terminals 13 and 14 | "Terminal 14 is empty. The wire has pulled clear of the clamp." In job 3: "never moved across from the old contactor" | Both wires tight | Both wires tight | Both wires tight |
| The overload's flag | Not tripped | Not tripped | Not tripped | Not tripped, in every fault, as the reports say |
| The coil's rating plate | "Coil 230 V AC" | "Coil 230 V AC" | "Coil 400 V AC" `[example]` | "Coil 230 V AC" |
| The joint, opened; dead only | Two connectors, one core each | **All four core ends in one connector** | Two connectors, one core each | Two connectors, one core each |
| The pump's Stop | Looks fine | Looks fine | Looks fine | Looks fine |

A Look costs nothing and is logged. Opening the joint is a dead task. It is offered only after the
isolation step, or in job 2 after "Isolate and prove dead, as before". **Cut:** the design's "Look at
the fuse". Saying a cartridge fuse can't be judged by eye needs a source, and the look teaches
nothing here.

### R7 `play` — what the pump does when he operates it

This is the functional proof, with the supply back on. Re-energising needs locks off and people
told `[HSG85 p.15 ¶51-52]`, and loads that could start unexpectedly checked `[NVQ-HS p.2]`.

| Fault | Press Start | Let go of Start | Panel Stop | Pump's Stop |

## 4. The lesson, screen by screen

### S0. The bench (look)

"Three jobs on one starter. Each starts with a report. You choose the tests and say what you
expect. Then read the meter. Name the fault and prove it fixed. Theory turns up when a reading
surprises you." (37) Buttons: Job 1 · Job 2 · Job 3. R: the bench, the first page after Keep this.

### S1. Warm-up (choose; review card)

"Quick one first. Pump 2 runs only while someone holds Start. Let go and it stops. What do you
check?" (19)
Options, with the right one at position 2:
1. The overload. Slip: "A tripped overload stops it pulling in at all. Here it pulls in."
2. **The hold-in contact and its wire.**
3. The coil. Slip: "The coil pulls in while Start is held. It works."
4. The motor's insulation. Slip: "The motor runs while Start is held. The fault is in what keeps it
   running."

Then Sure · Not sure. Right and sure: "Yes. The coil pulls in, so its whole path works. Only the path
that keeps it fed after Start springs back is in doubt." (26) `[M45]`, `[M46]`, `[M48]`, `[M50]`,
`[M52]`. Otherwise, the note card **N0**.
R: a worked example with its answer and why.

### Job 1: No click. Fault f2. Coached unless the first move is right and sure.

**S2. Report and first move** (choose; review card)
"Report: Pump 2 won't start from the panel. No click from the starter. The overload hasn't tripped.
What's your first move?" (20)
Options, with the right one at position 1:
1. **Isolate, then test along the coil's circuit.** "Yes. No click means the coil never pulled in.
   Find where its path is broken, dead."
2. Reset the overload and try again. Slip: "The flag shows no trip. There's nothing to reset."
3. Fit a new coil. Slip: "Nothing points at the coil yet. Swapping parts before testing is guessing."
4. Insulation test the motor. Slip: "No click means the contactor never closed. The motor isn't
   reached yet."

Sure · Not sure. Right and sure skips S3 and goes straight to S4. Method support: symptoms first,
then inspect and test `[NVQ-FF p.2]`.

**S3. Suspects** (coached only)
"Tap every part that could cause this." (7) Chips on the drawing: Fuse · Stop at the panel · Stop at
the pump and its cable · Start · Hold-in · Overload contact · Coil. The answer is all but Hold-in.
Feedback: "Anything in the coil's path could. The hold-in contact can't, because with Start held it
plays no part. The overload flag makes a trip unlikely, but its contact or wires could still be
open." (36) "Suspects left: 6." `[M45]`, `[M48]`. R: a worked example.

**S4. Make it safe** (put in order; first job only; review: order card)
"Before you touch a terminal, make it dead and prove it. Put the steps in order." (16)
1. Isolate. `[HSG85 p.14-15 ¶47-48]`: identify, then disconnect from every source.
2. Lock off and tag. `[HSG85 p.15 ¶49]` lock; `[HSG85 p.15 ¶52]` notices.
3. Prove the tester on a known supply. `[HSG85 p.16 ¶55]`, `[M260]`.
4. Test dead where you'll work. `[HSG85 p.15 ¶53]`.
5. Prove the tester again. `[HSG85 p.16 ¶55]`, `[M260]`.

Feedback: "That's the order. A tester that fails can show dead on a live circuit, so prove it before
and after." (21) `[M260]`.

**For the fact-checker:**
- HSG85 starts with identifying the circuit. Consider "Isolate the right supply" as step 1.
- HSG85 ¶48 says every source. A control circuit with its own supply needs that isolated too. One
  line, if it fits: "If the controls have their own supply, isolate that too."
- The tester is a two-pole tester, not a multimeter `[HSG85 p.16 ¶54]`. That is shown in the
  picture, not the words.

Later jobs: one tap, "Isolate and prove dead, as before". The log lists all five steps.
R: the ordered steps.

**S5. Live or dead?** (choose)
"How will you trace it?" Options: "Isolate, and test continuity dead" · "Leave it live and trace the
voltage".
- Dead: "Good. A dead test will find an open circuit, so there's no reason to work live." (16)
- Live, as a red note: "Accidents often happen in fault-finding, when there's pressure to get plant
  running. The law allows live work only when it's unreasonable for the circuit to be dead. Here a
  dead continuity test will find it, so working live can't be justified. Isolate instead?" (44)
  Buttons: Isolate · Carry on live. If he carries on live, R2 and R3 give the readings, and the log
  marks each one live.

Sources: `[HSG85 p.12 ¶38]` for the first sentence. `[HSG85 p.7 ¶23]`: regulation 14's three
conditions, of which this sentence states the first. `[HSG85 p.8 ¶24]`.

**Change:** the plan's "Many electricians trace control faults live" is unsourced. It is replaced
by HSG85 ¶38.

**Contested, and taught that way:** HSG85 ¶24 names fault-finding as a case where dead may be
unreasonable. So the screen says why dead is right *here*, never that live is always wrong. Job 3
is the counterpart. R: a worked example.

### Job 2: A Stop that won't stop. Fault f3. Coached unless right and sure.

**S8. Report and first move** (choose; review card)
"Report: Pump 2's cable was damaged and joined yesterday. Now the Stop at the pump doesn't stop it.
The panel Stop does. What's your first move?" (27)
Options, with the right one at position 3:
1. Change the Stop button at the pump. Slip: "It worked before the repair. Start with what
   changed."
2. Trace it live while it runs. Red slip: "A running pump with a Stop that doesn't work is a hazard.
   A dead test will find this. Stop it and isolate." `[HSG85 p.7 ¶23]`
3. **Stop it at the panel, isolate, then open the new joint.** "Yes. Make it safe, then start with
   what changed." `[NVQ-FF p.2]` covers the events which led to the fault.
4. Leave it, the panel Stop works. Red slip: "Someone at the pump would press Stop and nothing would
   happen. It stays off until it's found." `[NVQ-FF p.6]` covers leaving it safe.
   `[TBS: PUWER 1998 regulation 15, stop controls, or L22]`

**S9. Look, then prove it**
"Isolate and prove dead, as before" is one tap. Look: the joint, opened. "Both cores sit in one
connector. The pump's Stop is joined out. Current goes straight past it, pressed or not." (22)
Then: "Prove it. Hold the pump's Stop pressed and test across it." R1–R2 with the Stop held reads near
zero. P2–P3 with the Stop held reads the same and is accepted.
**Note for the author:** the expert tests R1–R2 at the pump, where one person can hold the Stop and
read the meter.
`[TBS: a joint error that bridges a stop control]`. If it is not found, this stays a worked case
computed from the drawing, with no claim about how often it happens.

**S10. Fix and prove it** (pick all that apply)
The fix: remake the joint, one core per connector. Then R1–R2 with the Stop held reads over range,
and at rest near zero, both by R1.
"Which checks prove the fix?" Options: Press the pump's Stop: it stops · Insulation test the motor ·
Start, then let go: it keeps running · Press the panel Stop: it stops. Right: the first, third and
fourth.

## 6. Keep this (S14: the first page of R and the Book chapter)

**For:** finding why a DOL starter won't start, won't stop or won't hold.
- Make it safe, then start with what changed. `[HSG85 p.14-16 ¶46-55]`, `[NVQ-FF p.2]`
- No click, overload not tripped: split the coil's path in half, dead. R5, `[TBS: half-splitting]`
- Test a contact the way it's used: Start held in, Stop held pressed. `[M42]`, `[M46]`, `[M262]`
- Runs only while Start is held: the hold-in contact or its wire. `[M45]`, `[M52]`
- Buzzes: read the coil's rating, then how it's fed. `[M60]`, `[M62]`, `[M248]`
- After any control repair, prove start, hold and every Stop. `[M262]`, `[TBS: functional test]`

**Traps:**
- A healthy Start reads open. A bridged Stop reads closed, just like a healthy one.
- 230 V at the coil is only right for a 230 V coil.
- One fix can hide a second fault.

**Where:** any DOL starter: pumps, fans and compressors. `[M67]`
