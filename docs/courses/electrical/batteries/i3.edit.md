## Five before-and-after examples

1. **Explain, the opening**
   - Before: "It gives fewer the faster you draw them, and fewer again in the cold."
   - After: "The faster you draw them, the fewer you get. Cold cuts them again."

2. **Explain, the common slip**
   - Before: "Read the emf there instead, a common slip, and you over-read by those 0.70 V."
   - After: "A common slip is to read the emf there instead. That over-reads by the 0.70 V."

3. **Explain, the two causes of the shortfall**
   - Before: "More current means more lost volts, so it gets there sooner, with charge left in the plates. The acid must also soak into the plates to react. One way to picture it: at a high current the acid deep in the plates is used faster than fresh acid can reach it."
   - After: "More current means more lost volts. So the battery reaches that voltage sooner, with charge still left in the plates. Second, the acid must soak into the plates to react. Picture it this way. At a high current, the acid deep in the plates is used up faster than fresh acid can reach it."

4. **Interactive, screen 5**
   - Before: "The choices are the current nearly doubles and the terminal voltage dips a little; the current stays at 50 A because the battery sets it; or the current halves."
   - After: "First choice: the current nearly doubles, and the terminal voltage dips a little. Second: the current stays at 50 A, because the battery sets it. Third: the current halves."

5. **Analogy**
   - Before: "So the far end reads full voltage with nothing on, and sags under load."
   - After: "With nothing on, the far end reads full voltage. Under load, it sags."
