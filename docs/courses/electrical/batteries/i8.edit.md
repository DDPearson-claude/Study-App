## Five before-and-after examples

1. **Explain, the opening point**
   - Before: "A large battery is an emf behind a few milliohms, so almost nothing limits a short across it. That lets it drive thousands of amps into a fault, even part discharged, and its cells cannot be switched off."
   - After: "A large battery is an emf behind a few milliohms. So almost nothing limits a short across it. It can drive thousands of amps into a fault, even part discharged. And its cells cannot be switched off."

2. **Explain, the real 480 V string**
   - Before: "On one real 480 V string, the rule predicted about 2550 A, and a test measured 2530 A.[^4]"
   - After: "The rule holds on real batteries. For one 480 V string it predicted about 2550 A, and a test measured 2530 A.[^4]"

3. **Screen 9, the time trace**
   - Before: "Its exposed metal is live all the time. Switching off the charger and the loads stops their currents, but the cells keep their voltage."
   - After: "Their exposed metal is always live. Switching off the charger and the loads stops their currents. The cells keep their voltage."

4. **Analogy, where it breaks**
   - Before: "A battery's is almost pure resistance, so its fault arrives within milliseconds, and nothing upstream can make its cells dead."
   - After: "A battery's impedance is almost pure resistance, so its fault arrives within milliseconds. And nothing upstream can make its cells dead."

5. **Practice, the safe way first**
