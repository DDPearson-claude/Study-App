## Five before-and-after examples

1. **Explain, the opening**
   - Before: "A charger puts a lead-acid battery back by holding a set voltage on every cell. ... A little too low, and sulphate builds on the negative plate."
   - After: "A charger refills a lead-acid battery by holding a set voltage on every cell. ... Set it a little too low, and sulphate builds on the negative plate."

2. **Explain, why a fixed current harms**
   - Before: "A fixed current would keep flowing into a full battery, and current a full cell cannot store turns into heat and gas."
   - After: "A fixed current would keep flowing into a full battery. A full cell cannot store that current, so it turns into heat and gas."

3. **Explain, boost and equalise**
   - Before: "A [[boost charge]], at 2.33 to 2.40 V a cell for one maker, recharges faster after a discharge, and it must go back to float once the battery is full.[^3] An [[equalising charge]], at 2.50 V a cell in one common rule, is a deliberate overcharge."
   - After: "A [[boost charge]] recharges faster after a discharge. One maker sets it at 2.33 to 2.40 V a cell, and it must go back to float once the battery is full.[^3] An [[equalising charge]] is a deliberate overcharge, at 2.50 V a cell in one common rule."

4. **Interactive, screen 7**
   - Before: "7 asks what a year at a float raised from 2.25 to 2.35 V a cell does, to keep the bank fuller. The choices are a faster-corroding positive grid and warmer cells; more ampere-hours in store; or nothing, because a full cell takes no more charge."
   - After: "7 says the float has been raised from 2.25 to 2.35 V a cell to keep the bank fuller. It asks what a year of that does. First choice: the positive grid corrodes faster and the cells run warmer. Second: more ampere-hours in store. Third: nothing, because a full cell takes no more charge."

5. **Practice, worked example**
   - Before: "Its maker leaves it alone from 10 to 30 °C, and above 30 °C takes off 0.003 V a cell for each degree over.[^10] ... The charger is set to 2.215 × 55 = 121.8 V."
   - After: "Its maker leaves the setting alone from 10 to 30 °C. Above 30 °C, it takes off 0.003 V a cell for each degree over.[^10] ... Set the charger to 2.215 × 55 = 121.8 V."
