Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- During charging, electrolysis decomposes the water of the electrolyte into hydrogen and oxygen. [R1]
- Hydrogen and oxygen are produced more quickly near full charge, and continued charging after full charge produces a lot of gas. [R2]
- Above the gassing voltage of 2.40 V per cell, one maker recommends limiting the charging current to 2.5 to 5 A per 100 Ah. [R3]
- HSE gives the natural-ventilation opening area as 0.3 x N x I x S cm2, with N cells, I the overcharging current in amps, and S the dilution factor. [R4][R5]
- HSE says to use the maximum current the charger is likely to supply during a malfunction, to include a suitable safety margin. [R6]
- HSE design limits for hydrogen are 0.4 per cent in areas people enter and 1 per cent in enclosures people do not enter. [R7][R8]
- Hydrogen in air ignites and explodes readily between 4 per cent (lower limit) and 75 per cent (upper limit). [R9]
- On discharge, lead at the negative plate reacts with sulphuric acid to form lead sulphate, freeing electrons. [R10]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- At the positive plate, lead oxide reacts with sulphuric acid, again forming lead sulphate. [R11]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- On discharge both plates become lead sulphate and the sulphuric acid is used up, leaving water behind. [R12]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The acid concentration rises during charging and falls during discharge, so charging runs the discharge reaction backwards. [R13]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- The nominal voltage of a lead-acid cell is 2 V, but a charged and rested cell measures about 2.1 V open circuit. [R14]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- A primary cell is single-use and cannot be recharged; a secondary cell, such as lead-acid, can be recharged by an external power source. [R15]  (from an idea this one builds on)
- Lead acid uses constant-current constant-voltage charging: a regulated current raises the terminal voltage to the upper limit, then the current drops as the cell saturates. [R16]  (from an idea this one builds on)
