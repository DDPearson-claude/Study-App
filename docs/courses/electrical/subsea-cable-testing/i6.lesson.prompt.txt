- A TDR uses the radar principle: a pulse goes down the cable and changes in impedance send reflections back, which are displayed as a map of the cable. [1][2][3]
- A positive reflection shows a higher impedance and a negative one a lower impedance, because conductors moving apart or closer together change the impedance. [4][5][6]
- An open conductor shows a large positive trace and a short circuit a negative trace. A joint shows a small positive followed by a small negative trace. [7][8][9][10][11][12]
- A wet splice or water gives a short positive and negative trace, and your notes list a ragged trailing section for water ingress. [13][14]
- The velocity factor is the ratio of the pulse speed in the cable to the speed of light, and it must be set for the cable. If unknown, test a known length and adjust it until the displayed end distance is correct. [15][16][17]
- Megger lists a velocity factor of 0.54 to 0.62, which is 162 to 186 m per microsecond, for power XLPE cable. [18]
