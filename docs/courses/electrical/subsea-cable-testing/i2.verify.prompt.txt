Topic: HV Subsea Cable Testing
His level: KNOWS A LITTLE: he has met the basics but may hold common misconceptions.
This lesson: i2 "Phases and conductors: the four-wire measurement" — Below 1 Ω the leads and contacts swamp a two-wire reading, so the DLRO drives current through the C leads and measures across the P leads inside them; three loop readings solve three cores, and a cold cable reads low until corrected to 20 °C. [skill]
Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i3 Insulation resistance over ten minutes: IR, DAR and PI — The tester's current is charging, absorption and leakage; as the first two die away a dry cable's resistance climbs, so PI and DAR, ratios taken minutes apart on the same cable, show moisture a single reading can pass.
- i4 The sheath test: 4 kV per millimetre — The sheath keeps water away from the insulation, so it is tested on its own, screen to earth, at 4 kV d.c. per millimetre up to 10 kV for one minute, with every metal layer linked and an electrode on its outer face.
- i5 VLF withstand: why 0.1 Hz — A long cable is a big capacitor, so a 50 Hz withstand needs a huge charging current; at 0.1 Hz the reactance is 500 times higher and a portable set can hold 1.5 to 2.5 U0 for 30 or 60 minutes, driving any severe defect to fail now.
- i6 TDR: reading the reflections — A pulse runs along the pair and part of it returns wherever the impedance changes, up for an open and down for a short; distance is time × velocity ÷ 2, so the velocity must be set, best on a known length.
- i7 LIRA: the cable's impedance spectrum — An unmatched line resonates at frequencies set by its length and velocity; a local change in the insulation's permittivity changes the impedance there and ripples the spectrum, which LIRA turns into a spot along the cable graded by DNORM, …
