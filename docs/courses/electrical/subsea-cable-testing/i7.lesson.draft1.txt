    "text": "LIRA treats the cable as a line, as a TDR does. Wherever the impedance changes, part of a signal comes back. But LIRA reads the echoes in frequency, not in time. It sweeps thousands of small sine waves into one end and records the cable's impedance at each frequency. That record is the cable's [[impedance spectrum]]. Every change along the cable leaves its mark on it.\n\nStart with a healthy core with its far end open. At low frequency the core and screen are just a capacitor, so the impedance is high and falls as the frequency rises. As the frequency climbs, each wave reaches the far end and comes back. Where a quarter wavelength fits the cable, the trip there and back is half a wavelength. The wave arrives back upside down against the one going in. The two cancel, and the impedance dips. Where half a wavelength fits, the wave arrives back in step, and the impedance peaks. Each extra half wavelength repeats the pattern, so the dips come every v ÷ 2L. A longer cable's resonances sit closer together. A far end matched to the cable sends nothing back, so then there are none.\n\nNow add a short length where water has got into the insulation. Water raises the permittivity, so the capacitance there rises and the impedance falls. A little of each wave reflects from it, after a round trip of 2x ÷ v. That echo puts a small ripple on the spectrum, repeating every v ÷ 2x. LIRA's software turns each ripple back into a round trip, and the round trip into a place, just as a TDR uses L = t × v ÷ 2. The result is the [[spot signature]]. Each spot is graded by [[DNORM]]. Its sign follows the type of change, and its colour gives the severity. This picture is simplified: real cable loss rounds off the resonances, and the real software reads the phase of the impedance, which shows small changes best. On String B, LIRA confirms TDR's fault from both ends, and the healthy cores' traces become the baseline for later years.\n\n**A cable's resonances come every v ÷ 2L, a local change adds a ripple every v ÷ 2x, and LIRA turns that ripple into a spot x metres along.**[^11]"
