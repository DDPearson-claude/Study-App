- A TDR uses the radar principle: a pulse goes down the cable and changes in impedance send reflections back, which are displayed as a map of the cable. [R1][R2][R3]
- A positive reflection shows a higher impedance and a negative one a lower impedance, because conductors moving apart or closer together change the impedance. [R4][R5][R6]
- An open conductor shows a large positive trace and a short circuit a negative trace. A joint shows a small positive followed by a small negative trace. [R7][R8][R9][R10][R11][R12]
- A wet splice or water gives a short positive and negative trace, and your notes list a ragged trailing section for water ingress. [R13][R14]
- The velocity factor is the ratio of the pulse speed in the cable to the speed of light, and it must be set for the cable. If unknown, test a known length and adjust it until the displayed end distance is correct. [R15][R16][R17]
- Megger lists a velocity factor of 0.54 to 0.62, which is 162 to 186 m per microsecond, for power XLPE cable. [R18]
- Distance comes from the time of the reflected pulse times the velocity, taking half of the time, because the pulse goes to the fault and back. Your notes write L = t × v ÷ 2. [R19][R20][R21]
- If the velocity factor is wrong for the cable, the displayed distance will be wrong. [R22]
- A clear open reflection is not necessarily the end of the cable, because it can also be a break or cut, so the end is proved with open and linked tests. Linked gives a negative peak, open a positive one. [R23][R24][R25][R26]

  "text": "A TDR sends a short, low-voltage pulse down a core and its screen and times the echoes. Wherever the impedance changes, part of the pulse comes back. The time tells you how far away the change is. The shape tells you what it is.\n\nThe pulse sees the cable as an impedance of its own, the [[characteristic impedance]]. The cable's inductance and capacitance per metre set it. While that stays the same, the pulse runs on and nothing returns. Where it changes, the share that comes back is (Z load − Z0) ÷ (Z load + Z0). An open end is infinite impedance, so the pulse comes back the right way up. A short is zero, so it comes back upside down. A fault from core to screen sits in parallel with the cable beyond it. Parallel paths give a lower impedance, so the reflection points down. The higher the fault's resistance, the less it lowers the impedance. On String B a 35 Ω fault sends back 0.28 of the pulse, but a 1000 Ω fault only 0.01. So a high-resistance fault can look like healthy cable.\n\nThe set measures time, and the time covers the trip there and back. So the distance is L = t × v ÷ 2. The insulation sets v, through its permittivity. It is often given as a [[velocity factor]], v divided by the speed of light. Every distance is worked with the same v, so a wrong v stretches them all by the same fraction. That is why the velocity is set on a known length, with the far end open.
