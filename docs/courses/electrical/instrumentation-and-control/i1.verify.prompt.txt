This lesson: i1 "The measurement chain: from the plant to a number" — Calibration checks an instrument's straight input-to-output line against a standard: the zero adjustment shifts the whole line, and the span adjustment tilts it. [skill]
Later ideas in the course (every claim must stay true after Dan has learned each of them):
- i2 Temperature: the PT100 and the thermocouple — Both temperature sensors can be fooled by their wiring: a PT100's leads add resistance, and a thermocouple reads its hot end against its cold end.
- i3 Pressure, level and flow — Pressure gives level, from the pressure at a tank's foot, and flow, from the drop across a pipe's narrowing, which grows as flow squared.
- i4 The 4-20 mA loop — A 4-20 mA loop is one series ring, so the same current carries the reading over any cable length the supply can drive.
- i5 Control loops: measure, compare, act — A controller compares the measured value with the set point and acts; on/off control always swings, and swings further when the reading arrives late.
- i6 PID: tuning a loop by feel — A PID controller acts on the error: proportional alone leaves an offset, integral removes it but can swing, derivative damps swings but amplifies noise.
- i7 Logic and PLCs: from relays to ladder — A PLC solves relay logic as a ladder, scanning over and over: read every input, solve the rungs top to bottom, then write every output.
- i8 Alarms, trips and fail-safe design — A trip circuit kept energised trips on a broken wire, and sensor voting trades missed trips against false ones; designers judge which failure matters more.

Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Span is the algebraic difference between the upper and lower range values; a 0 to 300 psig transmitter calibrated to 4 to 20 mA has a 300 psig input span and a 16 mA output span. [R1][R2]
