Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Design current is the magnitude of the current to be carried by a circuit in normal service; the course slides and an IET article give the same meaning. [R1][R2]
- For a three-phase load with known power factor and efficiency, Ib = P / (VL × Eff × PF × √3), where P is the output of the load and VL is the line-to-line voltage (400 V). [R3][R4][R5]
- Motors need more current than expected for their output because of reactance, so power factor matters: a lower power factor means more apparent power, and so more current, for the same real power. [R6][R7][R8]
- Efficiency is useful output power divided by input power, so the supply must provide more than the output; the slides show an efficiency and power factor left out would size the cable on 28.86 A rather than 41.47 A. [R9][R10][R11]
- Three-phase real power is √3 × line voltage × line current × power factor, which is why Ib divides by √3 × VL × Eff × PF. [R12][R3]
- If a separate voltage is given, for example at a sub-board, that value is used in place of 230 V or 400 V; the slides work 20 kW at 393 V as 29.38 A. [R13][R14][R15]
- In a balanced delta load the line current is the phase (winding) current times the square root of 3, so each winding carries the line current divided by √3; in star the line and phase currents are equal. [R16][R17][R18][R19]
- Diversity percentages are only found in the On-Site Guide and are guidance that need not be applied; the slides' example designs ten 100 W lamps at 66%, or 660 W, rather than 1000 W. [R20][R21][R22]
- Dan's course flowchart is headed "Electrical Circuit Design Calculation Process" for BS 7671:2018 + Amd2:2022, and its note 2 separates a suitable protective conductor size from the minimum protective conductor size. [R23][R24]  (whole topic)
- The showroom supply is three phase four wire 400 V, TN-C-S, with Ze declared as 0.1 ohm and a prospective fault current of 4.6 kA; the ambient temperature is 20 degrees Celsius. [R25][R26]  (whole topic)
- The showroom adds a 400 V three-phase 11 kW motor at power factor 0.8 lagging and efficiency 86%, controlled by a star-delta starter. [R27]  (whole topic)
- Overload protection is the condition Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz (Regulation 433.1.1), plus I2 ≤ 1.45 Iz, where I2 is the current causing effective operation of the protective device. [R28][R29][R30]  (whole topic)
- The course slides take √3 as 1.73, so their printed answers can differ in the last decimal places from the same sum worked with the calculator's √3. [R31][R32]  (whole topic)
