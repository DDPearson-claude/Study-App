  "text": "[[Maximum demand]] is the largest current an installation draws at any one time.[^6] It may be worked out for the whole installation or for a part of it,[^7] and it is the current the circuits, switches and protective devices that feed that part normally carry,[^51] so the supply and each submain are chosen for it. It is less than the connected load, because not everything runs flat out together: allowing for that with common sense rather than designing for the maximum is [[diversity]],[^16] and its figures are guidance from the On-Site Guide, not a rule.[^1] On a three-phase board there is a second question as well: which phase each single-phase circuit goes on.\n\nEach lighting circuit runs between one line and the neutral, so its current comes back along the neutral. The three line voltages are a third of a cycle apart, and each circuit's current keeps step with its own line voltage, so the three currents are a third of a cycle apart too; three equal currents spaced like that add up to nothing in the neutral: the balanced three-phase load your slides describe has no neutral current at all.[^13] Make them unequal and they no longer cancel;[^14] what is left over flows in the neutral, so the neutral carries the imbalance, not the total. With the course's practice values for the fittings, put DBL2's seven lighting circuits all on L1, and L1 and the neutral both carry 24.35 A; share them as the course does, and the phases carry 8.22, 8.21 and 7.92 A and the neutral 0.30 A. That is why single-phase circuits are spread about equally across the three phases,[^52] and why the heaviest phase, not the average, sets what the supply must carry.[^15] The picture treats all the fittings as having the same power factor, a simplified case; real fittings differ a little, which moves the neutral current very little.\n\nDiversity takes the total down by type of load. For small shops, stores and offices, for example, the On-Site Guide allows 100% of the largest standard circuit and 50% of every other.[^20] BS 7671 itself has no such table and leaves the assessment to the designer.[^3] Where diversity belongs is argued: your slides apply 66% to one lighting circuit's Ib,[^2] a training provider applies it only to the total at the origin,[^26] and an IET answer says it might only rarely be appropriate for a single circuit's design current.[^27] For EV charge points without load management the IEC wording sets the factor at 1,[^21] which the course follows, though it is argued. Common sense can also say none: the showroom's lights are all on from 7.30 am to 9 pm (your assignment, question 5a), so the course takes them at 100%.\n\n**Maximum demand is the connected load with diversity only where common sense allows, shared across L1, L2 and L3 so that the heaviest phase is as light as it can be.**[^53][^52]"
