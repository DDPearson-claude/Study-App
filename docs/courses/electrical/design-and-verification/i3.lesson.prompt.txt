- Regulation 433.1.1 requires In not less than Ib, In not above the lowest Iz of the circuit conductors, and I2 not above 1.45 Iz; the slides write the first two as Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz. [1][2][3]
- Anything that makes the cable artificially warm, such as thermal insulation or a higher ambient temperature, needs a rating factor; Ca is for ambient temperature, Cg for grouping and Ci for thermal insulation. [4][5]
- Rating factors are applied as divisors to In, giving the tabulated current It the cable needs; It is the tabulated figure for a single circuit at the table's ambient, while Iz is the capacity as installed. [6][7][8]
- Only factors that act together are combined; the slides' 3 kW heater gives It = 21.2 A (Ca and Cg) and 19.3 A (Ca and Ci) and designs to the higher, on the reference method with the lowest Iz. [9][10][11][12]
- A device's I2 is typically 1.45 times In (a 10 A type B breaker: 14.5 A), and the cable must withstand it: for Iz = 13 A the check is I2 ≤ 1.45 Iz, that is 14.5 against 18.85 A. [13][14][15][16][17]
