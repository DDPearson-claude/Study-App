  "text": "In a balanced three-phase circuit the three line currents cancel in the neutral, so a balanced motor may have no neutral connected at all.[^3][^5] Some loads, such as fluorescent lighting banks, rectifiers and variable-speed drives, draw currents that are not sinusoidal,[^7] and part of what they draw does not cancel: the third harmonics of the three lines add in the neutral,[^4] and can make it carry more than any line.[^40] Then the neutral current, not the line current, may have to set the cable.[^2]\n\nPicture each line current as two waves added together: one at the supply frequency, the fundamental, and a smaller one at three times that frequency, the [[third harmonic]]. The three fundamentals are a third of a cycle, 120°, apart, so at every instant they sum to nothing in the neutral.[^3] The third harmonic runs three times as fast, so that same 120° delay is a whole cycle of it: the three lines' third harmonics rise and fall together, in phase,[^15] and the neutral carries their sum.[^6] If each line's third harmonic is a share h of its line current IL, the neutral carries Ibn = 3 × h × IL.[^1] That passes IL once 3h passes 1, near a third: your slides' 150 A load at 35% puts 3 × 0.35 × 150 = 157.50 A in the neutral.[^1] This is a simplified picture, three equal lines and the third harmonic alone; the other triple harmonics add in the same way.[^4]\n\nThe current tables are worked for three loaded conductors,[^19] and a neutral carrying harmonic current is a fourth [[loaded conductor]] heating the same cable;[^9] BS 7671 counts it as one once the third harmonic and its multiples pass 15% of the fundamental line current (in NICEIC's summary of Regulation 523.6.1).[^20] More heat in the same cable means each conductor reaches its limit at a lower current, so the cable is derated: that is Table 4Aa's 0.86. A neutral that carries only the lines' imbalance is offset by less heat in a line;[^21] a harmonic neutral current comes with no such fall in the lines.[^10] So Table 4Aa, as your slides give it, picks the current and a rating factor by the third harmonic: up to 15%, the line current with 1.0;[^11] 15 to 33%, the line current with 0.86;[^12] 33 to 45%, the neutral current with 0.86;[^13] over 45%, the neutral current with 1.0,[^14] because by then the neutral carries over 135% of the line and the three lines are not fully loaded.[^24] The factor divides In with the others: It ≥ In ÷ (Ca × Cg × the Table 4Aa factor).[^25] Your slides start all of this from the maker's data: harmonics are considered only when it says one is present, and its percentage goes into the sums (p.37).[^27][^28]\n\n**Where the maker's data gives a third harmonic of more than about a third of the line current, the neutral carries more than the line, and the neutral current, Ibn = 3 × h × IL, sets the cable.**[^2][^1][^13]"
