## The explanation Dan reads after playing (use the same words for the same things)
The design current, Ib, is the current a circuit carries in normal service. The cable carries what the load draws from the supply, not what it gives out, so Ib is worked from the load's input: Ib = P ÷ (√3 × UL × efficiency × power factor), where P is the output in watts and UL the line voltage.

Start from the output, because that is the figure on the nameplate: your course slides take P as the output of the load. Efficiency is the output divided by the input, so the input is the output divided by the efficiency. At 87%, 20 kW out needs 20 ÷ 0.87 = 22.99 kW in: the difference is lost inside the machine, but it comes down the cable first. Then the power factor. A motor needs more current than its output suggests, because of reactance. The power factor is the ratio of the real power in kW to the apparent power in kVA that the supply gives, and with an inductive load such as a motor it is below 1, so the kVA is bigger than the kW. So the apparent power is the input divided by the power factor: 22.99 ÷ 0.8 = 28.74 kVA. The line current follows the apparent power, because three-phase real power is √3 × line voltage × line current × power factor; turned round, 28.74 × 1000 ÷ (√3 × 400) = 41.48 A. Do both divisions at once and you have your slides' rule. Sized on the output alone, the same load would be designed on 28.87 A, and your slides say that 'would cause big implications for …

### The pipeline
Controls register themselves; their values form `params` = `{id: value}`.
```
K.model(fn)        fn(params) -> outputs {name: number | string}. Pure: no DOM, no lists or objects.
K.update(fn)       fn(params, outputs): draw everything here. More than one is allowed.
K.at(over)         -> outputs of the model at those params (others take each control's starting value)
K.check(label, fn, {source?})   fn() must return exactly true
K.ready()          call once, last: draws the opening state and runs the checks
K.complete()       at the lesson's end
K.params()  K.outputs()  K.refresh()   (refresh reruns the pipeline after you change your own state)
```
A readout whose id is an output key updates itself.

### Controls
Every control takes `into` (a selector or element). Ids are unique; use the lesson's ids exactly.
```
K.control({id, label, min, max, step, value, unit?, prefix?, fmt?, log?, snap?, hint?, into}) -> {el, get(), set(v)}
K.choice({id, label, options: [value | {value, label}], value, hint?, into}) -> {el, get, set}
K.toggle({id, label, value: false, hint?, into}) -> {el, get, set}
K.stepper({id, steps: [title | {title, text}], value: 0, compact?, label?, into}) -> {el, get, set, setSteps(list)}
```

content). `button` is an action (Send it in): use it, not plain buttons. `drag` lets Dan move
the thing itself: `toValue` maps the pointer (viewBox units; px from `el`'s top-left for HTML)

### Rules for a great interactive
- One idea. The answer to Dan's prediction shows within about 3 seconds of his first move (add
  a K.check that it does). Include something Dan causes and a case that surprises. One or two
  controls, three at most.
- Phone first: nothing wider than 340 px, no fixed widths over 300 px, rows wrap, no hover-only
  information, tap targets at least 44 px. Size tiles and grid columns in rem (`minmax(6rem,
  1fr)`): text grows a quarter at Text size XL, and no word may split across two lines.
- Every number shown is a control, computed by the rule shown, a constant from the lesson, an
  example value named once in the lead, or a date from the lesson. Never invent data, rates or chances.
- The `.say` sentence describes what is on screen now, plainly and warmly, and reads right at the
  extremes ("none of the 100"). Never claim Dan did something ("When you slid it to 20"):
  describe the state, or write conditionally ("Push it past 20 and…").
- 3-5 `K.check` known answers worked out by hand: an edge case, a shape fact (rises, halves,
  always last), and one answer from outside your formula. `source` may ONLY be a URL from the
  lesson's own sources, and only on a check whose label restates what that source's quote says;
  a result of your rule, or a fact about your drawing, takes none. Compare numbers with
  `K.near(a, b, tol)`: tolerance about a millionth of the size, a thousandth where rounding meets roots.
- `K.anim` for motion, never timers: soft, unhurried, nothing popping in. Parts arrive about 0.6
  s apart by `K.fade(el, K.arrive(T, t0))`; travel and growth use `K.ease`; a demonstration takes
  3-5 s. No `vh` units, `position: fixed` or `height: 100%`.
- Ideas without numbers (mechanism, process, structure, history, concept): prefer a timeline
  slider, a named choice, a sorter or a drag; use `K.stepper` only when every step changes the
  drawing. Checks assert order and structure facts.
- A timeline runs forward: a slider rising left to right, its `fmt` showing the date (−500 as
  "500 BC"), or a stepper of named events in order. A lesson's calendar-year control keeps its
  range, its `fmt` showing the year; years after the first date are for new controls. Each dated
  event appears as he reaches its date.

### The self-test
Before Dan sees it, the app loads your body 340, 720 and 1040 px wide, runs every check, moves
every control, button, animation and drag, reveals the after-move parts, and looks again at Text
size XL and in the other theme. It fails on an exception, NaN or undefined on the page, an update
over 150 ms, sideways overflow, text cut off or over other text, SVG text under 11 px on a phone,
a line or arrowhead through a label, text under 3:1 contrast with what is behind it, a colour
name, a blank hole until a move, or no `K.ready()`.

<script>
  // gamma: ratio of specific heats for air, the value NASA Glenn's Brayton page uses.
  const GAMMA = 1.4;
  const SOURCE = 'https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/brayton.html';
  const pct = (r) => 100 * (1 - Math.pow(r, -(GAMMA - 1) / GAMMA));

  const r = K.control({ id: 'r', label: 'Pressure ratio', hint: 'Pressure after the compressor ÷ pressure before it', min: 1, max: 50, step: 1, value: 8, unit: ':1', into: '#controls' });
  K.readout({ id: 'eff', label: 'Ideal efficiency', unit: '%', decimals: 1, big: true, into: '#outs' });

  K.stage('#plot', '#controls');   // stacked on a phone, side by side on a laptop

  const plot = K.plot('#plot', {
    x: { min: 1, max: 50, label: 'Pressure ratio' },
    y: { min: 0, max: 100, label: 'Ideal efficiency (%)' },
    label: 'Ideal efficiency rises steeply at low pressure ratios, then flattens',
  });

  // The pure model: pressure ratio -> ideal efficiency in percent.
  K.model((p) => ({ eff: pct(p.r) }));

  K.update((p, o) => {
    plot.draw({
      series: [{ fn: pct, label: 'Ideal efficiency' }],
      marks: [{ x: p.r, y: o.eff, guides: true }],   // its value is in the readout alone
    });
    document.getElementById('work').style.width = o.eff + '%';
    document.getElementById('split').setAttribute('aria-label', 'The filled part becomes work: ' + (o.eff < 50 ? 'under' : 'over') + ' half the heat');

    let s;
    if (p.r === 1) {
      s = 'With no squeeze at all (r = 1), the hot gas has no extra pressure to push the turbine with, so no work comes out: 0%.';
    } else if (p.r * 2 <= 50) {
      const more = pct(p.r * 2) - o.eff;
      s = 'At ' + p.r + ':1 an ideal engine turns ' + o.eff.toFixed(1) + '% of the fuel\'s heat into work. Doubling the squeeze to ' +
        p.r * 2 + ':1 would add ' + more.toFixed(1) + ' more points.';
    } else {
      const less = o.eff - pct(p.r / 2);
      s = 'At ' + p.r + ':1 an ideal engine turns ' + o.eff.toFixed(1) + '% of the fuel\'s heat into work. Halving the squeeze would cost only ' +
        less.toFixed(1) + ' points: the curve has flattened.';
    }
    // Until Dan moves the slider the sentence invites rather than answers his prediction.
    document.getElementById('say').textContent = K.moved ? s : 'Does doubling the squeeze double the work you get? Move the pressure ratio and see.';
  });

  // Known answers, worked out by hand from the formula.
  K.check('No squeeze (r = 1) gives 0% efficiency', () => K.near(K.at({ r: 1 }).eff, 0, 1e-9));
  K.check('r = 10 gives 48.2%, from the ideal Brayton formula', () => K.near(K.at({ r: 10 }).eff, 48.205, 0.001), { source: SOURCE });
  K.check('Efficiency rises with every step up in pressure ratio', () => {
    for (let x = 1; x < 50; x++) if (!(K.at({ r: x + 1 }).eff > K.at({ r: x }).eff)) return false;
    return true;
  });
  K.check('Diminishing returns: 1 → 2 adds 18.0 points, 20 → 40 adds only 7.6', () =>
    K.near(K.at({ r: 2 }).eff - K.at({ r: 1 }).eff, 17.97, 0.01) && K.near(K.at({ r: 40 }).eff - K.at({ r: 20 }).eff, 7.63, 0.01));
  K.check('Even at r = 50 the ideal stays below 100% (67.3%)', () => K.near(K.at({ r: 50 }).eff, 67.30, 0.01));

  K.ready();
</script>


## Refresher lessons: typed answers, written answers, the case file, the summary

A refresher course's steps lesson (a ready-made course built in Claude Code) has four more kit
pieces. The app passes the page two pieces of checked data, as it passes the theme: the course's
case file (`K.caseFile`) and the lesson's own `{written, summary}` (`K.lesson`). Name what you want
by id; the kit draws the checked words, so never retype a figure, a point or a summary line.

```
K.figure(id) -> {id, label, value, unit, text, part, lesson}     a given or worked figure; print f.text ("23.08 A")
K.scenario({into, open?})                                        the case's scenario panel (folds)
K.carried({parts: ['1a'], into})  -> {el, refresh()}             earlier Section A parts at the course's figures
K.answer({id, label?, unit, answer, decimals?, tolerance?, accept?, slips?, into, onCheck?, onEnter?})
    -> {el, input, check(), result, right(), missed(), reveal(), reset(), set(text)}
K.answer.grade(text, spec) -> {state: 'right'|'wrong'|'empty'|'unreadable', value, accepted?, slip?, hint?}
K.written({id, into, onMark?}) -> {el, id, stage(), answered()}
K.summary({into, kept?})
```

**Typed answers** (Section A, where a C&G paper has him write a figure). A decimal-keypad field;
your Check button calls `a.check()` (Enter does too) and you write the feedback from the result.
`answer` is the course's figure, each line worked from the rounded figures shown; it is accepted
within half a unit in its last place (`decimals`, default 2). `accept: [13.32]` adds the answer
worked from unrounded figures; `slips: [{value: 50, hint: 'You left out √3.'}]` names a usual slip
and its hint (`r.hint`). A unit typed after the number ("13.33 A") is fine. Give the part's
case-file id as the answer's id ('1b'), so `K.carried` knows when he missed it: a part got wrong
and never right, or shown with `a.reveal()`, is carried forward at the course's figure and says so.
After two wrong tries, show the working and `reveal()`; Continue once `right()`.
The self-test grades every answer: its figure and each accepted one right (with the unit too), a
near miss and each slip refused, and the figure typed into the field and checked on its step.

**Written answers** (Section B: "Explain why…", "State two…"). `K.written({id: 'b1', into})` shows
`lesson.written.b1`'s question, a box for his words and Check my answer. Claude marks them against
the lesson's points through the app (never from the page): each point comes back with an ink tick
or a dashed ring, a statement that is wrong named in red, one line on what he did well and one
nudge; one more go, then the model answer. Without Claude, the points are a checklist he ticks.
No marks, no grades, nothing to score. `onMark(r)` runs when a result is drawn; let him Continue
once `answered()`.

**The summary** (Keep this, the last step). `K.summary({into: '#keep'})` draws `lesson.summary`
as a card (what it is for, the rule, the traps, the case's figures so far, where it lives) and says
it is the first page of the chapter in his Book. Then the Finish button calls `K.complete()`.

**STEPS entries** (tools/course/guide.mjs reads them for the Book): a typed-answer step keeps
`want` (the answer as printed, '13.33 A'); a written step is `{kind: 'written', written: 'b1',
name, task}`; the summary step is `{kind: 'summary', name: 'Keep this'}`; the recall opener is a
DO step like any other.
