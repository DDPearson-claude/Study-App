## 1. Recall question (page, screen 1)
- Before: Your slides give a diversity percentage for a lighting circuit. Is diversity a rule you must apply?
- After: There is a diversity percentage for lighting circuits. Is diversity a rule you must apply?

## 2. The red warning (page, screen 2)
- Before: too low, and the supply fuse, or a submain's device and cable, carries more than it was chosen for (the course's reasoning); too high, and the cables are bigger and dearer than they need be (your slides p.10).
- After: Too low, and the supply fuse or a submain's device and cable carries more than it was chosen for. Too high, and the cables are bigger and dearer than they need be.

## 3. The why (page, screen 3)
- Before: Equal currents on L1, L2 and L3 cancel in the neutral, as your slides say of a balanced three-phase load (p.33), but unequal ones do not: the neutral carries the imbalance, and the heaviest phase sets what the supply must carry.
- After: Equal currents on L1, L2 and L3 cancel in the neutral, but unequal ones do not. So the neutral carries the imbalance, and the heaviest phase sets what the supply must carry.

## 4. The allowances (page, screen 5)
- Before: The allowances this lesson uses, from the On-Site Guide's column for small shops, stores, offices and business premises. The course reads a showroom into that column, its own assumption; a training provider warns that commercial work may need measured loads instead.
- After: These are the On-Site Guide's allowances for small shops, stores, offices and business premises. We treat the showroom as one of those. Larger commercial work may need measured loads instead.

## 7. Explanation (lesson JSON, explain)
- Before: Where diversity belongs is argued: your slides apply 66% to one lighting circuit's Ib,[^2] a training provider applies it only to the total at the origin,[^26] and an IET answer says it might only rarely be appropriate for a single circuit's design current.[^27]
- After: Where to apply diversity is argued. Some apply 66% to a single lighting circuit's Ib.[^2] Others apply it only to the total at the origin.[^26] Another view is that it is only rarely right for a single circuit's design current.[^27]

## 8. Section B model answer (lesson JSON, written.b1.model)
- Before: Not every load runs at full current at the same time, so designing for every load at once overstates what the installation draws; your slides call diversity designing with common sense rather than for the maximum (p.10). Allowing for it keeps cable sizes smaller and costs lower (p.10), and lets the supply, submains and switchgear be chosen for the maximum demand they will really carry.
- After: Not every load runs at full current at the same time. So designing for every load at once overstates what the installation draws. Diversity means designing with common sense rather than for the maximum. It keeps cable sizes smaller and costs lower. It also lets you choose the supply, submains and switchgear for the demand they will really carry.
