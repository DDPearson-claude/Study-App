# i9 plain-English edit: Verification and handover

Only the wording changed. Every number, unit, rule, figure id and [^n] citation is the same.
The same edits were made in i9.lesson.final.json wherever it holds the same text. It lacks four of the
fact-checked sentences, so those edits were made in the verified file only. Its older explain sentence
on direct readings got the matching edit. 46 sentences were rewritten. `validate.mjs lesson ... --final`
passes on both files. The brief keeps its required "The one thing you should see is ___ when you ___." form,
and the Section B cite fields and the summary's "where" are left as data.

## 1. Screen 9 and explain: no "industry guidance suggests"
Before: "Industry guidance suggests 5 years for commercial premises, and 3 for industrial.[^3]"
After: "The recommended maximum is 5 years for commercial premises and 3 for industrial.[^3]"
The same change was made in the explain, the Section B point and the model answer.

## 2. Screen 9: two semicolon-chained lists turned into short sentences
Before: "To the inspector, before the tests: each circuit's expected R1+R2 at the test temperature; each circuit's maximum permitted Zs, and where it comes from if not Table 41.3; a chart or schedule of each circuit, ...; anything a test could damage, such as SELV equipment; and the supply's characteristics."

A chart or schedule of each circuit, ... Anything a test could damage, such as SELV equipment. The supply's characteristics."

## 3. Screen 3: a semicolon chain and a "Drag" with no read sense
Before: "A thermometer beside them marks 10 °C, where the maximum measured Zs values are set; 20 °C, where the resistance tables are set; and 70 °C, the cable running hot." ... "Drag it down to 10 °C and the reading falls to 0.348 Ω."
After: "A thermometer beside them marks three temperatures. The maximum measured Zs values are set at 10 °C. The resistance tables are set at 20 °C. At 70 °C the cable is running hot." ... "At 10 °C the reading falls to 0.348 Ω."

## 4. Screen 8: one long "or" sentence split, the point first
Before: "A reading well over the expected figure means something differs from the design, such as a longer run or a poor connection, or the test itself went wrong. Find out which."
After: "A reading well over the expected figure means something is wrong. The installation may differ from the design, such as a longer run or a poor connection. Or the test itself went wrong. Find out which."
