Notes (claims, and the sources that support them):
- Regulation 433.1.1 requires In not less than Ib, In not above the lowest Iz of the circuit conductors, and I2 not above 1.45 Iz; the slides write the first two as Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz. [R1][R2][R3]
- Anything that makes the cable artificially warm, such as thermal insulation or a higher ambient temperature, needs a rating factor; Ca is for ambient temperature, Cg for grouping and Ci for thermal insulation. [R4][R5]
- Rating factors are applied as divisors to In, giving the tabulated current It the cable needs; It is the tabulated figure for a single circuit at the table's ambient, while Iz is the capacity as installed. [R6][R7][R8]
- Only factors that act together are combined; the slides' 3 kW heater gives It = 21.2 A (Ca and Cg) and 19.3 A (Ca and Ci) and designs to the higher, on the reference method with the lowest Iz. [R9][R10][R11][R12]
- A device's I2 is typically 1.45 times In (a 10 A type B breaker: 14.5 A), and the cable must withstand it: for Iz = 13 A the check is I2 ≤ 1.45 Iz, that is 14.5 against 18.85 A. [R13][R14][R15][R16][R17]
- A semi-enclosed (rewireable) BS 3036 fuse has an I2 that can be up to 2 × Iz, so its In must not exceed 0.725 × Iz, which the slides put in as Cf = 0.725. [R18][R19][R20]
- Circuit-breaker types B, C and D relate to the start-up current of the circuit; the assignment asks for cables from the starter to the motor to be chosen on delta running conditions and other factors present. [R21][R22]

The table's figure, It, is for one circuit at the table's ambient;[^6] what the cable can carry where it is actually installed is its [[current-carrying capacity]], Iz.[^7] Anything that makes the cable artificially warm, a hotter room or thermal insulation, needs a [[rating factor]],[^10] and so do other circuits beside it: Ca for the ambient, Cg for grouping,[^11] Ci for insulation.
