    "text": "The design current, Ib, is the current a circuit carries in normal service.[^1][^2] The cable carries what the load draws from the supply, not what it gives out, so Ib is worked from the load's input: Ib = P ÷ (√3 × UL × efficiency × power factor), where P is the output in watts and UL the line voltage.[^3][^4][^5]\n\nStart from the output, because that is the figure on the nameplate: your course slides take P as the output of the load.[^4] [[Efficiency]] is the output divided by the input,[^9][^10] so the input is the output divided by the efficiency. At 87%, 20 kW out needs 20 ÷ 0.87 = 22.99 kW in: the difference is lost inside the machine, but it comes down the cable first. Then the [[power factor]]. A motor needs more current than its output suggests, because of reactance.[^6] The power factor is the ratio of the real power in kW to the apparent power in kVA that the supply gives,[^7] and with an inductive load such as a motor it is below 1, so the kVA is bigger than the kW.[^8] So the apparent power is the input divided by the power factor: 22.99 ÷ 0.8 = 28.74 kVA. The line current follows the apparent power, because three-phase real power is √3 × line voltage × line current × power factor;[^12] turned round, 28.74 × 1000 ÷ (√3 × 400) = 41.48 A. Do both divisions at once and you have your slides' rule.[^3] Sized on the output alone, the same load would be designed on 28.87 A, and your slides say that 'would cause big implications for the operation and safety of the circuit'.[^11]\n\nTwo more things set the number. The voltage is the one at the board: if you are given a separate value, it must be used,[^13] and at a sub-board it is lower than 400 V because the submain drops some on the way.[^14] For the same power a lower voltage needs more current, so your slides' 20 kW at a 393 V sub-board is 20 000 ÷ (√3 × 393) = 29.38 A,[^15] against 28.87 A at 400 V.[^32] Diversity is a different kind of thing: its percentages come from the On-Site Guide and are guidance that need not be applied.[^20] Ten 100 W lamps at 66% could be designed at 660 W rather than 1000 W.[^21] BS 7671 itself has no diversity table; it has the designer assess maximum demand and allows diversity to be taken into account.[^22]\n\nThat is the first half of the course's puzzle. The showroom motor's 11 kW at 400 V is 15.88 A on its output alone, but at its 86% efficiency and power factor of 0.8 it draws 11 000 ÷ (√3 × 400 × 0.86 × 0.8) = 23.08 A.[^27] **Size from what the circuit draws: Ib = P ÷ (√3 × UL × efficiency × power factor), at the voltage at the board.**[^3][^13]"
