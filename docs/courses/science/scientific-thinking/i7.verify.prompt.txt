  "text": "Light bends where it crosses into water or glass because it changes speed there. Snell's law, which links the angles on each side, says by how much.\n\nLight is slower in water than in air.[^2] One way to picture it: its front is a row of marchers, arm in arm. Meet the water at a slant, and one end crosses first and slows first. The other end still moves at the faster speed, so the row swings round towards the normal. So a ray entering a slower material bends towards the normal, and a ray leaving it bends away. Square on, both ends slow together, so the ray goes straight on, just more slowly. The [[refractive index]], n, measures the slowing. It is light's speed in empty space divided by its speed in the material.[^3] Light in empty space travels at 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. Fresh water's index is 1.333,[^1] so light crosses water at 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.333 = 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s. You know this kind of ratio from cable fault location.
