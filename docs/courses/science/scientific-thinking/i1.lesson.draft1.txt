  "explain": { "text": "Where two gears mesh, their teeth pass one for one. That single fact sets which way each gear turns and how fast.\n\nLook at the point where two gears meet, their [[mesh]]. Each tooth on the driver pushes a tooth on the other gear along with it. So at the mesh the teeth of both gears move the same way, and the two gears turn opposite ways.[^1] Say the driver turns clockwise. Its teeth on its right-hand side move down. They push the next gear's teeth on its left-hand side down too, which turns that gear anticlockwise. So each mesh flips the direction, and along a line of gears the directions alternate. An odd number of gears in a line turns the last one the driver's way, and an even number turns it the other way. A gear set between two others makes them turn the same way, and is called an [[idler gear]]. Textbooks add that a ring gear, with its teeth on the inside, turns the same way as a gear inside it. This lesson keeps to teeth on the outside.\n\nNow close a line into a loop, so the last gear meshes with the first. Round the loop the directions still alternate. With an even number of gears, the last gear turns opposite to the first, as their mesh needs, and the loop turns. With an odd number, the last gear and the first try to turn the same way. At their mesh the teeth would have to move in opposite directions, and neither can give way. So an odd number of gears in a closed loop, lying flat in one plane, will not turn, whatever their sizes and tooth counts.[^3]\n\nThe same count gives the speed. In any minute both gears pass the same number of teeth through the mesh. A gear passes its whole tooth count each time it turns once. So the teeth it passes in a minute are its speed in rev/min × its number of teeth. Call the speed n and the teeth T. Then n₁T₁ = n₂T₂ for any two meshed gears.[^2] A 12-tooth driver at 60 rev/min passes 720 teeth a minute. A 36-tooth gear takes in those 720, so it turns at 720 ÷ 36 = 20 rev/min. The gear with more teeth turns slower. An idler passes on the same 720 teeth a minute, whatever its size, so it changes the direction but not the output speed. In a winch, a small gear on the fast motor drives a gear with many more teeth on the drum, so the drum turns far slower than the motor. Textbooks add that the slower gear turns with a bigger turning effect, which is where the winch's extra pull comes from.\n\n**Meshed gears pass teeth one for one, so neighbours turn opposite ways.[^1] Speed × teeth is the same on both: n₁T₁ = n₂T₂.[^2]**" },
