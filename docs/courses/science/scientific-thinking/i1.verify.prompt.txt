  "text": "Where two gears mesh, their teeth pass one for one. So neighbouring gears turn opposite ways, and both pass the same number of teeth a minute. Look at the point where two gears meet, their [[mesh]]. Each tooth on the driver pushes a tooth on the other gear along with it. So at the mesh the teeth of both gears move the same way, and the two gears turn opposite ways.[^1] Say the driver turns clockwise, with the next gear on its right. The driver's right-hand teeth move down. They push the next gear's left-hand teeth down too, and that turns it anticlockwise.\n\nSo each mesh flips the direction. Along a line of gears with their teeth on the outside, the directions alternate. An odd number of gears in a line turns the last one the driver's way. An even number turns it the other way. A gear's size changes how fast it turns, not which way. One gear set between two others makes them turn the same way. It is called an [[idler gear]]. Now close a line into a loop, so the last gear meshes with the first. With an even number of gears, the last turns opposite to the first, as their mesh needs, and the loop turns. With an odd number, the last gear and the first try to turn the same way. At their mesh the teeth would have to move in opposite directions, and neither can give way. An odd number of gears in a closed loop in one plane will not turn, whatever their sizes and tooth counts.[^2]\n\nThe same count gives the speed. In any minute, both gears pass the same number of teeth through the mesh. A gear passes its whole tooth count each time it turns once. So the teeth it passes in a minute are its speed in rev/min × its number of teeth. Call the speed n and the number of teeth T. Then n₁T₁ = n₂T₂ for any two meshed gears.[^3] A 12-tooth driver at 60 rev/min passes 60 × 12 = 720 teeth a minute. A 36-tooth gear takes in those 720 teeth, so it turns at 720 ÷ 36 = 20 rev/min. Turn the ratio upside down and you get 60 × 36 ÷ 12 = 180 rev/min. That cannot be right, because the bigger gear has three times as many teeth to take in. An idler passes on the same 720 teeth a minute, whatever its size. So it changes the output's direction, not its speed.\n\nIn a winch, a small gear on the fast motor drives a gear with many more teeth on the drum. So the drum turns far slower than the motor.
