Gears fixed to one shaft turn the same way.\n\n**Rule of thumb:** gears fixed to one shaft share one speed. A train that cuts the speed by some ratio raises the torque by up to that ratio, less what friction takes.\n\n**Worked example:** a motor turns a 14-tooth pinion at 960 rev/min. It drives a 42-tooth wheel. A 15-tooth pinion on that shaft drives a 75-tooth wheel. Stage ratios: 42 ÷ 14 = 3 and 75 ÷ 15 = 5. Overall ratio: 3 × 5 = 15. Output speed: 960 ÷ 15 = 64 rev/min. Two meshes, so the output turns the motor's way. A motor giving 10 N m gives at most 10 × 15 = 150 N m at the output.\n\n**Common mistakes**\n- Adding the stage ratios. In the example, 3 + 5 = 8 would give 120 rev/min, nearly twice the real speed.\n- Treating the two gears on a shaft as meshed. Where each sits in the train decides its speed.\n- Expecting a train to raise both speed and torque. It trades one for the other.\n- Leaving a pull in kN.
