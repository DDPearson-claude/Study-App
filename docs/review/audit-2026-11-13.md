# App audit, 13 Nov 2026

Dan (9 Nov): "run an audit of our app and suggest improvements on either the code itself, the
content, the visuals or just something were missing". Audited at commit 1fcce74, which is the build
that is live. Four checks ran side by side (code; look and flow; Maths content; Electrical,
Engineering science and Science content); the findings marked **confirmed** were then re-checked
by hand against the code, the lesson files or a screenshot. Nothing was changed by the audit.

What was not covered is listed at the end. Nothing here is fixed yet.

## What is sound

- 321 of 321 unit tests pass. The live page is byte for byte the repo's dist build, and the six
  live shelf docs match docs/shelves-*.json. All 34 built courses are visible; none is hidden.
- Round 3's blocker and 23 majors (docs/review/audit-round3.md): 21 fixed, 1 superseded, 2 partly
  (K.stage has no `<img>` case; the sandbox removes a navigating frame but head.html has no
  `frame-src 'none'`).
- The code rules hold: no model text reaches innerHTML, kit frames are `sandbox="allow-scripts"`,
  no blocked viewer feature is used, every prompt starts `TASK:`, no doc is over 250 KB.
- No wrong numeric answer was found in any published lesson: 381 Maths figure answers and 190
  typed Electrical answers recomputed by hand, plus about 4,300 stated sums checked by script.
- No guilt wording anywhere. No leadership or management content. No statement about the Royal
  Navy, a submarine's systems or "your boat".
- Insulated systems and Generators are the best teaching in the app; Statistics and Differential
  equations are the best of Maths. Inside a steps lesson the screens look right in both themes.

## 1. Fix first: safety and accuracy in the content

1. **Batteries never mentions electric shock** (confirmed: no "shock" in any of its 8 lessons).
   It works with a 480 V string (lesson 8) and 55-cell banks, and its only word on voltage is that
   12 or 24 V "drives little current through you". It also never says no naked flames.
2. **Batteries lesson 5: "leave the battery to stand for at least 20 minutes after charging"**
   before spark-making work (confirmed, stated three times). It reads as "wait, then hot work is
   fine". It needs proving the air (ventilation running, gas tested). Check the figure: the
   battery-room standard (EN IEC 62485-2) is said to treat gassing as continuing for an hour.
3. **Batteries, figures to check against a maker or the standard:** equalise fixed at 2.50 V a
   cell (stationary flooded makers often quote 2.33 to 2.40); the vent-area rule given as "The
   rule" with no word that the standard sizes differently; the level falls because "Water
   evaporates" (lesson 2) against "the gas that left" (lesson 5); a full cell at 2.07 V in lesson 2
   against 2.1 V in lessons 1 and 3; "little better than tossing a coin" for resistance testers.
4. **Motors lesson 2, star-delta** (confirmed): "star draws 1 ÷ 3 of the DOL current and about a
   quarter of the torque". By the lesson's own reasoning torque is a third. Say a third in theory,
   and that one maker quotes about 30% current and 25% torque in practice. Generators lesson 8
   already uses torque ∝ V².
5. **Motors lesson 5, capacitor discharge** (confirmed): "about 20 kΩ, 5 W, for five seconds". On
   a 250 µF capacitor that is one time constant, about 37% of the voltage left. Electronics
   lesson 3 teaches five time constants.
6. **Motors lesson 2, step 8:** "Measure that live, then isolate again" gives no conditions for
   the live measurement.
7. **Other figures to check:** winding resistances "within about 1%" (Motors 1; common guidance
   is 2 to 5%); seawater specific heat 3,850 J/kg°C (Heat and fluids; usual figure about 3,930 to
   3,990); "Marine rules set a minimum: the alarm must sound by 50 Ω/V" (Insulated systems 5);
   "about one batch in five" (Statistics 3, step 7; a normal model gives about 3 in 10).

## 2. Fix first: answers that print or mark wrongly

1. **Read-through prints "Answer 1" or "Answer 2"** where the real answer belongs (confirmed):
   Linear systems guides i4 and i5 (14 examples), Quadratics guide i2 (7). The guide holds the
   option's index, not its text.
2. **Complex numbers lessons 2 to 8: 50 examples with an empty answer** (confirmed), so no Answer
   line and no review puzzles. Already noted in NEXT.md.
3. **Fractions options lost their fraction bar** (confirmed): "513" for 5 1/3, "338" for 3 3/8,
   "332", "1016", "58" in guides i4, i7 and i8.
4. **25 examples can never be asked in review** because the answer has a non-breaking space and
   the options an ordinary one, so `puzzleSpec` (60-today.js, about line 438) finds no match:
   Batteries 15, Exponentials and logarithms 8, Transformers 1, Mechanics 1. One fix: compare with
   spaces normalised, in the app and in guide.mjs.
5. **Review would refuse a correct answer:** Mechanics lesson 8 gives 69 N and wants 277 N, but
   4 × 69 = 276 (confirmed); Mechanics lesson 6 gives 5.76 kJ and wants 23.1, but 4 × 5.76 = 23.04.
   AC circuits lesson 10 asks "in volt-amps" and accepts 13.9 kVA.
6. **Rounding is never stated.** 98 of the 381 Maths typed puzzles have a decimal answer and only
   12 say how to round ("A pipe is 10 cm across. How far is it round?" wants 31.42, so 31.4 is
   wrong). About 157 Maths puzzles need the lesson's picture or an earlier screen to be answered.
7. Small text slips: Fractions lesson 1 says six puzzles and has seven; Exponentials lesson 8
   prints 3 × 27.73 = 83.18; Statistics lesson 2 says 27.000 then 27.001; Generators lesson 7 has
   two different titles.

## 3. The app: behaviour worth changing

1. **The right answer is usually the first option, and lessons never shuffle** (confirmed: the
   kit has no shuffle; only review cards and problem sets do). First in 49 of 56 questions in
   Functions and their graphs, 35 of 42 in Proportional reasoning, 35 of 52 in Differential
   equations; ten Maths courses lean this way, and whole lessons can be passed by tapping the top
   button. Shuffling in the kit, seeded per step, fixes every lesson with no rebuild.
2. **A whole-number answer accepts anything within ±0.5** (confirmed, 41-cards.js `gradeTyped`):
   2.5 and 3.5 are "right" for 3 A. 220 of the 727 typed problems have a whole-number answer under
   20. Suggested: `min(0.5, max(0.05, 2% of the value))` for answers with no decimals.
3. **A steps lesson does not resume** (confirmed on screen: Trigonometry 4, left part-way,
   reopened at "Step 1 of 15"). The kit posts no place and the host saves none.
4. **Spaced review is off entirely.** Hiding the puzzle box (25 Oct, `U.flags.puzzleBox`) also
   hid every other review card and the "Learn it again" offers. Cards are still made and
   scheduled, unseen. CLAUDE.md lists spaced review as a product rule, so either gate only the
   puzzle cards on the flag or write the rule down as suspended. **Dan's decision.**
5. **On a phone the lesson's question starts below the fold** (confirmed on screen): the course
   line, title, summary, "Quizzing you through it" and "Read it through instead" take about 440
   of 760 px. In the refresher lesson the summary also gives away the opening puzzle.
6. **The course page buries the lesson he is on**: four to five phone screens down, under the
   hook and a 100-word amber "In one breath" panel. A "Continue" button at the top would do.
7. **One "Left open" slot.** Opening any lesson replaces it, and nothing lists courses in
   progress; the half-done course is then four taps and a long scroll away.
8. **Two screens read far more than they need.** The course page downloads every lesson doc on
   each open (1.8 MB for a 12-lesson course, to count `verified` for one sentence). Look-up
   re-reads whole lesson docs on every keystroke (132 reads for "power factor" on a slow line).
9. **Wrong or alarming words:** "That was the last lesson in…" on lesson 1 of a finished course;
   "Lesson read" for a lesson he was quizzed through; an unreadable db shows "No shelves yet" and
   "This topic is not here any more. It may have been deleted on another device."
10. **The lesson's top bar never moves** (a steps lesson is one step to the shell) and is rose,
    not ink; the real progress is the dashes inside the card.
11. **Laptop layouts are a stretched phone:** the read-through picture fills the screen with no
    text; a steps lesson is a 480 px column in a 1090 px card; shelf and folder pages leave the
    right half empty; the docked calculator covers the question at XL.
12. **Calculator:** tapping into a finished sum then pressing a digit wipes it; a typed `sin 2π`
    gives sin(2) × π.
13. **Smaller:** the look-up placeholder is cut mid-word ("power f"); a stray "·" starts a line
    under finished lessons; the read-through says "Drag the end of arm a" where nothing drags;
    page text shows under the floating tab bar; two switches are 52 × 32 and one link is 35 px
    high; picture wells stay bright white in dark mode; study minutes stop counting while he types
    answers inside a lesson (suspected); no time limit on a Claude call (suspected); "Start with
    new numbers" drops keyboard focus.

## 4. Decisions on the limits and the writing rules

1. **Reactor and naval lines, all from public sources, for Dan to keep or cut:**
   - Differential equations 2: "A reactor's power grows this way. Its period is…" (three places).
   - Matrices 7: "A reactor core code builds one large grid for the core".
   - Differential equations 4 and Linear algebra 7: a submarine's dive model as an example.
   - Motors 1: "Some warships use one for their electric propulsion." Motors 3: "NATO warship
     rules cap voltage distortion at 5%." Motors 6: the US Navy's 1907 boat cranes.
   - AC circuits 10: "A US Navy training problem…", sourced from a public US Naval Nuclear Power
     Training Command question sheet.
2. **Narrating sources** (against the 20 Oct rule): about 30 places in Maths ("In one study, 76%
   of students…", "A college's marine notes say…", "(not from this lesson's sources)", "Scottish
   examiners reported…") and AC circuits ("A marine college's notes (a secondary source)…", "From
   a City & Guilds 2366 sample paper"). Design and verification says "Question 10 of the
   assignment asks for it".
3. **Words that describe the build, on screen:** "matching Brilliant's maths paths", "Each course
   is built in full before you open it" (three times on the home), "22 courses built", "Idea 1 is
   ready", "Research found 175 sources". Four names for two things: idea, lesson, topic, course.
   Settings shows "Z-Image Turbo", "Hugging Face connector" and a build id.
4. **Long sentences and walls of text** in the older courses: AC circuits (83 sentences over 30
   words), Calculus, Trigonometry, Exponentials, Quadratics; Subsea has 13 screens over 120 words.
   They predate the plain-edit step.
5. **Colour:** the lesson bar is rose; course covers and shelf bands use green, salmon, magenta
   and teal as decoration; "In one breath" is a large amber surface.
6. **Imperial sizes in Fractions** (3/8 inch drill bits): keep or change.

## 5. Teaching shape

1. **No Maths lesson opens with a puzzle**: all 154 open on a screen that states the idea. AC
   circuits, Insulated systems and most of Mechanics do the same. The product rule is predict,
   then play.
2. **No lesson has typed answers, a recap screen or a say-it-back.** So "In your own words" in
   the Book can never fill for a ready-made course.
3. **The Foundations folder is pitched below him** ("Colour 1/4 of the bar", "Solve 3x = 24").
   A test-out per course would let him skip what he holds.
4. **Motors reads as a reference card**: 20 puzzles in 8 lessons against 55 to 71 elsewhere.
5. **Order:** Calculus 9 and 10 say logarithms and trigonometry "come later" but both sit
   earlier; Differential equations uses calculus the Calculus course does not teach; Generators,
   Batteries and Transformers lean on courses from later shelves.
6. **Overlaps:** gears, moments and collisions in both Scientific thinking and Mechanics (same
   figures); changing the subject taught twice; starting current "6 to 8 times" in four courses.

## 6. What is missing

**Content, for the whole path** (none of it Instrumentation and control, leadership, reactors or
the Navy course):

1. DC circuits and network theorems (Kirchhoff, Thévenin, superposition, internal resistance).
   Level 3 principles units examine it and nothing built teaches it.
2. Capacitors and inductors on DC (RC and RL time constants). Electronics and Subsea use them
   untaught.
3. 440 V distribution and protection on a generator-fed system (fault level, discrimination,
   breaking capacity, shore supply). CLAUDE.md names it first; only fragments exist.
4. DC switching and protection (why DC arcs persist, DC ratings). Sits beside Batteries.
5. Rounding, significant figures and estimation. Review depends on it and nothing teaches it.
6. A second calculus course (sin, cos, eˣ, ln x, product and chain rules, numerical integration).
7. Hydraulics and pneumatics, and reading drawings (schematic symbols, single-line and piping
   diagrams). Dan asked about hydraulic diagrams on 8 Nov.
8. Already queued and still right: Waves and radiation (for January), Digital circuits, Logic,
   Probability for reliability.

**In the app:**

1. A list of courses in progress, on the home and Today.
2. A view of the whole programme: how many courses are finished and what comes next. Shelf and
   folder cards show no progress at all.
3. A "Rules" list across courses. Every lesson ends in a good "Keep this" card, reachable only
   through Book, dossier, chapter.
4. Search inside lessons (look-up reads titles and one-liners only).
5. Not-built lessons marked on the course path.
