# How other self-study products and institutions structure and teach pre-built courses (2025–2026)

Scope: Khan Academy, Math Academy, Duolingo (and Duolingo Math), the UK Open University, MOOCs (Coursera/edX), coding platforms (Codecademy, Exercism, Execute Program, freeCodeCamp), explorable explanations (Victor, Case, Patel, 3Blue1Brown, Seeing Theory), Art of Problem Solving and the Moore method, the Oxbridge tutorial, and AI tutors (Khanmigo, Synthesis Tutor, Google LearnLM/Guided Learning, Brilliant's AI). Lens: one adult learning alone, mostly on a phone. Where a source is older than 2024 or is about children/classrooms, that is flagged.

Tool-access record (so the writer knows what was and was not reached): the container's WebFetch tool failed with DNS errors (ENOTFOUND) on every domain tried; direct curl through the egress proxy was refused (CONNECT 403) for mathacademy.com, blog.ncase.me, redblobgames.com and research.duolingo.com; the Parallel Search MCP reached those sites but returned "Too Many Requests" on roughly half of all calls, so several planned fetches (Duolingo's own 2022 path announcement, Duolingo's Birdbrain post in full, the OpenLearn overview PDF, the MOOC Pivot post-print) never came back. brilliant.org and classcentral.com were not attempted directly per the brief; Brilliant's blog was read via search excerpts only. Where a figure comes from a third-party site rather than the product's own page, that is stated.

## Key question 1: What is the course and lesson skeleton for each (parts, in order, typical length)?

### Takeaway
Every serious product converges on the same four-level shape — course → unit/section → lesson/topic → item — but they differ sharply on what a "lesson" is: Khan's is a bundle of videos/articles plus one exercise; Math Academy's is 3–4 "knowledge points" (worked example → 2–5 problems) with no video; Duolingo's is a ~15-exercise session with a guidebook sitting beside the unit; the OU's is a multi-hour study unit of text interrupted by numbered activities; Coursera's is a ~30-minute cluster of short videos ending in a quiz.

### Cited Findings

**Khan Academy (current, 2026 pages)**
- A Khan course page lists units; each unit page lists lessons, a quiz, and a unit test; each lesson is split into a "Learn" block of videos/articles ("Opens a modal") and a "Practice" block of one exercise with the prompt "Get 3 of 4 questions to level up!"; the unit ends with "Unit test — Level up on all the skills in this unit and collect up to 400 Mastery points!"; the course ends with a "Course challenge" — [Khan for Families course page](https://www.khanacademy.org/khan-for-parents/k4p-us/x47d2dcef03b6d5f6:welcome-to-khan-academy)
- A course challenge is "30 questions • 30 - 45 minutes" and lets learners "earn mastery points for what you already know" — [Pre-algebra course challenge](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/pre-algebra/test/xb4832e56:course-challenge)
- Each skill is worth 100 Mastery Points (Familiar = 50, Proficient = 80, Mastered = 100); a unit page shows total points (e.g. "1900 points on this unit— 100 for each skill"); before any activity a card says how many questions there will be and, for quizzes/unit tests/course challenges, roughly how long other students took; after it, a card lists skills levelled up/down and may recommend lessons — [Khan Help Center: What are Course and Unit Mastery (updated May 2026)](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/fr/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery)

**Math Academy (current)**
- "At the most fundamental level, a lesson is a sequence of slides with instructional text, interspersed with active problem-solving. Each lesson starts out with an introduction, and then moves to a worked example, followed by 2-5 practice questions on the same type of problem as the worked example. The worked example and active problem-solving we collectively refer to as a 'knowledge point' or KP." Lessons contain "about 3-4 'knowledge points' of increasing difficulty" — [The Math Academy Way (PDF), FAQ: The Practice Experience / Curriculum](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)
- Task types are "lesson, review, multistep, or quiz", each earning XP; "Each lesson begins with a tutorial that introduces a new concept along with a method for solving the particular category of problem"; "Next come a series of up to 5 practice problems. If the student gets two practice problems correct in a row, then they move on to the next example, otherwise, they're given additional problems"; "Every question has an explanation" — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works)
- Onboarding: "an adaptive diagnostic assessment, which will take approximately 30 to 45 minutes, but it doesn't have to be completed all at once"; it "measures both mastery and automaticity" and identifies "missing foundational knowledge from lower courses"; the student receives a diagnostic report with placement, estimated completion dates at various daily XP goals, and gaps to remediate — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works)
- "a quiz is made available to students every 150 XP and covers a variety of recent topics… Quizzes are timed and students may not refer back to examples… Any topics missed during the quizzes are then immediately assigned as review topics. After adequate review, the quiz is available for an optional re-take" — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works)
- Unit of work: "One XP represents one minute of fully-focused, fully-productive work for an average serious (but imperfect) student"; baseline pace 40 XP/weekday; "courses contain about 3000 XP assuming that a student knows all the necessary prerequisites… prealgebra is ~2000 XP" — [The Math Academy Way (PDF), Pace](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)
- The published book's table of contents shows the whole pedagogy is written down: chapters on Bloom's two-sigma problem, the knowledge graph, diagnostics, active learning, mastery learning, spaced repetition, interleaving, targeted remediation, gamification, and technical deep dives on "Hierarchical Spaced Repetition (HSRS) • Fractional Implicit Repetition (FIRe)" and diagnostic exams — [Justin Skycak: The Math Academy Way (TOC)](https://www.justinmath.com/the-math-academy-way/)
- A learner-written curriculum review says the program "recommends 40-50 daily XP 4 to 5 times a week", i.e. roughly 4–5 hours a week, and describes it as "concise explanations and lots exercises" — [recess.gg: Math Curriculum Review: MathAcademy](https://think.recess.gg/math-curriculum-review-mathacademy)

**Duolingo (language, current path; Duolingo Math)**
- Unofficial but detailed data site: "Each Unit has Levels and Chests, and each Level has Lessons"; "Each Lesson has from 2 to 18 exercises (usually 15)"; course pages list sections tagged with CEFR levels plus an optional "Personalized Practice" section and a "Daily Refresh" unit — [DuolingoData German-from-English course page](https://www.duolingodata.com/dat/defen137.html); September–November 2025 saw new fourth sections (60 units, CEFR A2) for Italian, Portuguese, German and then CJK languages — [DuolingoData news](https://duolingodata.com/news.html)
- Duolingo's own redesign post: the Guidebook now sits "at the top right corner of each unit" rather than before a lesson; "For Super subscribers, Practice Hub is now a tab (barbell icon) in the bottom menu" — [Duolingo blog: new home screen design](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)
- After completing a node learners choose a review (5 XP) or a "Legendary" lesson (40 XP) with "more challenging exercises and no hints"; Side Quests are timed lessons placed on the path — [Duolingo blog: ways to practice](https://blog.duolingo.com/ways-to-practice-in-duolingo)
- Older structure (2020, pre-path, for comparison): "five sections, each of which concludes with a 'checkpoint'… 114 units in the beginning level of the Spanish course… Each unit includes 4-5 lessons at 5 difficulty levels, where higher difficulty is achieved through exercises requiring progressively more recall and production"; learners averaged 141 hours (median 112) to finish the beginner course — [Duolingo efficacy whitepaper 2020 (PDF)](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/duolingo-efficacy-whitepaper.pdf)
- I found no official Duolingo statement of minutes per lesson; third-party guides give 5–10 or 5–20 minutes — [calendar-canada FAQ](https://www.calendar-canada.ca/frequently-asked-questions/how-long-is-the-average-duolingo-lesson)
- Duolingo Math: third-party guides describe a beginner and an intermediate course, short lessons (one says five-minute micro-lessons, another under two minutes), manipulative-style exercises such as dragging a clock hand or moving blocks for fractions, and points/streaks/leaderboards — [duolingoguides: Duolingo Math](https://duolingoguides.com/duolingo-math/); [androidguias: the new Duolingo Math app](https://en.androidguias.com/the-new-duolingo-math-app/) (both unofficial)

**Open University (UK)**
- A review of Rowntree's OU-rooted handbook describes its structure chapters as "how to organize a lesson, build in activities, provide variety, and make the structure clear to the learner", with chapter 5 illustrating features of print self-instruction "using examples from Open University courses" — [IJEDE review of Rowntree, Teaching Through Self-instruction (1986)](https://www.ijede.ca/index.php/jde/article/download/321/215?inline=1)
- OpenLearn free courses start at an Introduction, are navigated section by section with Next/Previous, show a progress bar that reaches 100% when all pages are viewed and quizzes done, and award a Statement of Participation — [OpenLearn FAQ: how to start a course](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/get-started/frequently-asked-questions/how-start-course)
- The OU's current learning-design framework classifies every activity in a module into five observable types — assimilative ("read, watch, listen, think about, observe, review"), communicative, finding & handling information, productive, and practice — "Assessment can involve any of the activity types, or a blend of several"; the tool is used "to review the balance of activity types" in a module — [OU Learning Design: Activity Types Framework, Oct 2021 (PDF)](https://www.open.ac.uk/blogs/learning-design/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/OU-LD-Activity-Types-Framework-October-2021-FINAL.pdf); the earlier seven-type OULDI profile (assimilative, finding and handling information, communication, productive, experiential, interactive/adaptive, assessment) allocated study hours per type and "the results often show a skew towards Assimilative activities" — [UCL Digital Education: Golden OULDI (2013)](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/digital-education/2013/03/05/golden-ouldi)
- A distance-education design handbook built on OU practice states that print and web materials are essentially passive, so designers must deliberately build in activity, and sketches a unit pattern ending in a self-test keyed to the unit's objectives — [OER Africa ODL instructional design handbook, section 4 (PDF)](https://www.oerafrica.org/sites/default/files/course_design/odlinstdesignHB_section4.pdf)

**MOOCs (Coursera/edX)**
- Coursera on-demand guidance (reproduced by Technion's MOOC unit): "A lesson is a cohesive unit of content that should take a learner around 30 minutes to complete"; "Most modules in existing on demand courses include 2-3 lessons"; "Each lesson includes several videos and at least one assessment (usually a quiz)"; "Each module should include a comprehensive review quiz"; module workload 1–2 hours — [Technion: MOOC structure](https://mooctech.net.technion.ac.il/resources/mooc-structure/)
- A university course-production deck built on Coursera requirements: video "Goal: 6 to 9 minutes"; "Minimum one In-Video Question or about one for every 5 to 7 minutes of video"; per video "1 In-Video Question; 2 Practice Quiz questions; + 0 or 1 Graded Quiz question"; every module ends with a summative assessment — [University of Minnesota MSLT deck (PDF)](https://pubs.lib.umn.edu/index.php/mslt/article/download/735/739/976)
- NUS course guide: "2 hours of pre-recorded video per week broken up into 8-15 minute chunks with embedded quiz questions in each chunk"; in-video quizzes "complement the video lecture rather than test students" — [NUS Coursera guide](https://nus.atlassian.net/wiki/x/3F8rAg)

**Coding platforms**
- Codecademy's author standards: a lesson "is composed of a series of ordered exercises"; "An exercise itself contains a narrative and at least one LE component (code editor, terminal, output terminal, image, or video)"; the lesson "provides introduction to new material through the narratives and guided practice through the checkpoints"; "Instructions should be used when there is an open-ended or non-testable interaction"; "When possible, the narrative length should be shorter than 250 words" — [Codecademy Lesson Standards](https://curriculum-documentation.codecademy.com/lessons/lesson-standards/)
- Execute Program: "Execute Program combines 4,821 explanatory paragraphs with 3,751 interactive live code examples, so you both read and write code"; "When you complete an EP lesson, you automatically receive a short review the next day"; reviews "require you to answer questions correctly before moving forward" — [Execute Program: Why EP?](https://www.executeprogram.com/why-ep); course sizes e.g. "35 lessons 212 code examples… 47 lessons 261 code examples" — [Execute Program home](https://www.executeprogram.com/)
- Exercism: tracks with Learning Exercises run in "Learning Mode" where "complete Learning Exercises to unlock further Learning and Practice Exercises"; "Practice Mode" unlocks everything; "in either Practice or Learning Mode, one can ask for mentoring on a particular exercise" — [Exercism docs: Unlocking Exercises](https://exercism.org/docs/building/product/unlocking-exercises); 83 languages — [Exercism home](https://exercism.org/)
- freeCodeCamp's Certified Full Stack Developer path (announced late 2024, rolling out 2025) is built from Workshops ("Interactive step-by-step coding courses", 64 planned), Lectures ("Short videos covering computer science concepts, with multiple choice questions", 513 planned), Labs (blank editor plus test suite, 83), Review pages (62), Quizzes (66), a capstone reviewed by community teachers and a 90-question proctored exam — [freeCodeCamp: Major Curriculum Updates (Japanese mirror of the English announcement)](https://www.freecodecamp.org/japanese/news/christmas-2025-freecodecamp-curriculum-updates/); the year-end 2025 review calls it the tenth edition, bundling seven certifications and "well over a hundred" hands-on builds — [freeCodeCamp forum: Top Contributors of 2025](https://forum.freecodecamp.org/t/freecodecamps-top-contributors-of-2025/768516)

**Explorable explanations (single-page lessons)**
- Victor's explorable has three layers: "The hurried reader can skim it. The casual reader can read it as-is. The curious reader can adjust the author's scenarios. The engaged reader can explore scenarios of their own devising" — [Bret Victor: Explorable Explanations (2011)](http://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/)
- Patel: "I generally design my pages to be mostly static, with sections of the page being interactive. Each section is usually its own separate interactive section that doesn't interact with the rest of the page" ("islands of interactivity") — [Red Blob Games: Little Design Things](https://www.redblobgames.com/making-of/little-things/)
- Seeing Theory (Brown, built by undergraduate Daniel Kunin in D3) runs chapter by chapter from coin and dice examples up to Bayesian inference and regression, each visual with notes on method and data — [TNW 2017](https://thenextweb.com/news/interactive-webiste-statistics-boring)

**Problem-first textbooks, Moore method, tutorials**
- AoPS Introduction to Algebra: "each section starts with problems, giving the student a chance to solve them without help before proceeding. The algebra techniques are then taught through the solutions" — [AoPS store: Introduction to Algebra](https://artofproblemsolving.com/store/book/intro-algebra)
- Moore method: "Instead of using a textbook, the students are given a list of definitions and, based on these, theorems which they are to prove and present in class"; "The Moore method typically limits the amount of material that a class is able to cover" — [Wikipedia: Moore method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_method)
- Oxbridge: students "taught by college fellows… in groups of one to three on a weekly basis"; "The student is required to undertake preparatory work for each tutorial: for example, reading, essays or working through problems"; Cambridge: "one or two hour-long supervisions each week" — [Wikipedia: Tutorial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutorial_system)

### Inferences
- The smallest reusable "atom" across the best products is Math Academy's knowledge point (one worked example + 2–5 near-transfer problems) and Codecademy's exercise (≤250-word narrative + one hands-on checkpoint). Both are 2–5 minutes, which suits a phone session.
- Khan's lesson/unit/course trio of checks (exercise → quiz → unit test → course challenge) and Math Academy's "quiz every 150 XP" are the same idea at different granularities: a mixed, closed-book check every ~2–3 hours of work.
- The OU's activity-type balance sheet is a design-time tool rather than a learner-facing structure, but it is the one institution that audits how much of a unit is passive reading.

### Gaps
- No official Duolingo page giving exercises-per-lesson or minutes-per-lesson was retrievable; the "usually 15 exercises" figure is from a fan-maintained data site.
- Duolingo's own 2022 announcement of the path (units, levels, unit review) could not be fetched (rate limit); descriptions of "unit review" nodes rest on third-party guides.
- Math Academy's pages describe lessons as text slides and problems; I did not retrieve an explicit statement that the product contains no video, so the "no videos" claim in the brief is consistent with but not directly confirmed by the retrieved excerpts.
- The OU's internal author style guides (how an SAQ is written and placed) were not found online; only Rowntree's handbook reviews and the learning-design framework were reachable.

## Key question 2: How does each decide what the learner does first in a lesson — read, watch, try, predict, or play?

### Takeaway
The products split into "show first" (Khan: watch a video, then one exercise; Coursera: video with an embedded question; Math Academy: read a worked example, then try; Execute Program: read a paragraph, then run code) and "try first" (AoPS: attempt problems before the section; Moore: prove the theorem before any exposition; Case's "Place Your Bets"/"Puzzle It Out" patterns: predict or solve before the reveal). Duolingo is "do first" with the guidebook optional and off to the side; explorables aim to be readable as plain text with interaction as an optional deeper layer.

### Cited Findings
- Khan lesson pages put the "Learn" block (videos/articles) above the "Practice" exercise, and the exercise is labelled "Up next for you" once the videos are listed — [Khan for Families course page](https://www.khanacademy.org/khan-for-parents/k4p-us/x47d2dcef03b6d5f6:welcome-to-khan-academy)
- Math Academy: "every knowledge point starts with a fully worked out example for students to follow along with" (the worked-example effect), and "we take advantage of… dual-coding theory by including helpful visualizations and diagrams when possible"; "We intersperse active problem solving with instruction so that students receive only the necessary doses of information right before they use it" — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works); the book's position on skipping worked examples: solving "without referring to worked examples… is far less efficient (even if you have fun with it)" and "whenever a student refers back to the worked example, they should only peek at the part they're stuck on" — [The Math Academy Way (PDF)](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)
- Math Academy's stated ratio: "for every 3 worked examples that a student reads, they will be actively doing about… 21 practice problems, putting the net ratio at about 7 active practice problems per passive worked example" — [The Math Academy Way (PDF), Ch. 10 Active Learning](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)
- AoPS tells students to attempt a section's opening problems before reading it; unsolved problems are fine because "those problems will all be fully solved as you read the section", and to read every solution even when right since "the book's solution is even simpler or easier to understand than your own" — [AoPS handbook: Working Through Class Material](https://artofproblemsolving.com/school/handbook/current/work); the premise: "students learn best when they are challenged with hard problems that at first they may not know how to do" — [AoPS: About the Online School](https://artofproblemsolving.com/school/handbook/prospective/about)
- The research name for try-first is productive failure: learners "attempt to solve challenging problems ahead of receiving explicit instruction", which is proposed to "activate and differentiate learners' prior knowledge" — [DIY productive failure (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6414542/); but a study of 228 fifth-graders "found no empirical support for either of our hypotheses", suggesting age as a boundary condition (children, classroom) — [Probing boundary conditions of Productive Failure (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6435728/)
- Moore: "That student is taught the best who is told the least"; students are told not to read the subject and to "find proofs of their own and also to construct examples to show that the hypotheses of the theorems could not be weakened" — [Wikipedia: Moore method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_method)
- Duolingo: lessons are exercise sequences; the guidebook is a side panel per unit — [Duolingo blog: new home screen design](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/); "When you miss a challenge, Duolingo quickly shows you how to improve" (archived 2021 homepage) — [Wayback: duolingo.com 2021](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20210404165602/https://en.duolingo.com)
- Execute Program: "You get clear, detailed explanations, then immediately test your knowledge by writing and running real code"; "Lessons introduce concepts through real code examples, not just walls of text" — [Execute Program home](https://www.executeprogram.com/)
- Codecademy: beginners "start small in an environment that has constant feedback and gradually progress" — [Slant: Learn to Program vs Codecademy](https://www.slant.co/versus/3393/3026/~learn-to-program_vs_codecademy)
- Victor: the explorable "works as a static explanation — it can be read like normal text. The reader is not forced to interact in order to learn. The reader interacts if they wants to go deeper… There are no UI elements screaming for attention. The reader is not transported off to a separate 'interactive' context"; "Most interactive widgets dump the user in a sandbox and say 'figure it out for yourself'. Those are not explanations… the author holds up their end of the conversation" — [Bret Victor: Explorable Explanations](http://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/)
- Case's patterns name the first move explicitly: "Puzzle It Out" (for "topics that lend themselves to simulation, e.g. math, programming, science"), "Place Your Bets!" (for "topics with a clear right or wrong answers", citing the NYT "You Draw It" series where the reader sketches the line before seeing the data), "Role Play" (philosophy, politics, ethics) and "Sandbox Mode" — [Nicky Case: 4 More Design Patterns](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/)
- Case's earlier workshop notes: "Don't try to explain everything with something interactive. Use interactivity only when interactivity works best… Text: Best at describing very abstract concepts. Graphs: Best at showing broad relationships at a glance. Animations: Best at showing temporal relationships. Interactives: Best at showing processes, systems, models" — [Nicky Case: Explorable Explanations (2014)](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations/)
- 3Blue1Brown's method as described by a sponsor: "starting with a natural question or problem that leads to other questions. At some point, you discover the core issue, and definitions emerge from the minimal structure a problem needs" — [Jane Street: We're sponsoring SoME3](https://blog.janestreet.com/were-sponsoring-some3/); Sanderson on leading with imagery first and explaining meaning afterwards so viewers feel ownership of the idea — [Stanford Daily interview, Jan 2020](https://www.stanforddaily.com/2020/01/24/3blue1brown-creator-grant-sanderson-15-talks-engaging-with-math-using-stories-and-visuals)
- Coursera's guidance embeds a question inside each video rather than before it — [University of Minnesota MSLT deck (PDF)](https://pubs.lib.umn.edu/index.php/mslt/article/download/735/739/976)

### Inferences
- The evidence-backed products (Math Academy, Execute Program) choose "small dose of instruction immediately before the matching problem" rather than pure try-first; try-first is favoured by the discovery traditions (AoPS, Moore, 3Blue1Brown) whose own claims rest on anecdote and whose research base (productive failure) is mixed.
- Case's "Place Your Bets" is the cheapest way to add a predict step to any interactive without changing the lesson: ask for a guess, then reveal.
- Victor's layering (skim / read / adjust / explore) is the design that best fits "nothing ever gets in the way" on a phone: a lesson must remain readable with interaction as an optional second pass.

### Gaps
- Grant Sanderson's own list of explainer principles (the SoME1 advice video) could not be read; the page exists but returned no text — [3Blue1Brown: SoME1 announcement](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/some1). Phrases such as "concrete before abstract" could not be verified as his wording.
- No source states how Khanmigo-enabled lessons change the first step (it is a side panel on existing content).

## Key question 3: How does each handle practice, mastery checks and spaced review, and what evidence of effectiveness has it published?

### Takeaway
Khan ties mastery to performance on mixed-skill assessments that can also demote a skill; Math Academy makes spaced repetition the core scheduler (reviews are tasks, repetitions "flow" to prerequisite topics via FIRe) and gates quizzes closed-book; Duolingo runs a trained forgetting model (half-life regression, later Birdbrain) and reviews mistakes at the end of each lesson; Execute Program reviews every lesson the next day then at widening intervals; MOOCs mostly stop at quizzes. Published efficacy is strongest for Khan (MAP Growth association), Duolingo (peer-reviewed proficiency studies) and the DARPA digital tutor that Synthesis cites; Math Academy publishes pedagogy, not outcome studies.

### Cited Findings

**Khan Academy**
- Levels: "Attempted — fewer than 70% correct on an exercise"; "Familiar — 70%-99%"; "Proficient — 100% of the questions correct"; "Mastered — Students were already at the Proficient and correctly answered questions on this skill on a mixed-skill assessment"; levels fall when questions are missed on mixed assessments ("students were at Mastered and missed questions on this skill" → Proficient) — [Khan Help Center: How do Mastery levels work](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work)
- "Mastery is not easily earned, and that's intentional" — [Khan Help Center: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/fr/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery)
- Mastery Challenges: "The Khan Academy mastery system is designed to measure long-term learning… the Mastery challenge is something that makes sure you have learned and retained your math skills not just in the short term, but in the long term… The main reason for the wait time in the Mastery System is to test knowledge retention over longer periods", with a link to spaced repetition (community support reply, undated) — [Khan support community](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/es/community/posts/360043463471/comments/360008042871)
- Efficacy: "Using Khan Academy for 30+ minutes per week, or 18+ hours over the duration of the school year, is associated with ~20% greater-than-expected learning gains, as measured by performance on the nationally normed MAP Growth Assessment" (school-age, classroom, correlational) — [Khan Academy Efficacy Results, Nov 2024](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024)
- Khanmigo RCT (middle schools, Tennessee, two years): assignment "raises math achievement by 1.3 national percentile ranks per term, or about 0.06 to 0.08 standard deviations over a school year… These gains resemble those from Khan Academy practice without AI assistance" — [NBER w35620, Oreopoulos & Low 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)

**Math Academy**
- Spaced repetition model: "repNum = how many successful rounds of spaced repetition the student has accumulated on a particular topic… a 'repetition' is a successful review at the appropriate time… interval = the ideal number of days to be spaced between repetition repNum and repetition repNum+1" — [The Math Academy Way (PDF), Ch. 18](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)
- Task selection: the algorithm picks what "will lead to the greatest overall gain in spaced repetitions across your entire knowledge profile (while simultaneously climbing the knowledge graph evenly)"; repetitions flow implicitly to prerequisites ("Fractional Implicit Repetition (FIRe)… Partial Encompassings… Non-Ancestor Encompassings and Mastery Floors") — [The Math Academy Way (PDF)](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf); [TOC](https://www.justinmath.com/the-math-academy-way/)
- "Math Academy's curriculum is intentionally structured so that earlier topics are applied and reinforced in higher level topics" (layering / retroactive facilitation); "non-interference" means new concepts are taught alongside dissimilar material so related ideas don't interfere with recall; quizzes every 150 XP are timed and closed-book — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works)
- Supplemental diagnostics: when the graph changes, "we assign tiny diagnostics called supplemental diagnostics" to refresh the knowledge profile — [The Math Academy Way (PDF), Glossary](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)
- Effectiveness evidence: the book argues from the literature (desirable difficulties, spacing, interleaving) and a claim of "Accelerating Student Learning by 4x" (chapter title); I found no independent outcome study — [TOC](https://www.justinmath.com/the-math-academy-way/)
- Adult self-learner reports completing Multivariable Calculus on the platform — [Frank Hecker: Math Academy posts](https://frankhecker.com/tags/math-academy/)

**Duolingo**
- Half-life regression (HLR): "a trainable spaced repetition algorithm, marrying psycholinguistically-inspired models of memory with modern machine learning" — [Settles & Meeder, ACL 2016 (PDF)](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf); open dataset of 13 million user-word pairs — [Duolingo Research](http://research.duolingo.com/); code — [GitHub duolingo/halflife-regression](https://github.com/duolingo/halflife-regression); still studied in 2026 — [Ilídio et al., PMLR 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v339/ilidio26a.html)
- Birdbrain "is continuously studying both how much learners know and the difficulty level of the different kinds of language material" and "makes an educated guess about whether a learner will get a given exercise right" — [Duolingo blog: Introducing Birdbrain](https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain); the 2020 AI post reports A/B tests in which lessons assembled at the right difficulty "consistently improved learning outcomes" — [Duolingo blog: Duolingo AI model (2020)](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-ai-model/)
- "At the end of a lesson, you'll review any mistakes you made. We wait until the end of the lesson to take advantage of a little spaced repetition"; "You'll still practice each word 20 times, but spacing it out over several days makes it more likely they'll stick"; "aim to practice things right before you're about to forget them" — [Dear Duolingo: spaced repetition](https://blog.duolingo.com/spaced-repetition-for-learning)
- Practice Hub modes: Target Practice, Perfect Pronunciation ("10 speaking exercises"), Listen-Up (10 listening exercises), Unit Rewind; a "Your collections" library of saved items — [Duolingo blog: ways to practice](https://blog.duolingo.com/ways-to-practice-in-duolingo)
- Efficacy page (2024–2026 entries): Sudina & Plonsky 2023 — "Learners who did more lessons on Duolingo had better learning outcomes, and lessons mattered more than time spent learning"; Jiang et al. 2024 (CALICO) — beginner English course learners "scored above the beginner level on language tests"; 2025 peer-reviewed — after one semester "Duolingo learners had higher proficiency test scores than classroom learners"; 2026 peer-reviewed French study — "communication skills were comparable to classroom learners"; 2026 Duolingo research report on Duolingo Math — "rising 8th graders improved their scores by 14%" after 4 weeks in summer (children; in-house report) — [Duolingo Efficacy Studies](http://duolingo.com/efficacy/studies)
- 2020 whitepaper (self-selected completers, in-house): beginner-level completion in ~141 hours with proficiency equivalent to four university semesters — [Duolingo efficacy whitepaper (PDF)](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/duolingo-efficacy-whitepaper.pdf)

**Open University**
- Self-assessment tests in ODL "usually cover a specific section of a course – say, one unit or one chapter", aim to expose misunderstandings and suggest remedial study, and should take little time and use diagnostic single-answer questions (Commonwealth of Learning guidance, not OU-authored) — [COL: Self assessment (PDF)](https://tell.colvee.org/pluginfile.php/68/mod_book/chapter/31/Self-assessment.pdf)
- A small survey of OU students found in-text and end-of-text questions were seen as "useful supplementary aids to learning" (1980) — [Programmed Learning & Educational Technology abstract](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/0033039800170106)
- OU work on computer-marked free-text questions with tailored feedback reported students "enjoy them and find the feedback helpful" (2007, informal) — [REAP case study (PDF)](https://www.reap.ac.uk/reap/reap07/Portals/2/CSL/feast%20of%20case%20studies/Extending_the_pedagogic_role_of_online_interactive_assessment.pdf)
- OU-wide effort to link outcomes, assessment and teaching explicitly after the Dearing Report — [Coats et al. 2005, ORO](https://oro.open.ac.uk/5557)

**MOOCs**
- Video engagement (6.9 million sessions, four edX courses, 2014): "median engagement time is at most 6 minutes, regardless of total video length"; "students often make it less than halfway through videos longer than 9 minutes"; recommendation "segment videos into short chunks, ideally less than 6 minutes"; talking-head-plus-slides videos engaged "nearly twice as long" at 6–12 minutes and "nearly 3x" beyond 12; Khan-style tablet drawing beat slides/screencasts; informal desk recordings beat a TV studio; engagement rose "up to 2x" with speaking rate (range 48–254 wpm, mean 156); "students only watch, on average, 2 to 3 minutes of each tutorial video, regardless of the video's length" and re-watch tutorials more than lectures — [Guo, Kim & Rubin 2014 (PDF)](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf)
- In-video quizzes (Coursera ML course): "74% of viewers who start watching a video will attempt its corresponding in-video quiz"; learners seek backward from quizzes to review; the paper measures behaviour, not learning — [Kovacs 2016, Stanford HCI](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/paper.php?id=314); a quiz-driven viewer improved scores on the in-video questions but showed "no significant improvement in scores on the original unit exam" — [QuizCram (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01864)
- Completion: "3.13 percent of all MOOC participants completed their courses in 2017-18, down from about 4 percent the two previous years and nearly 6 percent in 2014-15"; verified-track completion 46% in 2017-18 — [Inside Higher Ed on Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/7744); the Science piece: "the bane of MOOCs—low completion rates—has not improved over 6 years" — [Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente, The MOOC Pivot (MIT DSpace)](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/136215/post_print-MOOC_Pivot.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2)

**Coding platforms**
- Execute Program: SRS "only exists to speed users through our lessons, which contain thousands of carefully-designed, interactive code examples along with explanations"; "The 'review everything every day' method doesn't work at all for learning programming tools" — [Execute Program: Spaced Repetition](https://www.executeprogram.com/spaced-repetition); lessons "assume readers have already retained its prerequisites" and prerequisite reviews gate the next lesson — [Andy Matuschak notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zH26uarYCm9y6tqzuxaGUzt)
- Exercism's rationale for human review: "When learning a new language, the hardest part is not being aware of the gaps in your knowledge. Our mentors can look at your code and immediately see the ideas you're not familiar with" — [Exercism home](https://exercism.org/)
- Codecademy as a course supplement "fulfilled 3 of the 6 course skill outcomes" (university Python course) — [ISEDJ 2019](https://isedj.org/2019-17/n3/ISEDJv17n3p20.html)
- freeCodeCamp Labs give "a blank editor and a test suite"; lectures are followed by multiple-choice checks; review pages recap each module — [freeCodeCamp curriculum update](https://www.freecodecamp.org/japanese/news/christmas-2025-freecodecamp-curriculum-updates/)

**AI tutors**
- LearnLM: "training with pedagogical instruction following produces a LearnLM model… that experts substantially prefer across a diverse set of learning scenarios, with average preference strengths of +31% over GPT-4o, +11% over Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and +13% over the Gemini 1.5 Pro model on which LearnLM was based" (rater preference, not learning outcomes) — [LearnLM: Improving Gemini for Learning (arXiv, v3 Aug 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16429)
- Google-reported outcomes: an RCT in Sierra Leone where ≥12 hours over eight weeks moved students "from the 50th to the 64th percentile in mathematics", and LearnLM tutoring sessions made students "5.5 percentage points more likely to solve novel problems" (company page; no independent replication found) — [Google Cloud: LearnLM](https://cloud.google.com/solutions/learnlm)
- Khanmigo usage in the RCT: "96 percent of students tried Khanmigo at least once, but the median student messaged it on only a third of the days they practiced, and in only 17 percent of the exercise sessions in which they made a mistake. Messages that students did send were mostly bare answers or clicks on suggested prompts" — [NBER w35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)
- DARPA Digital Tutor (adult Navy IT trainees): three cohorts of twelve — 16 weeks of Digital Tutor, 35 weeks of classroom ITTC, and fleet specialists averaging 9.2 years' experience — with tutor graduates completing more troubleshooting problems than either comparison group — [Fletcher & Morrison, IDA 2014 (DTIC PDF)](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1002362.pdf); a 2022 article gives tallies of 104/140 (tutor), 52/100 (fleet), 33/87 (classroom) — [AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/verticals/ai-system-trains-us-navy-recruits-in-it-skills-in-just-16-weeks)
- Synthesis Tutor positions itself on that result and says it "leverages AI where appropriate while relying on our team of expert educators and neuroscientists to handle much of the pedagogy and interactive experiences. We… do not simply outsource your child's education teaching to an LLM" (ages 7+, K-5 maths, $29/month) — [Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor); [Synthesis: Does the Tutor get results?](https://www.synthesis.com/blog/does-the-synthesis-tutor-get-results)
- Brilliant: stayed out of "AI tutoring chatbots" after ChatGPT because of product testing and hallucination problems; uses AI to build learning games faster; each concept needs "at least twenty problems", a course "over a thousand individual problems"; GPT-3.5 made natural-language control of the game engine workable — [Brilliant blog: Hand-crafted, machine-made (Jan 30 2025)](https://blog.brilliant.org/hand-crafted-machine-made/)

### Inferences
- The products with the strongest internal logic treat review as scheduled work rather than an optional tab: Math Academy and Execute Program both put reviews in the same queue as new lessons and both gate progress on passing them. Duolingo and Khan treat review as something the learner must choose (Practice Hub, Mastery Challenge).
- "Mixed-skill, closed-book, after a delay" is the shared definition of mastery (Khan's Mastered level, Math Academy's quizzes, Duolingo's Legendary with no hints). A single-skill exercise answered right after the explanation never counts as mastered anywhere.
- The Khanmigo trial is the clearest evidence that an on-demand tutor bolted to pre-built content adds little unless the product makes the learner use it at the moment of a mistake.

### Gaps
- No independent outcome study of Math Academy, Execute Program, Exercism, Codecademy (beyond one course supplement paper), Synthesis Tutor, Brilliant, or Duolingo Math was found.
- Duolingo's current (2025–26) blog posts on Birdbrain/path could not be fetched in full; the A/B claim is from the 2020 post.
- Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente's own text was not readable; the 3.13% figure is via Inside Higher Ed's report of their analysis.

## Key question 4: What do they do about motivation, pacing and dropping out in self-study?

### Takeaway
Three distinct strategies appear: Duolingo's streak/XP/league habit engine (effective at return visits, widely criticised as compulsion); Math Academy's calibrated work unit (1 XP = 1 minute) plus daily XP goal, weekly leagues and an estimated completion date; and the OU's supported model (tutor, assignments, study calendar), whose own data still show ~45% leaving during a module. MOOCs show what happens with none of these: ~3% completion.

### Cited Findings
- Math Academy: "Students have a daily XP goal, with each XP being roughly equivalent to 1-minute of focused effort… The daily XP goals can be adjusted"; "Students are placed into leagues depending on how quickly they're earning XP, and can be promoted or demoted… on a weekly basis. While students never lose their accumulated XP, each week is a fresh start"; "frequent, low-stakes quizzes with immediate review and opportunity for retakes can be an effective method for reducing math anxiety" — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works)
- Math Academy gives agency through choice: "The student is presented with a set of learning tasks they can choose from, which will range over a variety of topics, task type, and XP" — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works); XP is explicitly designed to reward quality as well as quantity ("Incentivizing Quantity of Work • Incentivizing Quality of Work • Closing Loopholes • Progress vs XP") — [TOC, Ch. 22 Gamification](https://www.justinmath.com/the-math-academy-way/); a warning sign is "Consistently earning half or fewer XP per minute worked" — [The Math Academy Way (PDF)](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)
- Math Academy's advice to adult upskillers is deliberately austere: "Upskilling is Hard and That's a Good Thing… Enjoyment is a Second-Order Optimization… Be Willing to Do Tedious Work" — [Justin Skycak: Advice on Upskilling (TOC)](https://www.justinmath.com/the-math-academy-way/)
- Duolingo: "Duolingo uses bite-sized lessons to make learning a new language feel less daunting" and streak/notification mechanics "encourage Duolingo learners to return to their lessons" — [Duolingo efficacy whitepaper (PDF)](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/duolingo-efficacy-whitepaper.pdf); Duolingo optimises push-notification wording with a bandit algorithm trained on 200 million reminders — [Duolingo Research: data & tools](http://research.duolingo.com/)
- Duolingo's research finding on pacing: "Learners who did more lessons on Duolingo had better learning outcomes, and lessons mattered more than time spent learning" and "learners with high grit and motivation did more lessons" (Sudina & Plonsky 2023) — [Duolingo Efficacy Studies](http://duolingo.com/efficacy/studies)
- Khan: progress is visible as per-skill levels and unit/course percentages; after each activity a card shows skills levelled up or down and "may also recommend lessons" — [Khan Help Center: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/fr/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery); the Khanmigo RCT identifies engagement as "the binding constraint" — [NBER w35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)
- OU: Ormond Simpson's dropout slides track an OU module from 100 starters, losing students at each assignment, with "overall dropout during one module = 45%" (conference deck) — [Simpson, EADTU slides](https://www.slideshare.net/EADTU/student-dropout-in-distance-education-how-many-who-when-what-are-the-consequences-for-them-why-they-dropout-and-how-do-we-reduce-dropout); Rowntree stresses that distance students "depend on the quality of their learning materials" which "must to a large extent stand alone" — [IJEDE review of Rowntree](https://www.ijede.ca/index.php/jde/article/download/321/215?inline=1); OpenLearn shows a progress bar and awards a Statement of Participation and sometimes a badge — [OpenLearn FAQ](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/get-started/frequently-asked-questions/how-start-course)
- MOOCs: completion fell from ~6% (2014-15) to 3.13% (2017-18) among all enrollees; 46% among those who paid for the verified track — [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/7744); many MOOC learners "are auditors who engage primarily with videos while skipping over assessment problems" — [Guo et al. 2014 (PDF)](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf)
- Execute Program pitches "Only a couple minutes a day" and free first 16 lessons; its reviews are short and interactive — [Execute Program home](https://www.executeprogram.com/)
- Brilliant limits free users to a daily number of lessons via "keys", removed by subscription — [Feedbagel: Brilliant](https://feedbagel.com/post/brilliant-learn-by-doing) (third-party)
- Victor's framing of motivation: "giving control to the reader. Encouraging readers to ask questions, verify assumptions, make connections, and follow their own interests" — [Bret Victor: Explorable Explanations](http://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/)
- Case asks whether an interactive can be "like having a tête-à-tête conversation with an expert on a subject, patient enough to explain to you everything" — [Nicky Case: 4 More Design Patterns](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/)

### Inferences
- The only pacing device that is also a learning device is Math Academy's XP-as-minutes: it turns "how long will this take?" into a number the learner can set per day, and it converts an estimated finish date into a function of that number. Duolingo's streak and Khan's points measure attendance, not learning.
- Dropout in the OU model clusters at assignments; in MOOCs it clusters after the first videos. Both suggest the first check should come early and be low-stakes, and that each later check should not feel like an exam gate.

### Gaps
- No product publishes retention curves for adult self-learners outside Duolingo's lesson-count correlations; Math Academy publishes no completion or churn data.
- Simpson's 45% figure is from slides, not a peer-reviewed paper, and its year is not stated.

## Key question 5: Which structural ideas repeat across the best products, and which are unique to one?

### Takeaway
Repeated almost everywhere: small atomic steps with an immediate check; a mixed, delayed, closed-book test as the definition of mastery; explanation of every wrong answer; spaced review driven by a model of forgetting; a short diagnostic to place the learner; visible per-skill progress. Unique: Math Academy's knowledge-graph task scheduler and "fractional implicit repetition"; Duolingo's guidebook-beside-the-unit; Victor's readable-without-interacting layering; Case's "Place Your Bets"; Patel's "islands of interactivity" and controls-below-the-diagram; Exercism's human mentoring; the OU's activity-type balance audit; AoPS's problems-before-text; Khan's demotion on mixed tests.

### Cited Findings
- Small step + check: Math Academy KPs ("2-5 practice questions on the same type of problem as the worked example") — [The Math Academy Way (PDF)](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf); Codecademy exercises (narrative + checkpoint) — [Codecademy Lesson Standards](https://curriculum-documentation.codecademy.com/lessons/lesson-standards/); Coursera in-video questions every 5–7 minutes — [UMN deck (PDF)](https://pubs.lib.umn.edu/index.php/mslt/article/download/735/739/976); Execute Program paragraphs interleaved with live code — [Why EP?](https://www.executeprogram.com/why-ep)
- Mixed delayed closed-book mastery: Khan "Mastered" requires a mixed-skill assessment — [Khan mastery levels](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work); Math Academy quizzes "timed… may not refer back" — [How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works); Duolingo Legendary "no hints" — [ways to practice](https://blog.duolingo.com/ways-to-practice-in-duolingo)
- Explain every wrong answer: "Every question has an explanation" — [Math Academy](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works); Duolingo "When you miss a challenge, Duolingo quickly shows you how to improve" — [Wayback 2021](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20210404165602/https://en.duolingo.com); OU free-text feedback — [REAP case study (PDF)](https://www.reap.ac.uk/reap/reap07/Portals/2/CSL/feast%20of%20case%20studies/Extending_the_pedagogic_role_of_online_interactive_assessment.pdf)
- Modelled spaced review: HLR — [Settles & Meeder 2016 (PDF)](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf); Math Academy repNum/interval — [The Math Academy Way (PDF)](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf); Execute Program next-day review — [Why EP?](https://www.executeprogram.com/why-ep); Khan Mastery Challenge wait — [Khan support community](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/es/community/posts/360043463471/comments/360008042871)
- Placement diagnostic: Math Academy 30–45 min adaptive — [How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works); Khan Course Challenge "earn mastery points for what you already know" — [Pre-algebra course challenge](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/pre-algebra/test/xb4832e56:course-challenge)
- Unique to Math Academy: tasks chosen for "greatest overall gain in spaced repetitions across your entire knowledge profile"; "non-interference"; "layering"; "Encompassings Enable Turbo-Boosted Learning Speed" — [How It Works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works); [TOC](https://www.justinmath.com/the-math-academy-way/)
- Unique to explorables: "the subtlety with which the explorable is integrated with the explanation" and no separate interactive context — [Victor](http://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/); Patel's phone rules: "I've moved sliders and buttons below the diagram so that when you're interacting with it, your finger/hand don't cover up the diagram"; "Be careful about how touch-drag interacts with scrolling… you might get trapped on a diagram"; "It's often better to show all the variants than to ask the reader to interact with the diagram to see the variants. Interactivity isn't always needed"; inline legends connect text and diagram by putting diagram elements in the sentence — [Little Design Things](https://www.redblobgames.com/making-of/little-things/)
- Unique to Case: "teach the pieces before the whole" (split a system into mechanics, introduce each alone) and make the ending depend on what was built up — [Nicky Case 2014](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations/); "Place Your Bets!" — [4 More Design Patterns](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/)
- Unique to Exercism: human mentoring on any exercise in either mode — [Exercism docs](https://exercism.org/docs/building/product/unlocking-exercises)
- Unique to the OU: auditing the share of assimilative vs practice/productive activity in a module — [OU Activity Types Framework 2021 (PDF)](https://www.open.ac.uk/blogs/learning-design/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/OU-LD-Activity-Types-Framework-October-2021-FINAL.pdf)
- Unique to AoPS/Moore: problems or theorems first, exposition second — [AoPS Intro to Algebra](https://artofproblemsolving.com/store/book/intro-algebra); [Wikipedia: Moore method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_method)
- What one-to-one teaching does that content cannot: Bloom's 1984 comparison put tutorial instruction at an effect size of 2.0 versus 1.0 for feedback-corrective mastery learning, 1.0 for cues and explanations, and 1.0 for time on task — [Wikipedia: Bloom's 2 sigma problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom%27s_2_sigma_problem); in tutorials "students are expected to orally communicate, defend, analyse, and critique the ideas of others as well as their own" — [Wikipedia: Tutorial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutorial_system); Math Academy's book opens with "Bloom's Two-Sigma Problem" and argues technology is how the cognitive strategies get applied — [TOC](https://www.justinmath.com/the-math-academy-way/)

### Inferences
- A pre-built course can copy mastery learning, feedback-corrective practice and time-on-task (each ~1.0 in Bloom's table) but not the "defend your reasoning out loud" step of a tutorial; the closest pre-built substitutes are Case's Role Play pattern and an AI tutor prompted to ask for an explanation — which the Khanmigo trial shows learners mostly ignore unless pushed.
- For a phone-first product, Patel's three touch rules (controls below the diagram, don't trap scrolling, show variants side by side instead of demanding interaction) are the most directly borrowable, because they are the only guidance found that was written for touch.

### Gaps
- No source compares these structures head-to-head for adults; the "repeated" list is my synthesis of separate product descriptions.

## Key question 6: What do critics say is wrong with each approach?

### Takeaway
Critiques cluster by design choice: Khan's videos were attacked (2012) for procedures over concepts and "drill and kill"; Math Academy for XP-driven shallow speed and a grind that spaced review did not catch; Duolingo for streak compulsion, leaderboard anxiety and translation drills over speaking; MOOCs for completion rates that never improved; AI tutors for going unused; discovery methods (Moore, AoPS, IBL) for thin evidence and slow coverage.

### Cited Findings
- Khan Academy (2012, dated): two professors' satirical critique led Khan to pull and re-record an integer video; teachers argued the clips skip "the heavy-lifting part of teaching", focus "on procedures over concepts", and don't ensure understanding; others called it an update of "drill and kill"; Khan's defence was pause-and-rewind — [EdWeek 2012](https://www.edweek.org/education/critique-of-khan-academy-goes-viral/2012/07); [Vice: Khan Academy under fire](https://vice.com/en/article/khan-academy-under-fire)
- Khanmigo: gains "resemble those from Khan Academy practice without AI assistance"; students "rarely engaged it in substantive mathematical dialogue" — [NBER w35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)
- Math Academy: "you only get that sweet XP for solving problems…so yeah, students are going to go faster, and learn shallowly as a result"; "I kept answering questions correctly, and MA kept pushing me onward, without a chance to deepen my understanding" — [Michael Pershan, Substack](https://pershmail.substack.com/p/math-academy-wants-to-supercharge); a sympathetic reviewer still calls it "ruthlessly effective, occasionally frustrating" at ~4–5 hours a week — [recess.gg review](https://think.recess.gg/math-curriculum-review-mathacademy); Math Academy itself concedes students can waste time "triple-checking against the worked example" and that "Many students need days or weeks to fully grasp" the required habits — [The Math Academy Way (PDF)](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)
- Duolingo: "many argue that the daily learning habits it promotes are more akin to addiction" — [UX Collective: the good, the bad and the ugly of Duolingo gamification](https://uxdesign.cc/the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly-of-duolingo-gamification-3a12f0e80dc7); the streak "keeps people opening the app even when they don't feel like studying" and leaderboards make users "anxious about falling behind rather than focusing on actually learning" — [Desirability Lab 2024](https://desirabilitylab.com/backfill/backfill-2024-111-duolingo-gamification-model); a competitor-leaning guide says the app "focuses on translating sentences. This doesn't help users learn to speak fluently" — [duolingoguides: Why Duolingo is bad](https://duolingoguides.com/why-duolingo-is-bad/)
- Duolingo's own research undercuts "time on app": lessons completed, not minutes, predicted outcomes — [Duolingo Efficacy Studies](http://duolingo.com/efficacy/studies)
- MOOCs: "the bane of MOOCs—low completion rates—has not improved over 6 years" and providers pivoted to paid degrees — [Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente 2019 (DSpace)](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/136215/post_print-MOOC_Pivot.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2); classroom lectures "adapted from old lecture videos" engaged far less than lectures "prepared with edX usage in mind" — [Guo et al. 2014 (PDF)](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf)
- Explorables: Case links to an essay on "the death of interactives" and asks whether explorables can teach skills, not just ideas — [4 More Design Patterns](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/); Victor: sandbox widgets "are not explanations" — [Victor](http://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/); Patel: "Interactivity isn't always needed" — [Little Design Things](https://www.redblobgames.com/making-of/little-things/)
- Codecademy: "if you want to use Codecademy to refresh your knowledge the lessons are not designed to be quickly done" — [Slant](https://www.slant.co/versus/3393/3026/~learn-to-program_vs_codecademy)
- Moore/IBL: proponents "are often only able to provide anecdotal evidence for its success", per an MAA volume chapter — [Cambridge Core: The Moore Method, evaluation chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/moore-method/evaluation-and-assessment-effectiveness-of-the-method/66C5BD9F96C5D6BD87049C1FB5E57794); the method "typically limits the amount of material that a class is able to cover" — [Wikipedia: Moore method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_method); a 2022 preprint argues the call for inquiry-based reform in tertiary maths "seems unjustified" — [arXiv 2206.12149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12149)
- Oxford tutorial: "detractors consider it open to potential inconsistency and misuse by tutors"; a 2017 law-school pilot found more support for the system's supporters but was small-scale — [The Law Teacher pilot, UEL repository](https://repository.uel.ac.uk/item/84v4v); earlier THE coverage of scrutiny — [THE: Oxford tutorials come under scrutiny](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/34960)
- Synthesis/DARPA: the headline result rests on three groups of twelve, and a commentator found the 2014 report's design details "too scant to build on" — [LessWrong: DARPA Digital Tutor](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/vbWBJGWyWyKyoxLBe); [DTIC PDF](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1002362.pdf)
- LearnLM: results are rater preferences and company-reported trials — [arXiv 2412.16429](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16429); [Google Cloud: LearnLM](https://cloud.google.com/solutions/learnlm)
- Brilliant (self-critique): abandoned chatbot tutoring because of hallucination problems in its own testing — [Brilliant blog](https://blog.brilliant.org/hand-crafted-machine-made/)

### Inferences
- The two best-documented self-study engines (Math Academy, Duolingo) are criticised for the same thing from opposite directions: their reward loop measures throughput (XP, streak) and can be gamed by speed. A product for one adult who is not competing with anyone can drop leagues and streaks and keep only the calibrated work unit and the mistake-driven review.
- Pershan's complaint — correct answers kept him moving with no chance to deepen — is the gap an "explain it back" or "say it in your own words" step is designed to fill; none of the surveyed mastery engines has one.
