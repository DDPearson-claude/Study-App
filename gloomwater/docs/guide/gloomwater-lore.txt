GLOOMWATER: THE LORE AND THE STORY
==================================

The whole story of the drowned kingdom, the Lamp Road and the small candle who fishes it, gathered
in one place.

Written 17 November 2026 from the game's design documents and from the words the game ships with
today. Where two sources disagree, the newer revision or the shipped game wins.

HOW TO READ THIS FILE
---------------------

  - (in the game now) means it is in today's build: Lake 1, the Mossmere, with the opening, the
    home barge and Murk.
  - (planned) means it is designed and agreed, but not built yet.
  - "Not yet written" means the canon is silent. Nothing has been invented to fill the gap.
  - Lines in double quotes are the game's own words. Most of the story is never said on screen at
    all: the game tells it with places, lights and a handful of short lines.


1. IN A BREATH
==============

Long ago a kingdom of tall, warm-handed people lived all along one great water road. They began in
a warm orchard forest in the south, where they kept bees and made candles from the wax, and over
many generations they followed the big fish and the long summer dusks north until the land ran
out. There, on a cold lake at the edge of the sea cliffs, they built their capital, and over its
water they hung a great lantern: their promise that someone would always be watching for whoever
was out on the water at evening. Every house set a small candle in its window for the same reason.

One evening, about four hundred years ago, the lantern's last chain link simply wore through.
There was no enemy, no curse and no crime. It was old. The lantern fell into its own reflection,
the great sluice it had held open dropped shut, and the lake rose over the city in a night. The
people took it as a sign that their kingdom's time had ended. They hung no second lantern, and
went back down their road to the orchard country where they began, and there their story quietly
stops. Everything they built along the way was left unkept, and over the centuries the water came
back in.

You are a Wick: one of the capital's little window candles, washed ashore on the night of the fall
and laid by a banked stove aboard an old barge-house by Old Murk, a centuries-old toad, the last
trader left on the road. Candles like you were lit for whoever was coming home. With nobody left
to come home, the ones that wake go out on the water themselves. "No one ever told it that it
couldn't."

So every evening you sail a little raft out onto the water, fish through dusk into the night, and
come home at first light with your catch. Murk buys every fish for clams and sells you the old
charts that lift the mist from new waters. Water by water you follow the people's road, the Lamp
Road, sixteen waters from the cold stone north to the green forest in the south, towing your home
behind you. At the very end, in the orchard forest where the kingdom began and ended, Murk rakes
his banked fire open at last, because somebody has finally brought the whole road home.

Nothing in this world is fought, nothing hunts you, and nothing can be lost but the fish that got
away. "You are still very small. You are still burning."


2. THE WORLD
============

2.1 ONE CONTINENT: THE WAXWOLD
------------------------------

The world is one continent with sea on every side: the Waxwold, which in the people's old word
means "the wax forest", for the south where the bees were. It runs about six thousand kilometres
from its cold north coast to its warm southern cape, two lobes joined at a long waist. The north
lobe is cold, high and stony: the old kingdom's highland, with a mountain range called the Spine
down its west side and an ice field, the White Crown, at its northern end. The south lobe is low
and wet: the Orchard Basin's flooded forest and the great brown river called the Siltway. Between
them lies the Fold, thousands of kilometres of empty steppe and desert coast that the kingdom only
ever crossed by sea. The old chart-makers drew it folded to a hand's width with a ribbon of wave
and dune marks, their sign for "this goes on a long time".

It is an ordinary world under an ordinary sun, with days and nights, seasons, winds and currents.
Rivers run downhill and cross mountains only through gaps. Nothing about the land is magic. Beyond
the map lie places nobody has charted, in the east, in the far south and over the Far Sea beyond
the far isle: room for later sheets, never a promise of anyone there.

2.2 THE PEOPLE AND THEIR ROAD
-----------------------------

The people began in the Orchard Basin, the low, warm forest in the middle of the south. They
planted orchards on dikes, kept bees, made candles from the wax, and fished at evening by the
basketful, laughing. Over many generations they went north along the water roads, the only roads
they had, following the big fish and the long dusks. The farther north you go, the longer a summer
evening lasts, and these were people who loved the hour when the big fish rise. So they kept going
until the land ran out, and on the highland's north lip, over the last lake before the sea cliffs,
they built their capital: the Sunken Gate and its city on the Mossmere.

At every water they settled along the way, the people of that water built a lamp at the landing
for whoever was out on the water at evening, and kept it themselves. Read in order, those lamps
are the Lamp Road, and the road is the Wick's route, walked from its north end back to its south.
Each lamp belongs to its own water. None was lit from another.

2.3 THE KINGDOM AND ITS GREAT LANTERN
-------------------------------------

The Lamplit Kingdom held the whole road, and it held its water back: a great sluice in the
capital's outer wall, gardens and a crystal vault in the highland basins, a summit canal, a weir
on a glacier lake, a lighthouse, temple stairs in the snow, a harbour town won from the sea behind
a mole, a lock stair down a great escarpment, a dam, a sea-gate, a straight canal, a barge road
with white stone mile-posts, and orchards on dikes in the forest.

Over the capital's lake, on chains in front of the Gate, hung the great lantern: the kingdom's
oath that someone would always be watching for whoever was out on the water. Its weight, through
the chains and a counterweight, also held the great sluice open, so the lake drained through the
outer wall and the lower city stood dry.

2.4 THE FALL
------------

One evening the chain's last link wore through. The lantern fell into its own reflection, and with
its weight gone the sluice gate dropped shut. The Gate's windows guttered out row by row while
their reflections stayed lit a beat longer. Over that night and the days after, the Mossmere rose
over the capital to the arches, until it spilled over the outer wall and fell into the sea cove
below as the Sluice Run, which is how it falls still. On the Toppled Bridge the chain still hangs,
ending in one worn-thin link.

As the lantern sank, a great pale shark that had come up under it at dusk for longer than anyone
could remember took its ember. She is the Lantern-Swallower, older than the kingdom, and the ember
still shows through her six gills. She is not a thief and not a keeper of anything: she is a big
old fish people tell stories about.

Why did they not hang another? The lantern was their oath, and they took its fall as the end of
their kingdom's time. They kept their word by letting it end, and went back south the way they had
come, carrying what they could. Murk poled the last barge of their things down the road. It sank
under him at the great bend of the Siltway, empty of people, and he swam.

The folk call the four hundred years since "the long evening". It is a saying, not a sky: the sun
still rises.

2.5 THE SLOW DROWNING
---------------------

"The lantern falls. The kingdom follows." The capital went under in a night; everything else went
over centuries, as nobody was left to keep the works. The weir on the glacier lake broke and the
river spread thin over its sand. The lock stair broke and a river fell down it. The harbour's
sea-wall gave way and coral took the town's roofs. The forest dikes went and the river took the
orchards, and the delta's edge sank until the orchard by the sea became a mangrove lagoon. The
folk say "The water rose to look for it." That is a saying, never a rule of the world.

2.6 THE STONE NORTH AND THE GREEN SOUTH
---------------------------------------

The road runs from a cold coast and a stone highland, through a dry rain-shadow coast, into the
wet tropics, and every drowned work is overgrown at the speed of its climate: bare in the north,
furred in the dry west, swallowed whole in the wet south. So each water is one small step greener
than the one before, sixteen steps in all, from a trace of moss on the Mossmere's stone to a black
river under a closed roof of leaves. The north is where the light fell; the farther you go from
the fall, the more the world has grown over it. "Moss has kept the vigil the candles could not."
The kingdom stopped; the world did not.

Only living things turn green. The sky over every water is the same violet-dusk sky at the same
hour, and the ending never changes it.

2.7 LIGHT AND DARK
------------------

The great lantern went out; the small lights did not. That is the heart of the world: a window
candle set for whoever is out on the water, a lamp at each landing kept by its own people, a
toad's banked fire carried aboard. Some of the people's lamps still burn with nobody to feed them:
eight candle slits in the Gate, a sluice lamp under a broken weir, a lighthouse that turns for
ships that left, temple fires in the snow, two signal lamps on a broken lock stair, one amber lamp
on a dam. Nobody tends them, and the game never explains why they burn.

The dark is just the dark. The old word for it is the Gloom: night on cold water, patient, not
cruel. There is nothing in it to fight. The pairs of amber eyes at the edge of the lantern light
are only animals watching.

Of all the warm lights in the world, only the Wick's lantern lights the water around it; every
other warm glow is a pinpoint that lights nothing. The only green lights are Murk's sage-green
swamp-lamp, the south's colour carried north, and its one twin among the stilt lanterns of
Lanternroot Lagoon.

2.8 THE NIGHTS: WHY EVERY TRIP IS AN EVENING
--------------------------------------------

A Wick is a window candle, and a window candle is lit at dusk and burns until first light. So a
Wick wakes in the last of the day, fishes through dusk and night, and comes home at first light to
doze through the day. And the big fish come up at dusk: "The big ones come up at dusk. They always
did." Nothing runs out. A Wick never needs feeding and never burns low; a dozing Wick is asleep,
not lost, and nothing a Wick does or fails to do puts it out.

2.9 THE MIST AND THE MAPS
-------------------------

Forgetting looks like mist. A water nobody remembers sinks into standing lavender mist and cannot
be found; on clear nights you can sometimes see something big turning inside it. A chart is a
remembering: somebody who knew the way drew it. When a living flame carries that chart, the mist
lets go of that water for good. That is why buying a map is the only way to find a new water, and
why only Murk can sell them: he holds the last of the kingdom's charts.

They are the kingdom's own sheets, collected in trade over the centuries, in four hands: the court
surveyors' fine ink, the sea-road wardens' lamp marks, the harbour-masters' soundings and the
barge pilots' strip maps. The last of all, the Inkwood's, is the oldest sheet there is: a plain
orchard plan from the people's first villages. Murk seals each chart with wax pressed into the
shape of the water's legend, because he names waters for whoever lives there.

(planned) A tale is a remembering too: a far-water Wick can tell you the way to a small corner no
chart-maker drew. But no tale ever tells the way to a whole water. Only a chart holds that.

2.10 THE FOUR SMALL WONDERS
---------------------------

Four things are never explained and never need to be. Nothing else is magic.

  - Some lamps keep burning with nobody to feed them.
  - A window candle can wake.
  - A forgotten water hides in mist until it is remembered.
  - A kept lamp remembers the way. That is how a raft towing a whole house makes a long leg of the
    road between one dawn and the next evening, and why going back to a water your home has lain
    at is no voyage at all.

2.11 WHAT LIVES HERE, AND WHAT DOES NOT
---------------------------------------

  - Fish, above all: 164 kinds, from thumb-sized fry to legends longer than the barge.
  - Frogs, the south's voice: none in the stone north, a few in Sporewood's warm hollow, a chorus
    from Lampmoss Slough on, and a roar in the Inkwood. The world's sound greens with its colour.
  - Moths at the Wick's flame; fireflies from the Thin Water south; herons (one already fishes the
    Mossmere's reed edge); night birds from the Salt Gate's shipyard on; bats under the Inkwood
    canopy; and bees, carved over a garden door in the north and humming for real only in the
    Inkwood. Bees made the Wicks' wax.
  - The old toads, a long-lived people of the warm south, the kingdom's neighbours and traders,
    never its subjects. Murk is one.
  - The Wicks, window candles that woke, and (planned) a few creatures of the road with something
    to say.

What does not live here: no people are seen on any water, and none will appear; they are shown
only by what they made and left. No enemies, no curse and no villain. Older versions of the game
had night creatures, fighting, oil and death; all of that is switched off and is not part of this
story. No relic hunt, no lighthouse keeper, and no single first flame that every light was lit
from. Nothing can be lost. There is no way to fail.


3. THE STORY IN ORDER
=====================

3.1 HOW THE STORY IS TOLD
-------------------------

Gloomwater tells its story with almost no words. "Art, atmosphere and fishing" come first, and the
story explains the fishing rather than interrupting it. There are no quests, no puzzles and no
reveals in writing. Instead there is an opening film of four short lines; one landmark at each
water, framed by the camera as the mist lifts on your first evening there, a clue you cannot miss;
the tow, which shows each leg of the road once; four silent greetings from old lamps; one short
line on each map card and one from Murk when he sells a lake's map; sound; and the Chart, which
draws the continent sheet by sheet in the order you travel.

The arc is a road walked in four acts, one for each reach: What Fell, The Cold Road, Where They
Turned and The Way Back. When a reach's fourth map is bought, its act's name is written into the
Chart's border, so at the end the border spells the whole arc.

3.2 PROLOGUE: "ONE WARM LIGHT" (IN THE GAME NOW)
------------------------------------------------

You press New Descent, choose your Wick, press Start, and the film begins at once: eighteen
seconds, wordless but for four lines shown one at a time.

    "A kingdom hangs a great lantern over its lake."
    "The lantern falls. The kingdom follows."
    "Its last candles fish from lamp to lamp."
    "You are one of them."

First the lit kingdom: the great lantern over the lake, every window amber, all of it doubled in
the water. A chain parts. The lantern drops to meet its own reflection, and the windows go out
behind it. Then ink and deep water: the ember sinks, and a pale flank far too big for the frame
takes it and carries it away. Then the surface, centuries later: a small flame catching, guttering
in the cold, catching again. It is you, out on the Mossmere with your line already out. The film
jumps to now; the opening that follows shows how you got there.

3.3 WAKING AT THE LANDING (IN THE GAME NOW)
-------------------------------------------

On the night of the fall the capital's window candles were carried out of their windows on the
rising water, down the Sluice Run, and washed up at the Landing, a sea cave under the outer wall,
on the first dry stone below the drowning. In its cove lay an old barge-house, the Slow Hearth: a
barge family's floating home that had come north with a barge train and was waiting there when the
lantern fell. Murk carried the candles aboard and laid them by the fire he banked in its stove,
low, the way toads and people both banked a fire for a household that was away, to be stirred when
someone came home with news of the road.

There you wake, by the banked stove among the dozing candles, and Murk gives you the whole economy
of the world:

    "Awake, little Wick? Good. Fish sell. Clams buy. That's the whole of it."

He means it. He is wry, not secretive. Far down the channel, once, a single warm point pulses.

3.4 THE FIRST TOW (IN THE GAME NOW)
-----------------------------------

You take the barge's line, and your raft tows the old house out of the cove and up the Sluice Run
and its locks to the Mossmere: the first water up the road, and the door the last candles came out
of. Murk's own barge comes alongside. The Chart shows the leg go by and inks in the Mossmere's
sheet, and then you see the raft towing the barge in to its mooring at the Jetty, under the
water's old landing lamp. From that morning on, the barge is home, wherever it lies. "A hearth
goes where the household goes." When you take the household's line, Murk's fire goes up the road
with you, and so does Murk.

3.5 THE FIRST NIGHT ON THE MOSSMERE (IN THE GAME NOW)
-----------------------------------------------------

The Chart opens over the moored barge with one water on it, already chosen. Under it the hold's
empty slots ink in: three, or four for Tallow.

    "Three slots in that hold. Fill them and come home."

You press Sail, and the raft glides out into the moat of the drowned capital in the last of the
day: violet water under lavender mist, pale drowned stone, reeds and lily pads, and in the Sunken
Gate's face eight candle slits still lit. You press on the water and let go to cast. When the
float dips you tap ("Now. Tap."), and the play runs itself: the line shows how big a fish is, but
not what it is, until it breaks the surface. The Wick holds it up to the light, and a card shows
its name, its journal line and its price. Keep it, or let it go: "Let one go and the water
remembers." The spot gets its good bite back, and the next fish there run a little bigger.

Dusk falls after the first minute, and the big fish start to rise. Night lasts until you choose to
turn for home ("Quiet water. Move on, or go home."). When the hold is full, the raft sails home
through the dark to the barge's warm windows, and you arrive at first light. At the tally Murk
weighs every fish, writes it in his Ledger, pays you in clams, and before dawn lets every one go
at the gate. He is buying the news of the waters, not the fish. "Bring me something with a story."
And on that first morning: "Clams. Spend them here. I'm the only shop left."

That is the shape of every evening from then on: out at dusk, home at dawn, the tally, the shop,
and out again.

3.6 LIFE ON LAKE 1 (IN THE GAME NOW)
------------------------------------

  - The four place maps, each a corner of the lake hidden in standing mist: the Lily Maze ("Koi
    glow under the pads like lanterns."), Under the Span by the Toppled Bridge ("The bridge kept
    its chain. Something kept it."), the Buoy Lights ("The harbour lights went under, still lit.")
    and the Bell Pool ("The moon looks in on the old bell."). Buy one and Murk says "It's in the
    mist. Go and look."; on your next evening the mist lifts for good while the camera frames its
    landmark.
  - The giants: the Thousandcast Pike under the Span and the Hullkiss Lamprey at the Buoy Lights.
    "That one had a name before you met it."
  - The legend: the Belled Matriarch in the Bell Pool, who takes only her own bait, the Heartmoss
    Ball, one try at a time. Land her and Murk says "Her. You brought me her. Sit down, I'll count
    it twice." He lets her go, and her print hangs along the cabin eave.
  - The heart: when an Elder or an Ancient takes your bait, your heart races.
  - Finds: now and then the line comes up heavy with something that sank with the kingdom (section
    7). "The water keeps what it takes. Mostly."
  - Home fills: a bigger hold, better tackle, lures and bait, the raft's oars, keel and lantern,
    Murk's order board, lights and a wall for your best prints, all sold from Murk's own printed
    catalogue. The Journal holds an ink print of every fish you land.
  - The road ahead: on the Chart, Sporewood Pools waits under a seal, "Coming soon". In the full
    game three keys open each new water: its map, the right gear, and one of each of the last
    water's ordinary fish, landed or let go. "Every fish in this water. I'll write that down."

3.7 THE JOURNEY ACROSS THE WATERS (PLANNED)
-------------------------------------------

The rest of the story is the road, water by water (each water's entry is in section 5). Each time
you sail to a newly charted water, you tow your home there down its leg of the road: the Chart
shows the leg go by, a cold coast's cliffs, a lock stair's towers, a canal's lock, the black water
of the Seam, and the raft brings the barge in to the new water's old landing, under its kept lamp.
Murk inks that water's stone in the sill.

  - Act I, What Fell (the old kingdom). Through the drowned capital and its highland basins: the
    moat, the gardens, the vault of shelved light, the place of remembrance. The question it
    raises: what happened here? By its end you know: a great city drowned because something old
    let go; its people were gardeners and keepers of light; nobody did this to them.
  - Act II, The Cold Road (the cold rim). Out by a glacier fjord to the open sea: frozen
    bell-buoys, a sluice lamp, the last lighthouse, a snow temple. The question: the lamps out
    here are still burning; is anyone still out here? By its end: the kingdom was a road, not only
    a city; its lamps still burn; a few small fishers are still out on the far waters; a window
    candle is lit for someone coming home.
  - Act III, Where They Turned (the falls and the gate). After the long voyage down the cold
    coast, the kingdom's warm working coast: a harbour town under coral, a broken lock stair, a
    dam, a sea-gate left open beside a shipyard of unfinished hulls. The question: where did the
    people go? By its end: they were not destroyed and did not vanish. At the edge of the warm sea
    they turned inland, back toward where they began.
  - Act IV, The Way Back (the green heart). By canal and barge road into the rain country, to the
    lagoon where the orchard met the sea and the drowned forest beyond. The question: what became
    of them? The answer waits at the top of a stone stair: a hearth raked over with ash, the way
    you bank a fire for the night, and never stirred again.

3.8 MEETINGS ON THE ROAD (PLANNED)
----------------------------------

Two kinds of meeting are designed for after the first lake, rare, gentle and never sought: no
marker, no quest, no way to call anyone, and at most one meeting an evening, never during a bite.
Now and then at dusk a small lantern lifts across the water, and another candle that stayed out on
the far waters says a line or two: one of the four far-water Wicks. Or one of five creatures of
the road comes to you: a snail, a family of moths, a kingfisher, a night bird, a young bat. Their
portraits are in section 4.

3.9 THE ENDING: "THE HEARTH STIRRED" (PLANNED)
----------------------------------------------

The last chart lifts the mist on the blackwater forest. On your first evening in the Inkwood,
after the mist lift has framed the banked hearth on the Kitchen Stair, something comes up to
breathe far off in the Breathing Mere: a long warm glow rising through the lavender, a back
turning, never the whole fish. It is the Banked Fire, named for the people's hearth because she
rises to breathe the way a banked fire breathes. Nothing is caught, fought or won.

Then you come home. The closing film plays once, on your first trip home from the Inkwood, with
any catch or none; the road triggers it, never a fish, so it cannot be missed. It is first light,
the barge moored below the Kitchen Stair. At the tally Murk counts in silence. He takes the
Journal and turns its pages slowly: the whole road, print by print. He closes it, goes into the
cabin, and rakes his banked fire open with his pole.

On Murk's barge, in the birdcage it let itself into at the Salt Gate, Supper the night bird hears
the rake: the one sound the phrase its people kept for four hundred years was waiting for. In its
own bird's call it says "Welcome home." It says it only here, once.

The fire comes up full and gold, the warmest light in the game, spilling out of the door and
windows onto the black water and up the stair's first steps, and it stays that way. The people's
own hearth at the top of the stair stays as they left it. The Wicks aboard turn toward the fire,
your own flame leans toward it, and under the awning two small amber eyes turn toward it too. Four
lines, one at a time:

    "Far away, a banked fire breathes."
    "At home, Murk rakes his open."
    "The road comes home with you."
    "You are still very small. You are still burning."

The title card becomes the home barge with its hearth full, and there is one button: Keep
Drifting.

The ending is small on purpose. The Wick saves nothing and relights nothing. The people banked
their fire and went; Murk banked his, and when a stubborn candle took the household's line he
carried it the whole road with them. By the toads' and the people's custom, a banked fire is
stirred when someone comes home with news of the road, and the Wick is the first to bring all of
it. The sky does not change. The evenings go on.

3.10 AFTER THE ENDING (PLANNED)
-------------------------------

The game never ends, because nothing can be lost. Every fish, giant and legend is still there, and
the hearth aboard stays full wherever the barge moors. Murk gains one line: "Warm in here. Don't
tell anyone." Flinch the bat flies home into the dark with its colony ("It's not so bad. It's just
night."); Supper stays aboard for good. The Banked Fire stays the final dream: land her and Murk
counts her twice and lets her go, and her print hangs facing the Lantern-Swallower's. (An older
ending, in which landing the great pale fish of the deep brought the light back, is retired.)

3.11 THE MYSTERIES
------------------

Left open on purpose, never to be answered: where the last of the people went after the final
hearth was banked, and whether any are still somewhere beyond the map; why some lamps burn with
nobody to feed them, why a candle can wake, and how a kept lamp remembers the way.

Not yet written:

  - who the cold candles dozing in the drowned towns are, and whether more will catch again (Dip
    is the only one shown to have done so);
  - how and when Taper, Stub, Tallow and Ashwick woke, and how the story reads when "you" are one
    of them;
  - Murk's life before the fall, and the old toads as a people: their numbers, homes, names and
    customs;
  - the stories behind each legend's name, and of the many small places of each water;
  - the meaning of the seven knots by the Lantern Hole, which the game keeps unspoken (the
    designers know it: a knot for each time the Long Winter has passed under that hole);
  - the never-charted east, the world beyond the far isle, an empty town by the salt pans whose
    wells failed, and a ruined watchtower on the north-east peninsula.


4. THE PEOPLE
=============

Nobody in this world is an enemy. Everyone is kind, busy or asleep. Named characters talk; every
other creature stays an ordinary animal. The Wick never speaks, and neither do the old toads, and
anything a character says fits two short rows on a phone screen.

4.1 THE WICK (YOU)
------------------

A Wick is a small, fat window candle. In the kingdom every house set one in its window at evening
so that whoever was out on the water could find the way home. The candles were made of orchard-bee
wax from the south, so every Wick is a small piece of the forest at the far end of the road.

Some of the capital's candles washed up at the Landing on the night of the fall, and Murk laid
them by his banked stove, cold. Now and then, over the centuries, one woke. A candle lit to see
someone off burns down; a candle lit for someone coming home keeps waiting. These waited so long,
with nobody left to come home, that the ones that woke took the old rafts and rods and went out on
the water themselves. You are the latest to wake, and the first to fish the whole road through:
not chosen, not special, just stubborn. Most Wicks that woke fished the near waters and dozed
again. None kept buying Murk's charts to the last one.

The Wick stands about knee-high to the kingdom's people, whose doors are four Wicks tall; its
exact height is not yet fixed. An egg-shaped body in a patched umber-rust cloak frayed at the hem,
a pale wax collar, a head that is a short, fat ivory candle with a melted rim and a few drips, two
small amber eyes glowing in a hollow face, and a living flame on top. It fishes with a long, bent
driftwood rod with a bone reel and a tiny brass bell, its stubby legs dangling over the edge of a
raft of lashed dark planks with a crooked pole and a hanging iron lantern. On the New Descent
screen it is "A Small, Stubborn Flame": "New kinds and new bests sell higher."

4.2 THE OTHER CANDLES OF THE STOVE: TAPER, STUB, TALLOW AND ASHWICK
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Four more candles doze by the stove aboard the barge and travel wherever it goes. In the game now,
any of the five can be chosen on the New Descent screen, and the one you choose is "the Wick" of
the story for that save. (The older idea, that the others wake one at a time as you fish, is kept
as an alternative; how the story reads when you play one of the other four is not yet written.)

  - Taper, "The Long Reach": tall and thin in a slate cloak, a pale-gold flame atop a crooked
    stovepipe of wax. The rare-fish seeker. "It would rather be fishing." Its gift: "Evenings
    start with a rarer fish."
  - Stub, "The Bright Little One": small, in a rust cloak, with a white-gold flame bigger than its
    body that moths stream into. "Tiny, quick and fiercely bright." Its gift: "Fish bite sooner
    and keep biting."
  - Tallow, "The Stout Candle": broad and squat in an olive hood, a low deep-orange flame, a
    wicker basket by its creel; always last to turn for home. "Slow to light. Slower to go out."
    Its gift: "One more slot in the hold." Where it came from among the window candles is not yet
    written.
  - Ashwick, "Burned Once, Burns Back": charred grey wax, a tattered charcoal hood, a red-orange
    flame and eyes like cracks in a coal. "It went out once. It came back angry." (A candle's joke
    about being blown out, nothing more.) Its gift: "A fish that gets away comes back." Its name
    is not final.

By the stove there is also an empty place that holds only an old wax drip. It has no story, and it
stays empty.

4.3 OLD MURK
------------

An enormous, hunched umber toad, tufted with rust-moss and lichen, with heavy-lidded, faintly
glowing eyes, a drooping lip and a moth-eaten shawl. His throat-sac swells before he speaks; his
tongue flicks at passing moths. He carries a dim sage-green swamp-lamp on a hooked pole, and his
towering pack holds jars of bait, empty birdcages, coiled rope, dead lanterns, a crooked umbrella
and a fish-bone wind chime. "Everything here was somebody's last hope once." His own barge is a
withered bronze lily-pad barge with a hand bell on a post, and that bell is the sound of home.

Murk was the barge-trader of the whole Lamp Road, from the orchards to the capital. He was in the
capital the night the lantern fell, and he poled the last barge of the people's things south until
it sank under him at the great bend. He swam. When the people were gone he came back up the road
alone to the cove where the candles lay, because a trader goes where his last customers are, and
these were the last of the kingdom. He banked his fire in the Slow Hearth's stove and waited.
"I've had centuries. Fewer than I'd like." He is never younger than the fall.

He buys every fish for its story and lets it go. His clams are the old toads' shell money, dug
from the Siltway's beds, the only money left since the kingdom's coins drowned with it. He would
tell you anything he knows if you asked, and you never need to. His kindness is never said aloud
("Murk is not a charity. Murk is barely a toad."); it shows in prices always just within reach, in
fish let go, and in a fire kept banked for four hundred years. Some of his lines: "Let one go and
the water remembers." "Nothing? The water keeps its own counsel. Go again." And, after the ending,
"Warm in here. Don't tell anyone."

4.4 THE OLD TOADS, MURK'S KIN (PLANNED)
---------------------------------------

A few very old toads live quietly in Lanternroot Lagoon's stilt village, never seen whole and
never speaking. You see only their signs: lanterns lit at every dusk, one sage green among the
amber; scraps on the offering posts for the Votive Shark; a moored barge; a smudge of pipe smoke;
a porch bell answering the bell on your rod. Out of old habit they still drop scraps from the
Kitchen Stair for Emberbelly, never enough. They do not trade. "My lot live there. We don't talk."

4.5 THE FAR-WATER WICKS: DIP, GLIM, LEAN AND FARTHING (PLANNED)
---------------------------------------------------------------

A few Wicks woke long ago, went out on the water and never came home. Nothing called them: a
fisher stays out while the fish rise, and home had no one awake in it. They are not sad. They tell
you where the fish are because a window candle was set in a window so that whoever was out on the
water could find the way, and telling another small fisher where the fish lie is the same light.
They are fishers, never keepers, guides, traders or rivals; they let every fish go, talk only of
fish, water and themselves, and are never named on screen. Glim, Lean and Farthing woke by Murk's
stove in his first years in the cove and kept going down the road until each found the water it
would rather be on; Murk knows them ("Still out there, that lot? Good."). Dip he never met.

  - Dip (the old kingdom). It went down with its window into the Mossmere on the night of the
    fall, lay cold on the bottom a long while, and caught again where it lay, so it never knew
    there was a cove to go home to: the only cold candle shown to have caught again. Worn into a
    flat oval like a river stone, old-pearl wax, a snail shell on one shoulder, a still flame blue
    at the root; its raft is its own window's shutter. "I lay down there a long while." "My
    window's still under the Gate." / "A trout lives in it now."
  - Glim (the cold rim). Small and round, white wax crazed by the cold, a quilted hood of faded
    rose, a cold flame with a lavender edge, a sled-raft on bone runners. The stool, the lit
    lantern and the seven knots by the Rime Sound's fishing hole are Glim's. "Quiet up here. Sit,
    if you like."
  - Lean (the falls and the gate). The only tall one, honey-gold beeswax softened by the heat so
    that it leans toward whoever it talks to, on a raft of fishing baskets lashed together. It
    fishes for the joy of it and laughs at every fish, landed or lost. "Another one out! Oh,
    that's good."
  - Farthing (the green heart). The cheapest candle in the kingdom, made from the dregs of the
    orchard wax, and the one that went farthest: crooked wick, a lichen-grown scrap of sacking, a
    sunk barge's hatch cover for a raft with a small frog riding its edge. "All this way? Me too."

4.6 THE FIVE MET ON THE ROAD (PLANNED)
--------------------------------------

Five creatures of the road with something to say, each carrying a habit that outlived its reason
or a belief out of step with the world, each with a payoff that you cause. They come to you, say a
line or two, and go. None trades, guides, asks or takes anything.

  - Tiller (Sporewood Pools). A pond snail with a moss-furred ochre shell and a row of seedlings
    along its ridge, who climbed onto a floating meadow as a hatchling and never got off. He
    waters straight rows each night with an old spore-cap, because the drowned gardens were
    planted in rows: "Rows. Whoever was here before me did rows." His island is really the
    Drifting Grove, the water's legend. "Island's wandered off again. Happens." Land her and he
    rides her up, furious: "Who moved my island?" The next night: "Came back. Restless sort of
    island." Murk knew all along: "Snail's happy. Leave him be."
  - The Velvets (Pyrefall Gorge, then back north). In the kingdom's time moths came from all over
    the south to the great lantern, and the habit outlived it. A moth lives a few weeks, so the
    flight north takes generations; every daughter is called Velvet, and none has heard that the
    lantern fell. The first comes to your lantern at the temple fires: "Is this the great
    lantern?" / "No? Shame. It's lovely." At dawn she rests on the barge's rail: "Your toad keeps
    looking at me." Murk: "I wasn't going to." No Velvet ever dies: each settles at a small light
    while her daughter flies on ("Mother stopped at the gorge. Liked the fires."). The last
    reaches the Mossmere and finds only the chain and the stars where the lantern hung: "Where
    does it hang?" / "...Oh." / "Smaller than they said. Warm, though." She settles in one of the
    Gate's lit windows.
  - Dart (Rushlight Fen). A small blue-violet kingfisher who touched the fen's legend, the Long
    Thunder, when he was young and has dived at her all his life, knowing he could never lift her.
    "That rod's too big for you. Good." When you land her he waits on the barge's rail at dawn:
    "Was she heavy?" / "...Good." Then he fishes the fen's tiny fish by the basketful, laughing,
    letting every one go, as the people did. "Forgot this was fun."
  - Supper (the Salt Gate, then aboard). A small, bark-coloured night bird of the shipyard with a
    voice far too big for it. The yard's birds picked up the people's words sound by sound and
    taught their chicks, and four hundred years on the words still go off at the sounds that came
    with them, always in a bird's own call: "Mind your fingers." when a fish flaps on the planks,
    "Is that you?" at Murk's bell, "Home before dark." as the raft casts off. It lets itself into
    one of Murk's empty birdcages ("Don't look at me. It let itself in."), and Murk names it for
    its favourite phrase, "Supper's on." At the very end it says "Welcome home."
  - Flinch (Lampmoss Slough to the Inkwood). A young fruit bat, wings wrapped round itself like a
    cloak, lost on its first flight and hiding from the night ever since. It lands on your lantern
    pole and shakes: "Don't put it out." It rides your lantern home through the swamp, down the
    barge road ("Frogs are very loud. Is that normal?" / "That's normal.") and across the lagoon,
    a little braver each evening, sees the hearth stirred, and flies off into the dark with its
    colony: "It's not so bad. It's just night." For once the Wick is exactly what a window candle
    is for: a light for someone coming home.

4.7 THE PEOPLE OF THE LAMPLIT KINGDOM
-------------------------------------

The people were people like us: tall, warm-handed folk of the warm south, so tall a Wick stands
knee-high to them. They planted orchards and kept bees, made candles, fished at evening by the
basketful, laughing, and built every work the Wick sails through. They remembered their dead at
the Pallid Deep and carved their names on the Long Pier. No catastrophe killed them and nothing
hunted them. When the lantern fell they let the kingdom end and went back the way they had come.
In the orchard country the kingdom grew small, as kingdoms do once they stop: a village, then a
few houses, until the last household banked its hearth one night and did not come back to stir it.

The game never shows a face or a figure, and never will. On screen the people are only ever "the
kingdom", "the people", "somebody" or "the gardeners". They are present in what they left (doors
far too tall for a Wick, tools hung in rows, baskets by the hundred, a kettle and a chair on a
canal bed) and in what still misses them: a fen fish that "misses them", a lantern-jelly "still
looking for the hand", lantern-gourds still ripening, a night bird still saying their words.

4.8 OTHERS ONLY SPOKEN OF
-------------------------

  - The First Wick: the first window candle ever lit, in the Inkwood's stilt village. A story the
    Wicks tell, never a character, and not the flame every other light came from.
  - The cold candles: candles that sank on the night of the fall and still doze in the drowned
    towns and the deep. A cold candle can catch again some day; nothing says when.
  - The chart-makers: court surveyors, sea-road wardens, harbour-masters and barge pilots, the
    four hands of Murk's charts.


5. THE WATERS
=============

The Lamp Road starts at the Landing and runs through sixteen waters in four reaches, about 7,300
kilometres in all, 4,600 of them the Long Coast through the Fold. It only ever goes forward, and
each water is joined to the last by a real river, canal, lock stair or stretch of coast. Six of
the sixteen are rivers, taking turns with the lakes and seas.

Only the Mossmere is in the game now; the other fifteen are planned, and their names and lines are
the agreed designs. "Clue" is the landmark the camera frames on your first evening there; "Murk"
is his line when he sells its map.

THE LANDING (IN THE GAME NOW)
-----------------------------

A sea cave on the Cold Coast at the foot of the outer wall, directly under the Sluice Run's fall,
where the kingdom's sluice locks once met the sea. Its broken arch faces north into the dark, and
it has a real stone ceiling. The capital's candles came ashore here, and the Slow Hearth lay in
its cove until you towed it out. It is not home, but it stays on the Chart as the road's first
mark.

REACH I: THE OLD KINGDOM (ACT I, WHAT FELL)
-------------------------------------------

Up one river and one canal into the stone highland. Drawn in the court surveyors' fine ink.

1. THE MOSSMERE (IN THE GAME NOW)
---------------------------------

A lake: "Shallows of the Sunken Gate." Up the Sluice Run and its locks from the Landing.

Mood: the starlit moat. Violet-dusk water under lavender mist, pale drowned stone under broken
vaulting, dun-ochre reeds and lily pads, candle-amber windows, and green only as a trace. In the
game's words: "The outer moat of the Lamplit Kingdom, drowned to the arches. Moss drinks the old
stone and roots hang from the vault like the beards of sleeping giants. The fish here are
forgiving."

Drowned works: the capital's moat, its Gate and its lower city. The great lantern hung 44 metres
in front of the Gate, and eight candle slits are still lit in its face. Clue: the chain on the
Toppled Bridge, ending in one link worn thin. Murk has no line: the Mossmere is yours from the
start.

Places: the Lily Maze (day), Under the Span (old water), the Buoy Lights (night), where two rows
of harbour lanterns still burn under the water, and the Bell Pool (the crown), with the Sluice
Run, the Cupped Hands and the Gatehouse Door on later sheets. (planned) A tale will one day show
the Low Windows, the Gate's lowest row of windows just under the water, where one empty iron
candle-ring was Dip's.

Legend: the Belled Matriarch, a vast catfish with a mouth as wide as a door and whiskers as long
as bell-ropes. "A harbour bell grew into her skull when she was young. Her scales have gone the
colour of tarnished bronze, and she has tolled for the drowned ever since." Giants: the
Thousandcast Pike under the Span and the Hullkiss Lamprey at the Buoy Lights. The full list of its
fish is in section 6.

2. SPOREWOOD POOLS (PLANNED)
----------------------------

A warm volcanic lake: "A Garden That Forgot the Sun." Up the Lockwater through one lock.

Mood: the ember garden, and the road's first moss. Giant mushroom caps over warm, sweet water,
olive moss round their edges, curled ferns, a violet-magenta haze and gold spores drifting like
embers. Warm vents keep it mild, and the first few frogs live here. Drowned works: the gardens,
where the fungi kept growing after the gardeners stopped. Clue: the gardeners' tools hung in rows
on the caps, and a bee skep carved over a garden door, the first sign of where the candles' wax
came from. Murk: "Gardeners. Tidy to the last. Mind the spores."

Legend: the Drifting Grove, a giant ray with a whole meadow on her back. "When she dives, it
blooms beneath the water, where no one sees." Tiller the snail lives on that meadow. Giants: the
Harpstring Eel ("It hums one note along the line. You feel it in your teeth, if you have teeth."),
the Emberbrood Snakehead and the Festival Puffer ("the last paper lantern of a festival nobody
finished").

3. GLASSWATER VAULT (PLANNED)
-----------------------------

A deep, clear, cold lake in granite and crystal, the head of the Lockwater.

Mood: sapphire water, lilac crystal and the starriest sky in the kingdom, with cold moss on the
monoliths and lichen hanging in beards from the chandelier chains. "Here the kingdom poured its
light into crystal and shelved it like books. The water is cold and clear as a held breath." Its
great chandelier fell the same night as the lantern. The Stilled Cascade on its shore is the
Summit Cut's lock flight, dry since the Cut's gates jammed. Clue: the Fallen Chandelier, still lit
under the water. Murk: "They kept light on shelves there. Like jam."

Legend: the Glass Sovereign, a great sturgeon. "Each scale is a window with no one behind it."
Giants: the Prismdrop Sturgeon ("It leaps to see whether the sky came back."), the Shardspine Eel
and the Wolfwake Trout. Of note: the Chime Jelly, "the only thing in the Vault still keeping
time".

4. THE PALLID DEEP (PLANNED)
----------------------------

The highest and deepest lake of the kingdom, whose floor, the Bottomless, lies below sea level.
Over the Summit Cut, which crosses the divide.

Mood: the requiem garden. Near-black water, bone terraces under pale sage moss, ivy up great ribs,
cedar spires, moonflowers that open at night, amber memorial candles. "Moss has kept the vigil the
candles could not." Drowned works: the kingdom's one quiet place of remembrance, with the Long
Pier of Names ("Every name on the pier was somebody's.") and thirty drifting remembrance rafts.
This is the one water where death lives in the story, and it is gentle. Clue: the Kneeling West
and the Kneeling East, two statues whose faces have weathered away, holding out a dark lantern.
Murk: "Down there's the oldest thing I know. Be polite."

Legend: the Lantern-Swallower, eight and a half metres of deep shark, older than the kingdom, the
ember of the great lantern still glowing through her six gills. Landing her is a big fish landed,
not a reveal, and afterwards a note appears on the Bottomless sheet: "She was under the lantern
every evening. Then she wasn't." Of note: the Lampwarden Angler ("The last lamp of the kingdom
hangs from its jaw. It is still waiting for someone to come home.") and the Slipknot Eel, which
"ties itself in a knot to remember something, and has forgotten what".

REACH II: THE COLD RIM (ACT II, THE COLD ROAD)
----------------------------------------------

Out to the sea and south down the fjord coast to the end of the range. Drawn in the sea-road
wardens' lamp marks.

5. THE RIME SOUND (PLANNED)
---------------------------

A fjord half a mile deep, down the Fallway through the Spine Gap: the first water outside the
walls, with no roof at all.

Mood: rain that never quite stops, moss-hung spruce from cliff top to tideline, white waterfalls
down both walls; only at the head, a glacier front and floe ice. Frozen bell-buoys mark the
kingdom's sea road out. Clue: the Lantern Hole, a lit lantern on the ice by one fishing hole, a
tiny stool, and a line of seven knots frozen on the ice. Someone small fishes here (it is Glim).
Murk: "Someone fishes the ice up there. Not me."

Legend: the Long Winter. "It was old when the kingdom was built. It has not hurried once since."
Of note: the Icedoor Flounder ("Knock, and it comes up swinging."), the Bellrope Cod ("Pull, and
something far below pulls back."), and the Coalwing Skate, "black on both sides, like a letter
nobody has written yet". A tale may one day show the Ice Foot, a pool of fresh meltwater under an
arch of old blue ice.

6. THE THIN WATER (PLANNED)
---------------------------

A braided river and a side water, up its own sand delta from the Sound.

Mood: below the broken old weir the river spreads wide and thin over pale sand, a hand deep and
always sliding, with floating islands of reed and willow that turn slowly past the raft at night,
fireflies and all. The sand is gold by day, pink at dusk, lavender by moonlight. Clue, a greeting:
on your first evening only, the sluice lamp at the weir brightens once as you pass. Murk: "That
lamp's never gone out. Don't ask me why."

Legend: the Buried Moon, a great round ray. "A moon that comes down to see the water and stays for
the sand. Nobody can talk her back." Of note: Weirflame, which "jumps the weir every dusk to see
if upstream is still there. It is. It checks again."

7. THE LAST LIGHT (PLANNED)
---------------------------

Open sea round the Lamp Rock, out over the Sound's sill on the cold current.

Mood: a high green island cliff with seabirds on every ledge, a long violet swell under the stars,
and the lighthouse's amber beam turning over it. At the Drift Line two currents meet in a floating
meadow of weed and wreckage. Far off a higher island stands in cloud, and nothing beyond it is
drawn: beyond lies the Far Sea. Drowned works: the kingdom's last lighthouse. Clue, a greeting: on
your first evening, the beam pauses on the raft for one beat, then turns on. Murk: "It turns for
ships that left. It's polite like that."

Legend: the Jetsam Shark. "It has swallowed half the kingdom's lost things, and kept them all." Of
note: the Unlit Lance, never landed: "Everyone has hooked it once. In every telling it is a little
longer."

8. PYREFALL GORGE (PLANNED)
---------------------------

A snowmelt gorge and the second side water, where the mountains come down to the sea.

Mood: a milk-pale river out of the snow, never quiet, rapids between still pools, pines and
hanging forest on the walls, the greenest in the north. Temple stairs walk down into the water, a
floating pyre gutters on every step, and ash drifts through the dusk. Drowned works: the snow
temple at the end of the cold road, where travellers lit a lamp before the long voyage south and
left a coin on the water for luck at the Coin Run. The fires still burn unfed. Clue: rows of
burnt-down travellers' tapers on the Alms Steps, and in one niche a small, fat window candle,
still lit. A candle lit to see someone off burns down; a candle lit for someone coming home waits.
Murk: "Temple fires. Nobody feeds them. They go anyway."

Legend: the Ashen Guest. "Uninvited to every funeral. Never once absent." Of note: the Pyregold
Carp ("Every scale is a coin someone left on the water. It kept them all."), the Unfinished, the
gorge's one that got away, and the Stone Lodger, a giant salamander that "has rented the same rock
for a hundred years and never paid". The first Velvet comes to your lantern here.

REACH III: THE FALLS AND THE GATE (ACT III, WHERE THEY TURNED)
--------------------------------------------------------------

Down the Long Coast, up the Great Step and down to the sea-gate. Drawn in the harbour-masters'
soundings.

9. THE WAXEN REEF (PLANNED)
---------------------------

A coral reef and the first warm water, at the end of the longest leg: 4,600 kilometres down the
Long Coast, until a headland called the Turn breaks the cold current.

Mood: the roofs and spires of a harbour town grown over with pale coral like run candle wax, the
highest ridges standing as small green keys of palm; at night the polyps open as thousands of
small amber flames. Drowned works: the kingdom's west port, won from the sea behind a mole that
went unkept; ordinary life lies under the reef, Lamp Row, chimneys, a spire. Clue: doors and steps
far too tall for a Wick. The people were tall, and they lived well here. Murk: "Warm water. You'll
hate it. Then you won't."

Legend: the Waxen Abbot. "It opens its mouth and the reef leans in." Of note: the Cabin Grouper,
which "has moved into the captain's cabin and changed nothing", and the Paper-Lantern Jelly: "A
lantern somebody let go of. It is still looking for the hand."

10. THE THUNDER STAIR (PLANNED)
-------------------------------

Cataracts where the river Rush comes off the Great Step. The reef town and the stair were one
port.

Mood: the kingdom's lock stair is broken, and a great river falls down it, squeezing through the
Throat into the Cauldron. It never rains here; everything green lives on the spray, and heat
lightning flickers in violet cloud. At night a pale moonbow stands in the spray. Clue, a greeting:
on your first evening, the two tower lamps blink once together, as the lock-keepers did for
barges. Murk: "Two lamps on the towers. Wave. They like that."

Legend: the Last Lock. "Thirty-two teeth and none of them hidden. Nothing passes the lock." Of
note: the Lampeye Perch ("Its eyes keep the light after the lamp has gone.") and the Soapthief,
which eats candle ends, so "the Wick keeps its feet out of the water here".

11. RUSHLIGHT FEN (PLANNED)
---------------------------

An inland reed delta on the dry Terrace above the great dam, reached by the towers' haul-way and
the dam's barge lock.

Mood: crystal channels over white sand between walls of papyrus whose seed heads glow amber like
rushlights, blue-violet lilies in the still lanes, reed frogs piping, and the pale dam with its
one amber lamp. Drowned works: the reservoir behind a dam that still holds. Fishing was a joy
here, by the basketful. The kingdom was not a sad place; it was a good one that ended. Clue:
fishing baskets stacked by the hundred on the dam's steps, big ones and very small ones. Murk:
"People laughed there once. Fish remember."

Legend: the Long Thunder. "You feel it before you hear it." Dart the kingfisher has dived at her
all his life. Of note: the Basket Fry ("It likes to be caught by the basketful, by people who are
laughing. It misses them.") and Packtooth: "They hunt as fifty and are caught as one. That is the
sad part."

12. THE SALT GATE (PLANNED)
---------------------------

A tidal river mouth, the kingdom's great sea-gate, down the Sweetwater across the Terrace.

Mood: the river and the sea argue every evening; as night falls the reach stands still for one
breath, then the tide turns and pushes back in. Mangroves on both banks, strangler figs on the old
quays, the stump of the harbour light still burning. Drowned works: the sea-gate, left open, and a
shipyard of unfinished hulls that simply stopped. Here the people left the sea road and turned
inland. Clue: the unfinished hulls on the slipway, and the canal's lock leading off inland, the
way they went. Murk: "Shipyard. Somebody's still at it."

Legend: the Old Shipwright, who works the slipway's timbers. "Still cutting ribs for a fleet that
will never sail." After you land him, a note on the sheet: "He is not sad. He is busy." Of note:
the Dusk Drummer, which "drums for the tide every evening. The tide comes. It takes the credit.",
and the Mudlark Crab, which "steals your bait, then comes up to see who to thank". Supper the
night bird lives in the yard.

REACH IV: THE GREEN HEART (ACT IV, THE WAY BACK)
------------------------------------------------

Into the wet basin, down the barge road and through the lagoon into the forest. Drawn in the barge
pilots' strip maps, and at the very end in the people's own orchard plan.

13. LAMPMOSS SLOUGH (PLANNED)
-----------------------------

A swamp, the Straight Canal's summit pound, the first country the eastern rains reach.

Mood: a millstream that barely moves under swamp trees hung with long curtains of grey-green
lamp-moss, duckweed in the still corners, fireflies caught in the moss like sparks, and a log that
is not a log. The first frog chorus, heard and not seen. Clue: a kettle, a chair, a lantern and a
chest of tools on the canal bed below the lock gate, dropped over the side on the way through, as
things are. Murk: "Last barge through there went south. I poled it."

Legend: the Last Sentry. "Still at his post in full mail. Nobody told him the kingdom drowned." Of
note: Old Portcullis in its den in the drowned lock gate ("It has not let anyone through since the
Fall.") and the Lampspot Snakehead, which "carries one small lamp on its tail, for whoever is
following". Flinch the young bat hides from the night here.

14. THE SILTWAY (PLANNED)
-------------------------

The great brown barge road, some 720 kilometres east from the canal.

Mood: a river so wide the far bank is a green line, slow and the colour of candle tallow, with
floating meadows rising and falling on the current and frogs everywhere. Sunk barges and pale
mile-posts mark the channel, and one ferry under the water still has its lamp lit. At the Seam the
black forest river pours in beside the brown and will not mix: the next promise. At the great bend
the Long Hole goes down, where Murk's last barge sank. Clue: the white stone mile-posts, counting
down the way you are going. Murk: "Loud, isn't it? That's what alive sounds like."

Legend: the Barge-Shadow. "It follows the last barge down the road. The barge sank long ago. It
follows anyway." Afterwards a note in Murk's own hand: "Sank under me at the bend. I swam." Of
note: the Sternlamp Catfish, keeping a red stern lamp lit "as barges do", and the Snufferjaw, with
two long teeth "shaped for putting out small lights. It looks at the Wick kindly."

15. LANTERNROOT LAGOON (PLANNED)
--------------------------------

A brackish mangrove lagoon on the eastern sea, where the orchard met the sea and the barge road
ended.

Mood: a mangrove forest on arched roots in glass-calm water, stilt houses hanging paper lanterns
low, and whole trees of fireflies flashing together like a slow heartbeat. The people went on from
here up the Monsoon Cut into the forest. Murk's kin live in the stilt village, unseen. Clue, a
greeting: as you pass the Stilt Shrine on your first evening, the lanterns lower one by one, one
sage green among the amber, and a porch bell answers the tiny bell on your rod. Murk: "My lot live
there. We don't talk."

Legend: the Silver Regent. "She rolls at the surface to sip the air, and the whole lagoon hears
her sigh." Of note: the Votive Shark, fed by the village ("It is not sure it likes being loved."),
whose oldest is first met as a note on a lost line: "It took the line and the hook and the
prayer."

16. THE INKWOOD (PLANNED)
-------------------------

A blackwater forest river, the road's end: the same black river first seen pouring in at the Seam.

Mood: the forest drank its orchards whole. A black river, clear as tea in a cup, winds between
trunks standing waist-deep under a canopy that closes overhead, so the forest is there twice in
the mirror. Lantern-gourds the people planted still ripen and drop in with a plop; bees hum, frogs
roar, and creeks slip away to hidden ponds where something big breathes. Drowned works: the
orchard forest where the kingdom began and ended, a stilt village kitchen, and window niches
ringed with old wax, where the first window candles ever lit once stood. Clue: at the top of the
Kitchen Stair, a stone hearth raked over with ash and never stirred again; and far off in the
Breathing Mere's mist, a long warm breath rising. Murk: "Last chart there is. Oldest, too."

Legend: the Banked Fire, the final dream. "It must come up for air, like any fire. It burns low
and waits for morning." Of note: Emberbelly ("It still waits under the kitchen steps for scraps.
It is owed a hundred years of seconds."), the Nightcreek Hound ("It is not angry. It is
thorough."), and Little Logjaw, a small caiman: "Pairs of amber eyes sit at the edge of the
lantern light every night. This one came closer."


6. THE FISH
===========

6.1 FISH ARE FISH
-----------------

The fish of Gloomwater are original creatures of the drowned kingdom: 164 of them over the sixteen
waters, plus a handful of curiosities that are not fish at all. They glow because deep water and
the dark make fish glow. The giants are simply very old. The legends are fish with habits the
people noticed and named. Nothing in the water is made of what was lost, no fish is cursed, and no
fish carries the plot. To Murk a fish is news: where it was, how big, at what hour. He pays for
the story and lets every fish go before dawn, which is why any fish, even a legend, can be met
again.

Sizes are told in pounds and ounces. The line shows how big a fish is, never what it is, until it
breaks the surface.

6.2 THE SIZE CLASSES, FROM SMALL TO ANCIENT (IN THE GAME NOW)
-------------------------------------------------------------

The same ladder runs for every kind of fish, so a Prize minnow and a Prize pike are equally lucky
catches for their kind.

  - Small. A little one. "Small ones make good bait": press and hold Let go and the Wick cuts it
    into strips for the hunters.
  - Keeper. A fish worth keeping, the size most fish run.
  - Prize. A good fish. Its card is inked with a stamp, and a soft chime sounds.
  - Trophy. A fine fish, pressed with an oxblood wax seal. "Now that's a fish."
  - Elder. A great old fish of its kind, sealed in black wax with a ribbon and given a name of its
    own in the old fishers' way, such as "Granny Bellwhisker" or "Old Chainjaw". When an Elder
    takes your bait, your heart races, and two words come low on the screen: "An Elder."
  - Ancient. The rarest of all: an Elder so old that a gold thread runs through its seal. The
    heartbeat is slower and deeper, and the words are "Something ancient." An Ancient is too heavy
    to lift, so it hangs from the rod.

Legends are measured apart: each first landing is fixed, and each time you land her again she has
grown a little, until she is fully grown.

6.3 GIANTS AND LEGENDS
----------------------

Each water has a few giants, 49 on the road: the biggest, rarest and hardest fish of their waters,
each living at one named place and keeping its own hours. A giant is never Small, and on clear
nights its back may be glimpsed turning in the mist of a place you have not opened. A giant that
gets away becomes a marked fish at its place, and your next hook there holds it a little better,
once.

Each water also has one legend in its deepest pool, the crown: sixteen in all, many of them older
than the kingdom (they are named in section 5). A legend takes only her own bait, one try at a
time, and landing one is never certain. When you land one, its stone in the barge's sill is gilded
and its print hangs along the cabin's eaves, so home slowly fills with the road's great fish; the
Lantern-Swallower and the Banked Fire, at the road's two ends, hang facing each other. The longest
fish in the game is the Lantern-Swallower. The last legend, the Banked Fire, is the most valuable
and lives in the most beautiful pool, but several legends before her are bigger, among them the
cold road's two great sharks and the Old Shipwright.

6.4 THE MOSSMERE'S FISH, IN FULL (IN THE GAME NOW)
--------------------------------------------------

  - Dusk Minnow: "Caught on a scrap of silver paper. It still thinks that was a fair trade."
  - Mossback Perch: "Wears a small garden on its back and takes the worm anyway."
  - Gutter Crayfish: "It hoards drowned buttons in its burrow. No one knows whose coat it is
    trying to finish."
  - Likely Chub: "It looks like a chub. Nobody has checked."
  - Overbold Bass: "Attacks lures its own size. It has never once won."
  - Rustfin Sucker: "Kisses every stone on the bottom, in case one of them is home."
  - Lampsipper: moth-wing spots and a weakness for lanterns. "It drinks the light off the water
    like milk."
  - Weeping Carp: "They have never once said they were not mourning."
  - Candlefin Koi: "Its fins burn without heat. More than one Wick has mistaken it for kin, and
    leaned too far."
  - Milkeye Perch: "Eyes like two spilt saucers of milk. It sees you perfectly well."
  - Kissmark Trout: "Star-spotted, and wearing one round scar where something let go."
  - Reedjaw Pike: "Lies in the reeds until it forgets it is not one."
  - Gloam Eel (night, Lumen Moss only): "Crossed an ocean to be here, and will not say what for."
  - Bridgechain Sturgeon (Under the Span): "The Mossmere's longest fish, and it knows it."
  - Thousandcast Pike (giant): "Follows the lure to the raft, looks at you, and leaves. Ten
    thousand times."
  - Hullkiss Lamprey (giant): "It only wants to hold on to something. Anything. You will do."
  - The Belled Matriarch (legend). Her bait is the Heartmoss Ball: "For the Belled Matriarch. One
    try."

The first twelve are the Mossmere's ordinary fish, the list you complete to earn the way onward.

6.5 CURIOSITIES, AND THE ONES THAT GOT AWAY
-------------------------------------------

A few catches are not fish and are still only animals: the Stone Lodger and the Potlid Turtle in
Pyrefall Gorge, the Mudlark Crab at the Salt Gate, the Paper-Lantern Jelly on the reef, the
Guttering Squid at the Last Light ("It goes pink, then white, then pink, like a candle
deciding."), and Little Logjaw in the Inkwood. Some are never landed at all: the Unlit Lance, the
Unfinished, and the Long Coil, only glimpsed: "Something long went under the raft. It did not
stop."

In the game now a lost fish is never named; you only know something big was there. (planned) A
short line will say what gear would have held it, such as "Too big for the Bent Spoon." or "It
wants a better lure.", and Murk will say once: "Lost a big one? Better gear holds them." Only
Ashwick's gift brings a lost fish straight back: once, tired, and never a giant or a legend.

6.6 THE SECRET FISH (PLANNED)
-----------------------------

In every water one fish is on no page and never bites until a far-water Wick has told you of it;
then it waits as a hatched shape with a small inked knot. Each is a different kind of fish from
the last. Three are named: the Casement Trout, which keeps to the Gate's drowned Low Windows "and
watches the water, as if it were waiting for someone"; the Snowmelt Cod at the Rime Sound's Ice
Foot; and the Hideaway Ray of Lanternroot's flats, which "lies under the flats with only its eyes
out, and has outwaited every tide so far". The other thirteen are working drafts. A secret is
never needed for anything.


7. THINGS FROM THE WATER
========================

7.1 SNAGS AND FINDS (IN THE GAME NOW, AT THE MOSSMERE)
------------------------------------------------------

Now and then the float slides under slowly and stays under, and the rod bows steadily: a snag. The
line comes up heavy, with no fight in it, and it is never lost. The Wick holds the thing up to the
light, dripping: a spoon, a coin, a lamp-glass, a bell, a purse that sank with the kingdom's
works. Keep it and it takes a slot like a fish; let it go and it drops back.

A find has no power and opens nothing. Murk buys one for the reason he buys a fish: a thing that
was somebody's is news of the road. "The water keeps what it takes. Mostly." Nothing human ever
comes up (no face, figure, name plate or bones), and nothing left on purpose: never the lagoon's
offerings, the forest's wax rings, or a story's clue.

There are three kinds:

  - Treasures, which Murk buys. Each water has five, from a plain one to its jackpot, somebody's
    whole fortune, which goes on the scale last and alone. "Somebody's whole fortune. Now it's
    yours."
  - Barge parts, fittings the Slow Hearth wears for good, each doing one small thing at its own
    water: an extra order on Murk's board, one more good bite each evening, or a free tin of bait.
    "That'll fit her. I'll see to it by morning." Fitting the barge with the road's own fittings
    is the household gathering the road as it goes, as the sill gathers its stones.
  - Looks, things to wear: one each for the Wick, the raft and the barge at every water.

7.2 THE MOSSMERE'S ELEVEN FINDS (IN THE GAME NOW)
-------------------------------------------------

  - Bent Tin Spoon (treasure): "Someone ate every meal with this, slowly."
  - Brass Door-Ring (treasure): "Off a door four Wicks tall."
  - Salt-Glass Lens (treasure): "Fitted to a lantern long since drowned."
  - Two-Headed Coin (treasure): "Lucky, they said. Heads every time."
  - The Toll-Keeper's Purse (the jackpot): "Full. Nobody came to collect."
  - Rust Buoy-Bell (part, the bow post): "It rang the boats home once." It rings as the raft ties
    up at dawn, and Murk's hand bell answers.
  - Gate Lamp-Glass (part, the cabin windows): "Amber, from the Gate's drowned lamps." The
    Mossmere's fish still know those lamps, and give one more good bite.
  - Moat Bollard (part, the stern deck): "Carved stone. Boats tied here for centuries." Mire Worms
    keep in the moss of its crown.
  - Knotted Rod Tassel (look, the Wick's rod): "Every knot, a fish someone was proud of."
  - Toll-Tower Vane (look, the raft's stern post), a little iron fish: "It still points into the
    wind."
  - Night-Lily Window Boxes (look, the barge's windows): "A window box, still flowering."

7.3 THE OTHER WATERS' FINDS (PLANNED)
-------------------------------------

Each water's set comes from its own drowned works. Their jackpots: the Seed-Keeper's Casket
(Sporewood), a Chandelier Heart-Drop (Glasswater), the Belfry's Silver Bell-Tongue (the Pallid
Deep), a Warden's Silver Compass (the Rime Sound), the Weir-Warden's Ring (the Thin Water), a
Lighthouse Lens-Prism (the Last Light), a Gold Votive Bowl (Pyrefall Gorge), a Coral-Crusted
Strongbox (the Waxen Reef), the Lock-Keeper's Brass Horn (the Thunder Stair), the Fen Match Cup,
which somebody won, laughing (Rushlight Fen), a Gilt Stern-Lamp (the Salt Gate), a Barge-Master's
Strongbox (Lampmoss Slough), a Brass Ferry-Lamp (the Siltway), a Pearl from the Orchard's Edge
(Lanternroot Lagoon), and the Beekeeper's Gold Smoker (the Inkwood), whose parts include a hive
for the barge's roof, so that bees hum at home.

7.4 HOME: THE SLOW HEARTH AND WHAT FILLS IT
-------------------------------------------

The Slow Hearth is a broad, low hull about twelve metres long, with a timber cabin of small square
windows, a stove pipe and a patched awning over a flat roof. The Chart's table stands under the
awning, the Journal lies on a lectern by the door, Murk's barge lies alongside and the raft is
tied at the stern. The cabin door's sill carries sixteen carved stones, one for each water, each
with its glyph and a one-word mark for its leg (river, lock, canal, coast, sea, cut), so the sill
reads as the journey: inked when home first reaches a water, gilded when its legend is landed. The
stones are not magic.

Home fills as you go (the first of it is in the game now): Murk's order board, lights along the
rails, a wall of your best fish prints, the legends' prints along the eaves, moss over the roof,
and the parts and looks you bring up from the water. From out on the water, home is warm windows
across the dark.

(planned) You will be able to step aboard and look around inside, at a person's eye height: the
deck, the hearth, the long wall, the rail, a bench, the roof, and a balcony at the stern to look
out from and fish from at dawn.

(planned) The Well. A round glass hatch amidships opens onto the barge's old wet well, where barge
families kept their catch alive. A fish worth keeping can go there instead of to Murk. Below it
you drift as a slow eye along an underwater road of lamp-stones, one room for each water the barge
has moored at, each built from that water's drowned works, your fish swimming at the size you
caught them. Each water's finds will gain pieces to grow and dress its room.

7.5 GEAR, FROM THE BENT SPOON ONWARD
------------------------------------

Everything else comes from Murk's shelf, sold from his own printed catalogue: rods, lines and
reels; lures (the Feather Fly, the Glimmer Spoon, the Bone Jig, and the Harbour-Bell Spinner "for
the Mossmere's giants"); tins of bait (Mire Worms, "Nearly every fish takes a worm.", and Lumen
Moss); and Cut Strip, "Meat, for the hunters", cut from a small fish for free. (in the game now)

(planned) A new save will begin with a small kit: a tin of Mire Worms and the Bent Spoon, "a
kitchen spoon Murk bent and holed", which takes perch, bass, chub and trout but never a pike. A
bare hook will bite slowly and bring only the small common fish.

7.6 LOST TACKLE, NOTEBOOKS AND THE SECRET STREAM
------------------------------------------------

Not yet written. No design in the canon describes lost tackle to be found, notebooks left by other
fishers, or a secret stream. The nearest things it has are the got-away line (planned, section
6.5); the far-water Wicks' tales, which leave a small inked knot on a Journal page or the Chart
(planned); and the four told places (planned): the Low Windows under the Gate, the Ice Foot at the
Rime Sound, the Ribs of an unfinished hull at the Salt Gate that only the night tide fills, and
the Hive Hollow in the Inkwood, a drowned orchard tree with wild bees in its crown. Each is water
that the drowning, or the long years since, made after the chart-makers drew their last sheet.
Anything more must be written and agreed before it becomes part of the story.


8. A TIMELINE AND A SHORT GLOSSARY
==================================

8.1 TIMELINE
------------

  - Before the kingdom. The Long Winter, the Lantern-Swallower and many of the great fish are
    already old.
  - The beginning. The people live in the Orchard Basin, keep bees, make candles, and light the
    first window candle ever lit, in a stilt village of the Inkwood.
  - Many generations. The people go north along the water roads after the big fish and the long
    dusks, building and keeping a lamp at every water they settle. The old toads trade along the
    road.
  - The kingdom's height. The capital stands on the Mossmere with the great lantern over its lake.
    The kingdom holds its water back along the whole road, and every house sets a window candle.
    Murk is the barge-trader of the road.
  - The fall, about four hundred years ago. The lantern's last link wears through. The lantern
    falls, the sluice drops shut, the Lantern-Swallower takes the ember, and the Mossmere rises
    over the city and spills out as the Sluice Run. The window candles wash down to the Landing.
    Dip goes down with its window. Glasswater's chandelier falls the same night.
  - The going back. The people hang no second lantern and go back south the way they came. Murk
    poles their last barge; it sinks at the great bend, and he swims.
  - The years after. The Lantern-Swallower goes up to the Bottomless while the Summit Cut stands
    open; later its gates jam and the Stilled Cascade stops.
  - Murk's return. He comes back up the road alone, carries the candles aboard the Slow Hearth in
    the cove, and banks his fire.
  - His first years in the cove. Glim, Lean and Farthing wake by his stove and go out down the
    road, and stay. On the moat's bottom, Dip catches again.
  - The long evening. Over four centuries the works drown one by one: the weir, the lock stair,
    the harbour's mole, the forest dikes, the delta's edge. Forgotten waters sink into mist. In
    the orchard country the kingdom grows small, until the last household banks its hearth at the
    top of the Kitchen Stair and never comes back to stir it. Moths keep flying north to a lantern
    that is gone; the shipyard's birds keep saying the people's words. Now and then a candle by
    the stove wakes, fishes the near waters, and dozes again.
  - Now. You wake, tow the house up to the Mossmere, and fish. (in the game now)
  - Then the road: fifteen more waters, the meetings on the way, the Inkwood, and "The Hearth
    Stirred". (planned)

8.2 GLOSSARY
------------

  - The Waxwold: the one continent, "the wax forest". The Fold is its long empty middle.
  - The Lamplit Kingdom: the people's kingdom along the road. On screen, only ever "the kingdom".
  - The long evening: the folk name for the four hundred years since. A saying, not a sky.
  - The Lamp Road: the sixteen-water road, read as its landing lamps.
  - A reach: a quarter of the road: the old kingdom, the cold rim, the falls and the gate, the
    green heart.
  - A Wick: a living window candle of orchard-bee wax.
  - A far-water Wick: one of the four who stayed out on the water: Dip, Glim, Lean, Farthing.
  - The five met on the road: Tiller, the Velvets, Dart, Supper and Flinch.
  - The Slow Hearth: the old barge-house you tow from water to water; home.
  - Banked fire: a fire raked over to burn low until someone comes home with news of the road.
  - A kept lamp: a landing lamp still burning. It remembers the way.
  - The sill: the sixteen carved stones of the road in the cabin door sill.
  - The tally: Murk counting and buying the catch at first light. He writes each fish in his
    Ledger.
  - The Journal: the Wick's book of ink prints of every fish.
  - The Chart: the hand-drawn road chart that draws the continent as you go.
  - Clams: the old toads' shell money, and the only money left.
  - The mist: forgetting. A water hides in it until a chart is carried there.
  - The Gloom: the dark of night on cold water. Patient, not cruel. Never a creature.
  - A place: a named corner of a water, opened by its own map; the crown is the deepest, where the
    legend lives.
  - A giant, a legend: a very old, very big fish of one place; a water's one greatest named fish.
  - A snag, a find: a bite that brings up a thing; the thing itself, a treasure, a barge part or a
    look.
  - A tale, a secret, a told place: what a far-water Wick tells you; a fish on no page; a corner
    no chart drew.
  - The Seam: where black forest water meets the brown river and will not mix.
  - The Well: the barge's old wet well, where kept fish will live.
  - Keep Drifting: the one button after the ending.

    "You are still very small. You are still burning."
