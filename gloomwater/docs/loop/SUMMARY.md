# Gloomwater: the Full Hold, in one page

## Who we are

You are **the Wick**, a small living candle, one of the last of a drowned kingdom. You fish from a raft. Home is **the Landing**, a quiet cove where an old trader, **Murk**, keeps his barge. Murk buys every fish you bring home and sells you everything else.

## The loop in five steps

1. **Pick a water** you own a map for, and sail out for the evening.
2. **Fish.** You cast and strike; the rest is automatic. You see how big a fish is on the line, but not what it is until it comes up.
3. **Keep it or let it go.** The Wick holds the fish up, then a card shows its price. Keep puts it in your hold.
4. **Go home** when the hold is full (or sooner). Murk weighs the fish one by one and pays you in **clams**.
5. **Spend the clams**, then sail again.

A trip takes 2 to 6 minutes. Dusk falls after the first minute; night is when the big fish rise, and it lasts until you turn for home. There is no way to fail.

## The hold rule

- The hold starts with **3 slots** and grows to 8.
- **A kept fish stays kept.** You cannot swap it out later for a better one. When the hold is full, you cannot cast again.
- Each spot has **only a few good bites each evening**. After that it goes quiet and the fish run small. Going home brings it back. This stops "throw everything back and wait for a giant" from being the best way to play.
- **Letting a fish go is never wasted.** The spot gets its good bite back, and the next fish there run a little bigger.
- A big fish is always a chance, never a promise: half of all first trips bring home nothing bigger than an average fish.

## Maps: the only way to a new place

Nothing is found by exploring. A place you have not bought is **standing mist** on the water, and on clear nights you can see something big turning inside it. Buying its map lifts the mist for good. Every map's card shows one fish drawn life-size beside the Wick, so you know what you are paying for.

Your first lake, **the Mossmere**, is yours from the start. Its first maps are **the Lily Maze** (60, glowing koi), **Under the Span** (150, the Thousandcast Pike), **the Buoy Lights** (260, the Hullkiss Lamprey) and **the Bell Pool** (500, the Belled Matriarch). The next lake, **Sporewood Pools** (2,000), opens with two of its spots already clear. There are **16 waters and 55 maps** in all. A new lake is always better than the one before from its first evening, and every map is worth what it costs.

## Switched off for now

The night creatures and all fighting, weapons and levels; health, hunger and oil running out; death; long journeys of several days; the old rewards between runs (memories, relics, the shrine); tolls; Murk visiting in the middle of an evening. All of it is kept in the game and can be switched back on later.

## What gets built first, and after

1. **Three slots**: catch, keep or let go, a hold of three, home, Murk's sale, a bigger hold and better gear. Built first on today's fish, and tested hardest, because it changes how the game works underneath.
2. **The first new fish** of the first two lakes, and the second lake's map.
3. **The mist lifts**: spots, maps, the first giants and the first legend.
4. **Bait, lures, the raft** and Murk's orders.
5. **Home**: the cove, the opening scene, the very short first evening, home upgrades and fish prints.
6. **The rest of the first four lakes.**
7. **Twelve new waters**, four at a time: the rivers and marshes, the great rivers, then the sea.
8. **The extras**: the other four candles you can play, and small touches.
