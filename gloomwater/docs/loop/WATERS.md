# The Waters: the greening ramp and the rivers

*Binding with SPEC v7.1 (`docs/loop/SPEC.md` §5.2–§5.3). It answers the user's words of 14 November 2026: "I want the lakes to get progressively more and more lush and green. I also want some of the levels to be rivers similar to the rivers fishes in the [series]. As we unlock new lakes and rivers we need to slowly but progressively start getting more and more green and lush." Design only: no `src/` file changed with this document. Real places appear here as design notes only, like the roster's `inspiredBy`; nothing in this file's "modelled on" column, its section 4.1 or its section 9 ever ships. v7.1 revised it after three critics (the review log at the end).*

**The rule in one line.** The Mossmere keeps today's lavender night with green only as a trace. Every water you unlock after it is one visible step greener and lusher than the one before, slowly and steadily, until the last waters are deep green rainforest. The green arrives as **living things on surfaces** (moss, fern, reed, leaf, vine, root, weed), never as a colour laid over the air, the light, the water or the frame. The violet-dusk sky, the lavender moon, the clear sharp air of Clear Night and the candle's one warm light are the same on every water.

**The journey.** After the Pallid Deep the Wick crosses the kingdom's **cold rim**: a rainforest fjord under a glacier, a braided sand river, the open sea under the last lighthouse, a snowmelt gorge. Then **the falls and the gate**: a drowned reef town, a broken stair of cataracts, a papyrus fen above the great dam, the tidal sea-gate. Then **the green heart**: a swamp forest, the great brown barge river, a mangrove lagoon, and the blackwater forest at the centre of everything. Rivers and stills take turns from slot 5, so no two rivers come together; four of the six rivers lie in the second half of the run, and the three lushest river kinds come last.

**Why the order changed** (v6 → v7). In v6 the order ran fresh waters, then great rivers, then the sea and the far north last. That put the four waters with almost no land or plants (the Waxen Reef at 14, the Last Light at 15 with "no shore anywhere", and the Rime Sound at 16 with its sea ice) exactly where the user now wants the lushest green. Re-theming them into green places would have taken away their giants' homes: the Long Winter lives under the ice, the Lantern Hole is a hole in the ice, and the Jetsam Shark crosses the last of the light in open swell. So the cold and salt waters move early and the rainforest waters move late. Every fish keeps its water (ROSTER §3). **The cost:** the two largest legends (the Long Winter, 560 cm, and the Jetsam Shark, 480 cm) now come at hours 21–26, and the last legend (the Banked Fire, 325 cm) is not the biggest. SPEC §16 question 6 puts that trade to the user, with the other way (keep the cold waters late and re-theme them lush) beside it. v7.1 keeps the order and makes the two early salt waters honest steps up: the Rime Sound becomes a **temperate-rainforest fjord** (moss-hung spruce to the tideline, fern on every ledge, waterfalls, the ice only as a glacier front and floe ice at the head, so the Lantern Hole and the Long Winter keep their home), and the Last Light sets out from under a **near lighthouse island** with a turf-and-fern cliff that fills the near side of the view.

**How to read it.** §1 is the ramp: one row per water. §2 says each water in the game's voice. §3 is how green is done and what never changes. §4 is the rivers. §5 is the vegetation kit and its cost. §6 is the gentle repaint of the built waters 2–4. §7 is how the ramp is measured and gated. §8 is the colour script. §9 lists the real looks behind the ramp (design notes). §10 is the review log.

## 1. The ramp

**Lushness step** runs 0 (the Mossmere) to 15 (the Inkwood), one step per water, so it rises strictly with unlock order. The engine reads it as `lush = step / 15` (0 to 1). **Green share** is the target share of green (hue 65–160°) in the frame's chromatic weight (saturation × value, as `tools/hue-report.mjs` measures it), in per cent, for day, dusk and night, averaged over three fixed capture poses per water (the spawn, the day water, the old water) at 960×540 on **medium** (§7). The day targets rise **2.5 points a water**, from a trace to 38%; dusk is about 0.88 of day and night about 0.55 of day. Every number in the column is **measured** on the colour script's painted frames (§8), which meet every gate of §7 at once, so the column is known to be reachable without a cast, neon or a green sky. **What grows** lists the kinds and the number of plant instances on high (low is 0.4×; §5). The colour cells are **day · dusk · night**: the sky at the horizon (the zenith is the shared violet sky), the fog, the water's own colour (never green: green on the water comes only from reflections, pads, weed and duckweed), the **mid foliage tone** and the accent (flowers by day, the same under the rose dusk, fireflies or glow at night). The Mossmere's row is today's palette.

**The foliage track.** The mid tones are not picked one by one: they follow one smooth track, so a step reads as "greener", never as "a different green". By day the hue is 84 + 2.9 × step (87° to 128°, olive to deep green), the saturation min(cap − 0.04, 0.24 + 0.02 × step), where cap = min(0.62, 0.25 + 0.04 × step) is the neon cap of §3.2, and the value 0.38 + 0.01 × step. At dusk the hue is 16° warmer (the rose key turns lit greens olive-bronze), the saturation ×0.86 and the value ×0.95. At night the hue is 127 + 1.8 × step (to 154°: moonlit slate-greens), the saturation 0.16 + 0.008 × step and the value 0.27 + 0.009 × step. Each water's character comes from its kinds, its silhouettes, its accents and its `leafDark` / `leafHi` spread (§3.2), not from a jump in the mid tone. Hue and value never fall from one water to the next; `tools/loop-colour-script.py` lints it.

The green share by day climbs 2.5 points a water, from a trace to 38%; dusk holds about 0.88 of the day's share (lit greens turn olive-bronze under the rose key, shadows blue-green) and night about 0.55 (moonlit slate-greens with lavender rims, fireflies, glow-moss). v7's 85% and 60% could not be reached with any night palette that stays out of neon and teal (the critics measured 0–5% at night); these are the measured values. The steps are even on purpose: every water is visibly greener than the one before when the two stand side by side, and no step is a jump.

**The four built waters gain a little green too** (§6). The Mossmere does not change at all.

## 2. The waters, in the game's voice

These are the looks the build paints and the roster keeps (ROSTER §3, `roster.json` `look`). Kinds of water, never real places.

**1. The Mossmere** (lake · step 0). As built: the starlit moat. Violet-dusk water under lavender mist, pale stone, the Toppled Bridge and the old harbour's bell, dun-ochre reeds and pads, candle-amber windows. Green only as a trace. This is the look the user approved, and it stays exactly as it is.

**2. Sporewood Pools** (lake · step 5: LAKE2 §1A, the user's "more greenery than the moss mere"). The ember garden gone green. Two pools joined by a bent neck under a ragged window of sky framed by cap rims; moss in carpets over every bank and isle, ferns under the caps, bronze-green reeds and pale sedge on the Reed Flats, lily and leaf rafts in the coves, moss curtains hanging over the Veil and the cap trees' gills over the neck like strings. The haze stays violet-magenta, the water plum, the caps autumn-ochre with amber gills that lay pools of warm light on the water and the moss under them; the spores gold, cream and rose blossom by day, pale fireflies over the reeds at night. The Mother Cap stands 30 m over the East Pool. Green on every surface, never in the air.

**3. Glasswater Vault** (lake · step 2). Moss on the crystal. Cold green moss sits on the tops of the monoliths and the cliffs; tree ferns stand in the colonnade's broken arches; grey-green lichen hangs in beards from the chandelier chains. The sapphire water, the lilac crystal and the most starlight in the kingdom stay as they are. At night periwinkle glow-worm threads hang under the shelves.

**4. The Pallid Deep** (lake · step 3). The requiem garden. Moss has kept the vigil the candles could not: pale sage moss on the bone terraces and the cairns, ivy up four of the nine great ribs, dark cedar spires between the ruins of the funeral colonnade, moonflowers on the terraces that open at night, and pale jade glow-moss in the cracks after dark. The near-black water, the bone, the ink, the gold and the candles are unchanged.

**5. The Rime Sound** (sound · step 4). A drowned fjord where the rain never quite stops by day and the cloud lies on the walls. Moss-hung spruce stand from the cliff tops down to the tideline, every ledge is a fern garden, and white waterfalls hang down the walls on both sides. Only at the head does the cold hold: a pale glacier front, and floe ice across the last of the water, with one fishing hole and a lantern beside it: amber on lavender snow, and olive kelp swaying in the black water under the hole. Frozen bell-buoys, a breath of mist, long nebula-pink ribbons in the sky. The water is black-violet and half a mile deep. At night pale glow-moss lights the wet walls.

**6. The Thin Water** (river · step 5). Below a broken royal weir the river spreads wide and thin over pale sand: a hand deep over the bars, a pole deep in the channels between them, and always sliding. Islands of reed and floating meadow ride the current and catch on the bars. Willows grow on the old ones, and after dark one or two come loose and turn slowly past the raft, fireflies and all. By day the sand shows gold through the water, at dusk it holds the nebula pink, and by moonlight it is lavender, with rays lying on it like the shadows of moons. At the weir the whole river comes white over the sill under one amber sluice lamp, and something gold strikes in the foam.

**7. The Last Light** (open sea · step 6). Open sea, and the last lamp of the kingdom on its island. The raft sets out from under the Lamp Rock: a high green island cliff with a cap of turf and fern, wind-bent trees leaning out over the water, and seabirds on every ledge; it fills the near side of the view. Beyond it a long violet swell runs under the whole star field, and the lighthouse's amber beam turns over it. Where two currents meet lies the Drift Line: a floating meadow of olive and gold-green weed with wreckage caught in it, and things hunting under it. Far off a second, higher island stands in cloud, waterfalls white down its cliffs, and a line of reef breakers foams round the edge of the water the Wick may sail. At night the weed glows faintly where the swell turns it over.

**8. Pyrefall Gorge** (river · step 7). A cold river comes down out of the snow through a gorge of stone-white rock. Its water is milk-pale, clear to an arm's depth and then clouded with powdered stone, and it never stops talking. It pours over boulders and through one rapid after another, and between the rapids lie long still pools and beaches of pale sand. Temple stairs walk down into the water. On every step a floating pyre gutters candle amber, and ash drifts through the dusk. Green has taken the walls: dark pines on the rim, a hanging forest and stands of bamboo on the ledges, old terraces grown over with grass, and ferns and moss in every crack the spray can reach; the shelves of the pools are furred with weed. Something old turns in the pool below the last pyre, in the push of the current, and the ash above it circles the wrong way.

**9. The Waxen Reef** (reef · step 8). The roofs and spires of a drowned harbour town, grown over with pale coral like run candle wax. The ridges of the highest roofs stand above the water as small green keys of palm and sea-grape; vines hang down the spires; meadows of seagrass sway round the town in clear water, and the wax-coral roofs wear a fur of green algae. Sea fans in lavender and nebula pink. At night the polyps open as thousands of small amber flames below, and fireflies hang in the palms above. A wreck lies across the drop-off into violet dark.

**10. The Thunder Stair** (river · step 9). The kingdom's stair of locks is broken, and a great river falls down it. At the foot there is a broad calm reach, then the step pools, then the Throat, where the whole river squeezes through a gap three raft-lengths wide and comes out roaring into the Cauldron. The standing waves in the chutes stand still and never move on. Spray hangs over everything, and everything the spray touches is green: moss in sheets on the old lock stones, ferns in every crack, figs and palms leaning out over the pools, vines down the two lock towers whose amber signal lamps still burn. Heat lightning flickers in violet cloud, and it never rains. At night the spray turns silver, a pale moonbow stands in it, and the green walls drip.

**11. Rushlight Fen** (fen · step 10). A delta of bright channels above the kingdom's great dam: crystal water over white sand, between walls of green papyrus and reed whose seed heads still glow amber like rushlights. Islands of palm and fig stand in the channels; lilies open blue-violet in the still lanes. Pale birds by day, black lanes at dusk, and beyond them the pale stone dam with its one amber lamp and a black pool at its foot. At night fireflies drift over the reeds.

**12. The Salt Gate** (river, tidal · step 11). Here the river meets the sea at the kingdom's sea-gate, left open, and the two argue every evening. Through the day and the dusk the river runs out past the drowned sluices, brown and quick, and the mudflats come up shining. As night falls the whole reach stands still for one breath, then the tide turns and pushes back in. Mangroves crowd both banks on their stilts, roots slick and crowns a deep glossy green; strangler figs have the old quays; salt grass and samphire carpet the flats. A harbour stair goes down into the water, the stump of the harbour light still burns amber, and inside the gate a drowned shipyard keeps the ribs of unfinished hulls standing in rows. At the top of the reach, under a small fall, the water is sweet again. At night the mangroves fill with fireflies.

**13. Lampmoss Slough** (swamp · step 12). A drowned millstream that barely moves, under living swamp trees that stand on flared knees and wear long curtains of grey-green lamp-moss. Duckweed lies in soft yellow-green carpets in the still corners; ferns grow from every stump; nebula-pink lilies open on the pads. Violet-dusk water, lavender haze, fireflies caught in the moss like sparks. A straight stone canal, a broken weir, a drowned lock gate, and a log that is not a log.

**14. The Siltway** (river · step 13). The kingdom's barge road is a river so wide that the far bank is a green line. It is slow and heavy, the colour of candle tallow under violet dusk. Its banks are a wall of forest in tiers, standing knee-deep in the flood. In front of the forest float meadows of tall grass, whole fields rising and falling on the current, thick with giant lilies that are white at dusk and rose by night, and loud with frogs. Sandbars come and go. Sunk barges and pale stone mile-posts mark the channel, and one ferry under the water still has its lamp lit. Where the black river comes in from the forest, amber water runs beside ink water and will not mix, and the Long Hole goes down on the outside of the great bend.

**15. Lanternroot Lagoon** (lagoon · step 14). The drowned orchard has become a mangrove forest. It stands in brackish, glass-calm water on arched roots, its crowns a deep glossy green over palms and seagrass flats. Stilt houses hang paper lanterns low: candle amber on dusk water. Pale sand flats, ankle deep, go lavender under the stars, and blossom drifts nebula pink down the creeks. At night whole trees of fireflies flash together, over and over, like a slow heartbeat.

**16. The Inkwood** (river, blackwater · step 15). At the heart of the kingdom the forest drank its orchards whole. A black river, clear as tea when you hold it in a cup, winds slowly between trunks standing in water to their waists, and the canopy closes over it: green on green on green, leaves wider than the raft, curtains of vine and hanging root, ferns and pale flowers on every limb, moss on everything. The water is a mirror, so the forest is there twice. Lantern-gourds still ripen and drop in with a plop, and nebula-pink blossom turns on the surface. Narrow creeks slip away under the roots to hidden ponds where something big breathes, and sighs. At night the moon comes through in coins, fireflies hang in the green, and every leaf the lantern touches shines.

## 3. How green is done, and what never changes

### 3.1 Four channels: three carry green, one never does

| channel | carries green? | how |
|---|---|---|
| **Materials** (moss, foliage, floating meadows, weed beds) | yes, the main channel | new palette keys `leafDark / leaf / leafHi / moss2 / bloom`; a new `mossG` layer over the built moss (§6); `colorRock`'s moss amount by step; the vegetation kit (§5) |
| **The mirror** (the planar reflection) | yes, held down | the water reflects the green banks, but **the reflection never turns the water green**: at the camera's grazing angle the reflected colour keeps at least 35% of the sky (violet), and reflected foliage is darkened and desaturated (×0.6). The water's own pixels (reflections included, floating plants not) stay at most 0.6× as green as the banks (§7). The Siltway stays tallow-brown, the Lagoon violet-blue and the Inkwood black, with green reflections in them |
| **Night emitters** (glow-moss, fireflies, night-blooming flowers) | yes, small and dim | pale jade glow-moss, pale lime fireflies, white or lavender flowers that open at night; no point lights |
| **Air, light, sky, grade** (fog, ink, ambient, key light, nebula, lamp, glow, candle, grade tint) | **never** | they stay at hue ≥ 200° or near-grey on every water, so a green cast cannot come from them. **Sporewood Pools** wears its own air under the loop (the user's "too purple", then "possibly too much green", LAKE2 §9.11): Clear Night-led and never green (a blue-teal and slate air, cream light, a dark teal water, a plum-rose accent), its three lights apart (a cream-gold day, an amber-rose dusk, a deep blue-teal night); its green is its muted leaves' and moss's own |

### 3.2 The rules

- **Constant on every water:** the clear sharp air of Clear Night (fog density stays in today's band, 0.0042–0.0072; humid waters are not foggier; spray only within 25 m of falls); the violet-dusk night sky (ink #0a0c19 → deep indigo #1d245d → violet dusk #433e74); the lavender moon #c4b8ff; the candle #ed9e3b as the one saturated warm light; original shapes; **no ring or disc round the raft or the Wick** (the candle is a pinpoint flame with a short streak on the water and a little warm light on the raft's own planks, never a halo on the water).
- **Some sky stays.** At least 12% of every frame is sky (6% under the Inkwood's canopy, through its gaps and moon coins). A river's camera-side bank and its north-bank trees keep a setback and a height cap so that sky exists (§4.2).
- **Three value tiers on every green mass:** `leafDark`, `leaf`, `leafHi` (and a young yellow-green for new leaves, tufts and duckweed). `leafDark` is up to 40° bluer than the mid tone (to 150° at most), `leafHi` up to 32° yellower; from step 6, yellow-green (65–100°), green (100–140°) and blue-green (140–195°) each carry at least 10% of the green. The early waters (1–5) are moss: olive by design.
- **Backlit leaves.** The key light stays upper-left behind the scene on every water, so foliage is mostly backlit. A leaf-translucency term adds `leafHi` × 0.3 at leaf edges facing the key, by day and dusk; at night the moon does the same in lavender. This is the strongest single lushness cue.
- **Aerial perspective is lavender, through grey:** green near, lavender far. Far greens are first desaturated, then blended to the fog with distance (ring 1 at 0.35, ring 2 at 0.6, rings 3+ as today), so a far green never passes through teal on its way to lavender, and the horizon is always Starlit.
- **Day, dusk and night.** By day: backlit leaves, moss on stone tops, the banks in the mirror. At dusk: the rose key turns lit greens olive-bronze (hue 60–90°) and shadows blue-green (140–150°); fireflies start at dusk k ≥ 0.5; glow-moss fades in from k 0.6; night flowers begin to open. **At night lushness is carried by three things, never by saturation:** silhouette coverage (the masses of leaf against the sky and the water), the night emitters (fireflies, glow-moss, night flowers) and the moon's translucency through the leaves. Night mid tones stay at hue 125–158° and saturation ≤ 0.30, their value rises with the step (0.28 → 0.41), lavender rims and the moon's translucency do the lifting, and lit night foliage is never under luma 14/255: **never black**. The night key mixes toward the moon by 0.25 + 0.035 × step (the Mossmere's formula is unchanged at step 0), and each water's moon translucency is set so its night share lands on its §1 target (the colour script fits it: 0.79–1.15 × the base).
- **Fireflies** are pale lime (#e0e49c to #e4e898, hue about 63°, saturation ≤ 0.4), carry no light, sit 0.4–4 m above banks and foliage, never over open water and never within 6 m of the raft, and are at most 0.6% of the frame height. They start at step 5 (the Thin Water); before that the built waters' own night glows (spores, glow-worm threads, glow-moss) do the twinkling. Their count, and the night emitters in all, never fall from one water to the next. In the Lagoon whole trees flash together.
- **The candle stays the one warm light.** Lantern-gourds, paper lanterns, rushlights, pyres and polyp flames are `candle` pinpoints on wood and stone. Inside the lantern's pool, leaves go warm olive-gold, never yellow-neon (the pool's warm-drain rule extends to foliage).
- **Never neon, never teal UI.** Leaf keys: HSV saturation ≤ min(0.62, 0.25 + 0.04 × step), value ≤ 0.70. Glow-moss saturation ≤ 0.32. Duckweed and new leaves are soft yellow-green, never chartreuse. The water's own colour stays outside 65–195° (green on the water only from reflections, pads, duckweed film and weed beds). The UI is untouched.
- **Grade:** each water's `tint` stays violet (tintAmount ≤ 0.2) and `saturation` stays in 0.92–1.06; the grade never fakes lushness. The violet shadow split-tone keeps green shadows violet-green, the painter's violet shadow.

### 4.2 What a river level is

A river is a full 3D water: banks, bends, a current you can see, the raft on moving water. It is a new arena layout, `arena.layout: 'river'`, beside the four lake layouts (`bowl`, `twoBasins`, `ringTrench`, `drop`). **It is seen as a river**: the channel recedes into depth ahead of the raft, its banks converge on a bend, and the landmark stands at the head of the bend.

| item | rule |
|---|---|
| **the arena** | Not a disc: an **authored strip**, `arena.bounds` {x: ±60, z: ±150} (120 × 300 m), its long axis north–south. The reach runs from the spawn at the downstream (south) end up to the upstream (north) end. The sailable reach between the end mists is at most 2 × 150 − 60 = **240 m**. The centreline stays inside the strip with its half-width and 15 m of bank on each side, and no leg turns more than 60° off north. |

| **the camera** (new: `BALANCE.cameras.sail.river`) | On a river the sail camera does not hold north. ### 4.3 The current

| item | rule |
|---|---|
| **flow** | Along the centreline's tangent, fastest mid-channel (speed × (1 − (offset / half-width)²)), faster where the channel narrows. Round every rock and snag it is tangent to the stone. |
| **speeds** | Sailing lanes 0.2–1.2 m/s: at most 20% of the raft's base 6 m/s, so going upstream always works. Places, docks, the spawn and the legend's pool are slack water (≤ 0.25 m/s). |
| **white water** | A rapid, chute, sill or throat **never cuts the lane**. Each one either leaves a **sneak lane** at least 5.2 m wide (the raft's 3.2 m and 2 m to spare) at ≤ 1.2 m/s beside the white water, or is a **sailable foam band**: the raft enters it and is carried through at a scripted crawl (1 m/s, steered along the band's centre) and out into the pool below. The white water itself is scenery and a fence only where the sneak lane runs beside it. **Above every fence the flow decays to ≤ 0.25 m/s over 10 m** (a pool head), so a drifting raft never pins against a fence. The validator checks flow · (the fence's normal) ≤ 0.25 m/s within 3 m of every no-sail edge. |
| **a current you can see** | A `GW_RIVER` water-shader variant (compiled only on river waters; lakes pay nothing): the chop scrolls along the river's own coordinates with **two-phase flow** (two samples half a period apart, cross-faded by a triangle wave every 4–6 s, so lanes moving at different speeds never shear the texture apart over a long night; the river term wraps its own time by the period, so precision holds all night), foam lines run along the seams and banks (denser where faster), standing waves stand in the chutes, V-wakes trail every post and snag, and duckweed and weed film drift with the flow. About +55–75 instructions per water pixel on high and medium (two-phase flow included), +30 on low, no draw calls. Swell is halved on rivers. |
| **what drifts** | Only small things ride the current: each river's own leaves and petals and its own twigs (60 + 20 on high, 30 + 10 on low), from the upstream mist to the downstream mist. The Thin Water: seed fluff. The Gorge: ash and silk-cotton fluff. The Stair: spray-wet leaves. The Salt Gate: weed and mangrove seedlings on the ebb. The Siltway: lily debris. The Inkwood: petals and dropped lantern-gourds. **Big floating things** (floating islands, grass rafts, floating meadows) are **anchored**: they bob and sway in place, and one or two at the Thin Water turn slowly on a tether at night. Every drifting instance is pushed off the raft (within 4 m), the docks and the place discs (it slides along the channel edge). Drift is mirrored on high (+1 mirror call), so it never looks pasted on a mirror river. |
| **the tide** (the Salt Gate only) | **The flow turns; no geometry moves.** The flow is a pure function of the evening clock: it ebbs seaward through the day (40 s) and the dusk (20 s), stands slack for about 5 s as night falls, then floods inland for the rest of the night (± 0.4–1.0 m/s). The water plane stays at y = 0, with the mirror, the raft, the float, the fish and the wet bands. The mudflats are their own small mesh, the only thing that moves: they sink 0.4 m below y = 0 as the flood comes, and the water shader's shore threshold (`getShoreDist` offset by `uTide`) moves the waterline foam and the wet-sand mask with them. The docks always work. |

### 4.4 The raft on moving water

| state | what the current does |
|---|---|
| idle (no input for 1.5 s, no line out) | the raft drifts at half the local flow, at most 0.8 m/s, and turns slowly with it; after 2 s still in flow over 0.3 m/s it is nudged **toward the nearest slack cell** (down the gradient of flow speed), never toward a fence |
| paddling | adds a quarter of the flow: felt, never limiting |
| charging, casting, waiting, a bite, a fish on, landing, the measure, the card | **nothing**: the Wick drops a stone over the side (a plop and a ripple, no button, no words). These states are data, `LOOP.river.holdStates`; today's anchor covers only reeling, landing and lost, so the rest are new. A hooked fish's tow is clamped so the raft stays inside its place disc |
| arrival, Set Out and the sail home | nothing: the glide is scripted along the river and ignores the current |
| tap-to-sail or auto-sail (later) | commands the speed over the ground, so arrivals are on time |
| the float | stays where it landed, with a small V-wake showing the current (later: it may swing up to 1.5 m downstream, inside the 4 m line-break distance) |

### 4.5 Places along a river, docks and casts

- **Places.** A river has its four or five places 35–50 m apart along the channel, each 5–8 m across (a disc in a pool, an eddy or a seam), each in slack water and at least 80% open water, measured along the channel. They come from the river's own forms: **a pool** (slack; crowns and old water), **an undercut bank** at an outer bend (deep, roots and canopy over it), **an eddy** behind a point or a rock, **a creek mouth** or a creek's pond, **the tail-out below a rapid**, and **a bar edge** (shallow; day water). **No place is in white water**: a place named for a rapid, a sill, a throat or a falls sits in the slack eddy or tail-out beside it, with the white water as its backdrop (§4.7). An unowned place is a mist bank across the river's width that the raft sails through (SPEC §5.1 rule 6 unchanged).
- **The spawn** is at the downstream end, facing upstream, and the camera rule of §4.2 puts the landmark at the bend's apex in view.
- **Docks** sit on the inner banks, on the point bars, on the slack side: on a river a dock's yaw comes from the bank's normal in the field (not from the arena centre), it is snapped to the slack side with `getShoreDist`, and it is never nudged toward the origin. The channel at a dock is at least twice the dock's footprint (the barge, its two posts and its buoy take about 7–8 m) plus the lane; the validator checks it.
- **Casts.** On a river the default cast aims along the local channel tangent (upstream when the place is upstream), not up the screen, so it lands in open water. A hooked fish's run (the play's `F.dist` and `F.lat`) is clamped by `getShoreDist(x, z) − 1 m`, the fish turning along the channel, so a Prize run never swims into a bank.
- **Creeks** are at least 11 m wide (a lane of 5.8 m), and a creek's hidden pond is a pool of at least 16 m at the creek's end, so a place fits there at 80% open water.

### 4.6 No way to fail

Nothing can capsize, strand or carry off the raft. The flow is tangent to every bank and rock; no lane runs faster than 1.2 m/s; white water always leaves a sneak lane or carries the raft through; the water above a fence is slack; places, docks and the ends are slack; within 2.5 m of a bank a soft push turns the raft parallel to it (it slides, never bounces); the idle nudge points to slack water. **The validator proves it** for every river: the spine fits its strip with its bend rule; the lane (the water shrunk by the raft's radius + 1 m) is one connected piece holding the spawn, every dock, every place and the legend's pool; every place is 80% open water; the flow limits hold, and flow into any no-sail edge is ≤ 0.25 m/s within 3 m of it; every dock has its channel width. **The drift sim** imports `player.js`'s integrate and collide through a pure shim (so it is the real integrator), drops an idle raft at 200 random lane points and leaves it for 120 s, for both tide directions at the Salt Gate: no raft reaches an end, none sits against a bank in fast water for more than 3 s, and at most 5% end within 20 m of the downstream dead water. **The cast sim**: from 200 lane points at least 3 of 8 cast directions find open water 6 m out, and the default aim finds open water at least 8 m out.

A kind's count at a water is full × (0.12 + 0.88 × u^0.85), with u = (lush − start) / (1 − start), and 0 below its start: each new kind arrives small, so every step is visible and none is a jump. Rivers plant 0.8× a lake's count at the same step, because two near banks fill more of the frame; the sea plants on its island, the far island and the weed only. **The measured green share is binding, not the count.**

**Where things grow** (one placement function for lakes and rivers): pads where the depth is under 0.25 and the flow under 0.3; reeds 0–2 m from the bank; ferns, bushes and tufts 0–4 m up the bank; trees 4–15 m back, leaning 10–25° over the water, with strands hanging from crowns over the water; far crowns up to the bluff; **on a river's camera-side band only kinds under 1.2 m** (§4.2). Lakes use cliff faces facing upward (normal y > 0.45) between 0.3 and 5 m up, island rims and tops, and the shallows. New lake waters from lush 0.3 may take a cliff terrace (`arena.shoreShelf`, 3–6 m wide) to plant on.

**The Mossmere is not touched.** Its pads (a 48-triangle `CylinderGeometry` instanced mesh drawn from the shared art `rng`) and its reeds (drawn in the same `rng` stream inside the islands loop) keep their builders exactly: the `theme === 'mossmere'` branches stay as they are. The flat pad and the generalised reeds and pads builders apply only where lush > 0, on the flora's own random stream.

### 5.2 How it is built and drawn

- **Merged by material, in culled cells.** On a lake the flora is cut into a **3 × 3 grid of about 40 m cells**; on a river the near bank and the far bank are separate meshes, cut every 60 m along the channel, so culling and the opaque sort both work. Per cell, two materials: `flora` (trees, bushes, blades, strands and the canopy: mirrored, casts) and `flora.low` (tufts, fronds, pads: not mirrored), plus the canopy's ink-outline hull on high only. Trunks join the existing `organics` mesh. About 1.8 MB at lush 1.
- **Built from prototypes.** Each archetype variant is built once and prepped (merged, coloured with its baked vertex colour); instances are stamped by matrix copy into preallocated typed arrays, with an instance tint, never through `prep()`'s per-piece merge and per-vertex fbm. The arena build stays within **250 ms on desktop and 900 ms on a phone at lush 1, a river's field included** (slice 5's test).
- **Sway** is a vertex attribute (weight by height, phase by root position) and shared uniforms for the wind and the raft's push (blades bend away from the raft). About 12 instructions per vertex; reflections sway too; canopy shadows stay still (a few centimetres of sway).
- **No alpha test and no transparency on foliage**: opaque blades and fronds with ink darkening baked into the vertex colour at bases and tips. It keeps early depth testing and avoids the software-GL discard cost; near the camera the fronds are solid low-poly geometry, so phones without MSAA stay sharp. Cells are drawn near to far.
- **Night**: a flora floor tint (leaf shadows stay deep green-violet, not grey), moon translucency through the leaves, a lavender rim from the water's `light`, fireflies fading in with the night. **The night emitters** (glow-moss, moonflowers, fireflies) are their own small mesh with a night uniform (+1 call); the shared `glowMat` and the built glow mesh are untouched.

## 8. The colour script

`tools/loop-colour-script.py` (Python 3, Pillow) reads the ramp table in §1 and the repaint table in §6 and does three things.

1. **Lint.** Every colour in §1 and §6 obeys §3.2's caps and §7's track (the leaf caps, the hue bands, hue and value never falling, the air and the water out of green). The doc cannot ship a colour its own gate rejects.
2. **Paint.** One small frame per water and phase, in the frame the sail camera sees. A lake: the far shore with its forms (the Toppled Bridge, the cap reef, the monoliths, the ribs, the glacier, the dam...), the side shores receding from the bottom corners, the water with its mirror. A river: the channel receding up to its landmark at the bend, banks converging, foam lines following the channel, only low plants on the camera-side band. Each kind has its own silhouettes: spruce ranks on the fjord's walls, the lighthouse island's turf cliff, the gorge's hanging forest, green keys on the reef's roofs, the spray forest and the lock towers, papyrus walls with mop heads, mangrove prop roots, swamp trees on flared knees with moss curtains and duckweed, the tiered flood forest with emergent trees, floating meadows and giant lilies, the Inkwood's closed canopy with hanging roots, trunks in the water and moon coins. The raft's candle is a pinpoint flame with a short streak on the water; no halo.
3. **Measure and gate.** Each cell is measured the way `hue-report` measures a capture, and every §7 gate that a still frame can show is applied: the share against the target, the day, dusk and night steps, night against day, the water band against the bank band, the cast on what is not foliage, the sky, neon, teal, variety and the night lights. It prints a table and exits 1 on any failure; the sheet prints measured / target under every cell and draws the measured day, dusk and night lines under the columns with the doc's targets as ticks.

Each water's frame is painted with one **foliage amount** and, at dusk and night, one **light on the leaves** (the rose key's and the moon's translucency): `--fit` re-derives them so the day, dusk and night shares meet the targets, and the default run paints with the fitted values and measures. The geometry each amount drives is fixed per kind, so the gates (sky, cast, water band, neon, teal, variety, steps) are real tests, not fits.

```
cd gloomwater
python3 tools/loop-colour-script.py          # writes tools/out/loop/colour-script.png (untracked); exit 1 on a lint or gate failure
python3 tools/loop-colour-script.py --fit    # after a palette or target change
```

Read left to right it should look slow, steady and beautiful: the Mossmere exactly as today, a lavender night with a trace of ochre reeds; then moss on the caps, moss on the crystal, the requiem garden's ivy and cedars, the rainforest fjord's spruce, the braided river's reed islands and willows, the lighthouse island's turf, the gorge's hanging forest, the reef's green keys, the spray forest, papyrus, mangroves, swamp forest, the flood forest and its meadows, the mangrove lagoon and finally the closed green hall of the Inkwood, under the same violet sky and the same lavender moon, with the same small candle on the raft.
