## 0. The user's document, the four answers, and what binds

**The document.** Five characters to meet on the road, each built from one recipe: an odd small want, a habit or belief out of step with the world, a story that moves on between visits, and a payoff the player causes; every line short, like Murk's; no enemies, no quests, no human figure, no new warm light on the water, Murk still the only shop. Its craft, described here without the games it cites: the best small characters are personal stories inside a big loss, telling you what happened to the world without being about it, by six means: **a habit that outlived its reason**, **one small want**, **a story that moves on while you are away**, **heard before seen**, **one of you knows something the other does not**, and **a payoff the player causes**. The five: **Tiller**, a snail gardening the meadow on the Drifting Grove's back, sure it is an island (Sporewood, water 2); **Velvet**, a family of moths flying north over generations to the great lantern (met at Pyrefall Gorge, 8, her daughters at the Rime Sound, 5, Glasswater, 3, and the Mossmere, 1); **Dart**, a kingfisher who has dived at the Long Thunder all his life (Rushlight Fen, 11); **Supper**, a shipyard night bird that says the people's old phrases at the sounds that once came with them, moves into one of Murk's empty birdcages and travels with home (the Salt Gate, 12); **Flinch**, a young fruit bat afraid of the dark who rides the lantern home to the Inkwood (13–16).

## 1. In one breath

Five creatures of the road have something to say. On Sporewood's warm water at night, a patch of meadow drifts past the raft, and a snail with a spore-cap is watering it; it is sure it lives on an island, and it does not. At the snow temple a moth comes to your flame and asks if it is the great lantern; her daughters fly on north, each stopping at a small light along the way, until the last of them reaches the Mossmere and finds only the chain. On the fen a kingfisher lands on your lantern pole to look at your rod; he has dived at the Long Thunder all his life and touched her twice. In the shipyard at the sea-gate a bark-coloured night bird calls "Mind your fingers." when a fish flaps on your planks, and a few dawns later it is sitting in one of Murk's empty birdcages with the door open. At the canal's summit a young fruit bat lands on your lantern pole, swings underneath and shakes: "Don't turn it off." It rides your lantern home to the forest, a little braver each evening. They come to you; they say a line or two; they never take, block, sell or ask anything; and they are never more than one a night.

## 3. The rules all five keep

### 3.1 Who talks

**Named characters talk; every other creature stays an ordinary animal.** The speakers are Murk, the four far-water Wicks (Dip, Glim, Lean and Farthing) and the five met on the road (Tiller, the Velvets, Dart, Supper and Flinch). Everyone else is an animal behaving as one and never speaks: every fish (the giants and legends included: fish are fish), the frogs, the herons, the fireflies, the ordinary moths at the flame, the bees, the bats of Flinch's colony, the ordinary night birds, Farthing's small frog. The old toads stay unseen and silent. The Wick never speaks, and neither do the four other playable Wicks: their one lines are card lines under New Descent, not speech. A new speaker is the user's call, character by character.

The five never talk to the far-water Wicks or to each other, except the one exchange the barge makes natural (Supper's "Home before dark." and Flinch's answer, §8). Murk talks about them, once each, at the tally.

### 3.2 The one line budget (for every speaker)

The lead ruled one budget for Murk, the far-water Wicks and the five. **The budget:** a line a character says sets in **at most two rows**, word-wrapped, and **a row holds at most 36 characters and 8 words**: one row at 18 px on a 358 px phone, the measure the far-water design and the passives already use. So a line is at most 72 characters and 16 words, and **one row wherever it can be** (the writing target, above all on the water, where the ink pool sits beside a raft). It shows one line at a time on the feathered ink pool beside the speaker, held at least 2.4 s and 0.05 s more a character, or until a tap, 0.3 s apart; never during a bite, a play, the show or a card; read aloud by the built live region. **At most three lines a beat**, from one speaker or two in turn.

**Why two rows, and not one.** One row (36 characters, 8 words) for everyone would break sixteen of Murk's lines (`SPEC.md` §8.5–§8.6), nearly all of them M1's and the opening's wake line among them ("Awake, little Wick? Good. Fish sell. Clams buy. That's the whole of it.", in the opening's words the user ruled on 14 November), and M1 changes in nothing. The document's 60 characters would also fail the wake line (71) and is not measured on the screen. Two rows of the far-water row hold every line already written, Murk's on the water too (his lore toasts after a card, his sailing-out lines), with no word changed, and they keep the far-water Wicks' reason: a row is what one glance reads on a phone. **What each speaker's lines must also do is its own rule**, unchanged: a far-water tale line is an observation, never an instruction, names no uncaught fish and speaks of fish, water and itself (`FARWICKS.md` §8); Murk may tell you to go home; the five never instruct (§3.6).

**The scan** (`validate-data --loop`, the characters' slice): every `TEXT_LOOP` line of every speaker, wrapped at 36 characters, sets in at most two rows of at most 8 words; no numeral; the guards' word list; and for the five, no fish named that the player may not have caught (the five never name a fish at all) and no imperative to the player.

### 3.3 They come to you: a visit on the water

A **visitor** (Tiller, a Velvet, Dart, Supper before it moves aboard, Flinch before it boards) is never sought: there is no lantern to tap, no target, no marker, no ring and no count. On its evening (§3.4), at its hour, it waits for **a quiet moment**: nothing is biting, playing, showing or on the card (the float waiting on the water, as when a far-water lantern may be tapped, or the moment just after a card closes). Then:

| moment | what happens | time |
|---|---|---|
| it comes | it arrives at the raft: a bird or a bat to the lantern pole, a moth to the lantern's cap, the meadow drifting up alongside on the side away from the float; **the clock and the bites wait** from its arrival (as alongside a far-water raft); the camera eases a little closer to the raft (10%, 0.6 s) | about 1.2 s |
| it speaks | its line or lines, one at a time, on the ink pool beside it, no name and no glyph | ≥ 2.4 s each, or a tap |
| it goes | it leaves the way it came; the camera eases back; the clock and the bites run on | about 1 s |

About 5–9 s of the player's time, none of it the evening's: the clock is stopped throughout, so the sim charges nothing (§12.4). **Reduced motion**: no camera ease; the visitor fades in at its perch and out. If the player goes home before its hour comes, it simply does not come that evening (§3.4).

### 3.4 The meeting rule: one for the far-water Wicks and the five

One rule decides every **meeting**: a far-water Wick's lantern lifting, or a visitor coming. It is `FARWICKS.md` §3.2's rule with the five added as sources, so the voices never crowd one another and never share an evening (`FARWICKS.md` revision 3 carries the same table).

| rule | value (`LOOP.farwicks.meet`) | why |
|---|---|---|
| the save has ended at least 5 evenings, and the tutorial is over | `fromEvening: 6` | the first trips are the game's own |
| never on a water's first evening, a told place's lift evening, the opening, the tutorial or the tow | — | one beat an evening; a first evening is the mist lift's |
| **at most one beat on the water an evening**: a meeting, or an aboard character's line (§3.5) | — | the lead's "never two meetings in one evening" |
| at least **4 evenings** ended since the last meeting of any kind (`meet.last`: the evening a lantern lifted, tapped or not, or a visitor came) | `gap: 4` | rare, and never two close together |
| someone is ready at the start of the evening: this water's visitor has a beat due (its trigger met, §4–§8), or the reach's far-water Wick has a tale ready (`FARWICKS.md` §3.2) | — | nothing comes with nothing to say |
| tonight's roll: 1 in 2 on the first and second eligible evenings, certain on the third (`meet.wait`) | `chance: 0.5`, `sureOn: 3` | now and then, never more than three evenings late |
| **who comes**: a *reaction* first (a beat answering something the player has just done or seen: a legend landed or lost, the hearth stirred); else the ready source that showed least recently (`meet.by`); a tie goes to this water's visitor | — | a reaction lands while it means something; otherwise the five and the far-water Wicks take turns, so a water with a visitor still hears its tales |

Readiness is checked once, at the evening's start; the roll is a hash of (`meet.seed`, `meta.loop.evenings`, `waterId`), so a reload gives the same evening. A lantern counts for the gap when it lifts; **a visitor counts only once it has come**: if the player goes home before its hour, the evening counts as an eligible evening that met no one (`meet.wait` steps), and its beat stays due. Nothing expires, nothing is counted against the player, and no beat is ever withheld for having been missed.

How often *(est.)*: about 24 meetings of the five over the road (Tiller 7, the Velvets 5, Dart 9 if the Long Thunder ever gets away, Supper 1, Flinch 2: its first landing and its leaving), at nine waters, beside the far-water Wicks' 48; the far-water tales at the five's nine waters arrive about one to two evenings later than without them.

### 3.5 Home, and aboard

**A visit home** runs from the raft tying up at the barge's stern at dawn, through the tally and the barge, to the sail out of the next evening. In it, **at most one character beat** (at most three lines, one speaker or two in turn). Murk's built lines (`SPEC.md` §8.6) keep their words and come first: a character's home beat waits for the next visit when a Murk line is due, unless the beat is a scripted exchange with Murk in it (the rail, the Drifting Grove's tally, Supper's cage and name), which then is that visit's Murk line, and any other Murk line due comes at the next visit. Murk's new lines about the five (§9) are themselves home beats.

**Aboard** (Supper from the Salt Gate, Flinch from Lampmoss Slough to the Inkwood): §10.2 gives the barge's rules. An aboard character's line **on the water** (Flinch's, at dusk, from under the lantern) takes the evening's one beat only on an evening with no meeting, needs no gap, waits for a quiet moment as a visitor does, and is said once.

### 3.6 What none of them ever is

- **Not a trader, keeper, guide, quest-giver or rival.** None sells, gives or takes anything; none tends a lamp or a fire (Velvet only rests at one); none leads, follows or waits for the raft to go anywhere; none asks anything of the player; none takes a fish the player could catch, or stops one being landed. Murk is the only shop.
- **Never an instruction.** A line of the five is talk, never a thing to do: "Don't rock the beans." and "Don't catch her." are frets, not tasks; nothing changes whatever the player does.
- **Never a marker, a number or a sign of its own.** A character's fishing help is a sight on the water that the water was already making (§4.4, §6.4): no rate, share, size or count changes, and nothing is drawn on the HUD, the Chart or the catalogue.
- **Never during a bite**, a play, the show, a card, the opening, the tutorial, the tow, a water's first evening, or the closing film (except Supper's one line, the user's answer).
- **Never on the Chart, the catalogue, the shelf or the Well.** No page, glyph, knot, count or log of them anywhere.
- **Never harmed and never hunting.** Nothing eats a Velvet (Murk "wasn't going to"); Dart lets every fish drop back; Flinch ignores the moths at your flame.
- **Never human, never a story-teller.** No human voice (Supper's calls are a bird's); none explains the fall, the people, Murk's past or the road's end. What they know of the world is what an animal would know; the player knows more.
- **Their light** is cold or a pinpoint that lights nothing; their reflections are threads.

## 4. Tiller (Sporewood Pools)

### 4.1 Who

**Look.** About the size of the Wick's head. An autumn-ochre shell furred with moss, a neat row of seedlings planted along its ridge; two pale lavender eyes on stalks, faintly glowing at the tips, and nothing else for a face; a soft grey-violet foot. He carries water in an empty spore-cap, a small cap from Sporewood's mushrooms, tipped like a can. Living green only, on the moss and the seedlings; nothing on him glows warm.

**Who he is.** A pond snail who climbed onto the meadow as a hatchling and has never got off. He plants in straight rows because the drowned gardens he drifts past are laid out in rows, and he waters his rows each night because the gardeners hung their watering cans in rows on the caps: he has never met a gardener; he copies what they left. **His two gaps**: he keeps the gardeners' habit without knowing it was theirs, and the player slowly works out what he never does: his island is the Drifting Grove, Sporewood's legend.

**What she is** (ROSTER, unchanged): a round giant ray nearly as wide as the raft, plum-backed, a whole meadow growing on her back whose small blooms glow amber (pinpoints that light nothing); she lives on the floor of the Grove Well, in the Mother's Cup, and bites there only, by day and dusk, on her bait ("A whole meadow rides on her back. It blooms where no one sees."; the crown's card: "The Mother breathes. Big things breathe with her."). **New here, a sight only:** at night, off her hours, she rises and drifts through Sporewood's open water, her meadow just at the surface, and by the next late day she is back on the Well's floor.

## 5. The Velvets (Pyrefall Gorge, then back north: the Rime Sound, Glasswater Vault, the Mossmere)

### 5.1 Who

**Look.** Wings about as wide as the Wick's head, lavender-grey and soft like old velvet, with two pale eye-spots; a faint cold lavender glow along the wing edges that lights nothing. **Each generation's eye-spots carry one more pale ring**: the first Velvet's are plain, her daughter's have one ring, her granddaughter's two, the last Velvet's three.

**Who they are.** In the kingdom's time moths came from all over the south to the great lantern. The habit outlived the lantern. A moth lives a few weeks, so the flight north takes generations, and every daughter is called Velvet too; the story travels north with them, so the moths still setting out from the south have never heard that the lantern fell. **Why they matter**: a habit that outlived its reason, carried by a whole family; each Velvet thinks she might be the one who arrives, and every warm light on the road might be it. The player, who saw the lantern fall in the film, knows what the moths do not. **The user's answer**: the mothers never die; each **settles** at a small light of her water, and her daughter flies on.

## 6. Dart (Rushlight Fen)

### 6.1 Who

**Look.** Small: with his long dagger beak, about as long as the Wick's candle head is tall. A deep blue-violet back that reads almost black against the night, an autumn-ochre breast, one bright eye that catches the light as a pale pinpoint. He perches on papyrus stems and on top of the basket stacks on the dam's steps, and moves in sharp, sudden flicks.

**Who he is.** When he was young, the Long Thunder rose under him as he dived, and he touched her back. He has been after her ever since. He knows he could never lift her; that was never the point. **Why he matters**: he is out of step with the fen itself. Rushlight Fen was where fishing was a joy ("People laughed there once. ## 7. Supper (the Salt Gate, then aboard)

**Who it is.** In the kingdom's time birds nested in the shipyard and picked up what the people said, sound by sound, and taught their chicks; four hundred years on, the words are still being passed down, and nobody has answered them in all that time. **Why it matters**: the purest habit that outlived its reason. **Tells beside the tips.** The far-water Wicks and the five help the fishing in two different ways that never overlap:

| | a far-water Wick's tip | Tiller's or Dart's tell |
|---|---|---|
| what it is | knowledge, told once | a sight on the water, shown again and again |
| what it changes | that fish's bites and Hold at that water (`FARWICKS.md` §4.1) | nothing: a sign's rate, kind and share are as they were; only where it shows is moved (the meadow) or pointed at (Dart's dive) |
| where the player sees it | the knot; the number in the player's own logs | the water itself |
| a number anywhere | in the logs only | none, anywhere |

A tipped fish's sign may well be the one that rises at the meadow's edge, or the one Dart dives at: the tip makes it come more often, the tell shows where it rose. Neither ever marks a fish.

**What they share and do not.** Both kinds speak in the one budget, never during a bite, at most three lines a beat; both are never sought; neither is ever at the tally but by Murk's mention. The far-water Wicks are met by tapping their lantern at dusk and you sail to them; the five come to you, at their own hour (night for Tiller and the Velvets; dusk for Dart, Supper and Flinch). The far-water Wicks speak only of fish, water and themselves; the five may speak of the great lantern and their own small world, because that is what they are. Murk knows of both, and says so once for each.

### 10.2 Aboard: the barge's rules

- **The barge never sinks, drifts or needs keeping, and nothing aboard needs anything.** Supper and Flinch need no feeding, cleaning, calling or care; nothing about them can go wrong; there is no button, menu, item, count, page or slot for either; they never enter the hold, the Well or the catalogue.
- **Where they live.** Supper in one of the empty birdcages in Murk's pack, on Murk's barge alongside the Slow Hearth, door open: out at night (a night bird), home by first light, roosting by day. Flinch under the Slow Hearth's awning by the Chart's table by day, under the raft's lantern each evening, from Lampmoss Slough until it leaves at the Inkwood.
- **They travel with home.** On every tow (§6.5 of the SPEC) the glide-in shows Supper's cage on Murk's barge and, while aboard, Flinch under the awning; the ink dip back to an earlier water takes them too.

### 10.3 Velvet's way back north, and the kept lamps

Going back to an earlier water is one ink dip, because a kept lamp remembers the way (`LORE.md` §2.12): following the moths north costs a tap on the Chart and an evening at a cheaper water, nothing more, and it is never needed. Two of the four Velvets settle on the road's own kept lamps (the Rime Sound's and Glasswater's, over the barge's mooring), so a player who goes back sees her from home before she comes to the lantern; the first rests by the temple fires that nobody feeds, the last in a lit window of the Gate. The kept lamps themselves change in nothing: a moth rests on one; it is no brighter and no one keeps it.

### 10.4 The empty birdcage
