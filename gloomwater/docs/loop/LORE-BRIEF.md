# Gloomwater: the lore so far

A brief for anyone designing new characters for the game. Everything here is already settled by the game's design. Where something is still open, it says so (section 13). Nothing here is new lore.

---

## 1. What the game is

You are **the Wick**, a small living candle, one of the last of a drowned kingdom. Each evening you sail a little raft out onto a water you have opened and fish through dusk into the night. You turn for home when you choose, usually with a full hold, and reach home at first light. Home is **the Slow Hearth**, an old barge-house you tow behind your raft from water to water. **Old Murk**, a centuries-old toad and the last trader on the road, moors his barge alongside it. He buys your fish for clams and sells you everything else, including the old charts that lift the mist from new waters. Water by water you follow the kingdom's old water road, **the Lamp Road**: sixteen waters from the cold stone north to the green forest in the south.

**The tone.** Quiet, warm and a little melancholy, never sad for long. Small lights are kept out of habit and kindness, and the world has grown green over what was lost. The voice is wry, not grim: "Awake, little Wick? Good. Fish sell. Clams buy. That's the whole of it."

---

## 2. The world, and why it is like this

**An ordinary world.** There is one continent, **the Waxwold** ("the wax forest" in the people's old word, for the south where the bees were), about 6,000 km from its cold north coast to its warm south cape, with sea on every side. Its long, empty middle of steppe and desert coast, which the kingdom crossed only by sea, is **the Fold**, drawn folded small on the old charts. It has days, seasons, winds and currents like any world. There is no curse and no villain.

**Why every trip is an evening.** A Wick is a window candle, lit at dusk and burning until first light, so it wakes in the last of the day and comes home at dawn to doze. And the big fish come up at dusk ("They always did."). **"The long evening"** is only the folk name for the four hundred years since the great lantern fell: a saying, not a sky.

**The people and their road.** The people began in the **Orchard Basin**, a low, wet forest in the south, where they kept orchards and bees and made candles from the wax. Over generations they went north along the water roads, following the big fish and the long dusks, until the land ran out, and built their capital, **the Sunken Gate**, on the Mossmere. At every water they settled, its people built a lamp at the landing for whoever was out at evening, and kept it themselves. Read in order, those lamps are the Lamp Road.

**The Lamplit Kingdom** held the whole road and held its water back with sluices, weirs, dams, locks and sea-walls. Over the capital's lake hung **the great lantern**, its oath that someone would always be watching for whoever was out on the water; every house set a small window candle for the same reason.

**What fell, and why.** The lantern's weight, through chains and a counterweight, held the great sluice open and kept the lower city dry. One evening the chain's last link wore through. No enemy, no curse, no crime: it was old. The lantern fell into its own reflection, the sluice dropped shut, and the lake rose over the city. The people took it as the kingdom's time ending, hung no second lantern, and went back south the way they had come. Everything else drowned slowly over centuries, with nobody left to keep the works. The folk say "The water rose to look for it": a saying, never a rule.

**Why the land greens.** The road runs from cold stone through a dry coast into the wet tropics, and every ruin is overgrown at its climate's speed: bare in the north, furred in the dry west, swallowed in the wet south. "Moss has kept the vigil the candles could not." Only living things turn green. The violet-dusk sky is the same everywhere.

**The four small wonders.** Never explained; nothing else is magic.
1. Some lamps keep burning with nobody to feed them.
2. A window candle can wake.
3. A forgotten water hides in mist until it is remembered.
4. A kept lamp remembers the way.

**The mist and the maps.** Forgetting looks like lavender mist: a water nobody remembers sinks into it and cannot be found. A chart is a remembering; when a living flame carries one, the mist lets that water go for good. So buying a map is the only way to find a new place, and only Murk, who holds the last of the kingdom's charts, can sell them.

**The dark.** "The Gloom" is just night on cold water: patient, not cruel.

## 3. The story by acts

**The arc.** The kingdom's great lantern fell and its capital drowned; its people went back down their old water road to the orchard country where they began, and their story stops there. You, one of the capital's last window candles, follow that road, towing the last candles' home behind your raft, and you bring the whole road home. Each water shows one chapter and one **clue you cannot miss**: a landmark the camera frames as the mist lifts on your first evening there (section 4).

**Prologue: One Warm Light.** A short opening film, four lines shown one at a time: "A kingdom hangs a great lantern over its lake." / "The lantern falls. The kingdom follows." / "Its last candles fish from lamp to lamp." / "You are one of them." As the lantern sinks, a pale flank (the Lantern-Swallower's, section 6) takes its ember. Then you wake at **the Landing**, the sea cave where the capital's candles washed ashore, aboard the barge-house by Murk's banked stove, and tow it up to the Mossmere. From then on it is home.

**Act I: What Fell** (waters 1–4). *What happened here?* A great city drowned because something old let go; its people were gardeners and keepers of light; nobody did this to them.

**Act II: The Cold Road** (waters 5–8). The kingdom's road out to the sea and down the fjord coast to a snow temple. *Is anyone still out here?* The kingdom was a road, not only a city; its lamps still burn; a few small fishers are still out on the far waters; a window candle is lit for someone coming home.

**Act III: Where They Turned** (waters 9–12). The first warm water, the kingdom's working coast. *Where did the people go?* They were not destroyed and did not vanish: at the edge of the warm sea they turned inland, back toward where they began. The kingdom was a good place that ended.

**Act IV: The Way Back** (waters 13–16). By canal and barge road to the orchard forest. *What became of them?* Their story stops at a stone hearth, raked over with ash the way you bank a fire for the night, and never stirred again.

**Epilogue: The Hearth Stirred.** The first time you come home from the last water, the closing film plays (section 9). Then the evenings go on.

The road only ever goes forward, and each leg is a real river, canal, lock stair or stretch of coast. It starts at **the Landing**, a sea cave on the coldest coast with a broken arch facing the dark north. The reaches are I the old kingdom, II the cold rim, III the falls and the gate, IV the green heart. "Murk" is the one line he says when he sells you that water's map (the Mossmere's is the first, so it has none).

## 5. The player character: the Wick

**What a Wick is.** A small, fat window candle of orchard-bee wax, so a small piece of the forest at the road's end. Some of the capital's candles washed down to the Landing on the night of the fall, and Murk laid them by his stove. Over the centuries, now and then, one woke. A candle lit to see someone off burns down; a candle lit for someone coming home keeps waiting. These waited so long, with nobody left to come home, that the ones that woke went out on the water themselves. "No one ever told it that it couldn't."

**Why it goes out.** Out at evening, home by morning: the shape of a window candle's life. A dozing Wick is asleep, not lost, and nothing a Wick does or fails to do puts it out.

**You.** In the story, "the Wick" means whichever of the five you play; the first of the five is also named the Wick. You are the latest candle to wake and the first to fish the whole road through. Not chosen, not special, just stubborn.

**Size.** Knee-high to the kingdom's people, whose doors are four Wicks tall: roughly 0.6 to 0.85 m; the exact height is not yet fixed.

**The look.** An egg-shaped body in a patched umber-rust cloak, its hem frayed into three or four points, with a pale wax collar. The head is a short, fat ivory candle with a melted rim and a few drips. A deep, hollow face recess holds two small glowing amber eyes, with no mask plate. A living flame burns on top. Stubby legs dangle over the raft's edge; it carries a long, bent driftwood rod with a bone reel and a tiny brass bell. Idle, a wax drip falls and the head tilts toward splashes. **The candle head is the signature.**

**The raft.** Lashed dark planks, a crooked pole with a hanging iron lantern, a tiny brazier, a lidded wicker creel, a bone rod-rest, an oar, an oxblood pennant and a small chime.

### The five Wicks

All five can be chosen. Each has a look and one always-on passive with no button; on screen it gets only its name and one line.

| Wick · title | look | its line | its passive, plainly | its nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Wick** · A Small, Stubborn Flame | round; umber cloak; amber flame and eyes | "Firsts and new bests sell higher." | the first of each kind, its first big one and every new best sell for more | collects firsts and bests of fish; its flame leans toward a first it holds |
| **Taper** · The Long Reach | tall and thin; slate cloak; pale-gold flame atop a crooked stovepipe of wax | "First bites are never common fish." | the first bite of each evening is never a common fish (where the water allows); in time, giants bite for it a little more | the rare-fish seeker, always one more water; "It would rather be fishing." |

Moths stream into its light |
| **Tallow** · The Stout Candle | broad and squat; olive hood; a low, deep-orange flame | "Stout enough for one more fish." | carries one more fish than anyone, and the water gives it a little more | the hauler, last to turn for home; "Slow to light. Slower to go out." A wicker basket beside its creel |
| **Ashwick** · Burned Once, Burns Back | charred grey wax; tattered charcoal hood; red-orange flame; eyes like cracks in a coal | "A fish that gets away comes back." | an ordinary fish that gets away comes straight back once, tired (never a giant or legend) | the second chance; "It went out once. It came back angry." (a candle's joke about being blown out, nothing more) |

**Where the other Wicks are.**
- **By the stove.** The ones not out on the water doze aboard the barge and travel with it.
- **On the far waters.** A few woke long ago, went out and stayed. They are never met, heard or sought. Their only sign is the stool, the lit lantern and the seven knots by the fishing hole at the Rime Sound. "That is all."
- **In the water.** Candles that sank on the night of the fall doze in the drowned towns and the deep. A cold candle can catch again some day; nothing says when.
- **An empty place by the stove** holds only an old wax drip. It has no story and stays empty.
- **The First Wick**, the first window candle ever lit, in the Inkwood's village, is only a story the Wicks tell, never a character.

## 6. The cast so far

The rules every character keeps are in section 12. In short: nobody is an enemy; everyone is kind, busy or asleep; **only Murk speaks**.

### Old Murk

**Look.** An enormous, hunched umber toad with tufts of rust-moss, ochre and pale lichen, heavy-lidded, faintly glowing pale-yellow eyes, a drooping lip and a moth-eaten shawl. His towering, teetering pack holds jars of murky bait, empty birdcages, coiled rope, dead lanterns, rods, a crooked umbrella and a fish-bone wind chime ("Everything here was somebody's last hope once."). He carries a dim sage-green swamp-lamp on a hooked pole, the south's colour carried north. His barge is a withered bronze lily-pad barge with a hand bell on a post: the sound of home. He blinks slowly, his throat-sac swells before he speaks, and his tongue flicks at passing moths.

**Story.** The old toads are a long-lived people of the warm south, the kingdom's neighbours and traders, never its subjects. Murk, the barge-trader of the whole road, was in the capital the night the lantern fell. He poled the last barge of the people's things south; it sank under him at the great bend of the Siltway, and he swam. When the people were gone he came back up the road alone to the candles, because a trader goes where his last customers are, and banked his fire in the barge-house's stove, as toads and people did for a household that was away, to be stirred when someone came home with news of the road. "I've had centuries. Fewer than I'd like."

**What he does.** At **the tally** he counts and weighs every fish, writes it in his **Ledger** and lets it go before dawn ("Bring me something with a story."). His charts are the kingdom's own, in four chart-makers' hands, each sealed with wax pressed into the shape of the water's legend.

**Nature.** Wry and tender, never secretive: he would tell you anything if you asked, and you never need to. His kindness is never stated ("Murk is not a charity. Murk is barely a toad."). He is never younger than the fall.

**His voice:** "Clams. Spend them here. I'm the only shop left." · "Let one go and the water remembers." · "That one had a name before you met it." · "Nothing? The water keeps its own counsel. Go again." · after the ending, "Warm in here. Don't tell anyone."

### The old toads (Murk's kin)

A few very old toads live quietly in Lanternroot Lagoon's stilt village, never seen whole and never speaking. You see only their signs: lanterns at dusk, one sage green among the amber; scraps on the offering posts for the Votive Shark (a fish the village feeds "and asks nothing"); a moored barge; pipe smoke; a porch bell answering yours. Out of old habit they still drop scraps from the Kitchen Stair for Emberbelly (a fish that waits under it), never enough. They do not trade.

### The people, then and now

**Then.** The people were humans: tall, warm-handed folk of the warm south who fished at evening by the basketful, laughing, and built every work the Wick sails through. Back in the orchard country the kingdom grew small: a village, then a few houses, until the last household banked its hearth one night and never came back to stir it.

**Now.** You never see one, only what they left: doors, tools, baskets, names on the pier, worn statues. On screen they are "the kingdom", "the people", "somebody", "the gardeners". Some things still miss them: a fen fish that "likes to be caught by the basketful, by people who are laughing"; a Paper-Lantern Jelly "still looking for the hand"; the lantern-gourds still ripening.

### Animals

Frogs are ordinary animals, not folk: the south's voice. None in the stone north except a first few in Sporewood's warm hollow; reed frogs in Rushlight Fen; the first chorus, heard not seen, at Lampmoss Slough; loudest on the Siltway; a full chorus in the Inkwood. There are also fireflies (keeping their distance from the raft), moths at the Wick's flame, herons on the warm coast, night birds in the south (Murk's empty cages are a joke that lands late), bats under the Inkwood canopy, and bees, humming for real only in the Inkwood. Pairs of amber eyes sit at the edge of the lantern light every night: atmosphere, always.

### The great fish

**Fish are fish.** They glow because deep water and the dark make fish glow; the giants are just very old; the legends are fish with habits the people noticed and named. No fish is made of what was lost or carries the plot. Landing one is meeting a very old fish, and Murk lets every one go home. Each water has **one legend** and a few **giants**.

**Who you meet on the road:** Murk, at home. Out on the water: no voices, only lamps, great fish, frogs and, at the very end, the Banked Fire's breath. The north is empty; the world fills up as it greens.

## 7. Home: the Slow Hearth and the Well

**The Slow Hearth** was a barge family's floating home that came north with a barge train and lay in the sluice cove when the lantern fell: a broad, low hull about 12 m long, a timber cabin with small square windows and a stove pipe, and a flat roof under a patched awning. The Chart's table stands under the awning, and **the Journal**, the book of the Wick's ink prints of every fish, lies on a lectern by the door. Murk's barge lies alongside; the raft is tied at the stern. The cabin door's sill carries **sixteen carved stones**, one per water, each with its water's glyph and a one-word leg mark (river, lock, canal, coast, sea, cut). Murk inks each stone when home first reaches its water and gilds it when that water's legend is landed. The stones are not magic.

**Home moves with you.** Every water has one mooring, at its old landing under its kept lamp. The first time you go to a new water you tow home there. Going back to an earlier water is no voyage at all, because a kept lamp remembers the way. The barge never sinks, drifts or needs keeping. Behind this sits a saying for this brief only, never on screen: "A hearth goes where the household goes."

**It fills as you go.** The legends' prints hang along the eaves, the Lantern-Swallower's and the Banked Fire's facing each other. Murk chalks orders on a board, moss spreads across the roof, lanterns line the rails, and things fished up get fitted aboard: a buoy-bell on the bow that rings at dawn (Murk's bell answers), amber glass from the Gate's lamps in the windows, window boxes of night lilies. From out on the water, home is warm windows across the dark.

**The Well.** A round glass hatch amidships opens onto the barge's old wet well, where barge families kept their catch alive. A fish worth keeping can go there instead of to Murk. Below it you swim as a slow eye along an underwater road of lamp-stones, one room per water the barge has moored at, each built from that water's drowned works, the fish at the size you caught them. The rooms are a picture, like the Chart, not a real place.

## 8. Fish, light, clams and finds

- **Why the Wicks fish:** a window candle was lit to wait for a fisher coming home. With no fishers left, the Wicks became the fishers.
- **Why a fish is worth anything:** to Murk a fish is news: where, how big, what hour. He pays for the story and lets it go. Sizes are told in pounds.
- **Clams** are the old toads' shell money, dug from the Siltway's beds, and the only money left; the kingdom's coins drowned with it.
- **Light is the theme.** Small lights kept by habit and kindness: a window candle, a landing lamp kept by its own water's people, a toad's banked fire. The great lantern went out; the small lights did not.
- **Finds.** Sometimes the line comes up heavy and slow, with no fight, bringing up a spoon, a coin, a lamp-glass, a bell or a purse that sank with the kingdom's works. Murk buys some; some are fitted to the barge or raft, or worn by the Wick. One find in each water is somebody's whole fortune ("Full. Nobody came to collect."). A find has no power and opens nothing. **Nothing human ever comes up** (no face, figure, name plate or bones), nothing left on purpose comes up, and the story's clues are never finds.

## 9. The ending

**At the Inkwood.** On your first evening there the camera frames the banked hearth atop the Kitchen Stair. Far off in the mist the Banked Fire comes up to breathe once: a long warm glow, her back turning, never the whole fish. Nothing is caught or won.

**The Hearth Stirred.** A short closing film plays once, on the first trip home from the Inkwood. The road triggers it, never a fish, so it cannot be missed. At first light Murk counts the tally in silence and turns the Journal's pages slowly, the whole road print by print. Then he rakes his banked fire open with his pole. It comes up full and gold, the warmest light in the game, and spills out of the door and windows onto the black water. The people's own hearth at the top of the stair stays as they left it. The waking Wicks turn toward the fire, and your flame leans toward it. Four lines, one at a time: "Far away, a banked fire breathes." / "At home, Murk rakes his open." / "The road comes home with you." / "You are still very small. You are still burning." The title becomes the barge with its hearth full, and there is one button: **Keep Drifting**.

**Why so small.** The Wick saves nothing and relights nothing. By the toads' and the people's custom, a banked fire is stirred when someone comes home with news of the road, and the Wick is the first to bring all of it. The sky never changes, and the evenings go on.

## 10. How the game tells its story

The story is told through the films, one landmark per water, the tow, the greetings, short card lines, Murk's lines, the legends' prints, the sill's stones, sound, and the Chart drawing itself. Almost everything in this brief stays off screen.

- **Who speaks:** only Murk, one line at a time, rarely, never during a bite. Nothing speaks out on the water.
- **How much:** film lines at most 60 characters, present tense. A card line (a map, a find) at most 8 words. A Wick's line at most 8 words and 36 characters; only the chosen Wick's shows. A fish has one or two short journal sentences. Murk says one line per map, once. Three one-sentence notes appear on the Chart once their fish is landed (the Lantern-Swallower's, the Old Shipwright's, and the Barge-Shadow's in Murk's hand).
- **Never:** panels of text, quests, puzzles, reveals in text, or odds shown during play.
- **Sound:** silence and bells in the north; wind and surf on the cold road; spray and heat lightning on the dry coast; frogs, insects and bees swelling into a chorus in the south.
- **The Chart** is an old hand-drawn road chart that assembles in the order you travel, each reach in its own chart-maker's hand: court surveyors' fine ink, sea-road wardens' lamp marks, harbour-masters' soundings, barge pilots' strip maps, and the Inkwood's plain orchard plan in an unsure hand. Nothing you do not own is drawn. At the end its border spells the acts.

## 11. The look a character must fit

The style is "hand-inked melancholy": a clear, starry night with no fog, drawn in ink.

- **Sky and air:** a deep indigo-violet starry night over drowned ruins, lavender mist, a soft pink nebula low behind the ruins, water that mirrors the stars. Depth comes from tone and layers, not fog. Mist is a dawn event, and dawn is pearl and pale gold, never orange-brown.
- **Palette:** night ink #0A0C19, deep indigo #1D245D, violet dusk #433E74, lavender mist #7E81B4, nebula pink #D296AE, candle amber #ED9E3B, pale gold #E8BE77, autumn ochre #B8893A, pale stone #D6D6E4. Blue and violet carry the frame; amber and gold are accents.
- **Warm light:** only the Wick's lantern casts warm light that lights the water. Every other warm glow (a lit window, a candle slit, a brazier, a stern lamp, a lantern head) is a small pinpoint that lights nothing around it, and its reflection is a thread, never a column. Lamps at water level are cold lavender. A new character may glow cold (lavender or pale starlight), or warm only as such a pinpoint. Murk is warm by colour, not by light: his umber hide is the one warm surface.
- **Green:** only on living things, greener along the road; never in the air, the sky or the words on screen. The only green lights are Murk's sage lamp and its one twin among the stilt lanterns at Lanternroot. Living things may glow dimly green (pale jade glow-moss, pale lime fireflies) but cast no light.
- **Form:** silhouette first. Near-black ink shapes with a pale rim light and even ink outlines. Faces are minimal, usually just glowing eyes. Shadows are violet, never grey or black.
- **Materials, drawn from what the world already shows:** old wax, worn brass, rust, rope, wicker, bone, driftwood, moss, lichen and wet stone; paper lanterns in the south.
- **Scale:** a Wick is knee-high to the kingdom's people. Fish are shown at honest size beside it, so a legend can dwarf it.
- **Never a ring or disc** round the raft, the Wick or any mark. Light is an edgeless pool.
- **The words on screen ("Drowned Filigree"):** no boxes. Words float on a soft, feathered pool of night ink, with pale-ink filigree (hook curls, a cast line and reeds) that draws itself in round a chosen word. Titles are spaced capitals; the catch card is an ink-wash field-journal page with a pale ink sketch, a handwritten pale-gold note and a red rubber stamp. Nothing loud.

## 12. Rules for any new character

**The creator's standing taste.**
1. "Art, atmosphere and fishing those are our key drivers." A character adds to how the world looks, sounds and fishes, not to its systems or its text.
2. There is no way to fail, and every catch feels like a win. No new character takes, threatens or blocks anything; Murk is the only one who trades.
3. Never draw a ring or disc round the raft or the player.
4. Real river and lake names are allowed, though every water and river so far has an invented name. No fish or creature takes a real species' name; every name is original.
5. Almost no words (section 10). **Only Murk speaks.** A new character has no dialogue.
6. Everything is original, borrowing nothing recognisable from other games.

**The world's guards, in plain words.**
- **No enemies.** Nothing is fought, nothing hunts, nothing is lost but the fish that got away. The Gloom is night, not a creature. *Not:* an eel that steals your catch.
- **No human face or figure, ever,** and the word "human" never appears on screen. *Not:* a ghostly fisherwoman on the pier.
- **No quests, puzzles or text reveals.** No character opens or unlocks anything. *Not:* a heron that shows you a hidden pool.
- **Fish are fish.** No corrupted or mutated catch as the plot, no fish made of lost things. *Not:* a fish woven from drowned letters.
- **No relic collector and no keeper.** The Wick collects firsts and bests of fish, nothing else. No one tends the Last Light or any lamp: the lamps that burn unfed have no keeper or lamplighter, and the burning stays unexplained. No keeper mystery either: the Wick has not done all this before, and Murk hides nothing. *Not:* an old lamplighter toad who keeps the Thin Water's lamp lit.
- **No single first flame.** The First Wick is only a folk tale, not the source of other flames. No Wick sits in a lamp to keep it lit, and the Wick never relights a fire as the climax. *Not:* a Wick revealed as the spark every lamp came from.
- **Death stays where it is:** the Pallid Deep's quiet remembrance and the temple's old lines at Pyrefall (the Ashen Guest's "Uninvited to every funeral", and a carp fed with alms). Wicks are not souls; no one ferries or guides the dead. Existing ferry names, such as the Sunk Ferry, stay; no new ones are added. *Not:* a ferryman frog.
- **No fleet, no lamp chain.** No fleet of the people sailing anywhere, and no chain of lamps lighting up along the road as you go. *Not:* a lamp that relights behind you at every water.
- **Names.** No "Kingdom's ___", "King's ___", "royal ___" or new "Pale ___" names. Avoid the words Soul, Charm, Vessel, Grub, Shade, Husk, Mask and Abyss in names; no new "Ashen" or "Ash-" names. No masks. A Wick never has horns, a white mask, black oval eyes or a grey-blue cloak. Never pair Ashwick or the Ashen Guest with the ending.
- **Fixed cast.** Murk is never younger than the fall and stays the only shop and the only voice. The old toads are never seen whole and never speak. The far-water Wicks are never met. The empty place by the stove stays empty: a wax drip, no story, no new Wick there. The playable Wicks are five.
- **Magic stays the four small wonders.** The ending never changes the sky.
- **Light.** No new warm light that lights the water, and no new green light (section 11).

**What a new character can be**, built only from the rules above:
- a fish of one of the waters (a common fish, a giant, or a small wry one), with a look and one or two journal sentences;
- an ordinary animal of the road (a frog, bird, bat, moth, salamander or turtle), behaving as an animal;
- an absent being shown only by the signs it leaves, like the far-water Wicks and the old toads;
- a sound, or a silent one-time greeting from a lamp.

It appears only as something seen, something heard, or one short card or journal line.

## 13. Open: where the docs leave room

1. Where did the last of the people go after the final hearth was banked, and are any still beyond the map? Left unanswered on purpose; the game never answers it.
2. Who are the far-water Wicks, who sat on the stool and tied the seven knots at the Rime Sound? That sign is their only one ("That is all."), and they are never met, heard or sought.
3. Who are the cold candles dozing in the drowned towns, and will any catch again?
4. How and when do Taper, Stub, Tallow and Ashwick wake? The story says one at a time, with no reason given, yet all five can be chosen from the start. How does the story read when "you" is one of the other four?
5. Tallow's lore line is settled ("Slow to light. Slower to go out."); its origin among the window candles is not. One idea, not adopted: the thick kitchen candle that burned all night, the sturdiest of them.
6. Ashwick's name is not final.
7. The exact height of a Wick (between about 0.6 and 0.85 m).
8. The old toads as a people: their numbers, other homes, names and customs beyond the banked fire. They still may not speak or be seen whole.
9. Murk's life before the fall, and his centuries on the road.
10. The never-charted east (a heathland, a long warm gulf with a drowned town at its head, an east-coast forest, an island, a southern peninsula of hills and savanna) is "room for later sheets, never a promise of anyone there", with no on-screen name.
11. The world beyond the far isle, over the Far Sea.
12. An empty kingdom town by the salt pans whose wells failed, and a ruined watchtower on the north-east peninsula: both unexplained.
13. The people named the legends (settled). The stories behind each name are open, told only through things left behind or a fish's journal sentences, never a text reveal.
14. The many small places of each water (the Lily Maze, the Bell Pool, the Gill Harp, the Well of Ledgers, the Coin Run, Lamp Row, the Sunk Ferry, Rootgate Creek and more) have fish and looks but little story.
15. More animals along the road, as long as they stay animals, not folk.
